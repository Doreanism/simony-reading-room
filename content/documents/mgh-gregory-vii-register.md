The *Registrum* of Gregory VII (Hildebrand, r. 1073–1085) preserves the official record of papal letters and synodal acts from the pontificate that gave the Gregorian Reform its name. The collection survives in the eleventh-century Vatican register (Reg. Vat. 2), copied from the original chancery rolls, and contains some 390 letters and decrees across nine books, including the *Dictatus papae* (II, 55a) and the Lent-synod legislation of 1074 and 1075 against simony and Nicolaitism. Erich Caspar's critical edition appeared in two fascicles (Books I–IV in 1920, Books V–IX in 1923) as *Epistolae selectae* II in the Monumenta Germaniae Historica, and remains the standard text. This volume is the 1955 photographic reprint (2. unveränderte Auflage) of both fascicles bound together.
