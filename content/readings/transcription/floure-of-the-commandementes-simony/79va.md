that gyueth the benefyce as of hym that whiche receyueth it/somtyme of the one alonely/somtyme ne by the one ne by the other as yf for example some gyueth vnto the famylyers of the bysshop ony thynge for to moeue hym to gyue ony benefyces. He that whiche knoweth nothynge of the gyfte ne semblably the bysshop by the meane albeit of the whiche gyft sholde be made donnayson of the benefyce. Suche donayson sholde be made symonyall/but alonely he that whiche hath made the donayson vnto the famplyers of the bysshop shal be in mortal synne/and symonyer as vnto that. And he that is beneficed by suche maner vnknowen sholde be boude whan it shode come vnto his knowlege to renounce vnto the sayd benefyce et to the other goodes presentes of the same/but he is not boude to restore the goodes dyspended durynge the tyme of the poore conseyence. for to knowe clerely whan a man comytteth symonye in permyttynge of benefyces/or whan ony renounceth vnto his benefyce/or whan ony gyueth a benefyce/or whan ony receyueth it. It behoueth to se et to consyder the codycyons/the maners/et the caules/et yf there ne be gyftes/ne promesses/ne prayers of lordes or of fredes. et cetera. Moreouer it behoueth pryncypally to regarde the fynall ende to the whiche a man hath entencyon for to come. for yf the condycyons et the fyne be made purely for the loue of god he is well entred in the benefyce by the dore. And yf a man entre otherwyse in benefyce than by god/symonye is to be dredde for he is not entred by the dore.
