To the objection — that this is either on the part of the soul or on the part of the flesh — it must be said that the reason is drawn from the *whole man*, whose role it is to contract marriage, and who is the subject of marriage. In this man, the impediment arises not by reason of *uncleanness* or of any *absolute quality* occurring in the flesh, but on account of a *defective signification*. For because he contracted marriage with one woman, he signified Christ; but because he departed from one and contracted marriage with a second, he departed from that signification; and so there is a *defect of the Sacrament*, namely of unity, which ought to have been signified in the Sacrament of marriage and of orders.

4. And by this the following objection, concerning the defect of the Sacrament, is also clear; for I say that in him the Sacrament *of unity* is defective, and whether this defect arises on his own part or on the part of the wife, it renders him less fit for the Sacrament of orders. If, then, you ask which Sacrament is defective, that of orders or that of matrimony, I say that the Sacrament *of orders* is defective, because there is not that perfection which can signify what the order requires. Furthermore, there is a defect of the Sacrament *of matrimony* — a defect, I say, not of mere *negation*, as it is in him who was never married, but a defect of *privation*, where there is something of the signification and something of privation, which generates a certain enormity or deformity, by reason of which he ought not to be admitted to so noble a Sacrament.

From these things the following questions are clear: namely, why division impedes and not corruption — because no Sacrament is taken away, nor is one rendered defective by reason of *corruption* as it is by reason of *division*.

To the question why corruption does not impede the consecration of a man as it does that of a woman: certain jurists say that the bishop signifies the Church militant, while the virgin signifies Christ, who was a virgin. But this does not stand up to sound reasoning. And so it must rather be said that the bishop signifies Christ, while the woman signifies the Church triumphant, or the Church itself, which is a virgin and has never been anything but the wife of Christ; whereas Christ, although He was a virgin, nevertheless had the Synagogue not as a wife, but as a concubine.
