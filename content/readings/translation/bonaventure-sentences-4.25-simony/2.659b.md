And thus the sufficiency \[of the enumeration\] is plain. — Or, alternatively, the *gift* may be distinguished as a gift of the hand, of the tongue, and of the heart, with two motives, fear and love; and thus there will be five modes. And by this the number and the sufficiency are made plain.

To what is objected concerning fame, it must be said that fame is a great good for one who does not seek it, as for a just man, but for one who seeks it and labors to attain it, it is not a great good, as for the hypocrite; whence Proverbs 10: *The memory of the just is with praises, and the name of the wicked shall rot.*

To what is objected concerning consanguinity, the case is not parallel: for the priesthood of the old law was carnal, but that of the new is spiritual. — And thus those things are plain which pertain to this part.
