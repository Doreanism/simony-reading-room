1. To the objection concerning the imposition of the presbyter’s hands, it must be said that “presbyter” is there called “elder”; and the Apostle understood himself by the imposition of the presbyter’s hand — the Gloss: “he calls the bishop a presbyter,” or “of the presbyter,” that is, “of the presbyters,” because, as the Gloss says, at least three priests ought to impose hands; nevertheless they do not impose hands as giving the power or the order, since this can and ought to be given by one alone. Hence they do not bless, but only the bishop does so; rather, this is done as a sign of his consecration.

2. And from this the next point is clear, since the imposition of hands does not effect ordination, except that imposition which is joined to a blessing.

3. To the objection concerning perfection, it must be said that there is a perfection of sufficiency and a perfection of overflowing. For something to make another like itself or perfect, it does not suffice to be perfect with the perfection of sufficiency, but with the perfection of overflowing; and since in the priest there is the perfection of sufficiency, in the bishop, by reason of the added eminence, there is the perfection of overflowing: therefore this perfection suffices to beget perfect sons, but not the first.
