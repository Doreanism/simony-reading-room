### Conclusion

*Bishops alone can confer holy orders.*

SOLUTION (*Respondeo*): It must be said that, as the sacred canons declare, by common law no one can dispense orders—especially holy orders—except the bishop alone. This statute has its origin in sacred Scripture and in the ordering of the Holy Spirit. From sacred Scripture: because the Lord had two kinds of disciples, some in the number of twelve, others in the number of seventy. The first he named Apostles, and upon them he founded the Church—though founding them first upon himself; the others he called simply disciples. To the Apostles was the power of imposing hands and consecrating first granted, but not to the simple disciples, as is clear from the text and from the *Glossa* on Acts 8. And since bishops succeed the Apostles, while simple priests succeed the disciples, as is held at Distinction 21 \[D. xxi\], chapter *In novo testamento*: thus it is clear that, according to the form of the primitive Church, the authority of ordaining and imposing hands is proper to bishops. Hence the Apostle, writing to Timothy, 1 Timothy 5: *Impose hands hastily on no one*.

The *counsel of the Holy Spirit* likewise agrees, by which both the Scriptures were brought forth and the constitutions of the Church were established. For since order is power, or is conveyed in being ordered, it ought therefore to be conveyed by one who has power, and to the extent that he has power. Now to be able to distribute and confer grades does not belong to just anyone who has power, but to him who has preeminence in power, as is evident even in the governance of a secular court. Therefore, in order that order be rightly conveyed, the Holy Spirit disposes that it be conveyed by one who not only has order but who has *orders*—through which he has power like others—and the *eminence of dignity*, through which he has power over others.
