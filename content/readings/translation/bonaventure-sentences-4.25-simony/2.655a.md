## QUESTION IV

*Whether the condition of freedom is necessary for the reception of orders.*

Fourthly and finally it is asked whether the condition of freedom is necessary for the reception of orders. And that it is, the following arguments seem to show:

1. Distinction 54: “Let no bishop presume to promote another man’s slave to the clerical state.”

2. Likewise, in the same decretal, chapter 2: “Let no one of servile condition be promoted to sacred orders unless he first obtains lawful freedom from his masters.”

3. Likewise, if a slave enters religion, he is returned to his master and cannot enter unless he is made free: therefore by parity of reasoning, unless he is made free, he cannot be promoted to sacred orders.

4. Likewise, no one can serve two masters, as the Savior himself testified: therefore, since the clerical office requires the whole man to be dedicated to it, no one who is in the power of another can be promoted to it.

ON THE CONTRARY: 1. “A wise servant shall rule over foolish sons,” as is said in Proverbs 17: therefore it seems that where there is wisdom and goodness of life, nothing on the side of servitude stands in the way.

2. Likewise, an order denotes spiritual power, while servitude denotes bodily subjection; but bodily subjection is not incompatible with what is spiritual: therefore etc.

3. Likewise, God is no respecter of persons: therefore the service of a slave pleases him as much as the service of a master: therefore just as masters can be taken up to divine worship and the clerical state, so likewise can slaves.

4. Likewise, slaves can receive the other Sacraments just as freely as masters, and are admitted to them just as commonly: therefore by parity of reasoning it seems that they can be admitted to orders.
