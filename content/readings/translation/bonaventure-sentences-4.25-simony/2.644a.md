## QUAESTIO II

Whether a heretical bishop can confer orders.

Second, it is asked whether a heretical bishop can confer orders. And it seems that he can.

1. Augustinus, against Parmenianus, says: “As we say of baptism, that it can be given by them, so also of ordination”; and there are several authorities to this effect in the text.

2. Likewise, by reason it seems so: for as the priest stands to the power of consecrating, so the bishop stands to the power of ordaining; but a priest, however heretical, if he has the intention of doing what the Church does, truly consecrates: therefore a heretical bishop also truly ordains, if he observes the ecclesiastical form.

3. Likewise, if a Catholic is ordained within the Church and afterward lapses into heresy, if he returns again, he is not reordained; therefore by lapsing into heresy he did not lose his orders, and by parity of reasoning neither does a bishop lose his dignity or episcopal grade: therefore, while remaining a heretic, he retains that dignity or excellence of order; therefore he also retains in fact the operation that follows from it: therefore if he ordains, the man has been ordained.

4. Likewise, the character is just as truly in the wicked as in the good, and is just as truly impressed upon the wicked as upon the good: therefore by parity of reasoning it is just as well given by the wicked or perverse as by the good: therefore, since the essence of order consists in the character, the conclusion is clear, etc.
