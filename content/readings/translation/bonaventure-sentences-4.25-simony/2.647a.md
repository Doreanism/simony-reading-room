## QUAESTIO IV.

Whether one who confers orders simoniacally thereby becomes a heretic.

Fourth it is asked whether one who confers orders simoniacally thereby becomes a heretic. And it seems that he does.

1. Gregorius in the *Register*, and it is found in *Causa* I, *quaestio* I, *Presbyter si per pecuniam*; and below: “That to buy or sell tithes or an altar or the Holy Spirit is simoniacal heresy no one of the faithful is unaware.”

2. Likewise, in the same place: “Whoever is ordained for money is promoted in order to become a heretic.”

3. Likewise, *Causa* I, *quaestio* I, *Eos qui per pecunias*; and below: “More tolerable is the impious heresy of Macedonius and of those around him who assailed the Holy Spirit; for they in their madness declared the Holy Spirit a creature and a servant of God the Father and the Son; but these men make the same Holy Spirit their own servant”: therefore it seems to be a most impious heresy.

4. Likewise, everyone who reckons that the gift of God can be possessed for money is in error and is to be judged a heretic; but everyone who sells or buys orders reckons that the gift of God can be possessed for money: therefore, etc.

5. Likewise, in matters of nature there is no greater error than to attribute to one contrary its opposite, as if one were to call white black; and in matters of morals none is greater than to call good evil: therefore neither in things divine is any error greater than to claim that the grace of God is not grace; but this is what everyone claims who strives to buy or sell it: therefore, etc.
