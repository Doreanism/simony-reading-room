# COMMENTARY ON DISTINCTION XXV.

On the dispensing of holy orders.

It is also asked whether heretics cut off from the Church, etc.

## DIVISION OF THE TEXT.

Above the Master treated of orders; here he treats of the power of conferring orders. And since this power requires both proper matter and proper administration, the Master first treats of the power of conferring orders; second, of the vice which arises in connection with the giving and receiving of orders, namely simony, at the place: *Concerning simoniacs, however, there is no doubt*; third, of the fitness of those to be ordained, especially with regard to age, at the place: *Concerning the age of those to be ordained*, in the final chapter.

The first part has three sections. In the first he argues toward the side—

## TREATMENT OF THE QUESTIONS.

For the understanding of the present distinction two principal questions are raised.

First, concerning the power of ordaining.

Second, concerning the capacity of those to be ordained.

Concerning the first, four questions are asked.

First, whether the bishop alone can ordain.

## ARTICLE I.

On the power of ordaining.

## QUESTION I.

Whether the bishop alone can ordain.

The first question proceeds as follows, and it is shown that the bishop alone can ordain.

1. Hebrews 7: *Without any contradiction that which is less is blessed by that which is greater*; but the conferring of orders is by way of blessing: therefore it is necessary that he who confers orders should have an eminence beyond all the orders. But no one is such except the bishop: therefore, etc.
