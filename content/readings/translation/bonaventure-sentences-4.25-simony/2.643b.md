5. To the objection that the priest surpasses the deacon by more, etc., it must be said that it is true that he surpasses him by more than a bishop surpasses an existing bishop over whom he has no power; but not by more than the one who is a bishop in act surpasses the one who is a bishop in potency. Moreover, although he may surpass him by more in the order according to which the character of being ordained is considered, nevertheless not in the order according to which the character of being one who ordains is considered, which exists in the bishop, not in the presbyter. And so that argument does not hold.

To the final question, how a bishop blesses a bishop, it must be said that there is no impropriety here, because the one blessed is blessed only insofar as he lacks the order or the episcopal dignity; and in this respect the other is greater. — But then there still remains the question how he blesses an archbishop, since the archbishop is superior, and “no one can give what he does not have.” If you say that he does this by the authority of the Supreme Pontiff, who is above him, then no less remains the question concerning the Supreme Pontiff himself: how is he blessed by the bishop of Ostia? If you say that Christ gave him this authoritative power, then on the contrary: suppose he did not exist, and the power were not committed to another; could not the Supreme Pontiff be consecrated? — And for this reason it must be said that what the Apostle says is infallibly true—that the lesser is blessed by the greater, in respect of the state in which he is blessed; without contradiction it is true that no one gives another a greater dignity than he himself has, nor can he give the least order that he does not himself have. Therefore the bishop, when consecrating an archbishop or the Pope, in no way confers upon him anything beyond episcopal consecration. But the latter receives an ampler authority and jurisdiction from the election of his subjects, from his own consent, and from the institution of the Lord, who ordained Petrus and his successors over the whole Church, willing that all who shall have been canonically elected have the same power that Petrus had.
