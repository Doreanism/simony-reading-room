6. To the objection drawn from continence, the response is already plain; for continence belongs to the sacred orders not as a matter of their being, but of their well-being.

ON THE CONTRARY: 1. Corruption of the flesh does not impede the reception of the Sacrament; therefore neither does division, by parallel reasoning.

2. Likewise, if division of the flesh impedes — namely that which takes place with a wife — then since that which takes place with a harlot is far more shameful, it would impede much more strongly; but it does not impede; therefore neither does the former.

3. Likewise, if division of the flesh impedes, this is either by reason of something occurring in the flesh or by reason of something occurring in the soul. By reason of something occurring in the flesh: this does not seem to be the case, for it leaves no different disposition than would the knowledge of a single woman. By reason of something occurring in the soul: this is not so, since, the wife having died, nothing remains of the marriage.

4. Likewise, I ask: why does it impede? If on account of a defect of the Sacrament, I ask: of which one? If of marriage, on the contrary: he who has never contracted marriage is more deficient with respect to the Sacrament of marriage than he who has contracted it twice; therefore he is not deficient with respect to the Sacrament of marriage. If of the Sacrament of orders, on the contrary: bigamy is not reckoned with respect to the Sacrament of orders, but with respect to marriage; therefore the order does not fail.

The question therefore is: why does division impede, and not corruption?

And again: why does corruption impede in a woman, so that she may not be consecrated, while it does not similarly impede in a bishop?
