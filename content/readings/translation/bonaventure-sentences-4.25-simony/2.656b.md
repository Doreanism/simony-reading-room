—and unwilling. If with his master’s knowledge and consent, or even without his opposition, he creates against himself a prejudice such that he can no longer reclaim him. If without the master’s knowledge and against his will, then either the presenter and the bishop are also ignorant, or one or both of them know. If they are ignorant, then the slave is bound to redeem himself, if he has money; but if he has not, and the Church is unwilling to redeem him, he must be returned to his master. If, however, the one ordaining and the presenter know, they are bound to the master in money, or in another slave, or in twofold restitution; and if they have not the means, the master can take his slave back. — Some, however, say that this does not hold if he has reached the priesthood; but in every case it seems that the master ought to be kept indemnified, while still retaining the right to claim him within a year; and some say \[the year runs\] from the ordination, others from the time of his coming to know of it. And all this is proved by those canons cited at Distinction 54.

There are many other matters determined in canon law concerning those who are to be ordained, which are drawn from various places of Scripture, and especially from 1 Timothy 3, and all of which are determined from Distinction 23 to the end of the first part. And since this matter belongs more to a lower science, let what has been said for the present suffice, lest we go beyond the bounds of our own science. It is true, however, that all such ordinances have their origin from Sacred Scripture, and all could be traced back to her as to their source, by anyone who had skill in canon and divine law.

## Doubts concerning the letter of the Master.

—ordained in two ways: either because he is ordained by one from whom he ought not \[to be ordained\], or because he is ordained otherwise than he ought to be. In the first way, in three ways: either because he is ordained by a heretic, or by a simoniac, as here in the case at hand; for then, if he knowingly does so and shares in the heresy or the simony, he cannot exercise \[his orders\], nor is dispensation easily granted, and this is what he means to say in the letter. — Or he is ordained by a Catholic not his own \[bishop\], without the leave and authority of his proper bishop; such a man ought to be deposed, Causa 7, quaestio 1, yet he can be dispensed with, if such an ordainer places some hope in the ratification of the proper bishop. — Or he is ordained by a bishop who has resigned both his place and office—
