Likewise, just as wisdom is attributed to the Son, so generosity is attributed to the Holy Spirit; but anyone who attributed foolishness to the Son would fall into heresy: therefore he who attributes avarice to the Holy Spirit is a heretic. But anyone who believes that the gifts of the Holy Spirit are sold or bought for money is of this sort, since he holds that the Holy Spirit is for sale: therefore, etc.

ON THE CONTRARY: 1. No heresy resides in a contract, but in belief; but simony resides in a contract of purchase and sale: therefore it does not seem that the simoniac becomes a heretic.

2. Likewise, heresy is a sin against the Son and against truth; but simony sins against the grace and goodness of the Holy Spirit: therefore he does not seem to be a heretic.

3. Likewise, he does not seem to fall into any sin by selling a gift of the Holy Spirit, because it is said in 1 Samuel 9 concerning Saul, and in 1 Kings 14 concerning the wife of Jeroboam, that both of them brought a payment to the holy prophets for a prophetic disclosure; and it is established that prophecy is a gift: therefore, etc.

4. Likewise, through the New Testament, 1 Corinthians 9: *If we sow spiritual things for you, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things?* Therefore it is permitted to receive carnal things for spiritual things: therefore he does not seem to sin.
