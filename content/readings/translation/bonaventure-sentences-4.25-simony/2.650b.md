And this position is more probable and can be proved by many authorities of the saints.

1. To the objection concerning Deborah, it must be said that hers was a temporal power, not a spiritual one. It is indeed permitted to women to rule temporally, but not with spiritual dominion — which is a sign that the one who rules bears the figure of Christ the head. Since, therefore, a woman cannot be the head of a man, she cannot for that reason be ordained.

2. To the objection concerning abbesses, it must be said that they do not hold the position of ordinary prelacy, but are substituted, as it were, in place of an abbot on account of the danger of cohabiting with men. Hence they cannot ordinarily absolve or bind. The priestly office, however, or that of any order to which this power and order are ordinarily given, has a signification that does not befit a woman, even though governance may befit women.

3. To the objection that orders has to do with the soul, it must be said that it does not have to do with the soul alone, but with the soul as joined to the flesh — and this by reason of the signification, which is found in the visible sign, and through this also in the body; and the execution and use of orders has to do with the joined \[soul and body\]. Since neither the signification of orders nor its dispensation befits a woman in the manner shown above, this point is clear.
