## QUESTION III.

Whether indivision of the flesh is necessary for the reception of orders.

Thirdly it is asked whether, for the reception of orders, indivision of the flesh is necessary. And that it is, seems to be the case.

1. In 1 Timothy 3: “A bishop must be the husband of one wife”; and the same is said of the deacon.

2. Likewise, Ambrosius writes to the people of Vercelli, and it is found in Distinction 34: “We acknowledge that the Apostle not only laid down this rule concerning the bishop and the presbyter, but that the Fathers also, in the proceedings of the Council of Nicaea, added that no man should be a cleric who has entered upon a second marriage.”

3. Likewise, he who has had a wife whose flesh is divided cannot be promoted; therefore much less can he who has had several wives. The first is plain from what Innocentius Papa says in Distinction 34: “If anyone, even a layman, has married a widow, whether before baptism or after, let him not be admitted to the clergy.”

4. Likewise, the priest or bishop bears the figure of Christ; but Christ was not divided, but rather united in unity; therefore in such a person the signification of the order fails; therefore the order itself also fails, since signification is essential to the Sacrament.

5. Likewise, bigamy is a property that is never lost, nor can he who is a bigamist become non-bigamist; therefore if it impedes the Sacrament, it seems that he renders himself incapable of receiving orders.
