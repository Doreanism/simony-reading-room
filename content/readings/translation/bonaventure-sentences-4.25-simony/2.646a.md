ON THE CONTRARY: 1. Gregorius says, in *Causa prima, quaestione prima*: “Whoever strives to receive a sacred order by the payment of a price is no priest, but merely desires to be called one in vain.” Therefore an order cannot be received through simony, and therefore neither can a simoniac confer it.

2. Again, Leo Papa argues thus, in *Causa prima, quaestione prima*: “Grace, if it is not freely given or received, is not grace; but simoniacs do not receive freely; therefore they do not receive the grace which is operative above all in ecclesiastical orders. But if they do not receive it, they do not possess it; and if they do not possess it, they can give it to no one, either freely or not freely.”

3. Again, the gifts of the Holy Spirit are in the power of God, not of man — for they are called and indeed are gifts of the Holy Spirit precisely because he himself bestows them. Therefore no one can possess them except from the Holy Spirit. But the Holy Spirit, being most generous, can sell nothing nor does he sell anything; therefore his gifts can neither be sold for money nor be measured by a price. If, then, order is a gift of the Holy Spirit, it is impossible for it to be sold or bought for a price; therefore it cannot be conferred by any simoniac either.

4. Again, in the sacred orders, namely the diaconate and the priesthood, the Holy Spirit is given, as is clear from what is said there: *Receive the Holy Spirit*. But the Holy Spirit cannot be sold; therefore neither can the sacred orders, which are conferred precisely by his bestowal. *Proof of the minor*: what can be bought and sold can be reduced to servitude; but the Holy Spirit cannot be reduced to servitude. Therefore, etc.

It is asked, then, since simony is the deliberate will to buy or sell something spiritual, how it can fall within the conferral of sacred orders, or of other spiritual things, since these, it would seem, cannot be sold.

### Conclusion

*A simoniac bishop can confer orders simoniacally.*
