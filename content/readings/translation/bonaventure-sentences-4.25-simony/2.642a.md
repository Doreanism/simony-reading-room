4. Likewise, it belongs to him to confer orders in the Church to whom it belongs to generate perfect sons from the Church, since order establishes one in a state of perfection; but this belongs only to him who is the spouse, and this is the bishop: therefore, etc.

5. Likewise, it is of greater dignity to dispense orders than to administer the Sacrament of confirmation; but the second is reserved to bishops alone: therefore the first as well.

ON THE CONTRARY (*Sed contra*): 1. 1 Timothy 4: *Do not neglect the grace which is in you through the imposition of the hands of the presbyter*: therefore in the primitive Church it belonged to the presbyters to impose hands on the bishops, and so likewise to ordain: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, the ordination of the principal orders is performed by the imposition of hands: therefore to whomever the imposition of hands belongs, the power of ordaining also belongs; but the former belongs to presbyters: therefore, etc. *Proof of the middle*: at Distinction 23 \[D. xxiii\]: “When a presbyter is ordained, while the bishop blesses him and holds his hand over his head, let all the presbyters who are present also hold their hands over his head alongside the hand of the bishop”: therefore, etc.

3. Likewise, that which is perfect has the capacity to produce one of its own kind; but the priestly order is a perfect order: therefore it has the capacity to produce one of its own kind: therefore it seems that a priest can make a priest.

4. Likewise, it is a greater thing to confect the Body of the Lord than to ordain someone to some ecclesiastical grade; but the former belongs not only to the bishop but also to priests: therefore by parity of reasoning it seems that the ordination of clerics belongs to them as well.

5. Likewise, a priest stands higher above a deacon than a bishop stands above another bishop; but a bishop can promote and consecrate another as bishop: therefore much more can a priest make a deacon.

For this reason there is a question: since the lesser is blessed by the greater, how can a bishop bless a bishop, and—what is more—an archbishop, indeed—what is still greater—the bishop of Ostia bless the Pope?
