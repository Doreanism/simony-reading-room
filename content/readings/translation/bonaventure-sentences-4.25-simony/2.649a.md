## ARTICLE II.

On those who can receive orders.

Next, with regard to the second article, an inquiry is made concerning the possibility of receiving orders, on which four questions are asked.

The first asks whether, in order for orders to be received by someone, the male sex is required, or whether

## QUESTION I.

Whether the male sex is required for the reception of orders.

As to the first question, that the male sex is required is shown as follows:

1. First thus: no order can be conferred on one who does not have the natural possibility or aptitude for it; but no one has the possibility for orders who does not have the aptitude for the tonsure and crown; and no one has natural aptitude for this whom it always befits to have the head veiled. If therefore by nature it befits only a man to pray with head unveiled, while it befits a woman to pray with head veiled, as is said in 1 Corinthians 11, where nature itself teaches this — therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, no one is capable of receiving orders unless he bears the image of God, because in this sacrament a man in some way becomes God or godlike, since he is made a partaker of divine power. But by reason of his sex, the man is the image of God, as is said in 1 Corinthians 11. Therefore in no way can a woman be ordained.

3. Likewise, in orders spiritual power is given to the one ordained; but a woman is not capable of receiving such power, as is said in 1 Timothy 2: I do not permit a woman to teach in the Church, nor to have dominion over a man. Therefore neither can she receive orders.
