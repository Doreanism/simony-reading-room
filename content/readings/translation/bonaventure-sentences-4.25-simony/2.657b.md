I REPLY: It must be said that Symon wished to buy precisely so that he might afterwards sell; therefore each evil and sin is imputed to him, and for this reason both parties are named from him.

As to the objection drawn from the will, it must be said that, since Symon, already in the time of revealed grace, wished to buy a power both greater and more spiritual, and moreover wished to make a pact, he therefore sinned far more gravely and more enormously than the one who asked out of a certain liberality and who believed it to be a small matter; but the penalty of perpetual leprosy showed how great that crime was.

### DUB. III.

Likewise, a question is raised concerning what he says, that both parties are struck by the same sentence. For this seems to be wrongly said, because, as it appears, those who sell sin more gravely, since they value spiritual things at less than the buyers do: therefore they ought to be punished more gravely. — On the contrary it appears, since the buyer affords the occasion to the seller and receives beyond the principal: therefore if the receiver of usury sins more gravely than the giver, the case is plain, etc.

I REPLY: It must be said that, as to the kind of sin, there is uniformity, because each does injury to the grace of the Holy Spirit; but the sin is weighed as more grave according to the greater appetite and intensity of desire and according to the rank of the person selling or buying. For a bishop who sells sins more gravely than a subject who buys; nevertheless, because the kind of sin is the same, both are struck by the same sentence.
