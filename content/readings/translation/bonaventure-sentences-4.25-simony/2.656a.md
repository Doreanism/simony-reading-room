Since a slave, so long as he is in such a state, is ignoble and can be compelled to ignoble works, and therefore cannot bind himself wholly over \[to God’s service\], being subject to another: for this reason he cannot be promoted to sacred orders, as far as the honor of the Sacrament is concerned, though he does \[validly\] receive the order.

Nevertheless, as to the question whether the slave ought to be returned to his master: the answer is that either he has been ordained with his master’s knowledge and consent, or without his knowledge and against his will.

## Dub. I.

In this part there are doubts concerning the letter \[of the Master’s text\], and first an inquiry is made about what he says: *Nor should any of these be substituted in any clerical honor, even the slightest*. For he seems to say it amiss, because either such a one has an order, or he has nothing. If he has nothing, then he can be promoted by a Catholic bishop with a dispensation, as is plain in the case of heretics. If he has orders, then he has already been promoted.
