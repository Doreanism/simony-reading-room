## QUESTION II.

*Whether the use of reason is necessary for the reception of orders.*

Secondly it is asked whether the use of reason is necessary for the reception of orders. And that it is, the following arguments seem to show:

1. Because the Sacrament of orders is a nobler and more perfect Sacrament than the Sacrament of matrimony, and places one in a higher state; but the Sacrament of matrimony cannot be contracted by the insane and those lacking reason, as is expressly stated below: therefore, etc.

2. Likewise, this seems to follow *from the form*: for no one offers anything to one who does not understand; but in ordination the one ordaining says to the one ordained: “Receive the power,” etc.: therefore he presupposes in him discretion. If, then, he has no discretion, it does not seem that he can receive orders.

3. Likewise, the use of this Sacrament can belong to no one except one having the use of reason: therefore if it is given to another, it is given in vain: therefore, since “God does nothing in vain,” it seems that the Sacrament of orders is never to be conferred on one who lacks the use of reason.

4. Likewise, if someone were to offer a power to one who refuses, there would be no reception of the power; for, just as *conferral* depends on the will of the *giver*, so reception depends on the will of the *receiver*: therefore if the *giver* does not give unless he wills and intends, the *receiver* does not receive unless he wills and intends.
