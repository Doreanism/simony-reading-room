Indeed, if someone possessing such a thing should wish to buy it, not only would he not acquire any more, but he would even lose what he had. — In another way, *spiritual* is what is *joined* to *a bodily thing*, which is nevertheless consecrated by reason of that spiritual element and takes on *the character of something spiritual*. This, because it is spiritual, cannot *by right* be sold, since it cannot be put under a price; but *in fact*, by reason of the bodily thing, it can be sold; and such are the sacraments of the Church. There is also something *spiritual* which is *joined to a bodily thing* that nevertheless retains *the character of a temporal thing*, such as a consecrated chalice, or the right of patronage; for in these the consecration is joined to the silver, and the right of patronage to the land. And such a thing is called *spiritual* because it is *annexed to something spiritual*; and this can be sold *in fact*, but cannot be sold *by right* — yet it can pass in the sale together with that to which it is annexed. For a consecrated chalice can be sold, provided that the *consecration* is not sold but the *value of the silver*; and the land to which a right of patronage is annexed can be sold, but not the right of patronage itself by right, without simony being committed.

These things being noted, we must come to orders, in which there is something of the first kind, namely sanctifying grace; and something of the second, namely a ministerial power dispensed by means of some external sign, to which it is inseparably joined. The first it is impossible to purchase for a price or to sell; the second, however, can be given for a price, because it is inseparably joined to an exterior sacrament; and since that can be given for money, that power too can be sold. — And thus it is clear that a simoniac can confer orders simoniacally.

1–2. To the objections drawn from Gregorius and from Leo Papa the reply is clear: they speak of sanctifying grace, which can only be given freely, and which is what makes the recipient holy — not of the power that is joined to an external thing placed within human stewardship.

3.
