Likewise, it belongs to the wise man to look not only to the present but also to what is future: therefore if many lack discretion, of whom it is presumed that they will at some time be discreet, it seems that at least the order may be committed to them and that its exercise may remain in suspense.

Likewise, if anything on the part of the recipient were necessary in the ordination itself, it would be required that he at least express his consent, and that it be required of him, as is done in matrimony; but it is not done this way in ordination: therefore it does not seem that consent is necessary, nor consequently the use of reason.

### CONCLUSION.

*For the reception of the seven orders the use of reason is not required by necessity of the Sacrament, but by necessity either of precept or of fittingness.*

I respond: To this some say that for the reception of any of the orders the consent of reason is required, because in every order an office is committed in respect of an act elicited by reason; and an office is committed to no one except to him who wills to receive it: and therefore they say that the use of reason is required in the one ordained, otherwise nothing whatever takes place. — But this seems contrary to the decretal *De Clerico per saltum ordinato*, where it is touched on that someone was promoted to minor orders before the years of discretion. Moreover, every day we see little ones promoted to minor orders who neither know what an order is nor what is given there. And again, the use of confirmation is not before adult age, and yet the Sacrament can be given.

And therefore there is another opinion of greater authorities, that the use of reason is required for some by necessity of the Sacrament, for one order by necessity of precept, and for others from honor and fittingness.
