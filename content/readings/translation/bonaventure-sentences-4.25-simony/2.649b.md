ON THE CONTRARY: 1. In Judges 4 we read that Deborah judged Israel and presided over it. Therefore it seems that judicial power belongs to a woman, especially when she abounds in grace. Therefore the priestly power belongs to her as well.

2. Likewise, in the new dispensation we see abbesses to whom the governance of communities is entrusted. Therefore it seems that the power of absolving and binding ought to be committed to them. Therefore by parity of reasoning it seems that the priestly order could be conferred on them.

3. Likewise, the priestly order and the others have to do with the soul and not the flesh; but with respect to the soul there is no distinction of sex — indeed, a woman is just as much the image of God as a man. Therefore she is equally capable of receiving orders.

4. Likewise, there is no greater perfection than the state of religion, nor greater fortitude than that found in the endurance of martyrdom; but women are admitted both to the religious state and to martyrdom. Therefore they ought to and can be admitted to sacred orders.
