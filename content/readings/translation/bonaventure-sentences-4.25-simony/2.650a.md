For it is expressly stated in Distinction 23: “It has been brought to the notice of the Apostolic See that among you women consecrated to God, or nuns, are touching the sacred vessels or consecrated palls and carrying incense around the altar; and none of those who rightly think can doubt that all these practices are full of blame and reproach. Wherefore, by the authority of this holy See, lest this plague be spread more widely, we command that it be most speedily wiped out throughout all the provinces.”

And thus all agree that they ought not to be promoted; but whether they are capable of it is in doubt. To be sure, there was an opinion of certain men — called the Cataphryges — that they are capable; and these rely not only on the authorities already cited but also cling to the authorities of the canons, adducing in their favor passages in which it is shown that women received orders in antiquity. For it is said in *Causa* 27, Question 1: “We decree that a deaconess ought not to be ordained before the age of forty”; and in the same question: “If anyone abducts or solicits a deaconess”; and likewise in Distinction 32 mention is made of a *presbytera*. But certainly, if one attends to what is said in Distinction 32, *Presbyteram*, etc., it is there shown that widows and elder women and matrons are called *presbyterae*; and from this it is gathered that those women who shared with the deacons in reading the homily, and on whom a certain blessing was conferred, were called deaconesses. Hence it is in no way to be believed that women were ever promoted to sacred orders according to the canons. And according to the sounder and more prudent opinion of the doctors, not only ought they not, or cannot by right, but they also cannot in fact.
