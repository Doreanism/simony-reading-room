And so it is clear how response is to be made to what was objected, that the simoniac is not a heretic; it is also clear that objections are raised on both sides.

3. To that which is objected, that it is not a sin to sell, on the example of the prophets; one must say that no prophet who was good and holy ever sold the gift of prophecy, but only the wicked, as such are expressly rebuked in Micah 3: *Her judges judged for bribes, and her priests taught for hire, and her prophets divined for money*; and below: *Therefore on your account Zion shall be plowed as a field*, and so on. — To what is objected concerning the wife of Jeroboam and Saul; one must say that it is never read that those prophets accepted anything; but those people said it ignorantly, because they had grown accustomed to take up this custom in some way from the diviners, who were not true prophets.

4. To what is objected, that one who sows spiritual things may reap carnal things; one must say that if he sows for this purpose, to reap those things as his final end, especially if a pact intervenes, this is not justice but rather wickedness and avarice; but if he does not intend this principally, but rather reaps carnal things in order to sustain himself and sow spiritual ones; there is no avarice, no merchandising, but rather manifest justice, as the Apostle proves by many examples in 1 Corinthians 9, since such a man ought to be sustained. Whence from this it is clear that if a teacher and a counselor and a physician and others who labor receive a payment for counsel or instruction on account of their labor and their sustenance; there is no fault, no simony. If, however, anyone were to sell a gift divinely given to him, not his labor, he would be judged a simoniac. The same is to be understood of the celebration of Masses and the like.

5. To what is objected, that the kingdom of heaven is bought; one must say that such buying is not a buying, because its price is nothing other than good will, which does not regard recompense; whence although one cannot serve without recompense, yet, because he serves liberally, no pact at all intervenes; whence the whole is spiritual.
