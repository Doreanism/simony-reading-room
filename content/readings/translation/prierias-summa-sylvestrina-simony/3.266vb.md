Indeed, according to Hostiensis, even if with a principal intention, the pact does not cease \[to be simoniacal\]; which is true, first, with respect to the church and by judicial presumption; and second, also when the intention is toward an uncertain benefice in general and not to a certain one, according to Dominicus Antonius—between which there is a difference, although Panormitanus says he does not see it—because an uncertain benefice is the same as remuneration in this place. For although one may principally intend \[a benefice\], although not this particular ecclesiastical benefice, unless secondarily. But if the service is wholly spiritual, such as celebrating Mass, then if it were imposed on some benefice, according to V., undoubtedly a benefice can be conferred by pact, such that the one on whom it is conferred promises such service—for example, by celebrating such-and-such a Mass (de preben. Significatum). And similarly, according to Thomas, if there were annexed to it the burden of teaching, a pact or exaction of a promise concerning the performance of such service could be made. And when \[the benefice\] is vacant, such a burden can be annexed to it. Say, however, that it is not properly a pact, but on the part of the one instituting the revenue it is a donation for a cause, or under a modus, between living persons or in cause of death; while on the part of the one to whom it is conferred, it is the acceptance of the thing with the burden of fulfilling the cause or burden. But if the service is partly spiritual, partly temporal—as if a bishop receives a cleric for spiritual and temporal service to be rendered to him, with the promise that annually he will pay him a certain salary until he provides for him a benefice—according to Hostiensis this is lawful, provided he has not specifically bound himself to render temporal service. Nor does he commit simony afterwards in remitting the salary when he confers a benefice, because the salary was conditional; once the benefice is conferred, the condition expires (Inst. de verb. obli. § omnia). It is otherwise if the promise of salary had been pure and perpetual (arg. c. Veniens eo. ti.). Panormitanus, in c. Cum esset eo. ti., indeed believes this is true, because the prelate is not led principally on account of the service but because that man deserves it. It is otherwise if the final cause is the service; that is not lawful (ar. c. Graue de prebe.). It is otherwise if it is the impelling cause, but say that this is true in the manner in which priests are hired—namely with the bishop establishing an annual revenue with a burden, and with the intention of spiritual service principally and temporal secondarily. Add also, according to V., that it is not simony if two priests hire each other on the ground that one will say a Mass that pertains to the other, and conversely, when no price or temporal recompense intervenes.
