### To this note

To this note that, so far as Panormitanus gathers in *c. i. eo. ti.*, as regards benefices that involve the administration of an order or the exercise of the power of the keys, he commits simony in the first manner; it is otherwise in other matters, such as those spiritualities and other temporal administrations. But this rule is not from Thomas, nor as representing the mind of Raymond.

### Third rule

The third rule is that, in order to avoid simony before God and in conscience, it suffices that one does not intend to receive any temporal thing for spirituals or for what is annexed to them as a price, even if one does intend to receive it as a foundation, namely a concession of right or of pious custom, as appears at *xxv. ubi supra ar. ii.* with respect to the sacraments, and ar. iiii. with respect to spiritual acts. And this holds even if such an intention is a *sine qua non* — for example, the intention without which a preacher would not at that time preach — as is plain from Thomas in *Quodlibet* viii q. vi and in II-II q. vi., with respect to spiritual acts in the third manner, i.e. effectively, as for example with respect to the celebration of the office on account of distributions, and with respect to preaching on account of temporals, as you also have at § iiii. and xv. clearly.

### From these things it is plain

From these things it is plain that sometimes there is simony with respect to God only; sometimes only with respect to the Church, as when one presumes to sell or buy a spiritual thing yet does not intend it, as will appear in what follows; sometimes with respect to both; and sometimes with respect to neither. And the same must be said with respect to sacred things or their administration — for example, a confessor would not otherwise hear this particular person now except because he hopes for alms, setting aside the case of necessity for the sacraments and the obligation to administer them. When in spirituality there is no difference from the foregoing except in degree, then in both cases there is simony or no simony in the same way, no difference intervening except in degree.

### As regards the Church

But as regards the Church, which does not judge of intention, this does not suffice; rather, to avoid simony and its penalty, every pact and every species of exaction must be absent, as is plain at *xxv. ubi supra ar. iii. ad secundum*. Hence Thomas says in his *Quodlibet* that in the dispensing of spirituals, in order to avoid simony, three things are necessary if anything is to be received: namely a concession of right or of pious custom; a pious, i.e. uncorrupted, intention; and an honest form, that is, that it not be required before the discharge of the pious office, nor that a pact appear to intervene; but, the office once performed, it may be exacted. So far from him. As to the rest of Thomas’s rule, however — as is plain from the passages cited — he understands it of those who are in need of sustenance, that is, those who otherwise cannot live decently; indeed, even of those who hold God for their end. It is understood secondly with respect to those not obliged to such offices; it is otherwise with parish priests with respect to their subjects, from whom one cannot even take for one’s sustenance — see *j. q. iii. uendentes*. Nor can one have such an intention of not doing the office, but is bound piously to provide spirituals freely.
