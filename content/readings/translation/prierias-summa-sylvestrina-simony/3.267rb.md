## The nineteenth question

In the nineteenth place it is asked how many kinds of penalty there are for simoniacs; and I say that they are manifold.

The first is suspension from the exercise of orders and from the administration of benefices. Concerning which, see below under “Suspension” § ru.

### The second

The second is deposition and infamy; and this ought to be imposed if it has been established in a judicial proceeding (15 q. 3 *sane*; and *de accu. inquisitionis*).

### The third

The third is papal excommunication, by the *extravagans* of Martin in the Council of Constance which begins *Sacro*, since those ordained simoniacally are suspended from the exercise of their orders; and the elections, postulations, confirmations, and whatever provisions of churches, monasteries, dignities, personates, offices, and benefices are by the law itself null; and the giver and the receiver *ipso facto* incur the sentence of excommunication, even if he shines with pontifical or cardinalatial dignity. And by another *extravagans* of the same pope, issued at Mantua in the second year of his pontificate, which begins *Damnabilis*, by which he confirms all the censures imposed by whatever supreme pontiffs against simoniacs, and one incurs them *ipso facto*, even occultly, wheresoever he has committed simony, and in whatever church, even of an episcopal or cardinalatial see, or temporal dignity — reserving absolution to the pope except at the point of death, notwithstanding whatever indult or faculty of absolving granted or to be granted by the Apostolic See, unless it contain specifically the authority to absolve from the sin of simony. And the same Angelus \[notes\] in the *extravagans* which begins *Cum detestabile*, given at \[the Church of\] St. Chrysogonus in the third year of his pontificate; and the same Paul II in the process of the Lord’s Supper concerning simoniacs in order or benefice, reserving absolution as above; and the same Sixtus IV, whether in the Roman Curia or outside it. And thus conclude that all simoniacs in orders or benefice, at Rome or elsewhere, occult or public, are excommunicated and can be absolved only by the pope, except at the point of death; otherwise concerning other simoniacs. The chapters *reperiuntur*, *quicunque*, and *placuit* (1 q. 1), which prohibit under anathema, are no obstacle, because they speak only of simony in orders and benefice, and so likewise of other laws.

### The fourth

The fourth penalty is common to them and to those who do not reveal them, because by the said *extravagantes* of Martin and Eugenius — *Damnabile* — it is enjoined under penalty of excommunication *latae sententiae* upon all of whatever dignity or status, who know that any have committed simony or procured it or have been mediators. So that if they are present in the Roman Curia, they are bound to reveal it to the pope, or to the chamberlain of the Apostolic See, or to whomever he shall say it is to be committed, in the presence of one or two witnesses, as quickly as they can, either by themselves or through others; nor can they be absolved until they have revealed it — which is to be understood if they truly know and can conveniently reveal.
