### Fourth

Concerning the act of presenting and preaching, the judgment is the same as concerning the act of celebrating divine offices, since these are spiritual acts on the side of the principle.

### Fifth

Correction also and visitation, according to Saint Thomas, is a spiritual act on the side of the principle, because it is sought by virtue of a spiritual office. Hence he commits simony who takes money in order not to reveal or punish what he is bound to reveal or punish. Nor for visitation should money be taken as a price, but as something due from the ordering of the Church for sustenance. And the same holds for the moderate procuration which bishops may receive at the consecration of churches according to the determination of the Church.

### Sixth

Prayer, according to Saint Thomas (in fij. xxv.), is spiritual on the side of the principle and on the side of the end, because it derives its efficacy from spiritual devotion and something spiritual ought to be sought in it. Hence it must in no way be set under a price; nor do those who give money for this reason buy prayer from the poor, but rather they entice the poor to pray freely for them and make them indebted to themselves. Thus from him. Hence, according to Petrus de Palude, it is simony to give money by pact for the recitation of the psalter; but not if it is given for keeping vigil over a deceased person — that is, by reason of the labor — even though the recitation of the psalter and prayers follows upon the vigils, according to Hostiensis.

### Seventh

To celebrate an anniversary, according to Saint Thomas in iv., is a spiritual act on the side of the principle. Therefore, it is in no way lawful to make any pact for its celebration. Yet if something is given to the church with devotion, the church is bound to celebrate for those for whom it is requested. Thus he. But understand this as was said in § parti. iv., and add that one may lawfully accept a legacy or alms for performing an anniversary; but if a pact intervenes, it is simony. But if it be argued against this, first by c. Significati de prebe., where certain canons gave a prebend in their church to a priest so that he should celebrate Mass of the Blessed Virgin daily, and the pope confirmed that agreement: Saint Thomas in iv. says that there was no pact there, but that the office of singing such a Mass was annexed to that prebend. And the same is held by Antonius de Butrio and the gloss. If it be argued, second, that according to Innocent a layman likewise may establish a rent with the obligation of celebrating an anniversary, I say that this is not true. If it be argued, third, that the promise made in establishing a rent passes into a pact when the one who is to execute it accepts it (l. Pactum. ff. de polli.), I say that there is no promise there but a donation under a mode or cause. See on all these matters at length in the aur. ro. casu lviii.
