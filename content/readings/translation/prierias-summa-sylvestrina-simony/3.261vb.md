### Fourth

In the same way and for the same reason, neither for confirmation, nor indeed for chrism, nor for extreme unction, nor for the conferral of the eucharist may anything be exacted, or given by way of contract or quasi-contract. j. q. j. dictum sit. Nevertheless, things spontaneously offered may be received. j. q. ii. sicut episcopus. Likewise, for penance or absolution money may not be exacted, according to St. Thomas, as the price of absolution, since this would be simoniacal; but it may well be exacted as a monetary penalty imposed for the preceding sin or fault. In this, however, he says in the fourth book, care must be taken that such an exaction be not ascribed more to cupidity than to correction. Thus he. He is speaking of absolution both from sin and from censures induced by sin, and Peter and Hostiensis say the same. But he who exacts something, with the intention of not otherwise hearing the confession, commits simony, and indeed one to be punished by the Church. j. q. ii. quapio. Indeed, according to Raymond, even a priest who refuses to give his parishioner license to confess to another except for money — understand this as above in part ii. Furthermore, according to the Archdeacon, one who exacts nothing but nevertheless hears confessions principally intending gain commits mental simony — understand this when he was bound by his office to hear, or when he intends this as a price and not as alms-like sustenance; or, what is the same, understand it when money is the sole cause of his hearing, but not when it is the cause of his hearing this person rather than another from whom something is hoped for, while nonetheless, if nothing were hoped for, someone would still be heard. ### Fifth

For the sacrament of orders, nothing may be exacted, extra eo. c. j., where Dominicus Andreas says that since in ordination three persons are required — namely the bishop, the minister, and the notary — just as the principal ordination ought to be gratuitous, so also everything accessory to it. Now concerning the bishop it is clear that if anything is received for ordination by contract, agreement, or exaction, it is simony. j. q. ii. q\[uod\] pro and c. sicut episcopum. Indeed, if something is given him so that he may not ordain by simony, it is simony on his part, even if not on the giver’s, according to Tancred and Bartholomaeus; b. ii. q. j. j. nullus. Likewise, if it is given so that he may not give spiritual things to a simoniac, according to Panormitanus on c. cum in ecclesia.
