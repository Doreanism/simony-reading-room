Hence if it has been received from one ordained without any title, it shall be given to the cathedral church; if from one ordained to the title of some church or benefice, it shall be applied to that one, according to v. t. 21; and if it has been received by a church or benefice, it shall be applied to it, unless the church was at fault — for instance, if the whole college consented to the simony, for then it must be distributed to the poor, lest the church reap a profit from evil. It could, however, be returned out of mercy to the one who gave, according to Raymond, if he passes into religion and is to pass with it, or even if he is poor and his own means are insufficient, or also if he gave from ignorance, according to Hostiensis. And the same is to be said of money received for other spiritualities, as for instance, of the church in which simony has been committed — yet in such a way that the one who gave it does not reap a benefit.

### The third point

If, however, money was received for entrance into a monastery or church: if we are speaking of a secular church, according to Panormitanus on c. *de hoc* (same title), if he who gave has nothing else to live on, restitution shall be made to him (argument c. *veniens*, same title); but if he has whereon to live, then if all in the church transgressed, it ought to be given to the church and the offenders expelled; and if they are not expelled, it ought to be given to the papal fisc, according to Hostiensis, which shall distribute it to pious causes, because shameful gains are extorted by the fisc (l. *fiscus* in q., ff. *de iur. fis.*). If, however, not all transgressed, but only the prelate or a particular individual, then it shall be given to the church to whose ignominy it was given. But if we are speaking of a regular church: if all transgressed, it is given to the one who gave it, that he may go elsewhere to another monastery (and the said c. *veniens*, since he cannot remain in the first, as in c. *quoniam*, same title); but if only the prelate or particulars are seeking it and they are not apprehended — then, if he is professed in a second monastery to which he transfers, he shall have the usufruct of those goods, and at his death they shall revert freely to the first monastery. And so understand the gloss on the said c. *de hoc*, and it agrees with Hostiensis and Dominus Antonius; but Archidiaconus limits this to those things which are not consumed or distributed by use, such as possessions. If, however, some dispensation be granted that it remain in the same monastery, Archidiaconus thinks it can be dispensed that that money remain with the monastery.

### The fourth point

If simoniacal money has been given to the pope by pact, according to Panormitanus on c. *si e. nt.*, nevertheless such a person cannot retain the benefice received, even in a case in which the pope would not commit simony — as in a similar case Joannes Andreae argues in the rule *cum licet* (*de regu. iur. lib. vi*), in *mercu*., concerning one playing at dice with a bishop, by a statute of the same bishop excommunicating those who play, because although the bishop is not bound by that statute, the one playing with him is bound; and the same in our proposition, as Panormitanus argues on c. *extirpande* (*de prebendis*). But Zenzelinus — as I said above § 19 toward the end — and his view is truer.
