### The minister

The minister of whom that decretal speaks is either the archdeacon who examines those to be promoted, or the one who summons those to be promoted (according to the doctors), or the one who responds at the scrutiny of the ordinand according to the form of *c. ij. de scrut.*, or the one who reads the Gospel since it forms part of the ordination, according to Hostiensis. If any of these were to receive money for that ministry, it is simony.

### The notary

Concerning the notary who registers those to be promoted and draws up the letters for the order already conferred, there are several opinions, as Dominicus Antonius and the Archdeacon recite at *iv. parte. titu. j.*; but Panormitanus, *eo. tit. c. j.*, after the gloss, and the doctors commonly hold that if he is salaried out of the public funds — for example, by the bishop — he ought to demand nothing. Likewise, even if he has no salary but his office is so necessary to the ordination that without his recording it the order would not be said to have been duly conferred (because then his office would belong to those conferring the order) — since, however, he says he does not see this to be so necessary — it may be said in plain terms that, since he does not receive a salary from the public, he may lawfully demand money for his labor, considered with respect to the quality of the labor or task and not of the order. Tanta* (*X eod.*), I am not a simoniac, even if without the money I would not have obtained \[ordination\]. If, however, I myself gave \[the money\], it is simony, even if I would have received the order without the money. Add, third, that if money is given to someone for access to a prelate with the intention of obtaining something spiritual from him, it is not simony; otherwise, if it is given to persuade him (*eu. f. qd. j. Si quis*).
