And if he refuses to compel him, it is safer to abstain than to give occasion of sin. But Panormitanus, on *c. dilectus el. ii. eo. ti.*, holds that for obtaining necessary sacraments it is permissible to give something, but not for voluntary ones; and so, holding the opinion that it is not lawful to consummate matrimony without the blessing, where there is such a custom, money may lawfully be given for it with the intention of avoiding harassment.

### Third

For baptism, namely when it is being administered, money is not to be given or exacted, as is expressly clear in *j. q. j. baptizandis*. The *Summa Angelica* and the *Summa Rosella*, against the Supplement following Panormitanus on *c. cum in ecclesia eo. titu.*, limit this in the case of an adult who, since he holds baptism *in voto*, is held to be baptized; and this saves the argument of *c. debitum de baptismo r. cu. de presbyteris non bap.* But with regard to an infant it is otherwise: when the priest is unwilling to baptize otherwise, and there is no one else who knows how and is able, then he does not buy the spiritual thing — nor must he intend this — but he redeems a vexation. However, since according to these same authors the redemption of a vexation has place only where someone is repulsed from the just possession of some right, which is not the case here — for, as the Supplement well says, this is not a vexation but a denial of the sacrament — and although water in itself may be bought, yet not so that the one baptizing should use it, because thus it is a spiritual thing, since it is a part, that is, the matter of the sacrament in act; and granted that it could be bought, the priest does not intend to sell it but to sell baptism, nor does the one who pays therefore intend to buy it, since he would do nothing thereby — therefore the Archdeacon, following St. Thomas, Peter of Tarantasia, Peter de Palude, and the gloss on *j. q. j. baptizandis*, and Richardus (as the Archdeacon reports, although the *Summa Angelica* wrongly cites him for itself) says better that in such a case another should baptize if he can; otherwise it is better to let the child die without baptism, because evils are not to be done that good may come.

### He could however in this case

In this case, however, according to St. Thomas and others, one might buy from the priest the water, which is the corporeal stuff of a pure element — indeed, according to the Supplement, on the word “baptism,” even if it were consecrated, though this would be false according to the Archdeacon (see *r. j. q. j. dictum*), because it would be simony. As to infidels — whether one may give or pay money so that they may be baptized — some say, as the gloss on *c. qua pio j. q. ii.* notes, that yes, after the example of blessed Sylvester, who promised temporal goods to pagans that they might become Christians, that is, drawing them to the faith; and this is held by Raymond. This is indeed true if it is done freely, to win their goodwill or even to spread the good odor of Christianity so that one may more easily convert them, because this is not buying but disposing them by way of cause toward their conversion. Argument from *xxxii. q. iii. dz homo* and *q. vii. quod aut di. xlv. qui sincera*. But with a contract it is not lawful, according to r. r.
