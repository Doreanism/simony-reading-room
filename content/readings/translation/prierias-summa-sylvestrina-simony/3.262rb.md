### Sixth

Sixth. For sacramentals also — such as for the blessing of abbots and abbesses, the veiling of virgins, holy water, and indeed for every blessing and the like — it is not lawful to receive anything, according to Petrus de Palude. The same applies to the other things mentioned above under “sacramental,” just as something sacred cannot be sold even by reason of its matter (*f. q. f. Placuit ij. and iij.*). Hence also, according to Panormitanus (*X eo. in c. ca.*), it is simony to demand anything for chrism with respect to the liquid, because it is spiritual not only insofar as it is a sacrament but also insofar as it is a liquid (*d. c. Placuit, el. secundo*). Likewise, nothing may be taken for blessed bread or candles. Similarly, according to Goffredus and Hostiensis, nothing for the blessing of the chalice and vestments.

### Selling the office

To sell the office of carrying holy water, according to \[???\], is not simony if by custom the priest may exercise it through another and receive the proceeds; otherwise it is. Or, better, say absolutely with Rodoricus that it is lawful to sell the proceeds, but to sell the office itself is base gain.

### It is asked

It is asked whether it is lawful to give or receive anything for the spiritual considered effectively, that is, for spiritual acts and so forth (*q. co. Multa*).

### First

First, Saint Thomas in *iv. and ar. ij. qd. iiij. r. viij. quodl. q. vj.* distinguishes, saying that a spiritual act is spiritual in two ways. First, on the side of the principle, when the act belongs to a person on account of some spiritual gift or office that is in him, as for a bishop to consecrate churches and for a deacon to read the Gospel. Second, only on the side of the end and not of the principle, as in teaching the liberal arts, whose truth is spiritual; but such teaching does not belong to anyone on account of some spiritual office, since it is permitted even to the Gentiles. And in the first kind, indeed, if anyone intends to sell his act (as he says in the *quodlibet*), or to sell what is spiritual in them, and (as he says at xx.) to rent out his acts, it is simony, as he says in iv. But to receive or give something for the sustenance of those ministering in spiritual things, in the ordering of the Church and by approved custom (as he says at xx.), is lawful, provided that the intention of buying and selling is absent and that nothing is exacted from the unwilling through withholding spirituals by those who would have them in hope of payment. But once spiritual things have been freely provided beforehand, the statutory and customary offerings, and those which are the actual proceeds, may lawfully be demanded from those who are unwilling but able to pay, with the authority of the superior intervening.

### Thus he

Thus he. In the second kind of acts, as he says in iv., since the spirituality is not on the side of the agent, one can sell and rent the works; but to sell the spiritual that is acquired from the work is simony, as he says in the *quodlibet*. And understand that in the first kind, sustenance can be received even by principal intention, without note, if one is needy and is not bound by office, as was clear above in §. iiij. reg. iij., and likewise as will appear in the following part.
