### Third

Neither bishops nor archdeacons nor other prelates may commit their offices in substitution under an annual rent (X *ne prelati vices suas committant fi.* c. fi. ii and iii); rather it is simony, because such a right is spiritual. It is otherwise if the bishop simply and purely commits his office to someone, fixing a salary for such a vicar, because it is not a pact but rather what he is bound to do (xii q. ii *charitate*). And as Hostiensis says: if for the commission of jurisdiction it is not permitted to receive a large sum, much less is it permitted to receive a fixed share, i.e. a fraction of the revenues. To priests, however, whom a prelate or rector of a church hires, or to whom he leases the fruits of the church, one may also entrust the cure of souls freely without any pact intervening, by the authority of the bishop (*de officio vicarii ad hoc*) — indeed even without said authority, when necessary perfection is at hand, for a short time, i.e. one or a few days, but not for long (e. q. f., throughout). Note, however, according to Panormitanus on c. fi. *ne prelati vices suas*, that the offerings of the church could be leased to a layman, provided he puts a cleric in his place for collecting them, lest he presumptuously approach the altar where the offerings are made (argument from e. f. and ii *ne clerici vel monachi*).

### Fourth

When, however, a prelate sells all the offerings of the church, the advantage of jurisdiction passes along with the rest unless it has been excepted. Yet jurisdiction by itself can neither nor ought to be sold, according to Hostiensis — understand, without simony. Indeed according to Archidiaconus, following the gloss on c. ii *ne prelati vices suas*, it is simony if a prelate sells temporal jurisdiction. Antonius de Butrio, however, limits this to a temporal \[jurisdiction\] held from the church, which is judged to be spiritual or annexed to a spiritual thing in respect of him on whom it depends. But if a prelate holds temporal jurisdiction in fief from a prince or by a temporal title — whether it would be simony to sell it, and whether a sin — and so it is understood what Innocent and Hostiensis say about jurisdiction on c. f. eo. ti. *de ex Archi.*

### Fifth

It is not simony, according to Archidiaconus, if I lease my church to you, a priest, and you \[lease\] your services to me to officiate there, with you to have no other jurisdiction there (argument from xii q. ii *charitate*). Yet according to \[???\] there is not properly a lease there, but the priest ought to officiate freely and the other to make expenses freely. Or say that it is rather an institution of a cleric in a benefice with an annual income with the burden of officiating.

### Sixth

It is not simony if a cleric or religious sells bread offered on the altar, or wax and other alms otherwise given, and the fruits of tithes; nor is it a sin, according to Archidiaconus in his *Rosarium*, because these are not spiritual. To sell blessed bread, however, is simony according to Ul., and much more so the right of receiving tithes, as has been said.
