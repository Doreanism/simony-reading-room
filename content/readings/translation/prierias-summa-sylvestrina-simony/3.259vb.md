## Second question

Second it is asked which of the foregoing may, or may not, be sold without the vice of simony. And I say, as regards the supernatural spiritual, that the spiritual by essence cannot be sold by right; indeed, not even a deliberate will to buy can fall upon it, according to Thomas (in IV) — understand this in a sound sense; on this see *s. q. s. c. s.* and the Gloss there. Nor can the spiritual by cause be sold, as in *c. non satis* and *c. cum in ecclesie, eodem titulo*, although insofar as it is corporeal it has a certain aspect which can be sold, but not because it is spiritual. Whence according to Thomas (II-II), it is simony to take money as a price for the consecration of the Eucharist or for a Mass sung, but not as a stipend for sustenance. Nor can the spiritual effectively be redeemed by money or resigned (*extra eodem querelam*, and *s. q. s. nullus episcopus, d. ii*), except in the way to be stated below. Nor can what is annexed to such a spiritual antecedently — by that reason whereby it is annexed, or by which it is ordered to the spiritual. About this Thomas’s view, as is plain in II-II, q. 100, art. 4, throughout, is that to sell the corporeal or temporal things themselves is permitted, but not under their ordering to spiritual things. And if they be sold to another church, which is permitted in case of necessity, they can be redeemed under their proper form; but if they be sold to lay persons, they must be destroyed and only their matter sold — on which see § xi. As to the right of patronage, which is not corporeal, it was discussed at § i, and at § v itself: how it cannot be sold in itself nor given in fief, but passes along with other returns. Nor what is annexed to it consequently, since, not befitting anyone except one having clerical office (according to Thomas), it cannot exist without the spiritual, and consequently when that is returned the spiritual is understood to be subjected to sale. Nor can the natural spiritual called by essence be sold; indeed not even in fact, since Scripture says (Proverbs 22) “what profits the fool to have riches, since he cannot buy wisdom”; nor can the spiritual by cause always be sold, but only sometimes, as below at § x. But what is annexed to such a spiritual can be sold by right, unless the church of the place has assigned it to a teacher of the schools.

## Third question

Third it is asked which keys and rules must be used to know whether in some given case there is simony or not. And I say that there are many.

### First

The first is the definition expounded above, which is understood of simony in itself or by its own nature — on which immediately.

### Second

The second is that some things are simoniacal by their own nature, namely all the foregoing — limiting this, however, as regards benefices, if they carry the administration of orders or the execution of the keys, in which the pope cannot dispense so that they may lawfully be done. He can, however, dispense as regards the penalties contracted from positive law, or to be contracted, lest it be contracted, such as excommunication, suspension, resignation of the benefice, and the like.
