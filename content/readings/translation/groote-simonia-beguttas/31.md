And the Church calls religious, properly according to her judgment, only those who bind themselves to this perfection by a solemnity — that is, with public profession before the people and in obedience under a rule. Nevertheless it is possible that a fellowship may keep these things better among themselves before God than people sometimes do who are bound by statute and outward status in cloisters, as one says concerning the fall of many religious persons and the fall of cloisters; many people may be perfect who are not outwardly in the Church in the state of perfection. So Thomas teaches in the aforementioned book, in question 184, article 4. As Christ says in chapter 31 \[recte 21\] of Matthew concerning the father who said to his two sons that they should go into his vineyard: the one said that he would go in, and he did not go in; and the other said that he would not go in, and he did go in \[Matthew 21:28–31\]. Thus the Church does not call any people religious, properly speaking with respect to the outward Church, except those who have vowed poverty, chastity, and obedience according to an approved rule. So Thomas and the doctors generally speak of religious persons, and so the canon law speaks properly of religious persons. And so the third order (19bb) or other orders, which hold private property or are married, are not a *religio* except in part. Insofar as they share in and possess certain things that belong to the state of religious persons, he says, they are *religio*. And the like may further be said of the state of the beguines, inasmuch as the pope tolerates them. But these have not so much in common with the Church’s bond as does the third order outwardly, since they make no profession and have not been approved by the Church; the Church merely tolerates them. Even so, they often have, or may have, more spirituality in themselves and among themselves according to the judgment of God in true brotherhood or sisterhood than many of those in the third order; and at times they are also poorer and have more fully gone out of their own will
