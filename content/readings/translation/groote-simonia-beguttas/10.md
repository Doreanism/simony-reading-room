What has a poor person done amiss, if he has virtuous qualities better than another, and yet you give him nothing? Likewise, you shut the poor out for that very thing in which he is more like Christ, namely poverty. Likewise, you shut him out for that very thing through which he is more blessed, and readier, and more suitable to serve God according to the aforesaid righteousness, that is, poverty and humble birth. Likewise, you shut him out for that very thing according to which Christ chose his own, those whom he loved most — his apostles and disciples — and wherein he gave us the true form of all election and dispensation. And good Saint Paul also teaches us this in the Epistle to the Corinthians, where he shows us how we ought to choose according to righteousness in Christ, and speaks thus: “Behold, brothers, your calling: for not many wise according to the flesh are called, not many mighty, not many noble. But God chose the foolish things of the world, that he might confound the wise; and God chose the weak things of the world and the things that are not, that he might destroy the things that are, so that no flesh might glory before the face of God.” Likewise, whoever doubts that poverty is a great preparation and disposition for going to God — speaking generally — has never known the gospel of Christ. And therefore, according to the aforesaid righteousness, a place is more fitting for a poor person than for a rich one, even if all other things were equal that pertain to suitability. Likewise, even if all suitability were equal, since a poor person has more need of it, one should for that reason give it to him the more, according to the aforesaid righteousness; for need and want are also things that righteousness takes into account, inasmuch as one is to distribute anything according to the need of the people receiving it. Thus it stands written *in actibus apostolorum* \[in the Acts of the Apostles\], that distribution was made to each according to his need. Likewise, behold, my dear sisters, how you exclude the perfect person who has surrendered all things and all worldly goods. (10a) Behold, shall they for this reason forgo your place, because they have taken on the state of perfection — on account of which they are worthy, according to the aforesaid righteousness, of all honor and all good — shall they for this very reason have to go without it?
