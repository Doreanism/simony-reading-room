Others speak otherwise about this and say that it is not certain that one should so act, and that under this color of poverty many a simony is committed among the monks and nuns in the German lands, where the poverty is more a matter of pretense and has no place, and where they could well feed more people, even if they did not take the persons \[for money\] — even if he brought nothing — given that they could nonetheless feed him. And above all, there is no like comparison with these beguines, since they give them no full feeding nor clothing, and it is no great hindrance to them to receive a person, since they may always well receive the right ones and keep them among those with whom they uphold the honor \[of the community\], and since each maintains herself \[at her own cost\], except for what advantage there is in pottage, in heating, or in drink, or in a little other expense. Building works they ought to maintain from the common goods, or to take collections for them, or to beg for them — it would be better for them to do whatever they were able to do, yea to suffer all hardship, yea to run away or to beg bread, than that they should give their full consent to such evil things as have been written above, and as each may read in her own conscience. And even if all the world were to do such things against righteousness, that would be no shelter against divine (13b) and natural reason. Wherefore there is no other counsel but to leave off these things altogether, and to have repentance for them, and a true and firm resolve no more to do them. And were there with you certain perverse persons who would not do so, then you who are willing to take pains may, with your consent, always speak against it whenever an agreement is made anywhere to bring in a sum, or \[to bring in\] some of the same goods which they have; and never any more give the prebend for property, nor promise any property in connection with it, nor even hint at it.
