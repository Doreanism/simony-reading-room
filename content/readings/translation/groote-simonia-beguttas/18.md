*Of contributing.* One might well establish, concerning matters of contribution, a common measure — out of the same goods that one had, whether little or much — for building needs or for expenses; but then one may not receive anyone on that account, nor have regard to the fact that he was to contribute much therein. Likewise they say that the priests promise by agreement to improve their altar by three or four pounds a year, or else they will not give him the altar. This is no shelter. For the priests who thus promise to improve the altar, or make agreements thereon, commit grave simony. So do also those who give him the altar on such conditions. The priests are evilly placed in the matter. For they must give up the altar and afterwards give over all the fruit of the church which they ever received, or which they might have received — this is plain to all persons who are willing to speak the truth out of the spiritual law. But should these things happen without a promise concerning the altar and without agreement, but rather that the one who gives the altar hoped that he \[the priest\] would improve it, and \[provided that\] he knew, if he did not improve it, that he would then not give it to him, and the priest would not improve the altar were it not given to him — then it is altogether equally evil and bad before God; yet he may remain in it, if the altar (14a) is fitting to his salvation, and he shall make satisfaction before God and before his confessor for the sin by true penance. For he has neither made nor promised any agreement or condition or pledge.

Righteousness lies commonly under foot; let each see for herself, let each bear her own burden — not for the judgment which is now permitted to him on earth, but according to the truth and the righteousness of God.
