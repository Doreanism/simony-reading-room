And in this virtue of *religio* there is more simony, and more properly so, and in its works and exercises, than in other virtues, or than in other moral virtuous works and exercises and offices. Now Saint Thomas says, in the aforesaid book, in the CLXXXVIth question, that, whenever a name applies to many things and is in many things, then one applies the name at such times — according to the manner of human speech — particularly to the things which most highly and most fully have that which the word signifies. Thus one names with the name of *fortitudo* (strength) that virtue in particular which is firmness in the very heaviest matters; and so one calls *temperancia* (temperance) that which is in the very greatest pleasures — although the virtue of *fortitudo* and of *temperancia* is found in many other things as well. Likewise one means Saint Paul when one says in common “the Apostle,” and Aristotle when one says “the master of the natural sciences”; even though there have been many other apostles and philosophers. Thus, says Saint Thomas, *religio* is named *anthonomatice* particularly for the perfect and high virtue of *religio*; and *religiosi* particularly are so named, from the loftiness and from the perfection of the virtue which is called *religio* in human beings — namely, in those who give themselves over altogether and devote themselves to the service of God. Thus Saint Gregory speaks upon the prophet Ezekiel: They are such persons as keep nothing for themselves, but their tongue, their life, and all temporal goods which they have received from God, they offer up to him (19ª); and they become, as in the old law, the offering which was wholly burnt, which one calls *holocaustum*. And this one may possess just as fully outside community life and outside cloisters and outside habits, as such people do in cloisters. For perfect love and perfect cleaving to God, according to our capacity here in this exile, is the very greatest spirituality, and this one may possess, and one finds it many a time outside professed cloisters.
