But Chrysostomus, in the homily on Matthew in the original, says that it was the case in that time that the Jews ought to have loosened themselves the more from all manner of observances and ordinances as time went on; yet they bound themselves the longer the more, with greater observance, and to this was added their wickedness, that men kept their commandments and allowed God’s commandments to be neglected. Therefore guilt lay upon them on both counts: that they made new things, and that they took vengeance on those who did not keep them; and it was a twofold sin in them, that they did not praise God, and that they did this for the sake of men — as if he would say, “This condemns you: that you praise all things in the elders, and have no care for the things that pertain to God.” These are the words of Chrysostomus. And therefore Christ says next how every planting or *plantatio* that is not of the Father is merely the ordinance of men. As Chrysostomus says, they shall all be rooted out, and all who follow them, and Christ calls them blind and leaders of the blind, for they were the ones called scribes and Pharisees, the wise and the highest of the people. And Christ brings to bear upon them the Holy Scripture which Isaiah writes in the twenty-ninth chapter, and he says thus in Mark: “Well has Isaiah prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written: ‘This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me; in vain do they worship me, teaching teachings (16a) and commandments of men,’ and they leave behind the commandments of God and hold the ordinances of men.” These are Christ’s words drawn from Isaiah. And likewise the Lord speaks in the tenth chapter of the prophet Isaiah: “Woe to those who establish unrighteous laws and who have written, in their writing, unrighteousness, that they may oppress the poor in judgment and do violence to the widows in their causes and to the lowest of my people”; these are God’s words in Isaiah. And oh, how exceedingly are these things present in the ordinances of the beguines, as has been written above, by which the lowly and the poor are pressed down and the rich and the mighty are exalted.
