But how one ought to hold God’s law foremost — namely, true faith and mercy — and after that not to neglect the laws of men that are just, such as giving tithes of dill and cumin and of small things, as Christ says — these words would take me too long to speak. But this alone is sure and right in human ordinance, and nothing else, namely that which is born out of God and flows there from the fount of all law and ordinance, which is the supreme reason of God. Likewise it is said that human ordinance is nothing other than a *determinatio*, that is, a distinguishing demonstration of natural and of eternal right in distinct matters and things. And whoever rightly knows and fully holds just human ordinance will find that natural right and reason, and eternal right and the right and reason of God, are one in human right. Blessed is he whose heart is opened to this. Have mercy upon us, Lord! ## This chapter shows that spiritual things are of many kinds with distinctions, in which simony may exist, and what things make men most spiritual, and wherein the greatest simony resides. Hereafter I wish to speak of simony, although it would be enough for all good hearts in the things that have here above been openly declared — that one neither may nor ought to keep such ordinances or establishments, and still more so since they have not been established by the beguines themselves — yet so that unlearned persons may the better understand simony and what has been said against it, and in order to see this clearly and to know (16b) the distinctions of the things in which simony lies, whether more or less, in the heart or outwardly in the church, and in order to answer the question which the *Wederboec* of Cologne asks: why and in what manner it is simony to buy places and prebends. So you shall know first, as Antisiodorus says in his *Summa* in the third book on simony in the first question, that one is accustomed to demonstrate with distinctions what a spiritual thing is.
