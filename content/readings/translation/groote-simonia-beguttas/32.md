For as the *Horologium of Eternal Wisdom* says: there one finds it a great wonder, that in many cloisters where outwardly in their habits and outward things they appear to be human beings and bear spiritual signs, they are inwardly lions and bears and beasts. And therefore it is open simony before the Church to buy entrance into cloisters, or also entrance into the third order; and it is also simony in the heart to buy entrance into any other fellowship or brotherhood where good people live together before God according to the virtue that is called *religio*. One must not establish any new perfect *religio* — that is, no new rule — without leave of the pope; but that two, three, or four or more should live together according to some approved rule, or in part according to it, or according to the rule of all rules, that is, according to the holy blessed Gospel, that is not forbidden, in my view. And if it were that people lived thus in brotherly fellowship according to the Gospel in the virtue that is called *religio* — whether the pope did not know of it, or whether the pope knew of it and tolerated it, or whether he knew of it and confirmed it — to receive anyone into it for money is open simony before God in conscience. This community is spiritual, and many times more spiritual than such as ought to live according to rules and do not live according to the rule, of whom there are now many. My (20a) belief is that the Church ought to judge this as simony, were it to come to judgment, in those states which she has approved and confirmed, as in entrance to the third order; but what they should do outwardly in judgment concerning entrance into the state of the beguines, of that I will not speak, although that they commit simony in conscience seems to me to be evident. And to understand this word *religio*, whence it comes, one may say from Isidore’s *Etymologies*, and also from St. Augustine, in the third chapter of the tenth book of *On the City of God* and in the last chapter of *On True Religion*, and as Thomas says in the *Secunda secundae*, that *religio* or religious people are so called from *religere* or *religare* or *relegere*: that is, insofar as one again and again reads over and reviews and chooses out the things that are pleasing to God, and turns them over in his heart; or insofar as one chooses again that which he has neglected and lost; or insofar as he again, or anew, or further, binds himself to it.
