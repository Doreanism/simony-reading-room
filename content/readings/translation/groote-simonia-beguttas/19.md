## This chapter shows how evil and wrong it is to admit persons into any convent for a fixed sum of money and not according to all suitability and necessity.

All law and human covenant that makes a binding agreement, as the saints and likewise other philosophers teach, flows and must flow — if it is to have the force of law — from the very highest and eternal Reason, which is God. So speaks the eternal wisdom of God in the eighth chapter of Solomon’s Proverbs: “Mine,” he says, “are both counsel and equity; mine are wisdom and strength; by me kings reign; by me the lawgiver lays down just things; by me princes command; by me the mighty discern righteousness.” If, then, it is true that all law and equity comes from the supreme eternal Reason, how could anything come from God or from the eternal Wisdom that would be better unestablished than established, or that would be unequal or far from equity and from reason? From the eternal Good comes nothing — neither gift nor present — except the very best and most perfect, as Saint James says in the first chapter of his epistle. Whereby it is evident that whoever first set in his heart anything other than to give according to all suitability — if any such thing existed — it was not born from God, nor did it come into him from God. For which reason it is no law or covenant, but is born from men or from the devil, both of whom, whenever they speak from themselves, lie and sin (14b) and miss the good and the law. Likewise, could one, by such a lie and failure, make any right, or thereby break the common good, or should one for that reason break the divine rules? That is far indeed from all good hearts. Likewise: should one follow this sinful establisher in this lie of his ordinance, and follow the devil? That would be most lamentable. This is the very thing by which those are shut out who otherwise according to righteousness have a better claim, and who would be more fitting according to all suitability.
