Alas, what greater simony is there than that in the heart, where one supposes or strives to buy God’s grace, or likewise to recompense or buy with money another person’s virtue, or to become a partaker of it — of which there are now many on earth. Indeed, as Antisiodorus says, that he who would buy God or have him with alms as with a sort of payment, so that God would enlighten his heart — that would be the very worst simony. Now, to pass on further to the third order and to the beguine state, prebends, places, and convents: you shall know that there is a virtue called *religio*, which is that whereby a person stands and inclines and binds himself to serve God, and to do and render to him fitting honor, reverence, service, and exercise. So Thomas says in the *Secunda Secundae*, in the LXXXIst question, in many articles. This virtue *religio* is most spiritual, and after the godly virtues — faith, hope, and love — it is one of the most spiritual virtues. And it bears rule and has authority over all the moral virtues, and it employs and offers up their deeds and works to the divine service and honor; and it governs and orders and arranges the other virtues and all things to the glory of God. This virtue the heathen did not have rightly, but they spoke of it and supposed and boasted themselves to have it according to their presumption; yet it is nowhere truer and more rightly found than in the Christian. And this Saint Augustine demonstrates in the book on the City of God, and in the book which is commonly called — as Saint Augustine himself names it — *Of True Religion*. This virtue, which is called *religio*, has works and deeds, which one calls inward and outward acts of holiness. As Thomas says in the aforesaid question, in the VIIIth article: its inward works and exercises are such as inward devotion and fervor, (18b) prayer and supplication and entreaty, and adoring inwardly and submitting oneself to God. Its outward works and exercises are: outward adoration — kneeling and prostrating and offering, and likewise praising God outwardly and giving thanks with the mouth; and further the works of this virtue are these, that it orders and arranges all works and all things, inward and outward, to the service of God.
