Likewise, herein lies fidelity, if you would be faithful to God in this matter. Saint Paul speaks to the Corinthians, concerning himself and his own, thus: so, he says, let men reckon of us, that we are servants of God and stewards or distributors of the services of God; and now, he says, it is required among stewards that each one be found faithful. And Christ speaks in Saint Luke’s gospel: Who is, he says, a faithful and wise steward, whom the lord sets over his household to give them a measure of wheat in due time — that is, temporal things as is fitting and according to the measure of the justice prescribed above? Blessed is the servant whom the lord, when he comes, shall find so doing; he shall set him over all that he possesses. What he shall do to the wicked he also speaks thereafter. And further Christ says on the same point: of him to whom much has been given, of him shall much be required; and of those to whom men have committed much, of them shall much be asked. These are the words of Christ concerning the unfaithful steward or the wicked keeper. And who can absolve him unless he becomes a faithful steward to God? And how should one entrust to him that which is true, when he is thus unfaithful to God in wicked riches? So Christ speaks in Saint Luke’s gospel in another place, and he also says: if you are unfaithful in another’s goods — that is, in what is not your own, such as these vacant places in your convent — who shall give you what is your own, that is, true good from within in knowledge, in taste, or in reward?

Further, my dear people. I propose that a person within Kampen, or an alderman, when one is to be elected, is elected according to the blood of his kindred, leaving the more capable out — this is surely against the justice prescribed above, and from this you may prove that no one can absolve him unless he repent of the sin and turn from it, so that he will do it no more; and likewise (11a) for every election to office or court. If this is so, as it surely is, then you may prove how you yourselves must repent of these things, which thus deflect men from the right way of God. For no injustice is worse and more harmful and more wicked than that which hinders, and injustice on the way of God, for this brings eternal ruin.
