For all sin lies chiefly in the will, and still more so when one would bind other people to it as well; and this binding of the will to enforce and the will to take in hand — what is it other than to consent to and to follow after the evil? And such a one becomes like to those who are bound, as the Psalm says: both the one who does this and those who acquiesce in it. For full consent to evil is the same as the deed itself, as one may often prove from Christ’s words. And therefore all my arguments stand fast and remain standing, those which I set forth in the other book concerning distributive justice and concerning the trustworthiness of the steward and concerning the principle that one ought not to take persons \[into account\] without due cause. For one may not break these things by ordinances of this sort, nor by such means may one shut out the poor and those who according to right are in need of these things, or those who by every fitness are best suited to them. Likewise, the taking of persons, which is called *acceptio personarum* — one distinguishes this and sets it over against the taking of the cause and over against the taking of the wherefore of every fitness for the thing that is to be allotted; and that is called *acceptionem causarum*, which one ought always to consider. And for the sake of a single sinful will of the ordinance in convents, in prebends, a person is in himself neither more fit nor less fit for the prebend or the convent, or because of money — whatever the case, he is equal to the evil wills of the ordinance which excluded the persons. Likewise, in this manner against God’s law no man may make a counter-ordinance, much less by a thousandfold than a bishop against the pope. And whatever (15b) is enacted against the law of God in sin, that is bad and evil and binds no one. For this reason our dear Lord Jesus Christ rebuked the Jews severely when they asked why his disciples did not keep the ancient command concerning the washing of hands; then he questioned them and rebuked them for transgressing God’s commandment for the sake of the commandment of men, as stands in the fifteenth chapter of Matthew and in the seventh chapter of Mark.
