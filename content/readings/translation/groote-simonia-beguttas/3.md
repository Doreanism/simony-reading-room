# Gerardus Magnus.

The following question has been put to me: whether it is simony to purchase a place or a prebend in a beguine convent. I have answered: if some spiritual thing is attached to the place or to the prebend, or if the person, in obtaining the place or the prebend, also thereby obtains some spiritual thing which follows along with it, then it is certainly simony. For so the canon law writes: when two things hang together in such a way, or follow one upon the other, that the one cannot be had or obtained without the other, then whoever buys the one is also said to buy the other. So it stands in *decret. I quaestio III cap. Salvator* with its *concordantiae* and in other places in the law, and every person can feel in his own conscience that this is true. And whoever in such matters promises anything or makes any agreement to bring in something — whether a sum of money or the very property he has — this is all simony, since in none of these matters may anything at all be given for the sake of any temporal giving or bringing.
