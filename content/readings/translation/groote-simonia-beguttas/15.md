\[virtu\]ous things hang upon, nor abiding things, and thereby you become a judge with evil thoughts. O what kind of judge is he then in wickedness, and how does he dishonor the poor and virtue, when he sets down the poor virtuous person and casts him away, and exalts the rich into the lasting position in which a person remains for his whole life, and into the position that is established for virtue and for God. Let each one who has any spiritual understanding examine himself on this point in his own conscience. And alas, who would not have pity on this, if such a setting down of the poor were done to him for the sake of temporal gain! That the one who lowered him in this way should then take \[payment\] for it — that would be still fouler. Likewise, it is to be noted that the more these things are established for virtue and for serving God, the more closely this acceptance of persons without regard to their worthiness approaches simony. And acceptance of persons without regard to their worthiness in any matters that bear upon God, and simony, stand on a single foundation and are born of one and the same evil and spring up after one fashion before the eyes of God. And for that reason it is also manifest simony when worldly power — such as being emperor, or being a ruler in the cities — \[is bought\] for money. And Hostiensis says in his *Summa* in the fifth book that this is simony, for he says that this power is spiritual; and I say that no one ought to be a ruler (12b), nor can he be a ruler with a good conscience, nor can one with a good conscience choose to rule cities or lands one whom one knows to be seeking money or his own outward honor. For these are all tyrants.1) Rather, one shall choose him who is useful for ruling the common land or city, and who has God before his eyes and directs his governance toward God; otherwise he is not a true ruler. And if he buys it with money, no person on earth can absolve the tyrant or the ruler unless he turns his heart to God and to the common good. From this learn how, then, spiritual offices ought to be conferred — offices which are set somewhat apart from the world 1) Archief III p. 142. But God forbid that in well-governed towns the temporal offices of alderman or councilor or any magistracies whatsoever, by which men are governed socially and politically according to the secular order — even among pagans — should be let out or hired in this way for an annual rent. And if it is done so anywhere, woe to that city, not only because of the scandal but also because of the intolerable harm! And truly such a thing cannot be called governance but tyranny, or else power usurped by money and a most shameful traffic.
