And this sin, the receiving of persons, is contrary to the distributive righteousness of God, as all the doctors say. And the receiving of persons is not with God, as Saint Paul says to the Romans, and also to the Ephesians. And if this taking-of-persons is so evil, how is it not a greater evil to do this for the sake of gain? But hear Saint James speak: My brothers, says he, do not have the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory with respect of persons. If into your sight there enters or comes a man who has a golden ring on his hand with shining clothes, and there enters or comes also a poor person with foul clothes, and you have regard to the rich man with the fine clothes and say: Sit thou here in a good place, and you say to the poor man: Stand thou there, or sit on the footstool of my feet (12a), then you judge among yourselves, and have become judges with evil thoughts. Hear, my dearest brothers, has not God chosen the poor, who are rich in faith, to be heirs of the kingdom which God has promised to those who love him — and you have dishonored the poor? These are Saint James’s words. And with this the doctors Saint Augustine and Saint Gregory and other doctors agree on this passage: that to the rich, merely for the sake of their riches, one shall do no honor nor mark of virtue. So much the less shall one give them, for their riches’ sake, offices and positions that bear toward virtue and are established for it.
