Now then, since the life of beguines and convents is there (7a) precisely for the purpose of being in some way separated from the world — in clothing and in manner of life — and of serving God, is that not a spiritual thing? For the wherefore and the ultimate end of things is what determines and gives moral things their name and their form. If, then, the wherefore and the end is God and a spiritual life, then the state and manner of life is likewise spiritual before the eyes of God and in the court of conscience. Although the outward Church does not regard these people as religious, and although they do not enjoy the privileges of religious, and although they must appear before the secular court, nevertheless their manner of life is set apart, and in its institution and its purpose it is meant to be directed toward a spiritual life and toward serving God; and all these are spiritual ends, and all bear toward God in the conscience and before God’s tribunal. Although they are not bound by the outward spiritual law any longer than they wish, yet inwardly they are bound to remain therein, so that they should not return to the world. Insofar as one is on the best and the surest way to God, one shall not willingly nor may one turn back without grave sin, nor may one even desire to do so without sin. For no one, says Christ, who puts his hand to the plow and looks back, is fit for the kingdom of God. And likewise no beguine may with a good conscience return to the world out of shame before the world and her fellow Christians. These things, and further all the bonds of the beguines and their good inward customs — such as reading, fasting, or other things — cause this state or manner of life to be spiritual before God and in the conscience, and accordingly more or less spiritual to the degree that these things more or less conform to the convent. Nevertheless, it is not a fixed bond according to the outward spiritual court. For this reason the outward Church does not write specially against this kind of simony in particular, and does not pass judgment outwardly upon this simony.
