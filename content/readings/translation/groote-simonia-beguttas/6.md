This I hope; but I will not say for certain truth that this is sufficient before God, nor will I say that it is the best or the closest way. But no one on earth may absolve them unless they have remorse for what they have done and have the firm will not to do so again; otherwise there is no penance, nor remorse, nor any satisfaction before God. And penance and satisfaction before God are necessary in all such simony as the Church does not judge, or which stands only in the conscience and in the thoughts. So speaks the last chapter on simony in the fifth book of the Decretals. Simony that stands in the heart and in the conscience, or in such-like things — although it is not called simony before the outward court, it is nevertheless often greater than, or equal to, or only a little less \[than what is judged outwardly\] in the eyes of God. What is less in the eyes of God than that I should give five hundred pounds to the chapter without express agreement — yet for the purpose that they should give me a prebend, and have it in my heart that I would not otherwise give it to them, knowing that they would not give me the prebend, and they then without express agreement give me a prebend which they would not otherwise have thought to give me, had I not given them the five hundred pounds? When this is done without express agreement, then the Church does not judge it to be simony in the outward court, and one may keep the prebend, provided that it helps and conduces to the person’s salvation, and that one makes full satisfaction to God with contrition in confession, in full will to do this no more. Yet the heart is wholly full of simony, and there is besides an evil appearance. But where one were to do the same thing with express agreement, the Church judges it as simony. And since the Church undertakes to judge this according to the command of the spiritual law, one must surrender it in confession. The hearts are here all one; only the words are differ\[entiated\] (8a): God speaks into the heart.
