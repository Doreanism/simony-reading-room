But the sacraments one may sell in deed though not by right, and likewise the things that hang upon the sacraments. And of these sacraments and sacramental things, and of those that hang upon them, the holy Church chiefly and especially speaks in the law when she speaks of simony. For the true virtuous person does not sell any virtue, nor virtuous works, nor exercises, nor life — even if one were able to sell them; and indeed one cannot well sell virtuous works or exercises insofar as they remain virtuous; and the virtues themselves are hidden in their ground, and so too their works and exercises, in respect of their being virtuous. The sacraments and consecrations and the priesthood and the offices and deeds and exercises and prebends that hang upon them, and also miracles — these are seen and known better from without, that they are real, than works or exercises of virtue are known to be wrought out of virtue. For there are many works and exercises that in their kind appear good, which are not well nor virtuously wrought, and which from without and in appearance are wholly like virtuous works and exercises and life. And therefore the Church does not speak so much of this simony which stands here as of the other simonies. (18ᵈ) And yet it is a worse simony and a greater unbelief and more unlike true belief, and it is more against love, to be willing to sell any degree of virtue or of the exercise of virtuous works or of life, than to buy any consecration or sacrament of matrimony or miracle.
