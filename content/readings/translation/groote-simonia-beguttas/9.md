And one ought above all to consider that which contributes more and helps more, and according to the measure of suitability and fitness for the matter, so one is to give and dispense the things. This righteousness lives uniquely in the heart by nature and by grace, and is formed originally in the eternal Word of God, and from there it springs forth in all just judgments, both by nature and by grace. And from there comes every just verdict. It will reveal itself worthily at the last judgment. Alas, who shall be able to hide himself from it on the last day? And oh, how sweet shall it be to behold, according to its eternal reason, for those who have loved it in their lives and who have remained with it in the things they have been appointed to give, to distribute, or to dispense. And oh, how worthily sweet and beautiful is it now also to behold for an upright heart that knows it and loves it and lives by it upon the earth; and who can excuse or shield himself from what he did not know? For it stands written by nature in every reasonable heart that has sense and reason, in those who do not willfully dull and blind themselves, or who do not willfully turn away from it. And oh, how dreadful is it to behold according to reason for those who have turned against it! Now be assured of this, before God and before all spirits, that you willfully act against it and contrary to it, and spit it in its very eyes, spiritually speaking, when you thus give the places that are appointed for serving God and unto God according to wealth, after your customary practice (9b) — this is devilish and altogether contrary to righteousness, so that it can neither shine nor remain in you, inasmuch as no person can be so suitable, or so virtuous, or have such worthiness according to the aforesaid righteousness, that — if he has no money or is of lowly birth — you will not exclude him from your dispensation and from your place; and instead you give them according to blood, that is, according to kinship and according to money.
