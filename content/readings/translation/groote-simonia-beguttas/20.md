Likewise: she is righteous in herself, and she shuts no one out except by righteousness, and \[shuts out only\] those who shut themselves out from her. Likewise: she shuts no one out for the sake of anyone’s ordinance that is not born from her, nor for the sake of any other ordinance born of the flesh and of the blood. And who is born of the flesh and is fleshly? To this Augustinus often replies, especially upon Saint Paul’s epistle to the Galatians. He is born of the flesh, he says, who seeks himself in temporal gain or honor. As the beguines do, or those for whom the ordinance was established, or for whom it was to be established — who all seek their own private temporal gain — and thereby every state falls, as the saints say, and especially because learned men often confirm this. Likewise Saint Gregorius, on the Gospel of the man who hired workmen into his vineyard — that is, in his nineteenth homily — speaks thus: “Brothers,” he says, “examine yourselves and see whether you are God’s workmen; let each one ponder what he does and consider whether he labors in God’s vineyard. For he who in this life seeks the things that are his own has not yet come into God’s vineyard.” These are the words of Gregorius; from which you may prove that to seek yourselves, below or above any other fitness, is no labor in God’s vineyard; nor is the ordinance — by which anyone established that you should so seek yourselves — an ordinance from God; and it cannot constrain or diminish common mercy or common righteousness, nor can it of necessity shut out from the community those whom righteousness does not shut out. Likewise, to seek what is yours, this is (15a) according to the world. For the world seeks what is its own, says Christus in Saint John’s gospel in the thirteenth chapter. Thereby this is worldly and fleshly wisdom, and that is not subject to God’s law, and it is folly and lawlessness before God. Likewise, said with a brief argument: the person who established the ordinance, or had it done, or gave according to acceptance of persons or according to wealth and not according to all suitability — this was sin, and would have been sin to do so, before it was enacted, when he agreed to it; and therefore it is all the greater sin where other people are to be drawn into it or bound to it.
