This fellowship, this brotherhood or sisterhood, this community, this friendship, this *communicatio* and mutual help, this life, this state, this office, this exercise—these are spiritual things, of which no upright person doubts. For as Autissiodorensis says: “The Holy Spirit is given in brotherhood.” And Christ said: “Where two or three of you are gathered in my name, there am I in the midst of you.” However perfectly or imperfectly one practices the virtue of religion in these matters—since the works and virtue are upright works of religion and true virtue in love—the fellowships and the brotherhoods are spiritual. For of the spirit nothing is born but spirit; and spirit and spirit, gathered together in the spirit, are nothing but spirit, and that increases and purifies all spirituality. Hence it is manifest that to wish to buy or sell these things is open simony, at least in the heart, even if it were not also forbidden in the Church. For these things are, according to the judgment of God and according to right virtue, religious spiritual fellowship or brotherhood and religious community or religious life or religious persons or exercises; they belong to the state of perfect religious persons—whether according to an order, or outside the state and fellowship and brotherhood of an order. Those, then, who more perfectly cleave to God and more despise temporal things in poverty, in chastity, and in obedience, these before the judgment of God are called *autonomatice*—that is, in the higher sense—religious persons. And because the surrender of temporal things—surrender of goods, of body, and of will—is more readily seen than the inward turning to the spirit, it happens that, according to human sight, one looks more to the outward surrender than to the inward (19ba) turning, in that people from the outside are wont to take note of and judge the perfection of the virtue of religion or of their brotherhood or sisterhood. Yet it is possible that a person who was outside any fellowship, or who had personal property and a wife, might in heart be more purely and perfectly separated from temporal goods and temporal pleasure and from his own will than someone who was in the community or in the fellowship, who had renounced all these outward things.
