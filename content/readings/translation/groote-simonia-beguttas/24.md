And one may truly say, says the same teacher, that a spiritual thing is one in which the Holy Spirit is had. For one truly has the Holy Spirit in the virtues and in the sacraments and in the working of miracles and in prophecy, with distinction. And among these three the highest spiritual good is virtue and love, and it is far more spiritual than the others, because they make men more spiritual in themselves and unite them closer to God in the spirit than do the signs of the sacraments or the gifts of working miracles or prophecy. For both good and evil — as is evident — may receive and give the sacraments, or also have gifts either to prophesy or to work miracles, as has often been seen and has come to pass, as Christ says and as is written in Matthew VII. And the sacraments and miracles and prophecies have been ordained by God and by the saints in order to obtain virtue, and in order to unite the soul more closely in love and with virtues to God, and to bind it to spiritual life and works. And not only the divine virtues — such as faith, hope, and love, which make a man spiritual and exalted in spirit — are more spiritual than the gifts of miracles or prophecy, but even the rational virtues are more spiritual in God than the working of miracles. This Saint Augustine demonstrates on Paul’s epistle *ad Galatas*, and speaks thus: “The Lord did not say: take up my yoke and learn from me, that I raise up dead bodies of four days, and drive out all enemies from men’s bodies and sicknesses, and other things of the like. But he says: ‘Take up (17a) my yoke and learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart.’ The first things — that is, miracles — are signs of spiritual things; but to be meek and humble of heart and a keeper of divine love, these are themselves spiritual things.
