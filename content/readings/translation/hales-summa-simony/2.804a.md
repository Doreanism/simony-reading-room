#### Problem II. Whether it is permitted to redeem tithes from laymen.

Next it is asked whether it is permitted to redeem tithes from laymen.

1. Since to redeem is to buy, and it is not permitted to buy, therefore neither is it permitted to redeem; for it is said in canon law: “Just as that church could not be bought, so neither can it be redeemed.”

2. Furthermore, they cannot be exchanged for a temporal thing; therefore, consequently, neither can they be redeemed.

3. Furthermore, laymen seem to possess them unjustly, according to what Gregorius says: “We forbid that tithes, which canonical authority shows to have been granted for the use of piety, be possessed by laymen; but if they do not restore them to the Church, let them know that they commit the crime of sacrilege.” Therefore they cannot sell them; therefore it is not permitted to buy from them.

\[Solution\]: To this it is answered that they can be redeemed, provided it be done by the authority of the bishop, to whose disposal the tithes belong. The authority of the judge has much weight in such matters, as is found in the *Extra*, *De praebendis*, *Nisi*, and in the *Extra*, *De rerum permutatione*, *Quaesitum*. They can also be received in pledge, provided they do not pertain “to other churches”: for then it is forbidden, as is found in the *Extra*, *De decimis*, *Interdicimus*.
