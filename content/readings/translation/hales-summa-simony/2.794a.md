### Article I. On the gift from the hand.

#### Problem I. Whether an ecclesiastical judge who receives gifts is a simoniac.

Concerning the gift from the hand, however, it is asked whether an ecclesiastical judge may receive any gifts, and, if he does receive them, whether he is to be judged a simoniac.

And it seems that he is. 1. For it is said in Matthew 10:8: *Freely have ye received, freely give*. And it is said in the Council of Toledo: “Whatsoever is bestowed by the consolation of invisible grace ought never at all to be sold for gain or for any sort of reward.” But one who receives gifts, or who gives them, exposes the thing to sale or purchase; therefore he is a simoniac.

2. Likewise, Ecclesiasticus 20:31: *Presents and gifts blind the eyes of judges, and, as it were with a muzzle, make them dumb, so that they cannot correct*. In no way, therefore, is a gift to be given to an ecclesiastical judge or to be received from him.

On the contrary. a. In human law it is held that food and drink may be received and are not forbidden to them, namely to secular judges; by parity of reasoning, therefore, ecclesiastical judges may receive gifts of this kind.
