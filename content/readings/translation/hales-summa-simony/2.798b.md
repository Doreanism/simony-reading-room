Hence Gregory, in the *Registrum*: «Before all things let it be your care that in an election neither any giving of rewards intervene in any way whatsoever, nor the patronage of any persons whatsoever prevail.» But if entreaties are made on behalf of a worthy person, simony is not committed: for it is presumed of the person himself that the intention is upright, and the one ordaining or electing does not principally have regard to the entreaties.

But it can be asked why simony is less committed in entreaties offered on behalf of a worthy person than in money: for if money intervenes, simony is committed. — And it is answered to this that entreaties are ordered toward making manifest that it is fitting for such a one to be ordained or beneficed: for the language of supplication on behalf of a worthy person is for this purpose; money, however, is not for this, but rather to move inordinately the affection of the one conferring the Order or the benefice.

## Chapter IV. On the material differences of simony.

The first, concerning the spiritual itself; the second, concerning what is annexed to a spiritual thing; the third, concerning that which is both spiritual and annexed to a spiritual thing.
