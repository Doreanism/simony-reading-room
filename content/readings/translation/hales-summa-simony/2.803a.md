IV. Likewise, it is asked whether tithes can be given in fief to laymen by the Church.

And it seems that they can, 1. by what Innocent says: “We understand this of those tithes which have been granted to laymen in perpetual fief.”

2. Furthermore, the custom of many lands holds this, which is the best interpreter of laws.

On the contrary. a. It is held in the *Extra*, *De decimis*, *Ad haec*, where it is said: “The donation of a tithe which an abbot granted to a certain layman to be held by succession, since the sanctuary of God ought not to be possessed by hereditary right,” we judge to be null.

I reply: I. To the first it must be answered that it is simoniacal to sell or to buy the right of receiving tithes. I mean the primary right, which has been granted by the Lord to clerics alone; it is also owed “as a sign of universal dominion,” which belongs to God himself, as Hieronymus says, *Super Malachiam*; furthermore, because “tithes are the tribute of needy souls.”

\[Replies to the objections\]: 1. To what is objected to the contrary \[it must be said\] that, although knights may have the right of receiving a share, they do not have the right of receiving tithes, inasmuch as tithes are established to be “the tribute of needy souls.” It is nevertheless possible to come to an agreement with those who hold lands that they should give the lord a certain fixed portion of the very fruits of the land.
