2. If, however, it be asked to which article he is principally opposed, it must be said that it is to the one concerning the Holy Spirit, by reason of the sacraments and sacred things. Whence “those who perform this iniquitous deed detract from the Holy Spirit,” placing what is beyond price under a price. Yet such a person is not called a heretic *per se* on account of false credulity in things that ought not to be believed, but on account of a disordered will, by which he is principally separated from the Holy Spirit, inasmuch as by him holiness is given or made manifest, as in the gift of prophecy or in the power of working miracles.

\[Solution\]: To this it must be said that one sin is called graver than another either on account of a greater proneness joined with enormity, or one is called graver than another because it is more contrary to the catholic faith. In the first way the simoniac plague is called the greater sin; in the second way heresy itself is so called. For heresy is precisely that in which the principal division is made from the faith of the highest truth.

\[Replies to the objections\]: 1. To the first objection it must be replied that it is said to “surpass other spiritual diseases” on account of its tenacity: for it is torn away with difficulty because of its tenacity, in that it arises from the root of covetousness.

2. To the second it must be said that what Gregory Nazianzen says is to be turned to this, that this sin by a certain singularity is more opposed to the sacraments of the Church on account of the proneness of human covetousness, and the Lord prefigured this in the casting out of the buyers and sellers from the temple—not that others are not worthy of being cast out. Whence in detestation of this kind of crime it is said that “as soon as” it has become manifest, “he must be expelled and driven from the Church of God.”
