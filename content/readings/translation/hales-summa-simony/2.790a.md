For it is said in Luke 6:35: *Lend, hoping for nothing in return*. From which it is gathered that usury is contracted in someone’s disordered hope “beyond the principal”. And the same is held in *Extra, De usuris, Consuluit*. Therefore much more should this be the case in simony, so that by deliberate willing alone the sin of simony is contracted.

2. Furthermore, since sin is contracted by the will itself as by an evil root, it is asked why the qualifier “deliberate” is added, since even a will that is not deliberate seems to constitute sin.

3. Next, an inquiry is made concerning what is added, “of buying or selling something spiritual,” etc. For it seems that simony could be contracted in ways other than by buying or selling, as in exchanges of dignities or ecclesiastical benefices where the authority of the diocesan bishop does not intervene. For the *Canon* says that in spiritual matters “every agreement must cease”; and Urban says “that exchanges of prebends cannot lawfully be made, especially when a prior pact has been made, which, with respect to spiritual matters or those attached to spiritual matters, carries the taint of simony.” Therefore simony is not contracted only in buying or selling. — To the same effect is what Alexander says, where it is stated “that there can be no transaction concerning a contested sacred thing; for sacred things, if they are possessed by means of something given or retained or by arbitration, we say have the appearance of simony.”

4. Likewise, an objection is raised concerning what is called “spiritual”. For one may ask what is meant by “spiritual”. On this account it is asked whether an advocate commits simony: for he sells just patronage, and just patronage is something spiritual. — On the contrary. Augustine says that “it is permitted to an advocate to sell just patronage, but it is not permitted to a judge to sell judgment”. Therefore simony is not committed in selling every kind of spiritual thing.
