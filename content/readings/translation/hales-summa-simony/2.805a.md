#### Problem IV. Whether a bishop who confers a prebend on the ground of kinship commits simony.

Next, it is asked whether a bishop who confers a prebend on a kinsman solely on the ground of kinship commits simony.

And it seems that he does not, because in conferring something spiritual he receives no temporal good as a price.

To the contrary. 1. If someone were to confer prebends on his nephews so that he himself, the bishop, might enjoy the fruits of those prebends, it would be simony; likewise, if a bishop were to confer a prebend on someone so that the bishop’s father or some other layman might enjoy the fruits of that prebend, he would commit simony; by parity of reasoning, the same holds if a bishop confers a prebend on a kinsman in consideration of kinship so that he himself may receive the fruits.

2. Again, simony is contracted through love of money; therefore, by parity of reasoning, it will be contracted through love of flesh.

3. Again, Bede says: “The sellers of doves are those who exercise the rank or spiritual grace in the Church, which they received by the Lord’s bestowal, not with a simple intention, but for the sake of some human recompense.” But those who confer prebends in consideration of kinship do not act with a simple intention nor with a view to God, but with regard to some temporal recompense; therefore they commit simony.

4. Again, Gregory says that the sin is “graver” when a bishop confers tithes “on his kinsmen”; and he determines that this is the sin of simony; therefore by the same reasoning, if prebends are conferred on kinsmen, it will be simoniacal.
