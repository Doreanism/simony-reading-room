\[Solution\]: To this it must be said that in the judgment of God, who beholds the heart, if the will of the ecclesiastical judge is moved to deliver judgment by the price of a gift, whether great or small, it is always simony. But the Church regards the outward deed, and because small gifts do not seem such as ought to corrupt the mind of an ecclesiastical judge, it is not reckoned simony before the Church. Hence “in the receiving and giving of gifts three things are to be attended to, namely the quality of the persons giving and receiving, the quantity of the gift, and the time of the giving. The quality of the persons, that is, by whom and to whom, namely whether it has been given by a poor man to a rich one or conversely, or by a rich man to one who is well-off; the estimation of the gift, whether it be of greater or lesser value; the time of the giving, whether it be conferred in pressing necessity or at some other time.” And according to this is the presumption to be had of simony or of its opposite.
