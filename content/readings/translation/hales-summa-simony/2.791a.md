As to what follows, it must be understood that under the name of buying and selling is included every contract concerning spiritual things except the contract of exchange, which is not fitting to be made except by the authority of a superior, namely the diocesan; whence it is taken there broadly for any illicit agreement concerning spiritual things by means of temporal things.

4. As to what follows, it is said that there is a different consideration in an advocate and in a judge. For a judge is bound to render a just judgment, but an advocate may receive a moderate salary for his labor, both according to civil and canonical laws; for that labor does not pertain to him by virtue of an ecclesiastical office he holds, as the judge’s does by virtue of the office enjoined upon him.

5. To the objection raised concerning a secular judge, the response is that, although a secular judge sins gravely in selling a just judgment, he does not however commit simony. For “spiritual” is said of that by which the Holy Spirit is possessed, or by which He is given, or by which He is signified as possessed: by which He is possessed, as virtue; by which He is given, as a sacrament; by which He is signified as possessed, as the gift of prophecy or the power of working miracles. But a secular judge can sell something spiritual in none of these ways.
