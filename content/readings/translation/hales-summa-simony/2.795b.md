\[Solution\]: To this it must be said that “it is licit to redeem a harassment” which is unjustly inflicted concerning “a spiritual thing, where the one from whom it is sought has no right,” nor can he obtain his right through a superior; nor is a spiritual thing bought there, but a temporal one: for harassment is a temporal thing. — That it is licit to redeem from one who has no right in the thing, although it is not licit for him to receive, is clear from what is said in *C. 1, q. 3, Quaesitum*, where Urban prohibits “all venality”; but he concedes that “tithes may be possessed in peace by monasteries under a redemption of this kind from thirty years and upward.”

it is gathered that it is licit for bishops and other prelates to receive a procuration for certain works performed.

\[Solution\]: To this it must be said that in such cases simony is not committed, if a moderate procuration is exacted, as is held in the *Extra*, *De simonia*, *Cum sit Romana*, where it is said that “for the consecration of churches nothing can or ought to be exacted beyond a procuration”; and in the *Extra*, *De praescriptionibus*, *Cum ex officii*, where it is said that “a procuration is owed also by reason of visitation, against which prescription cannot run.”
