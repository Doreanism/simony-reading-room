2. Furthermore, it can be granted in fief, as is held in the same place, *Cum saeculum*; and if it can be granted in fief, by parity of reasoning it can be sold once the sale of a town has been made, or by reason of that through which the right of patronage is acquired.

\[Solution\]: 1. To this it is answered that the right of patronage is not sold as a part of the whole nor by itself; therefore neither can it be sold with something else, but rather it “passes with the estate,” as is held from what was said above. And if a town, in which there is a right of patronage, is on this account bought at a higher price, this is said to be simony: for then the sale would in some way reach the right of patronage.

2. As to that which is asked, “whether it can be granted in fief”: it must be said that “it passes with the estate unless it is excepted,” and thus the “right of patronage is acquired by reason of fiefs that have been purchased or otherwise acquired.” But it cannot be given in fief by itself, as is held in the same place, *De iure patronatus*.
