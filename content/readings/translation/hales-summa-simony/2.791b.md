6. To the final point it must be said that there are two ways of speaking about consecrated objects, such as crosses or chalices: by reason of their matter or by reason of their form. By reason of their matter, when the consecrated form is no longer present, and in this manner they may be sold in case of necessity; whence Ambrose, in his book *De officiis*: “It is permitted, in determined cases, to break up, melt down, and sell consecrated vessels of the church”; “it is needful that the form of the mystical cup not depart from the church, lest the ministry of the sacred chalice be conceded to wicked uses.” But it is not so with oil or chrism: for these are consecrated by reason of their very matter.

But if it be asked concerning the timbers which are in a consecrated church, whether, if the church is destroyed, they may be converted into the construction of secular buildings, response can be made through what is said by Pope Hyginus, *De consecratione*, dist. 1, *Ligna*: “The timbers of a dedicated church ought not to be joined to any other work except to another church, or to be burned with fire, or for the benefit of the brothers in a monastery” — “and this in religious places, such as the chapter house or the dormitory, but not in the kitchen or for trenching vineyards” — “but they ought not be admitted into the works of laymen.” Whence in the Council of Chalcedon it is said: “Monasteries which have once been dedicated to God, with the counsel of the bishops, are to remain monasteries in perpetuity, and the things which pertain to them ought to be reserved to the monasteries.”
