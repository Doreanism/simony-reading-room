### Article III. On the gift of the tongue.

#### Problem I. Whether it is permitted to petition for oneself for some ecclesiastical benefice.

Third, we must speak of the gift of the tongue, and it is asked first whether anyone may petition for himself for a church or a dignity or some ecclesiastical benefice.

And it seems that he may not. 1. For it is said in Hebrews: *Let no man take the honor to himself, but he that is called by God, as Aaron was.* Therefore he ought not to put himself forward by word or deed; therefore no one ought to petition for himself.

2. Furthermore, in the very act of petitioning he shows himself to desire it; but Gregory says: “Just as a position of governance is to be denied to those who desire it, so it is to be offered to those who flee from it.” Therefore no one ought to petition for an ecclesiastical benefice for himself.

On the contrary. a. A man may be ordained and not have a sufficient benefice, or have no benefice at all; since therefore it is owed to him by right on account of the honor of the Church — as is had in the *Extra*, *De praebendis*: “It shall not be lawful for any bishop to ordain clerics and provide them no maintenance”; and again: “If a bishop ordain anyone to the diaconate or priesthood without a definite title from which he may receive the necessities of life, let him supply him with the necessities for so long” etc. — therefore he may lawfully petition for an ecclesiastical benefice.
