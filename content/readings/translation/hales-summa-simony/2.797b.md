\[Solution\]: To this it is answered that one may not petition for himself for Orders, or for a benefice to which the cure of souls is attached. He may, however, petition for a simple benefice, since it is owed to him by right, as for instance if he is ordained and has no sufficient benefice. For Gregory says: “Ordinations which are made with the intervention of a price, or of entreaties, or of service rendered to some person with that intention, we judge to be false, since those who are ordained in such a manner do not enter through the door, that is, Christ” — and they are said to be false with respect to the exercise of office — and this is expounded at \[C.\] 1, q. 1, *Non itaque*.

But what if some worthy person should offer entreaties on his own behalf — is he a simoniac? It seems so from what has been said, and some hold this very view. — But it is a hard thing to say, since the laws are not altogether explicit on this, and according to it many would be condemned. Yet it is safer that no one, however worthy, should offer entreaties for himself unless it be owed to him by right, as has been said: for by this very act he can render himself unworthy. Gregory: “He who voluntarily seeks office or thrusts himself forward unbidden is without doubt to be repelled. For one who thus strives to climb to higher things, what else does he accomplish but, by growing, to be diminished?”
