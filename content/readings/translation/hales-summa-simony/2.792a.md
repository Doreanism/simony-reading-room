### Article III. Whether simony is a graver sin than heresy.

The question is also raised concerning the comparison of simony with heresy.

It seems indeed that simony is a graver sin than some heresy. 1. For it is said in the *Extra*, *De simonia*, *Sicut*, that “the simoniac plague surpasses other diseases by its magnitude.” Now sins are called diseases; therefore it is a greater sin than heresy.

2. Likewise, Gregory Nazianzen: “The Lord, in order to show that those who buy and sell this grace have no share in it, by grace let all other sinners remain in the temple, but rebuking and scourging only those who bought and sold, he cast them out of the temple.” From this it seems that this sin is graver than other sins.

3. Likewise, *Causa* I, *quaestio* 1, *Eos qui per pecuniam*: “More tolerable is the impious heresy of Macedonius and those around him who assail the Holy Spirit. For they, in their delirium, confess the Holy Spirit to be a creature and a servant of the Father and the Son; but these men make the same Holy Spirit their own servant. For every master, if he wishes, sells what he has; and likewise he who buys, wishing to be its master, acquires what he has bought through the price of money.” From this it is argued that simony is a worse sin than the heresy of the Macedonians.
