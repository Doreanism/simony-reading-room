Now, just as it is simony to sell a blessed piece of land, not on account of the land, but on account of the blessing, which is spiritual, so also is there simony in certain very small things. Hence it is simony to sell blessed bread and blessed water: for they are spiritual things and blot out many venial sins.

### Article II. On what is annexed to a spiritual thing.

#### Problem I. On the sale of the right of receiving tithes.

And it seems that it is not lawful. 1. For Gregory says, *I, quaest. 3, Pervenit quod*: «If any bishop be henceforth found who confers the tithes and oblations of Christians upon lay persons such as knights or servants, let him be held a transgressor of the divine precept among the greatest heretics and antichrists; and, just as the Nicene Synod decreed concerning simoniacs, both the bishop who gives and those who receive for a price or benefit are to be consigned to eternal fires». Paschal says the like following thereupon.

To the contrary. a. The fruits of tithes are temporal things; but temporal things can be bought and sold without simony; therefore it is not simoniacal to sell or to buy tithes.

III. Next, it is asked whether laymen can receive oblations given to churches.

And it seems that they cannot. 1. For Damasus says: «Oblations which are offered within the holy Church are in no way to be held under the dominion of laymen».

2. Likewise, «the oblations which Christians offer for their sins» are offered to this end, that prayer may be made for the people; but it is not the office of laymen, but of priests, to pray for the people; therefore it is not lawful for the laymen themselves to receive oblations.
