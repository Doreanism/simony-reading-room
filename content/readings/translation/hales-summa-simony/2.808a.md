To the contrary. a. Testimony is owed; but Gregory says that “those who buy in the temple are those who, when they are unwilling to render to their neighbor what is just and disdain to do what is owed, purchase sin by paying a price.” Therefore, by the same reasoning, those who accept a price for that to which they are bound are also sellers, and so likewise for the testimony of the truth.

Furthermore, a just judgment ought to be rendered freely: hence simony is committed if money is accepted for a just judgment; whence Gregory: “He commits fraud against God who sells for the receipt of money the justice which he ought to have imparted freely.” Therefore, by the same reasoning, it can be said concerning testimony.

## Chapter V. On the penalty for simony.

Fifth, the question is asked concerning the penalty for simony. Concerning this it must be noted that simony is either in an Order or in a benefice. If in an Order, he does not receive the exercise of it, but is by the law itself suspended both with respect to himself and with respect to others; he is also punished by deposition, if the crime has in some way been established before his judge by inquisition. ## Chapter VI. On the cases in which it can be doubted whether simony is committed.

Sixth, the question is asked concerning certain cases in which it is doubted whether there is simony.

I. It can be asked whether it is simoniacal to conceal another’s sin, when it pertains to one’s office to reveal it, and this is done by the intervention of money.
