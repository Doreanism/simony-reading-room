To objection 1, however, it must be said that the case of true testimony and true counsel is not the same. For true testimony pertains to the very substance of the case to be settled, whereas counsel is a certain external aid, as is also patronage: hence witnesses are compelled to give testimony to the truth, but counselors are not compelled to give counsel in cases. — But here some draw a further distinction: that in all these cases, if knowledge is sold, it is simoniacal, both major and minor according to the differences among the kinds of knowledge. For his labor, however, an advocate or counselor can accept something, since they are not bound; but a judge and a witness cannot accept anything for their labor in judging or testifying, since they are bound to those acts.

Furthermore, one who receives a benefice simoniacally is bound to renounce the benefice and to restore all the fruits received, as is found in Augustine, *Ad Macedonium*: “If another’s property, on account of which sin has been committed, can be returned and is not returned, penance is not done but feigned; but if it is truly done, the sin will not be remitted unless what was taken is restored.”

And it seems that it is not \[simony\], 1. by the definition of simony. For what is the spiritual thing that is here sold? For it is the very concealment of sin that takes place for money; and this is not a spiritual thing, but the contrary of a spiritual thing; therefore there does not seem to be a deliberate will to sell something spiritual.
