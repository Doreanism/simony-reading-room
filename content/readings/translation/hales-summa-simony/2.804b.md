\[To the objections\]: 1. To the objection it must be said that, although it is not permitted simply to buy or sell, it is nevertheless permitted to redeem on account of the right which the Church has in the matter; and that which is redeemed is redeemed only in order to remove the impediment of the right.

2. To the second it must be said that, although an exchange does not occur between something annexed to a spiritual thing on the one side and something purely temporal on the other, as is found in the *Extra*, *De rerum permutatione*, *Ad quaestiones*, a redemption can nevertheless take place. Nor does that redemption occur between something purely spiritual and something temporal on the other side — for a purely spiritual thing could in no way be possessed by a layman — but between a temporal thing, which a layman was able to obtain, and a temporal thing: hence in such a case a temporal thing is redeemed by a temporal thing.

3. To the third it is answered that, although after the constitution of the Council laymen cannot justly receive the fruits of tithes against the will of the Church — and this is consonant with the primitive law of the Church, as is had from Gregorius — nevertheless those who redeem can justly redeem. This is because for a certain time, by the Church’s concession on account of a necessary cause, with the authority of a superior intervening, laymen were able for a time to receive the fruits of tithes, and this falls within the contract.

#### Problem III. Whether it is permitted for a bishop to buy tithes from some neighboring church.

\[Solution\]: To this it is answered that the case is not the same. For in those tithes which a layman holds, the bishop has a right, and therefore it is permitted to redeem them; but in others, which are outside his diocese and do not in any way pertain to his diocese, he has no right, and therefore it is not permitted to buy them: for buying and selling do not occur in things which are purely spiritual or annexed to spiritual things.
