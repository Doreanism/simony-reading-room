\[Solution\]: 1. To this it is answered that it is not licit to preach for temporal things in such a way that they be set as the price of one’s preaching, but it is licit to preach with a secondary intention, namely that the hearers be moved to works of piety and so come to the aid of others: for the principal intention is that the preaching be done for the sake of God. As for the fact that the Apostle preached for the sake of collections being made, this was a secondary aim, not in the sense that the principal end was established there, nor that this was the price of his preaching.

c. Furthermore, the law says that “the science of civil law is not to be valued at a monetary price.” Much less so the rational sciences.

\[Solution\]: To this it must be said that it is simony to sell knowledge. Nevertheless, the masters of the arts may accept money for their labor, but the knowledge itself they ought to give freely; otherwise they would commit simony.

\[To the objections\]: 1. To the first objection, then, it must be said that the Holy Spirit is possessed in two ways: as a light illuminating the intellect, and in this way he is possessed in every form of knowledge; or as a warmth inflaming the affections, and in this way he is possessed through the virtues and the sacraments of the Church.

2. To the other it must be said that that authoritative text is to be understood of a spiritual purchase. For the knowledge of truth is not to be bought with money, but in the manner taught in Proverbs 4:7: *The beginning of wisdom: possess wisdom*, as if to say: if you wish to have wisdom, take hold of it, because she offers herself to the one who seeks her; for if you seek her, you shall find her as though she were money or treasure. Concerning this kind of purchase, Proverbs 17:16: *What does it profit a fool to have riches, when he cannot buy wisdom?*
