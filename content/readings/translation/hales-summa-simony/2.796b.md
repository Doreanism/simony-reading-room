\[Solution\]: To which it is answered that it matters whether one renders worthy service, whether honorable or dishonorable, and again whether the person is worthy or unworthy, and again whether an agreement intervenes or not. “If a worthy person renders worthy service,” or honorable service, and no agreement intervenes, and he does not principally “have his mind set on this” — this is said on account of what is stated by Gregory, \[*C.*\] *1, q. 1, Ordinationes* — although he may have it secondarily, he does not commit simony: for it is called “a gift from service: service rendered when not owed.” “But if he is unworthy,” and especially if he renders dishonorable service, simony is committed, as Gregory says in the *Registrum*, and is found at \[*C.*\] *1, q. 1, Si quis*. But if an agreement intervenes, it is simoniacal: for a spiritual thing, such as a church, ought not to be brought into an agreement.

But it is still asked whether someone may render spiritual service for an ecclesiastical benefice, with a condition intervening.
