II. To the other point, it must be said that it is not simony if a condition of money intervenes in the matrimonial contract. For they say that in every ecclesiastical sacrament in which grace is conferred, if a bargain or giving of money intervenes by which it is bought, it is simony; but in marriage grace is not conferred, and it is a sacrament prior to both Laws under the law of nature, and therefore a condition of money may intervene as a stimulus to consent.

#### Problem II. Whether a priest may sing Mass for money.

q. 2, *Caritatem*, and the Apostle says the same; therefore they are not bound to labor in the Church unless they have the means by which they may be sustained.

\[Solution\]: 1-3. To which it must be answered that to take or to give for temporal goods is said in many ways: either so that the ministers of God may be sustained, or as a certain incitement to the praise of God, or so that a price be set upon those things, or so that an end be placed in them. If in the first way, it is lawful for him to sing Mass who is not bound to do so and who lacks his support, by receiving money, provided no bargain intervenes and the principal intention is not directed to this. And this is held by what Hieronymus says, *Super Michaeam*. For he says, reciting what Saul said to his servant: *What shall we bring to the man of God?* And the servant said: *Behold, there is found in my hand the fourth part of a silver stater; let us give it to the man of God, that he may show us our way*; and what is said in 1 Kings 14:3 of the wife of Jeroboam, where it is said: *Take in your hand ten loaves and a cake*
