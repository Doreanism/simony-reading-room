\[Solution\]: a. To this it is replied that it is lawful to give temporal goods to religious men so that they may pray for them. For the preposition ‘for’ (*pro*) here does not signify the relation of valuation, as when it is said ‘I give money for cloth,’ but signifies the relation of incitement, namely that they may be moved to spiritual friendship and consequently to praying for them; and in this way it is lawful, nor is anything beyond price thereby set under a temporal price.

#### Problem V. Whether it is lawful to give or receive money for burial

Next it is asked, I. whether it is lawful to give or to receive money for burial. By “burial” I mean the office which properly pertains to the priest.

That it is lawful seems to follow a. from the custom long observed in the Church; and a custom which has arisen from devotion in such matters is not to be abolished; therefore it can lawfully be received or given.

To the contrary. 1. It is said in *Extra*, *De simonia*, *Non satis*, where it is stated that “for burial and for the receiving of chrism and oil no exaction of any price whatsoever shall be attempted, nor may anyone defend his guilt under the pretext of any custom, since length of time does not lessen sin but increases it.”

\[Solution\]: To this it is replied through what is said in *Extra*, *De simonia*, *Ad apostolicam*, from which it is gathered that priests ought not to extort or demand anything before burial or for burial: for this would be simony, as is held in *Extra*, *Cum in Ecclesiae corpore*; but \[after\] burial one may demand something by the command of a superior, where such a custom exists, and this is stated in that Decretal, *Ad Apostolicam*.

II. a. Now just as it is simony to sell the burial of the dead, so it is simony to sell consecrated ground given over for burying the dead.
