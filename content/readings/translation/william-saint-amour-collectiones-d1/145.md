# Collectiones, Distinction I: On the *Opitulationes* (Helpers)

§. Of those who are called *Opitulationes* \[helpers\], some are already instituted in the Churches as ordinary officers, such as Archdeacons and Archpriests. Nevertheless, when the necessity or utility of the Churches demands it, helpers may be increased extraordinarily through delegation by superiors. Hence those whom Bishops, when hindered by various occupations, send through their dioceses to exercise the governance of souls in their stead—according as the Bishops themselves judge expedient—may be called extraordinary helpers, as one reads in Extra, *De Officio Ordinarii*, cap. *Inter cetera*. By the same reasoning, the same may be said of lesser Prelates, such as parish priests and other Rectors of Churches: that is, that they may send, summon, or invite Preachers through their parishes, as one reads in 7, q. 1, cap. *Episcopi vel Presbyteri*.
