§. Likewise, it is the power and office of the Bishops to resist Pseudo-Preachers. Hence on 1 Timothy 1, *In principio*, the Glos. says: *The Apostle, having left Timothy in Asia as Bishop, writes to him concerning the Episcopal office; namely, how he should resist the Pseudo-Apostles.* Behold, that the office and power of the Bishops is to examine preachers, and to resist Pseudo-Preachers.

§. Likewise, in this consists the honor, jurisdiction, and power of the Bishops, and the right, distinction, and peace of the Churches: that in one Diocese there be but one Bishop; in one Archdeaconry, but one Archdeacon; in one Parish, but one Parish Rector; for, as Hieronymus says, *Each Church has its own Bishops, its own Archdeacons*—supply: in one Archdeaconry—*its own Archbishops; and every Ecclesiastical order makes use of its own Rectors*—as if to say, *not of those of others*. 7 q. 1 cap. *In apibus*. For one Church ought not to have several heads, like a monster, Extra, *De Offic. Ordin.* cap. *Quoniam in plerisque*; otherwise the Church *would not be a wife, but a harlot*, 21 q. 2 cap. *Sicut in una*. Therefore another Bishop ought not, in the Diocese of one Bishop, to preach publicly or to perform other public offices or to govern the people, unless he has been invited by the Diocesan or by his Presbyters. 7 q. 1 cap. *Episcopi, vel Presbyteri*; 9 q. 2 cap. *Nullus primas*; cap. *Nullum Episcoporum*; cap. *Nullus Episcopus*; cap. *Episcopum*; cap. *Episcopi*; and cap. *Non invitati*.
