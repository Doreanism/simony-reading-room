Much less, therefore, ought they to burden the priests subject to them in procuring those sent by them—especially since they can demand nothing from their subjects beyond the statutes of the Fathers. Whence in the Council of Pope Leo it is read: *Let it be lawful for no bishop to demand from a subject priest, or from any other cleric, beyond the statutes of the Fathers, or to impose additional levies as corvées*. 16. q. 1. ch. *Nulli*. Likewise, since according to the doctrine of the Lord and of the Apostle, an apostolic preacher, or one canonically sent, ought to take the necessities of life from his hearers—that is, from those to whom he preaches, as has been shown above in the immediately preceding section—it remains that a parish priest is no more bound to the procuration of such a preacher than is any one of the other hearers of the same; unless perhaps that preacher were sent to supply the deficiency of the priest, as a coadjutor in spiritual matters. Which should not be done except upon judicial inquiry into the cause, or at the priest’s own request. 7. q. 1. ch. *Scripsit mihi*, and ch. *Quia Frater*.

§. It is therefore evident from the foregoing that, with respect to those who are canonically sent to preach the Gospel, the power of living from the Gospel cannot be removed or taken away from them; but they must take their expenses in different ways, according to their different missions. It is also evident that all preachers sent by the Lord Pope must live from the Gospel, and expenses must be supplied to them by those to whom they are sent, and this by the Lord’s right, that is, by divine precept. But it is not lawful to dispense or ordain against the Lord’s precept or his ordinance. 25. q. 1. ch. *Sunt quidam dicentes*. Nor can the preacher renounce this power, since it was not introduced in his favor, but in favor of the Gospel—namely, that through it he may be the more unhindered in preaching the Gospel, as was shown a little earlier; and that through it he may be able to restore his strength in the labor of the Gospel, as the Gloss says on Luke 10, *The labourer is worthy of his hire*, and on the same words in 1 Timothy 5, *The labourer is worthy of his reward*.
