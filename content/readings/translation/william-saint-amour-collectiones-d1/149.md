Hence in Book 4 of the *History of Saint Clement the Pope*, at the end, drawn from a sermon of Blessed Peter the Apostle in the city of Tripoli, it is read thus: *Lest the true religion of the one and true God be restored, the Devil—after the Lord said to him,* THOU SHALT WORSHIP THE LORD THY GOD, AND HIM ONLY SHALT THOU SERVE—*terrified and fearful, hastens continually to send into this world Pseudo-Prophets and Pseudo-Apostles and false teachers, who would speak indeed in the name of Christ, yet would do the will of the demon; on account of which, watch the more cautiously that you give credence to none of the teachers, except one who shall have brought sure testimony from James the Brother of the Lord at Jerusalem, or from whomever shall be \[in office\] after him; for unless someone has gone up there and been approved there as a fit and faithful teacher to preach the Word of Christ—unless, I say, he brings testimony from thence—he is by no means to be received. But neither prophet nor apostle in this time is to be expected by you, save us; for there is one True Prophet, whose words we, the twelve Apostles, preach.* It appears, then, that besides the Bishops, who succeeded the Apostles, and besides the Presbyters, who succeeded the seventy-two Disciples, as has been shown above in the same distinction §. *Quoniam igitur*, other uncertain preachers—not chosen by the Church or by the Prelates of the Church, or concerning whose election or canonical mission nothing is established—are to be deemed Pseudo \[-Preachers\].

§. Likewise, it is not the intention of the supreme Pontiff to diminish, or to constrain, or to infringe, or to impede the honor, power, jurisdiction, and offices of his Brothers and Fellow-Bishops, according to that saying of Gregorius: *Nor do I reckon that to be my honor in which I know my Brothers to lose their honor; for my honor is the honor of the universal Church; my honor is the accustomed Strength of my Brothers; then am I honored, when to each one the honor due him is not denied*, dist. 99 can. *Ecce in praefatione*. Likewise Gregorius: *Far be it from me to infringe the statutes of the Elders concerning my Fellow-Priests in any Church whatsoever; for I do myself injury if I disturb the rights of my Brothers*, 25 q. 2 cap. *Quod vero dicitis*. Likewise Gregorius: *If his own jurisdiction is not preserved to each one, what else is brought about, except that through us—through whom the Ecclesiastical order ought to have been kept unimpaired—it is thrown into confusion?* 11 q. 1 cap. *Pervenit*.
