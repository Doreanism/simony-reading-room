Likewise, 2 Timothy 2: *The husbandman that laboureth must be first partaker of the fruits*; Gloss: *That is, the necessities of life from his hearers.* For scandal must be avoided in this matter, 10. q. 3. ch. *Cauendum*. Whence on Luke 9, on the words *Take nothing for your journey*, the Gloss says: *The necessities of life are owed to preachers by those to whom they preach, when they can be taken without scandal.* But if they have been sent by a delegated mission, then a distinction is to be made: they have either been sent by the Supreme Pontiff, or by lower prelates. If by the Supreme Pontiff, then they can take their expenses from those for whose governance they are sent, as legates of the one sending them. *Extra*, *De Censibus, Exactionibus, et Procurationibus*, ch. *Cum instantia*. But if such delegated preachers have been sent by bishops or by other lesser prelates—who, because of the burden of governing their subjects, have stipends fixed for them by those subjects—then those sending them must furnish necessities to those they send, or assign them prebends from which they may be competently sustained in the office committed to them. *Extra*, *De Officio Iudicis Ordinarii*, ch. *Inter cætera*. And this on the model of an imperial legate assigned to a province, who must live not from the property of the provincials but from the property of the imperial treasury, because the emperor receives fixed tributes from the provincials, as is read in the *Authentica* *Vt nulli iudicum*, §. *Nulli*, Collation 9—which is not the case with the Supreme Pontiff. Therefore the subjects of bishops are not bound to provide procurations to extraordinary preachers sent by the bishops, unless perhaps the bishops themselves were to make them their vicars not only for preaching, but also for visiting, and for exacting and receiving the procurations owed to themselves in their stead.

§. But if it be said that bishops can compel parish priests to procure for all preachers sent by them, this appears to be contrary to law, since thereby the priests would be burdened and scandalized. For it is established that bishops, even when they visit their dioceses, cannot burden or scandalize the priests subject to them in exacting their own procurations.
