But it can more truly be said, as they assert, that a favor is done to the hearers themselves, and that more is bestowed or paid out to them, according to that of Luke 10: *Whatsoever thou shalt spend over and above, when I return, I will repay thee*; and so \[the Preacher\] lives in the Gospel, after the manner of the Apostle, who received nothing from his hearers, more mercifully and more laboriously.

§. To which it must be replied that he who says this, under the appearance of humility and piety, deceives wonderfully and speaks falsely. First, because since the Preacher who has been sent is a Shepherd, to take the necessaries from those to whom he preaches is not begging \[*mendicitas*\] but a power \[*potestas*\], as Augustinus says in the book *De Opere Monachorum*, chapter 14, at the end. And the same is said by the Gloss on that text of 2 Timothy 2, *The husbandman that laboureth*, etc., where it is said: *The Apostle reassures the chaste Evangelist, and wishes him to understand that for a Preacher to take expenses from those whom he feeds as a flock, or whom he tends as a cultivator tends a Vineyard, is not begging, but a power.* It is therefore clear that a Preacher canonically sent cannot take expenses from his hearers by way of begging, but rather by way of power, even though he asks for them for God’s sake; and thus he uses the Apostolic power. Secondly, he deceives and speaks falsely, because since the necessaries of life are owed to the Apostolic Preacher — that is, to one canonically sent — by the Lord’s Right, that is, by Divine Right, as Augustinus says in the same book, chapter 11, it is clear that he who asks for them for God’s sake, or for the Lord’s sake, asks for them by way of power \[*potestative*\], because he asks for them by that right by which he is able to ask, namely the Lord’s Right; for in no other way can the Preacher ask for them, except perhaps by canonical and human constitutions, in which provision is made only concerning the ordinary Preachers, that is, the Prelates and Legates of the Supreme Pontiff. The Apostolic Preacher, therefore, in asking his hearers for expenses for God’s sake, asks for them by way of power; therefore he does not ask for them by way of begging \[*mendicative*\], but uses the Apostolic power.
