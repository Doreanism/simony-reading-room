Gloss: *For the office of preaching is to be entrusted to those who are fit for that office.* See how the Apostle, by what he says — *who are fit* — teaches Timothy that those whom he is about to send to preach he should first take care to examine. Likewise in 1 Timothy 2 \[i.e. 3\] the Apostle commands that bishops, presbyters, and deacons, who are canonically chosen for the governance of the Church, be first tested before the ministry of God’s word and of other spiritual things is entrusted to them, saying: *And let these also first be proved, and so let them minister, having no crime.* Much more, then, must those be tested who, with no prior canonical election, offer themselves, or — over the objections of others — thrust themselves into preaching and into every governance of souls.

§. Blessed Paul too, chosen by the Lord himself after the Lord’s Ascension — according to what is said of Paul in Acts 9, *He is a vessel of election to me, etc.* — although in the Law he had been instructed at the feet of Gamaliel, as we read in Acts 22, nevertheless, before he was sent to preach, was taught by the Lord in his rapture, when *he heard secret words, which it is not granted to man to utter,* 2 Corinthians 12. And though so taught, he was not at once made a universal Apostle (*for he is shown to have come to the faith in the year of the Lord’s Passion and Resurrection*), but, after being tested and proved through many years, in the thirteenth year after his conversion he received the universal Apostolate of the Gentiles, as Beda’s Gloss on the opening of the Epistle *to the Romans* says. Likewise in Acts 13, on the words *Separate me Saul and Barnabas, etc.*, the Gloss says: *In the thirteenth year after the Lord’s Passion, according to the order of history, Saul appears to have received the Apostolate together with Barnabas, and the name Paul; and in the fourteenth year, according to the agreement of James, Cephas, and John, he set out for the teaching of the Gentiles; with which the Ecclesiastical History agrees.* Since, then, according to the Apostle, all to whom the office of preaching is committed must be tested by the one committing it before their commissioning; since also our Saviour, who cannot err, sent to the governance of souls only definite persons, chosen by himself, known and proved by his own experience; and did this solely for the instruction of his subsequent Ministers, as has been said above — *since his every action is our instruction*, as the Gloss says on Hebrews 5, *Who in the days of his flesh offered up prayers and supplications to him that was able to save him from death:* — it is not
