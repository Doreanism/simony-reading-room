§. Since therefore every Preacher sent by the Lord Pope has the power of living from the Gospel from his hearers — a power which cannot be taken from him, nor can the Preacher himself renounce it, as has been shown above — it follows that if the aforesaid indefinite and innumerable persons \[i.e. the friars\], by virtue of the said general license of the Lord Pope, were to have free power of preaching in every Church of Christians, by consequence they would have the power of taking the necessaries of life, or procuration, in every Church of Christians. But it is clear that, on account of their multitude and perhaps frequent arrival, the Churches would be enormously burdened by so many procurations on their behalf. Now it is not likely that the Supreme Pontiff, by the said general license, intends to burden the Church, contrary to that saying of the Apostle in 2 Thessalonians 3: *Working night and day, that we might not burden any of you*, etc. Especially since this would tend to the hindrance of the Gospel, contrary to that of 1 Corinthians 9: *But we have not used this power, lest we should give any offense to the Gospel of Christ.* *Gloss: Lest perhaps they should hate the Apostle, if he were to take from them.* If the Apostle feared the hatred of his hearers if he were to take from them, how much more ought those to fear who are not equal to the Apostle in power? It is therefore not likely that, by the said general license given to the aforesaid persons, the Lord Pope intends that those persons, as general Helpers \[*Opitulationes*\], should have the free faculty of exercising the governance of souls in every Church of Christians, without the Ecclesiastical Prelates being consulted, or even against their will.

§. But against this perhaps the Lover of the chief seats in the synagogues will object, wishing to show by the multitude of such Preachers that the Churches are not burdened, nor any offense given to the Gospel, saying as follows.
