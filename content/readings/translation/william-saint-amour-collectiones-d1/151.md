If therefore a bishop cannot do this in another’s diocese, much less can he who by the Church’s ordination presides over no jurisdiction or ecclesiastical authority.

§. Since therefore, as has been said, it is the power of bishops to examine preachers and to resist them if they are found to be false; and it pertains to their jurisdiction and honor that there be one bishop for one diocese and one rector for one church; and since no one not sent by the Lord Pope ought to exercise ecclesiastical offices in their dioceses without their being consulted or against their will; and since it is not the Lord Pope’s intention to diminish, alter, or impede the power, honor, or jurisdiction of these same bishops, as has been shown above; it is moreover plain that, if the aforesaid infinite and indeterminate persons could—without a special mandate or legation from the Lord Pope—publicly exercise ecclesiastical offices in any diocese and in any church, without the bishops being consulted or against their will, in such wise that the bishops could not by reason of their prelacy forbid them, or even examine them, nor resist them, even if they were found to be false; it remains that by this the prelacy—that is, the power, honor, and jurisdiction—of the bishops would be diminished, indeed broken and disturbed. It remains also as a consequence that from this the order of the universal Church would be thrown into confusion; for *the universal body could in no way subsist, unless the order of prelacy and subjection preserved it,* as is read in D. 89, can. *Ad hoc.* And once the order of prelacy is disturbed, the peace of the Church would also be disturbed; *since the order and grade of prelacy was devised for the concord of the universal Church,* as is read in D. 93, can. *Legimus*, at the verse *Quod autem posteà vnus.*
