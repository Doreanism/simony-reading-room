§. But if the Lord Pope should grant in a general way—to persons indefinite and uncertain, not chosen by himself or by the Church, and neither previously known nor proven to him (for example, to all the Monks of the Cluniac or Cistercian Order, or to whomsoever their Abbots shall have decided to designate)—a license to preach, to hear confessions, and to impose penances, in which the governance of souls chiefly consists, it is not plausible that by such a license granted in general he intends to make them universal Apostles, or even universal helpers of the universal Church—that is, possessing a general and unrestricted power of exercising the aforesaid offices in every Church of the Christians, without consulting the Ecclesiastical Prelates or even against their will. For the Lord Jesus Christ (of whom \[the Pope\] is the general Vicar, and on whose account he ought to imitate Him in his governance as His general Minister, according to John 10: *He that ministers to me, let him follow me*; Gloss: *that is, let him imitate me*), while He was in His mortal flesh, sent to the aforesaid governance of souls only certain persons chosen by Himself, tested by association with Him, and proven under His discipleship: namely, the twelve Apostles, Luke 9, and the seventy-two Disciples, Luke 10, saying to them in Matthew 10, BEHOLD, I—Gloss: *who chose, who taught*—SEND YOU—Gloss: *that is, from among the Disciples, I make you sent; therefore be gentle, humble, innocent*. As though He were saying: *For this reason I have chosen and taught and tested you under my discipleship, before sending you, that you may be such as you ought to be, namely gentle, etc.* And the Lord chose and tested His Disciples before sending them—not because He Himself stood in need of testing, who is ignorant of nothing, but to provide a pattern for those who send, that they should not send indiscriminately and without selection, but should pick out men who are chosen, learned, and long tested; especially since *the Governance of Souls is the Art of Arts*, *Extra*, *De ætate et qualitate Præficiendorum*, cap. *Cum sit Ars Artium*, and therefore it is to be entrusted not without examination, nor indiscriminately to anyone whatever, but only to suitable persons. Hence in 2 Timothy 2 the Apostle says: *The things you have heard from me through many witnesses, commend these to faithful men, who shall be fit*
