We answer: either by working bodily with their hands, or by entering a monastery where they may have the necessities of life. Concerning the first, it is said in Luke 12: *Sell what you possess, and give alms.* The Glossa: that having once for all spurned all your goods for the Lord, you may afterward by the labor of your hands work to gain something to live on. Concerning the second, it is said in Acts 4: *All things were common to them, and there was no one in need among them.* And concerning this matter an ecclesiastical constitution has been issued, 12. q. 1. cap. *Videntes*, where it is said: Bishops and the faithful stewards of the goods of the Church ought to administer all necessities to all who wish to live the common life, as best they can, so that no one among them may be found in want. And further on: If anyone shall arise in present or future times who attempts to tear these things away, let him be struck with the aforesaid condemnation.

This is also established by the example of the Lord, who had purses, John 12, where it is said of Judas, *that he was a thief, and having the purses* — the Glossa: keeper of the Lord’s purses; and Augustinus, 12. q. 1. cap. 17.: The Lord had purses, and preserving the offerings given by the faithful, he distributed them both to the necessities of his own and to others in need. Likewise, both the Lord and his Apostles led about Christian women with them, who both rendered services to them and ministered to their necessities, as the Glossa says on 1 Corinthians 9, on that passage: *Have we not power to lead about a sister, a woman?* But after the Lord — who ministered the necessities to the Apostles from his purses, as has been said — had bodily departed from the Apostles themselves through his death and resurrection, they did not turn to begging; but although the Apostles were preachers, and ought to have had their support from those to whom they preached, nevertheless they sought nothing from them, nor did they beg; but by their own lawful craft they sought a livelihood when they did not have means by which to live. Hence on that passage, John 21, *Simon Peter says to them, I go a-fishing*, the Glossa says: After receiving the breath of the Spirit, after his words, *AS THE FATHER HAS SENT ME, EVEN SO I SEND YOU*, suddenly the Apostles become what they had been — namely, fishermen, not of men, but of fish; but it must be known that they were not forbidden to seek their livelihood by their lawful craft, the integrity of the apostolate being preserved, when they did not have means by which to live; and so, as they labored, God added the necessities which he had promised.
