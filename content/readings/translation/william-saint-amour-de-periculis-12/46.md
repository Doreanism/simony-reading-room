# How One Must Proceed in Repelling the Said Peril. Chapter XII.

Concerning the twelfth point, namely, how the said perils are to be repelled, it must be known that six things will be necessary for repelling them.

The first is to contemplate who they are that penetrate houses, and whether they have already come into the Church of Christ. This is commanded in Isaiah 21 in these words: *Go, and set a watchman*, that is, *rouse watchmen to keep watch on the aforesaid matters*. And good watchmen willingly do this, in accordance with what Isaiah says in the same place: *Upon the watchtower of the Lord I stand continually.*

The second is that, when this has been discovered, it should be made known to those who do not know it. Hence in Isaiah 21, after it is said, *Set a watchman*, there is immediately added, *that whatever he sees, he may announce*; because the watchman cannot otherwise save his own soul, Ezekiel 33, throughout.

The third is, once they have been pointed out, to command that they be avoided, as the *Apostolus* says in 2 Timothy 3: *And avoid these also*; where he is speaking of such men.

The fourth is to forbid them the license of teaching or preaching; because, as it is said in 1 Corinthians 15, *Evil communications corrupt good manners*; and they will indeed have evil conversations, since, as it is said in 2 Timothy 3, *As Jannes and Mambres resisted Moses, so these also resist the truth.*

The fifth is to command those who are of their sect that they forsake it; for such men in the end forsake Christ, 2 Timothy 3: *Men corrupted in mind, reprobate concerning the faith.*

The sixth is to forbid those who are not of their sect that they should join it; because their sect *shall proceed no further*, by learning or by teaching, *but their folly shall be manifest to all*, 2 Timothy 3. And if these six things were done, the aforesaid perils, so far as concerns *Those Who Penetrate Houses*, would be sufficiently repelled.
