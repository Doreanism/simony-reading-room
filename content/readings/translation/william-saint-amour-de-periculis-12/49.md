And in 2 Thessalonians 3 he repeats the same point, saying, *For also when we were with you, this we declared to you: that if any man will not work, neither let him eat*. The Glossa of Augustinus: Some say that the Apostolus enjoined this concerning spiritual works; otherwise, were he speaking of bodily work, in which either farmers or craftsmen labor, he would seem to be contradicting the Lord, who says in the Gospel, *BE NOT SOLICITOUS, SAYING, WHAT SHALL WE EAT?* But they strive in vain both to draw a fog over themselves and over others, so that what charity usefully counsels they are unwilling not only to do but even to understand — since the Apostolus most plainly teaches in many other passages of the Epistles what his meaning is here: namely, that he wishes the servants of God to work bodily for their livelihood, lest they be compelled by want to beg for necessities.

If it be asked here what danger lies in asking for necessities, or in begging? We answer that those who wish to live by begging become flatterers, slanderers, and liars, and thieves, and turn aside from justice. Whence on the passage in 2 Thessalonians 3, *That we might give ourselves a pattern unto you, to imitate us*, the Glossa of Augustinus says: The Apostolus gave himself as a pattern to those who were slender in substance among the populace, that they might learn not to lose their liberty. And further on: He who frequently comes to another’s table, given over to idleness, must of necessity flatter the one who feeds him; whereas our religion calls men to liberty. Behold the danger of flattery. Likewise on the passage in 1 Thessalonians 5, *We beseech you, brethren, to know them who labor in the Gospel*, the Glossa says: Just as riches give birth to neglect of salvation, so want, while it seeks to be filled, turns aside from justice. And Proverbs 30, *Give me neither beggary nor riches*. And further on, *Lest perhaps being compelled by want, I steal and forswear the name of the Lord*. The Glossa: lest by the abundance or the want of passing things I fall into forgetfulness of things eternal.

But someone will say: Is it not a work of perfection to leave all things for Christ, and afterwards to beg for Christ?

We answer: To leave all things for Christ, and to follow Christ by imitating him in good works, is a work of perfection. Luke 18, *Sell all whatever thou hast, and give to the poor, and follow me* — namely, by doing good works; but not by begging, for this is forbidden by the Apostolus, as has been said above.

HOW then, you will ask, is the perfect man to live, after he has left all things?
