Likewise, when the Lord said in Luke 19, *Zacchaeus, make haste and come down, for today I must abide in your house*, this was not begging but urbanity. For the Lord knew that Zacchaeus, even though he was not yet inviting him, nonetheless bore in his mind the will to invite him. Hence the Glossa on the same passage: *Uninvited, he invites himself, because although he had not yet heard the voice of one inviting, he had heard the disposition.*

NOW THAT it is not permitted for those who are physically able-bodied to beg is expressly provided in human law, *C. De Mendicantibus validis, L. Unica*; and at 31. q. 1. cap. *Quomodo virginibus*, where Hieronymus, expounding the Apostolus’s saying in 1 Timothy 5, *Let a widow be chosen not less than sixty years old*, says, This entire precept concerns those widows who are supported by the alms of the Church. And below, *For this reason the age is prescribed, so that only those women may receive the food of the poor who are no longer able to work.* And at 82. cap. 1 it is said, *Let the bishop bestow food and clothing, insofar as it is possible for him, upon the poor and the sick, who because of weakness cannot work with their own hands.* Therefore, the alms of the begging poor ought not to be given to those who are able-bodied and capable of working; especially when, despising the precept of the Apostolus in 1 Thessalonians 4, *Work with your own hands, as we have charged you*, they unjustly ask. And so we ought not to give them what they ask, but rather correction. Matthew 5, *Give to him who asks of you*; Glossa, *and if not what he asks, then something better, when you have corrected him for asking unjustly*; for IT IS MORE PROFITABLE that bread be taken away from one who is hungry, *if, being secure in his food, he neglects justice, than that bread be broken for the same person, so that, seduced, he might acquiesce in injustice*; 5. q. 5. cap. *Non omnis*. Likewise, just as a cleric who is able to be sustained from the wealth of his parents, if he receives the alms of the poor, commits sacrilege, and by such abuse eats and drinks judgment unto himself, as is read at 16. q. 1. cap. *Quoniam quicquid*; so it seems that one able-bodied, who is able to live by his own labor or otherwise without sin, if he receives the alms of the begging poor, commits sacrilege.

But someone will say: There are certain Regulars who, although they are able-bodied, are nonetheless permitted by the Church to beg for a long time, or at least \[the Church\] connives at it; are such persons to be permitted to beg perpetually?
