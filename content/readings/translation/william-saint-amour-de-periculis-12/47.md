in the last chapter of Romans: *Now I beseech you, brethren, to mark them*; Glossa, that is, *to discern them*, WHO CAUSE DISSENSIONS AND OFFENCES, *contrary to the doctrine which you have learned*; Glossa, from the true Apostles; and in this he touches upon the Pseudo-Apostles, whom he warns must be guarded against throughout the whole Church, and to turn away from them; FOR SUCH *serve not Christ our Lord, but their own belly*; Glossa, they flatter some and disparage others, that they may be able to fill their belly; AND BY PLEASING SPEECHES *they seduce the hearts of the innocent*; Glossa, by composed words they commend their own tradition, by which they deceive the hearts of the simple.

Concerning the Idle, the Wanderers, and the Busybodies who must be avoided — since they too frequently become Pseudo-Apostles, and therefore are perilous to the whole Church, and live contrary to the doctrine of the Apostolus — it must be noted what the Apostolus says in 2 Thessalonians 3: *We charge you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*; Glossa, by Christ’s authority we command you; THAT YOU WITHDRAW YOURSELVES *from every brother walking disorderly*; Augustinus, in the book *De Opere Monachorum*, where he cites this authority of the Apostolus, puts “restlessly” \[inquietè\] in place of what is here said, “disorderly” \[inordinatè\]. And further on, *And not according to the tradition which they received from us; for you yourselves know that we were not disorderly among you*; Glossa, like those who attend to other men’s affairs, wandering hither and thither; NEITHER DID WE EAT *bread of any man for nothing, but in labor and in toil, working night and day*. Thus then it is clear in what manner the said perilous men are to be avoided, and in what manner the aforesaid perils, which threaten through them, are to be repelled.

But if the Prelates of the Church wish to remove the preaching of the aforesaid men by a shorter route — and consequently the perils that follow from their preaching — from the Church, let them see to it that these men can neither demand nor receive stipends; for without doubt, if they did not receive them, they would not preach for long, as the Glossa says on that passage of 2 Corinthians 11: *That I may cut off the occasion from them that desire occasion*; Glossa, the Apostolus was removing from the Pseudo-Apostles the occasions of taking stipends; for he knew that, if they did not receive them, they would not preach for long. For there are two things that supply fuel to their preaching, namely, that they wish, with idle hands, to live by the Gospel; and that they very often wish to live by busybody work, that is, by managing other men’s affairs — both of which are unlawful for them.
