Rather, they were content with what was offered, and sought the remaining necessities by the labor of their hands; hence Luke 10, on that passage, *Eating and drinking such things as they have*, the Glossa says: He grants support from preaching, but in such a way that they should be content with the food and drink offered. Indeed even when asked, the Apostles were unwilling to receive, fearing lest they should burden those who were offering; hence 2 Corinthians 8, the Apostolus says: *With much entreaty begging of us the favor and the fellowship of the ministry that is done toward the saints.* That is, as the Glossa puts it: When, fearing for them, I would not consent that we should permit them to have the favor and the fellowship of the ministry that is done toward the saints, they entreated us, that it might be lawful for them to give their goods, not as their own, but as common. And yet the bread of the Apostles was not free; hence on that passage, 1 Corinthians 9, *Who feeds the flock, and does not eat of the milk of the flock?* the Glossa says: The milk of the flock is called whatever is given by the people to those set over them; and by these words the Apostolus sufficiently indicates that his fellow Apostles do not usurp beyond what is owed, if, as the Lord ordained, living from the Gospel they ate the bread received from those to whom they were preaching the free grace. Moreover, in what they received they did not seek their own advantages, but the merits and fruit of the givers, Philippians 4: *I do not seek the gift, but I seek the fruit.*

But that the Lord ever begged, or his Apostles, is nowhere found; for that he once asked of the Samaritan woman, John 4, *Give me to drink*, was not begging, since the use of water is common to all; nor did he wish to drink bodily water, since he had not yet eaten but had sent his disciples into the city to buy food; rather, it is said in the same passage that he was asking of her spiritual water, that is, faith, and was admonishing her to ask for the Holy Spirit, as the Glossa says in the same place.

Likewise, that Christ could not have begged from those to whom he was preaching is plain in this way: for it is established that he was a shepherd, John 10: *I am the good shepherd.* Likewise, he was sent to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. But for a shepherd or a preacher to take support from those whom he feeds as a flock is not begging, but a power, as the Glossa says on that passage, 2 Timothy 2: *The husbandman who labors must first partake of the fruits.* Therefore, since Christ was a shepherd, and was sent to preach, it follows that he could not have begged from those to whom he was preaching.
