The eighteenth sign is that true apostles do not stir up against those who refuse to receive them the princes whose favor they have obtained; according to what is read in the life of the Apostles Simon and Jude, where it is said: *The duke, being angered, ordered a great fire to be kindled, that the pontiffs might be cast headlong into it, and all those who seemed to obscure the apostles of Christ; but the apostles prostrated themselves before the duke, saying,* WE BESEECH YOU, LORD, THAT WE MAY NOT BE THE CAUSE OF THEIR DESTRUCTION; NOR HAVE WE BEEN SENT HITHER FOR THE SALVATION OF MEN, TO BRING THE DEAD TO LIFE, THAT WE MAY BE SEEN TO KILL THE LIVING. Therefore those preachers who stir up princes more favorable to them against those who do not receive them, or whom they hate, are not true apostles of Christ, but pseudo-apostles.

The nineteenth sign is that the apostles of Christ have knowledge not only of those things which God has done, but also of those things which he is to do at the end of the world. Whence Apocalypse 4: *The living creatures were full of eyes before and behind*. Gloss: *That is, the faithful having knowledge of those things which God has done, or shall do at the end of the world.* Therefore those who say that they are ignorant of the perils of the final Church which have been foretold, or take no care for them as though they were ignorant of them, do not have eyes before and behind; whence they are not true apostles; therefore, when they say that they are apostles, they are pseudo-apostles.

The twentieth sign is that true apostles do not take the temporal goods of those to whom they preach; by which they are distinguished from wolves, that is, from pseudo-apostles. Whence Acts 20: *I have coveted no man’s silver or gold*. Gloss: *In this are wolves recognized, that they covet these things.* And below: *For such things as I and they that are with me needed, these hands have ministered.* Gloss: *An example of working even for bishops, and a sign by which they are distinguished from wolves.* For such men, who ask from those to whom they preach — or another in their name — seem to commit simony, after the manner of Gehazi, of whom it is read in 3 Kings 16 that he asked clothing from Naaman the Syrian, to whom his lord, namely Elisha, had given the grace of healing; and to that extent Gehazi is said to have sold the grace of healing to Naaman the Syrian, although he had asked the clothing without his lord’s knowledge, as Gregorius Nazianzenus says at C.1 q.1 c. *Qui studet*.

But someone will say: Can a preacher not ask sustenance from those to whom he preaches, at least by begging?
