Gloss: *Lest I should preach to those already converted by others*. Likewise 2 Corinthians 10: *We are not glorying beyond measure in other men’s labors*. Gloss: *where another had laid the foundation of the faith; for to do this would be to glory beyond measure*. Likewise in the same place: *Not having hope to glory in another man’s rule*; that is, in those things which have been prepared, Gloss: *by other preachers; because the Apostle preached to those to whom the Gospel had not been announced, in order to seek glory by his own labor*. Those preachers, therefore, who do not go to those who need to be converted, but rather to those who are already converted, who have their own apostles and their own bishops and priests, and thus glory in another’s people, are not true Apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The thirty-seventh is that True Apostles, when they are sent, go on their own mission, not on another’s. So Paul, sent to the Gentiles, went to preach to them, Acts 13: *Separate me Paul and Barnabas for the work to which I have taken them*. Gloss: *According to the agreement of James, Cephas, and John, he set out for the office of teaching the Gentiles*. But those preachers who *stand upon their feet* (in which posture the body touches the ground least), that is, those preachers who have least of earthly goods, on account of which they are more ready to be sent wherever it pleases the Lord — those, I say, the Lord sent to preach, not to the Orthodox, but to the infidels. Whence Ezekiel 2: After the Spirit of the Lord *set Ezekiel upon his feet* (in which posture the body touches the ground least), he said to him: *Son of man, I am sending thee to the apostate Gentiles, who have departed from me*; that is, to Jews, heretics, and those Gentiles who were once Christians, such as the Egyptians, the Babylonians, and all who hold the law of Mohammed. Such men, therefore, if they go to the Orthodox, who have their own apostles, namely their own bishops and presbyters, do not go on their own mission, but only on another’s; whence they are not true Apostles, but pseudo-apostles. And it is greatly to be feared that the Church may be endangered through such men, unless they are expelled from it. Just as happened with Jonah, who, when he had been sent by the Lord to Nineveh (which is interpreted as \[the\] Spacious \[city\]), did not go there, but wished to flee from the face of the Lord to Tarshish, which is interpreted as the exploration of joy; and therefore the ship in which he had embarked to cross over to Tarshish was endangered, until Jonah himself was cast out from it, Jonah 1 and 2.
