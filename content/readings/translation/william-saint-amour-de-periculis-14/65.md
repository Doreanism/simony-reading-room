We answer: if he preaches by authority, so that as a true pastor he may feed his flock with the food of the word, he may receive support; but then it is not mendicancy, but authority. 2 Timothy 2: *The husbandman that laboureth must be first partaker of the fruits*; Gloss: *He makes the chaste Evangelist secure, and wills that he understand that to take necessary things from those whom he feeds as a flock is not mendicancy, but authority.* But that a preacher may beg is nowhere found in sacred Scripture; rather, mendicancy is forbidden to every Christian by the Apostle, abhorred by Solomon, and reproved by Augustinus and other holy expositors, as was shown above in chapter 12. Thus it is clear that true apostles do not covet the temporal goods of those to whom they preach, nor do they beg. Therefore those who seek from those to whom they preach—or someone else on their behalf—do not appear to be true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The twenty-first sign is that true apostles are patient in tribulations and do not return evil for evil. Hence Matthew 10: *Behold, I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves.* Gloss: *He who undertakes the office of preaching ought not to inflict evils, but to endure them.* And this by the example of the Lord, of whom 1 Peter 2 says: *Who, when he was reviled, did not revile; but delivered himself to him that judged him unjustly.* Therefore those who do not endure evil but rather inflict it are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The twenty-second sign is that true apostles are ill received at their first coming, as the Lord says in Matthew 23 \[recte Matthew 10\]: *You shall be hated by all men for my name’s sake*; BUT afterwards they conquer, according to that of 1 John 5: *Whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world.* Therefore those who, on the contrary, are at first welcomed with joy and well received, but afterwards rejected, do not appear to be true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The twenty-third sign is that true apostles do not go to preach to those who already have other apostles, because they are unwilling to glory in another’s people. Romans, last chapter: *So have I preached the gospel, not where Christ has been named, lest I should build upon another man’s foundation.* And 2 Corinthians 10: *Not glorying beyond measure in other men’s labours.* Gloss: *Where another had laid the foundation of the faith.* Likewise in the same passage: *Not having hope to glory in another man’s rule*—that is, in things committed to another’s governance.
