The seventh sign is that the pseudo-apostles, since they have no authority from God, claim authority in their own name. Hence on 2 Corinthians 10, on the words *We dare not match or compare ourselves with certain ones*, the Gloss says: “The pseudo-apostles, having not received power from God but usurped it, wish to rule, claiming authority in their own name.” And therefore, although they may perhaps say they were sent by God through inspiration — as do all heretics — nevertheless, unless they prove their mission either by spiritual prophecy (as John the Baptist did when he said, *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, as the prophet Isaiah said*, John 1) or by miracle (as Moses turned a rod into a serpent and the reverse, Exodus 7), they ought to be excommunicated until they cease from preaching, *Extra, De Haereticis*, c. *Cum ex iniuncto*. And even if they should perform miracles, these would still not be sufficient testimony of a mission, since miracles are frequently performed by evil men, 1 q. 1 c. *Teneamus*. And most especially in the final Church, where miracles ought to be suspect, since the Lord says in Matthew 24: *There shall arise pseudo-prophets, and they shall give great signs and wonders.* Therefore those who claim authority in their own name, when they do not have it from the Lord, are not true apostles but pseudo-apostles.

The eighth sign is that the pseudo-apostles, making a show of wisdom in superstition and humility, call their own traditions “religion,” which are rather sacrilege; for they usurp the rights of others to themselves, glorying, namely, in congregations belonging to others. Hence in Colossians 2, the Apostle, speaking of the pseudo-apostles, says: *According to the commandments and doctrines of men, which have indeed a show of wisdom in superstition and humility.* Gloss: “That is, in feigned religion; because they apply the name of religion to a human tradition, so that what is sacrilege should be called religion; for what is against the Author has been invented by a sacrilegious mind.” Now it is certain that it is against the Author — that is, against God — to wish to rule a people without the mandate or authority of him who is its parish priest.
