Gloss: *In order that they may appear more religious, they pray at greater length, so that they may receive praises and money from the simple, as though they were their patrons; whose prayer becomes sin, because they can profit neither others nor themselves unto salvation; indeed, on account of those very prayers, since by them they have deceived others, they shall be more greatly damned.* Now by this — that they accept gold and silver — it appears that they preach not for the sake of the salvation of others, but for the sake of gain. Matthew 10: *Do not possess gold or silver.* Gloss: *Because those who possessed these things would seem to preach not for the sake of salvation, but for gain.* Likewise Hieronymus says on Micah chapter 3 that, *because certain prophets accepted money, their prophecy was turned into divination*; that is, their prophecy was declared not to be prophecy but divination — that is, not from God but from the Devil. And this is read at *C.1 q.1 c. Numquam*. Therefore those preachers who circumvent men so that they may give them temporal goods, whether in life or in death, are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The sixteenth sign is that pseudo-apostles, when some truth unfamiliar and harsh to them is preached, are provoked and bark out against it. Whence the Apostle says of them in Philippians 3: *Beware of dogs*. Gloss: *That is, recognize that these are dogs, not by reason but by habit, barking against unfamiliar truth.* And below: *He rightly compares them to dogs; for just as dogs follow habit rather than reason, so the pseudo-apostles hold to the law of custom, and irrationally bark and bite against the truth.* Likewise 2 Timothy 4: *According to their own desires they shall heap up to themselves teachers*. Gloss: *Who shall teach them those things which they gladly hear, because the truth shall seem harsh to them.* Therefore those preachers who bark against truth unfamiliar and harsh to them, and procure that such truth be silenced, are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The seventeenth sign is that true apostles inflict no trouble on those unwilling to receive them, but rather dismiss them, lest they appear to be seeking anything earthly. Matthew 10: *Whosoever shall not receive you, going forth out of the city, shake off the dust from your feet*. Gloss: *That you may show that the appetite for earthly things is not in your affections.* Therefore those preachers who, in order to be received by men, strive by means of lawsuits are not true apostles; especially since the Apostle says in 1 Corinthians 11: *If any man seems to be contentious among you, we have no such custom, nor the Church of God.*
