The ninth sign is that, since the pseudo-apostles by the power they possess are not evangelists or dispensers of the sacraments, they nevertheless wish to live off the Gospel and not by the labor of their hands; against that word of the Apostle in 2 Thessalonians 3: *Nor did we eat any man’s bread for nothing, but in labor and toil, working night and day.* Gloss: “These pseudo-apostles ought much more to live by their own labor, since they do not have this power that we have, namely, of living off the Gospel.” And Augustinus says this more expressly in his book *De opere monachorum*, in these words: “These brethren of ours rashly claim for themselves (so I think) that they have this kind of power, namely, of living off the Gospel; for if they are evangelists, if they are ministers of the altar, if they are dispensers of the sacraments, I admit they have it.” As if he said: If they are not such, they do not have the power of living off the Gospel. Therefore those preachers who, although they do not have the power of living off the Gospel, nor of dispensing the ecclesiastical sacraments (inasmuch as they have no congregation and are not set over the governance of souls), nevertheless wish to live off the Gospel, are not true apostles but pseudo-apostles.

The tenth sign is that the pseudo-apostles rejoice more to be commended themselves than God’s doctrine; but the true apostles, on the contrary. 1 Thessalonians 2: *Nor seeking glory from men.* Gloss: “He touches upon the pseudo-apostles, who wished themselves rather than God’s doctrine to be commended; but the Apostle, who sought glory not in the present but in the future, made himself humble, that God’s preaching might be exalted.” Therefore those preachers who wish themselves rather than God’s doctrine to be commended are not true apostles but pseudo-apostles.
