The twenty-fourth sign is that true apostles, even when they make great progress in the Church, are not therefore puffed up. Ezekiel 3: *Son of man, I have made thy face as an adamant.* The adamant, when it attracts iron, is not therefore puffed up, nor changed; likewise the true apostle, when by his preaching he attracts the hardest sinners—even as iron—is not therefore puffed up. Likewise Luke 17: *When you have done all the things that are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants.* And Psalm 113: *Not unto us, O Lord, not unto us, but unto thy name give the glory.* Therefore those who arrogantly boast of the fruit that they say they produce in the Church of God, saying, *We have illumined the universal Church, which before our time was blind, and we have extinguished the flame of sins in the Church of God*—when perhaps they have rather advanced hypocrisy—are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles, of whom it is said in the Canonical Epistle of Jude: *Their mouth speaks proud things.*

The twenty-fifth sign is that true apostles do not seek the favor of the world, nor to please men. Galatians 1: *If I yet pleased men*, Gloss: *that is, wished to please*, *I should not be the servant of Christ.* Hence 1 Thessalonians 2: *Not as pleasing men*, Gloss: *that is, seeking to please, for we do not even have the appearance of one pleasing.* Therefore those preachers who seek the favor of the world and labor for this end are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.

The twenty-sixth sign is that true apostles are content with the food and drink offered to them, and do not seek more sumptuous fare; according to that of Luke 10: *Eating and drinking such things as they have*, Gloss: *He concedes support from the Gospel, yet in such a way that they be content with the food and drink offered.* Therefore those preachers who, although they do not have authority \[to demand support\], nevertheless take offense when more sumptuous fare is not provided for them, are not true apostles, but pseudo-apostles.
