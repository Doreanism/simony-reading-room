\[The pseudo-apostles\] preach the Gospel out of opportunism, seeking their own advantage—whether of money, of honors, or of human praise—willing to accept gifts from anyone, seeking not so much the salvation of the one to whom they preach as their own profit. And below: *Whoever seeks anything from God other than God does not chastely seek God.* Hence 2 Corinthians 12: *I seek not what is yours.* Gloss: *Not your riches in gold or silver;* BUT YOU. Gloss: *Which would not be the case unless I abstained from \[receiving\] expenses.* And below: *So that, understanding that he preferred them to money, they might at last recognize his affection toward them.* Those preachers, therefore, who preach for the sake of temporal gain, or for worldly honor, or for human praise, are not true apostles but pseudo-apostles.

But how shall it be known when they preach for their own glory? We answer: when they preach without being called. 2 Corinthians 11: *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord.* Gloss: *Which he cannot do who does not have authority from the Lord.* And he does not have authority from the Lord who has not been called by God—that is, by the Church. Hebrews 5: *Neither does anyone take the honor to himself, but he who is called by God, as Aaron was.* Gloss: *He is called by God who is rightly chosen by the Church.*

The twelfth sign is that the pseudo-apostles pretend to have a greater zeal for souls than the rectors and pastors of those souls, although they have no care of them; contrary to what the Gloss says on 1 Thessalonians 2: *We became like little ones among you, as if a nurse should cherish her children.* Gloss: *A woman nurses other people’s children for pay, not out of love; but her own children out of affection.* Those preachers, therefore, who pretend to have a greater zeal for souls than their rectors do, are not true apostles but pseudo-apostles.

The thirteenth sign is that true apostles do not strive after eloquence or the composition of words; the pseudo-apostles, on the contrary, do. 2 Corinthians 11: *For though I be unskilled in speech.* Gloss: *The apostles were not eloquent, but the pseudo-apostles composed their words \[carefully\].* Likewise on the same passage, another Gloss: *The Corinthians preferred the pseudo-apostles to the true apostles on account of polished speech.* Likewise 1 Corinthians 1: *Not in the wisdom of speech, lest the cross of Christ be made void.* Gloss: *Not in charm and ornament of words, because the Christian religion has no need of pomp or cultivated speech.* Likewise 2 Corinthians 1\[?\]: *What we speak, not in the learned words of human wisdom.* Gloss: *as the pseudo-*
