## Question 11

What about gifts of hospitality? The answer, according to Hostiensis, “it is asked about gifts of hospitality”: they do not induce simony, provided they are not extended to too great a quantity. Understand this as: provided they are given without an agreement and without any corrupt intention; for otherwise simony is committed even in a small thing, as in *1 q. 1 Iudices vel duces*.

## Question 12

When does a gift from service induce simony and when not? The answer, according to Ulricus here, “a gift from service”: it must be said that a gift from service sometimes consists in services rendered improperly, and this is of two kinds: either the service is in itself improper — that is, unlawful and dishonorable — or, by reason of the intention of the one bestowing it, the service is improper, since any service is improperly bestowed which is bestowed for some spiritual thing. So that if “for” expresses the cause principally motivating toward this — hence in either case it is simony. But if the service is honorable, this is either bodily or spiritual. If it is bodily — as if someone goes to Rome for the affairs of some church, or serves the church or the bishop or some other prelate by another honorable service — for such a service a spiritual thing may be given, provided it is done without an agreement, and also provided the word “for” does not express the principal motive but rather what some call the “stimulative” cause, since it stirs the affection of the one giving some spiritual thing toward love of the one rendering the service, so that thereafter, out of the generosity of love, he confers that spiritual thing — yet looking principally to the gift and the merits of the person, which he has acquired through such services and other good works. But if the service is spiritual, then he ought not to bestow it by agreement or condition for some spiritual thing — as if, when a benefice falls vacant, some spiritual service has been imposed by statute upon that benefice itself, the one who receives the benefice is bound to bestow that service. As in *Extra*, on prebends, the chapter *Significatum*, Alexander III confirms a statute concerning a Mass of the Blessed Virgin to be celebrated by the one to whom a certain prebend had been conferred. Goffredus agrees with these things in the same title, under “in a gift from service.”
