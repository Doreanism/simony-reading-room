## Question 7

In how many ways simony is committed. According to Raymundus, under the same title, § 3, “It is committed”: it is committed in three ways, as the gloss says on that passage of Psalm 33: “Blessed is he who shakes his hands free from every bribe.” It does not say only “from a bribe,” but “from every bribe.” For a bribe of the hand is one thing, a bribe of service another, a bribe of the tongue another. A bribe of the hand is money. A bribe of service is servitude unduly bestowed. A bribe of the tongue is favor — 1 q. 1, *Sunt tamen nonnulli*. Goffredus says the same under the same title, § “It is committed.” Raymundus adds: Note also that an ordination is called simoniacal: one on the part of the ordainer only, another on the part of the one to be ordained only, another on the part of both, another on the part of neither — which is when, with both being ignorant, a friend of the one to be ordained gives or promises something to a counselor of the bishop — 1 q. 1, *Statuimus* and the chapter *Si qui a symoniacis*.

## Question 8

What of a simoniacal ordination, especially on the part of an intermediary, and what in similar cases concerning the conferral of a benefice. The response, according to Hostiensis, § 3, “In what manner it is committed,” at the verse “But also if anyone,” and the following, who says thus: If anyone should offer or promise prayers or money on my behalf when I have expressly forbidden it, it does not stand against me, as in *extra*, under the same title, *Sicut nobis tuis litteris*; § penultimate, *de homicidio*, the chapter *Petrus*. It is otherwise if it is done with my knowledge and consent, for then I am said to interpose my own will, and therefore, however hidden it may be, I ought to resign if I wish to save my soul. But if this is done while I am wholly ignorant, and by an enemy wishing my promotion, by which he hopes to hinder me, simony is not committed as regards me — lest by his deceit he obtain what he intends — *extra*, under the same title, *Sicut non tuis litteris*, and the chapter *Nobis*. The response of 1 q. \[1\], *Si amicus meus*: if money was given, I am promoted simoniacally, but I am not a simoniac; I am nevertheless bound to resign, as in *extra*, under the same title, *de symoniace* \[?\], the first chapter, *de regularibus*, and the chapter *Nobis*. Innocentius agrees with these things in the gloss on the chapter *Sicut tuis litteris*. And as for what is said there, that to one who is ignorant and forbidding nothing ought to be imputed as a penalty — this is to be understood, according to Innocentius, if the money was given after the election for the confirmation; it is otherwise if it were given for the election, even though he forbade it, unless this were done by an enemy. Vincentius says the same in his *Apparatus*; so does Bernardus in the same place, and Hostiensis in the gloss, and Raymundus below, § 13, “What shall be done,” at the end. The gloss of Guillelmus adds there thus: I believe, however, that if my enemy were to give money to the bishop to my prejudice, and I knew this before the bishop summoned me to it, and it were established for me that he would not summon me except on account of that bribe, I ought not to accept it.
