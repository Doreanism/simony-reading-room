## Question Two

Why simony is also called a heresy, as is clear in *1 q. ultima, c. Patet*, and in several other places. Answer, according to Thomas in the *Summa*, article 1 and article 10: Simony is called a heresy because, just as an outward profession of faith is a kind of religious act even if it is not in the heart, so conversely to sell spiritual things or the gifts of God is a kind of profession of unbelief, since by the very act, as it were, a person professes himself to be the master of the spiritual gifts, even though in his heart he does not disbelieve. Petrus in his commentary agrees with this, as does Ulricus, § 2. And Ulricus adds in the same place that this vice is called simony also from its first author, namely Simon, and that it sprang from the evil root of unbelief, on account of which Peter reproached him with heresy, saying: For you thought that the gift of God might be possessed with money. For Simon thought this, as Thomas says, because he said that the world was not created by God but by a certain higher power, as Isidorus says in the book of the *Etymologies*; and accordingly the simoniacs are reckoned among the other heretics, as is clear in Augustinus’s book *De heresibus*.

## Question Three

Why the simoniacal heresy is said to hold first place. Answer, according to Ulricus, *§ Cum ergo* and the following section: because simony is the greatest sin. For as is said in *1 q. ultima, c. Patet*, all crimes in comparison with the simoniacal heresy will be reckoned as almost nothing. But this is to be understood not of just any simony, but when there is a perfect imitation of the sin of Simon, which was joined with unbelief and malice, as is clear from the words of Peter in Acts 8: “You have no part nor lot in this matter,” where Ambrosius in his book *De penitentia* gives the text thus: “You have no part nor lot in this faith.” And he adds: “You see that he condemns this man all the more because he did not have a pure conscience of faith” — so unbelief is clear. And concerning malice he says: “I see that you are in the gall of bitterness and in the bond of iniquity”; by “gall” the greatest malice is signified, and the “bond of iniquity” signifies obstinacy in malice. But if someone commits simony without the error of unbelief, out of desire for honor or riches and not out of malice, then it is not the greatest sin, but unbelief and the sin against the Holy Spirit, and in a certain case even sacrilege, are greater crimes.

## Question Four

Why those who practice simony are rather called “simoniacs” from Simon, who wished to buy the gift of God, than “Gehazites” from Gehazi, who sold the gift of God. Answer, according to Ulricus, *§ Dicuntur autem*: that the completion of the formal character of this sin lay in the act of Simon and in the deed of Gehazi.
