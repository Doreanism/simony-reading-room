Richardus responds that such persons, by making an unlawful pact, sinned; nevertheless, since they do not proceed to the carrying out of the pact, they do not incur the penalty of deposition or irregularity. Likewise, it is to be said that if the bishop, afterwards forgetting the pact, gives him the benefice solely from a motive of piety, unless after the fact, knowing it, he receives what had been promised him — for such a one has, by interpretation, withdrawn from the pact while the matter was whole, according to God’s judgment.

### What if a bishop promises someone who is in some shameful sin that he will give a benefice to his son, if from now on he will not commit that sin?

Richardus responds that such a pact is lawful; whence the cleric is not bound to resign the benefice so acquired. And the same holds if the bishop should say to someone, “I promise you that I will provide for you a suitable benefice if you will be good and honest” — for such a condition is to the reverence and honor of God.

In addition, note that the synod of Chalcedon lays down at 1 q. 1 *Si quis episcopus*, that procurators of church property, defenders, and those subject to the rule are judged to be simoniacs if they are appointed for money. And it understands there by the name “procurator” any administrators of church property, such as the *prepositus*, *iconomus*, *vicedominus*. By the name “defender” it understands the advocate or *gastaldus* and judge. By the name “those subject to the rule” it means the presbyter, archdeacon, canon, monk, and anyone bound to ecclesiastical office: 1 q. 3 *Saluator*.

### But how do these three differ — *prepositus*, *yconomus*, and *vicedominus*?

The gloss there responds that the name *prepositus* is general and contains the others under it: argument from *Eo. de verborum significatione, Quoniam*. Or, *prepositus* and *iconomus* signify the same thing, or at any rate they may well exist in the same church. The *vicedominus* is the one who has charge of the bishop’s property: dist. 89 *Volumus*; the *iconomus*, however, of the canons’ property — though one is often put for the other. The advocate is the one who has charge of handling the church’s lawsuits, who is also otherwise called *syndicus*. For a *syndicus* is the advocate of a corporation. The *gastaldus* is the one who has the care of the bishop’s external affairs, who is also otherwise called *maior domus* — namely, the bishop’s judge, as in the church of Bologna.
