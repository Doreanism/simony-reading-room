But what would happen, in the preceding case, if no stricter order can be found? Raymundus, with Johannes \[Teutonicus\] and Bernardus, on the said c. *Quoniam*, holds that after the resignation he must be transferred to another monastery or place of the same order; and if this cannot conveniently be done, let it at least be done for a time (argument from D. 81 *Valet*), and afterwards let him be elected anew, as above. The same is to be done, according to Bernardus, if he cannot be conveniently transferred even for a time, especially where penance is at issue. Note that Raymundus understands the above-mentioned rigor to be observed regarding hidden simoniacs even in the penitential forum, even though the crime is not established in the judicial forum. Robertus also believes this on the basis of the decretal *Quoniam simoniaca*, which does not speak of inquisition. Some, however, say that this rigor is not to be observed unless the crime is established in the judicial forum (argument from X eod. *Dilectus filius*, where it speaks of inquisition). And so, even with those received in such a way, some are dispensed with by the pope. What has been said about monks extends also to abbots. Yet monks are not compelled to renounce spiritual goods, nor abbots temporal goods, acquired on either side, where no pact has preceded and intention alone is at issue, as was said above. But why is a secular convicted by way of inquisition degraded, while a regular is not? Hostiensis answers that the reason is this: in an inquisition concerning a secular the strict subtlety of the law is required, and so in offenses of this kind one pronounces according to strict law; but in an inquisition concerning a regular one proceeds summarily \[*de plano*\], and therefore he is also punished summarily. What if the monastery is not wealthy, but is meager and oppressed by extreme poverty: may money in such a case be given and received without the vice of simony? Reply, according to Hugo \[of Pisa\], Hostiensis, Raymundus, and Bernardus on X eod. *Non satis* and c. *Quoniam simoniaca*: in such a case it can be conceded that those of the convent may say in these terms to one wishing to enter, “We willingly and freely receive you into our spiritual fellowship; but since our goods do not suffice for us and you, bring with you the means of living,” or “Provide for yourself the means of living” — provided they do not say this fraudulently, and provided it is said without a pact. For then, if the entrant brings with him certain temporal goods, no simony is committed.
