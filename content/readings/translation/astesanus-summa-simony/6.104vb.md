Raymundus adds — and Bernardus agrees with him on Extra eodem, “Hoc,” in the final gloss — that if he transfers from the monastery he had simoniacally entered to another monastery, the money ought to be returned to him simply, and the possessions, at least as to usufruct, ought to belong to the monastery to which he transfers, so that, just as it has the burden, so it may have the benefit. And so it is read in something similar at [16, q. 6, “De lapsis,” 7] and [27, q. 1, “Si quis rapuerit”]. And that usufruct will, immediately upon his death, be ended, and \[the property\] will return to the first monastery. ### But what if it is the abbot and the entire collegium that are at fault? Rofredus and Thomas reply that if money was given, it ought not to remain with the monastery, since it has made itself unworthy of it; but at the disposition of the superior it is to be given to the poor or to another monastery. If, however, possession was given, it ought likewise to be given by authority of the superior to another monastery, or its price distributed to the poor. If, however, the entire collegium was not at fault — if only the abbot, or some of the collegium, was at fault — provided that no \[loss\] redounds to the harm of the church or monastery, what was given can remain with that monastery, especially since it was received to its injury, yet so that, as far as can be done conveniently, those who were at fault have no advantage from it. ### What if some secular cleric gave money to be beneficed in a parish or to be ordained: to whom shall the money so received be restored? Rofredus replies that if he gave it to obtain a benefice, it ought to be applied to the use of the church or prebend that he so obtained, with himself removed from that benefice — unless perhaps he had given the money for obtaining it to some secular collegium, for then it ought to be done with that money as has been said above concerning money given to an abbot and his monks. If, however, he gave it for obtaining an order, and he had no ecclesiastical benefice, that money ought to be given to the cathedral church to whose ignominy it was received.
