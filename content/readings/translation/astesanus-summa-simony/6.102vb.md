Yet afterwards he is to be excommunicated, if his contumacy requires it. And note that this determination — \[the question\] from which, *if* this word “*constat*” determines \[the meaning\], then such a one will be suspended only from the moment it is established that he is a simoniac. And this Martinus Hispanus said. If, however, it determines this word “*perpetrasse*,” then according to this even a hidden simoniac is suspended. This is noted in the aforesaid gloss. In the gloss, however, of *c. qui studet* of the same \[place\], it is said that he who is simoniac in a received order ought not to enter the church before penance. But Iohannes Teutonicus says that any sinner — even an excommunicate — can enter the church to pray, provided he does not take part in the divine offices, since he does not find this prohibited. Simoniacs are also punished by the judge For according to Raymundus and Hoff., a simoniac in his order, in whatever way it has been established before the judge — whether by way of accusation, or of inquisition, or of denunciation — is deposed and is made infamous. On the first, *extra de accu. super his*; and on the second, in the same place, *inquisitionis*; and *i. q. i. siquis. neque*; and *ca. q. 7. requiritis* — according to Raymundus and Hoff. For simoniacs are infamous (*i. q. 3. c. ult.*). And this is such an infamy that such a one ought not to be admitted to any ecclesiastical benefice unless dispensation be granted with him by the prince; yet as regards God, performed penance removes it (argument from *2. q. 7. § hinc colligitur*, according to Bernardus, *extra e. ex diligenti*, in the penultimate gloss). Rod., however, says that he does not believe that deposition or infamy ought to follow when simony is proved, because then it is proved by way of denunciation, which has no effect against penance alone. A simoniac in benefice and dignity, however, according to Hoff., when convicted by accusation is deposed from the order and from the benefice; but by inquisition he is removed from administration (*extra de elec. per inquisitionem*). The same is done when by denunciation. And the reason is that penance could not be performed while retaining that \[order\] or those \[goods\] (*vi. 12. Herui*).
