And according to Innocentius on *c. per tuas. 2*: such a one is understood to be suspended from the acts of all orders — yet not from the order received simoniacally — and consequently he loses administration in temporal matters (*distinction 81, eos etiam qui*). And he says the same of a simoniac in a notorious benefice. If, however, someone is a simoniac because he conferred an order or benefice simoniacally, or because he received a benefice simoniacally, or because he was a mediator of simony: if it is hidden, he is suspended *ipso iure* as regards himself, just as any other criminal, but not as regards others (argument from *extra co. ut si questiones. veniens. ex tue*). If, however, it is public or notorious, he is *ipso iure* suspended as regards himself and as regards others (*extra co. accusatum. quotiens*, and *distinction 22, § verum*). This is according to Raymundus. Hoff., however, distinguishes concerning him who has received a benefice simoniacally: if it is hidden, then if it is a simple benefice he is suspended only as regards himself; but if it is a dignity which requires a particular order, he is suspended as regards himself and as regards others — whence whatever he does in performing the office of the dignity, he does as one suspended, and he deceives the souls of those subject to him. And he says the same of a benefice having cure of souls: for the same judgment is to be had concerning these two (as in *extra de elec. dudum, retho*, and *eta. c. i*, and *extra de si. p. c. si*). So Hoff. and his colleagues Hostiensis. Innocentius, however (*extra co. super c. per tuas. 2*), notes concerning a hidden simoniac in a notorious benefice that, although such a one is not suspended *ipso iure*, nevertheless by reason of the benefice simoniacally sought he will not minister without sin — neither temporally nor spiritually — for instance, by conferring orders if he bought an episcopate or episcopal dignity. From other benefices, however, that he did not seek simoniacally, he will not be suspended. But what is hidden simony Rod. answers that this is that \[simony\] which is not established by sentence, or by confession made in court, or by the evidence of the matter — \[evidence\] which can be concealed by no evasion. But is a publicly simoniac cleric *ipso facto* excommunicated Yes — as in *i. qui reperiunt*, where it is said that whoever shall be found to have offered any gift for a spiritual dignity, from that very time should know himself condemned by the reproach of anathema and alienated from participation in the body and blood of Christ, from the moment it is established that he has perpetrated this.
