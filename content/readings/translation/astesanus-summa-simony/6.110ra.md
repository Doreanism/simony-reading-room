### What of one who from his own goods establishes a prebend in some church, retaining for himself the power of conferring it upon another?

I respond: if he established it with the consent of the bishop, then in its constitution he can, with the consent of the bishop and of his brethren if any are there, retain for himself the right of patronage, either in perpetuity or for a time.

### What of monks who give something to their abbots in order to obtain priorates: is there simony there?

I respond, according to Goffredus and Hostiensis, if there is something spiritual there which is given as the principal object, then there is simony, if he provokes the appraiser, or if it is the principal object.

### What if a prior gives something to a bishop so that he will not allow him to be removed from the priorate: is this lawful, and is there simony there?

Hostiensis believes that he commits simony there, especially if there is necessity or utility that such a one be removed; but if without gifts, services, or carnal entreaties he procures through the bishop that he not be removed, when neither utility required a useful removal nor was the intention corrupt, he does not sin.

### What of one who gives money to someone so as to be received to serve in a hospital, leprosarium, or almshouse?

Hostiensis responds that if such a one obtains thereby some spiritual right, he commits simony. But if he is received only on condition that he serve for a time, or for his life, in the kitchen or in some other place, it is not simony — although it is base to take or give anything for such things.

### Whether someone may be induced by money to enter religion?

It seems that he may, from c. *Dilectus filius* \[*Extra, de simonia*\], and *De pactis*, c. ult., and 1 q. 2 *Quam pio*. For money may be given so that some may be converted to the faith: 23 q. 4 c. pen., 7 q. 7 *Quod autem*. Under such a condition we may also lead a woman in marriage: 28 q. 1 *Cave*. With a heavier penance also we may burden someone so that he be converted: 22 q. 6 *Iam non*. For such a condition is good, according to Iohannes Andreae, note on c. *Dilectus filius* \[*Extra, de simonia*\].

### Is it simony to renounce a benefice so that it may be given to another?

I respond that it is not, unless it is brought into a pact: *Extra, de prebendis*, *Dilectio*. To have hope of this, however, is not simony: *Extra, de pactis*, *Cum pridem*, and noted at 1 q. 1 *Ordinationes*.

### Suppose a bishop gave a certain church with all its rights to some religious house,

and behold, when those persons appointed by the abbot in that church died, the bishop refused to install clerics there at his own will unless they gave him money. The response is given at 1 q. 2 *Quesitum*, 1 *Ter*, and the gloss, that such a pact is simoniacal. Argument: 16 q. 2 c. 1.
