Suppose someone has no title and wishes to be ordained, but the bishop refuses to ordain him because he lacks a title. He asks some priest to present him to the title of his church, which the priest refuses to do. Then the candidate promises him that, if he presents him, he will never seek any portion from his church; and so, with that priest presenting him, he is ordained. Is simony committed there?

Raymundus replies that such a man, unless he has been mercifully dispensed with, ought neither to ascend to higher orders nor to minister in the order thus received, [Extra c. “Per tuas,” 2]. Nor is he excused by ignorance of the law — namely, if he did not believe this to be lawful — as is said in the same place. But who shall dispense with him? Raymundus, with Iohannes, believes that only the pope can: and he is a simoniac in the order. The ordainer, however, \[is suspended\] from the conferral of orders, and the presenter from the execution \[of orders\] for three years, since the ordained ought to know themselves suspended, [Extra c. “Si quis ordinaverit”]. According to Rodericus, however, the bishop can dispense with him so that he may minister in the orders not duly received. But may a simoniac relinquish a benefice without the license of his superior?

Bernardus replies, on Extra c. “De regularibus,” that concerning religious it must be held as is said in [Extra, eodem, “Qui est,” § “Episcopi”]: indeed they cannot renounce without the pope’s license. But inferiors may renounce into the hands of their prelates, by the argument of [Extra, de renunciationibus, “Nisi,” § 1] and c. “Admonet,” that if he is unwilling to receive \[the renunciation\], he must nevertheless yield, because the sin is not remitted unless what was taken is restored, [14, q. 6, “Si res”].

### Suppose the pope has been elected through simony, and the simony has been proven: is he to be deposed?

I reply that he is not. The argument of [2, q. 4, “Interrogatum”] stands against this. Nor [does Distinction 79, “Si quis pecunia”] apply, where it is said that the one who is simoniacally elected pope is not to be considered apostolic. That speaks of one who is elected not by the cardinals but by others who do not have the power to elect.
