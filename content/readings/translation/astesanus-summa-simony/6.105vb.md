With one simoniacally promoted to a benefice who is also a simoniac, according to Raymundus, Hoff\[mannus, i.e. Hostiensis\] and Bernardus, only the pope can dispense, and no other—neither in that benefice nor in another—that is, if he remains in the secular life. As argument, according to Raymundus, *X eodem [de regularibus]*. But according to Hoff\[mannus\] and Bernardus, in *c. [nobis]*. For the end of this chapter, according to Hoff\[mannus\], should not be referred to its beginning, that is, to the case set forth earlier in it (namely of the simoniacally promoted non-simoniac, concerning whom it is certain that the bishop can dispense, as was said above), but should be referred to the case of the simoniac which was set forth in the precise text of that decretal—an interpretation Bernardus also adopts in the same place. According to Hostiensis, however, with such a one the bishop can dispense in a double \[benefice\], but in a simple one perhaps he can when the necessity of the church is pressing; otherwise not. Innocent\[ius IV\] notes thus on this matter, *X eodem*, on the chapter *[de simoniace]*: namely that one ignorantly simoniacally promoted remains in the order so received through dispensation of the bishop. *C.1 q.5 [presentium]*. In other orders or benefices not simoniacally held he will remain even without dispensation, and acquires new benefices. New orders, however, he cannot have unless one first dispenses with him in the simoniacally held order, when he has been promoted by leap. *D.52 c.1*. Concerning the one knowingly simoniacally ordained, he says the same as the others. Concerning benefices, however, he distinguishes thus: namely that if someone ignorantly obtained a prelacy simoniacally, the bishop cannot dispense that he should have it, but in a simple benefice he can; but he cannot dispense for him to ascend to higher orders. *C.1 q.5 §.1* according to Innocent\[ius\]. And the same says Hostiensis in the same place in the gloss. Likewise the bishop can dispense, according to Raymundus, with one who by will alone, without intention, secretly committed simony, because he does not find that this is forbidden to him. Argument from *X de sententia excommunicationis [nuper]*; indeed he is even wholly freed by worthy penance. *X eodem [mandato]*. Likewise according to Raymundus the bishop can dispense with these who follow after legitimate penance: namely, with one who conferred orders or a benefice simoniacally, or was a mediator of simony, provided it be hidden. Likewise with one who interposed carnal entreaties for an unworthy person, with no condition or pact intervening.
