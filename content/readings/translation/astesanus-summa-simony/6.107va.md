I respond: the doctors say without distinction that he may, if he does not receive a salary from public \[funds\]; for he is not bound to serve at his own expense, by argument from *12. q. 2 quicunque. 7. c. ecclesiasticis*. Therefore it has been decreed that in all places where the resources of the church suffice — whether a cathedral church or another — a master in grammar should be appointed, to whom a suitable benefice should be assigned, or who should receive the fruits of one prebend, but not in such a way that he should thereby become a canon, *extra de magistris. quia nonnullis. §. i.* — which anciently was \[decreed\] concerning all churches. And by Alexander it was decreed only concerning cathedral churches, *extra de magistris. c. i*. It is to be understood, however, that a master, for his labor, may receive money in any science whatsoever. Whence Proverbs 23: “Buy the truth”; yet he ought to give knowledge freely, otherwise he would commit simony. For knowledge is a gift of God. Hence Exodus 31: “God placed it in his heart” — where it is said that knowledge is a gift of God. And in Matthew 10 it is said: “Freely you have received, freely give.” And Proverbs 23 says: “Sell not wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.” Thus Alexander of England distinguishes. What if a master, on a feast that is to be observed by law or custom, will not grant it to the scholars to be observed unless money is given to him; or conversely, what if he grants a non-observed day to be observed for money. Raymundus responds that he commits simony. And Rodericus adds that if a master should, for money, consent that the scholars cease from lectures on some day for the sake of recreation — as is customarily done by the *auctoristae* — this would not be simony, but base gain. What if someone is received as a master into a canonry on this pact, that he should hold the schools. I respond: some say this is simoniacal, because every pact ought to cease; yet, with the prebend vacant, that burden could be annexed to it, *extra de preben. significatum*. Bernardus also notes this, *extra eo. quia nonnullis*. But also according to Hostiensis it seems that he can be received on such a pact, as though an exchange were made of spiritual for spiritual, as at *extra de rerum permu. ad questiones*.
