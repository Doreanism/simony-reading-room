### Whether a prelate may sell the fruits of a vacant church.

I respond that he may not.

### But to the contrary: since, as has been said, the *intitulatus* who is lesser may do so, how much more the prelate.

I respond that this does not follow, for in this respect the prelate is the lesser. For the prelate has no *ius in re* — that is, no right in the fruits — as is plain at *si. q. 2. si quis episcopus*. And he must confer it freely of necessity, *extra de preben. relatum*. But where money is given, the gratuitousness is removed, as at *insti. de mandato §. fi.*; nor is an eternal reward to be considered, *extra eodem c. penult.* The *intitulatus*, however, has a *ius in re*. And therefore one who lives a long time can sell, and is in this respect compared to a usufructuary, *extra de pecu. cleri. c. fi.*

### What if a prelate confers some spiritual benefice on someone while retaining the revenues for himself.

I respond that if the prelate did not receive the revenues before the donation, but another did, and by the donation it follows that the prelate receives them, this is simoniacal. But if he received them before the donation, then either he himself stipulated beforehand that the revenues of that benefice should be retained for a time for a just and necessary cause, and this is licit; or he agreed with the beneficed person, or with any intermediary whatever, that he would retain the revenues, and this is illicit. *Extra ut ec. be. di. confe. ut nostri in fi.*

### Whether the rector of a church may commit the care \[of souls\] for a time to a hired priest or to one who has leased the fruits of the church.

I respond that he may, but freely and without any pact, and with the intervention of the bishop’s authority, *extra de offi. vica. ad hoc*. But care must be taken that these two are not mixed together, *extra de rege. primum. ad questiones*. For it is indeed forbidden. And so understand that text at *12. q. 2. precipimus*. It also seems that the cure may be committed for a short time by the bishop’s authority when the necessity of setting out is pressing, by argument from *extra de magistris. querelam* — perhaps for one day or a few days. Otherwise the contrary holds, at *10. q. 1. per totum*, for the rector of a church cannot go on pilgrimage without the bishop’s authority, *de conse. di. 5*. Nor may he commit his entire jurisdiction, *ff. de offi. presi. legatus* — although in certain matters he may, *ff. de iudi. pretor. §. i.* and *extra de pre. et re. omis.*
