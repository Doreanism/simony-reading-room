# On Simony. Title 55.

Having examined in turn the wrongs committed by prelates against their subjects and conversely, we must now consider in particular the wrong of simony. And first, what it is and whence it takes its name; second, whether it is a sin; third, when and in what ways it is committed; fourth, with what penalty simoniacs are struck; fifth, on the restitution of things received simoniacally; sixth, what dispensation is to be applied concerning them and through whom; seventh, in particular, on the simony committed by prelates who lease out their offices or churches for an annual rent; eighth, whether simony is committed if money is exacted for teaching and granting a license; ninth, on certain doubtful cases concerning simony; tenth, who are admitted to accuse a simoniac and to bear witness against him.

## Concerning the first point several questions are raised.

First, what is simony? I respond, following Damascenus and many other doctors of canon law, that simony is the eagerness or eager desire to buy or sell spiritual things or things annexed to spiritual ones. And this comes back to the same as the magisterial description set down by the doctors of sacred Scripture, namely: simony is the eager will to buy or sell something spiritual or annexed to a spiritual thing. And it is drawn from the words of Gregorius Nazianzenus, 1, q. 1, “Qui igitur studet”: one who continually strives to traffic for a price cannot remain in sacred orders.

### An objection is raised, however, against the said description,

since it implies that simony is committed by intention alone, the contrary of which seems to follow from many passages of law, as in extra eo. “tua nos.” at the end, and the last chapter, and extra “ut ecclesie beneficia,” “ut nostrum.” For this reason Lancelottus said that the pact alone makes someone a simoniac. Bernardus seems to hold this view in extra eo. c. ult. and c. “consulere,” where he says that he does not believe that will alone makes a man a simoniac, although he sins in another way. But Hostiensis says, by way of reconciliation, that simony is committed by will alone with respect to God, but not with respect to the Church, as in extra eo. “tua nos.” at the end.
