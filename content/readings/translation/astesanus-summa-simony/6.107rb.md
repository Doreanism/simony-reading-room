## There follows the consideration of §. 8, on doctrine and the license to teach: whether simony is committed if money is exacted for them.

And concerning this, several questions are asked.

### First, whether a religious — and especially a monk — can and ought to be appointed as a master.

I respond, according to Hostiensis, that such a person either thrusts himself forward (and thus is to be repelled) or is sent by his superior and licensed by him to whom it pertains — and thus is to be admitted, *16. q. 1. moderamine* and *c. sunt nonnulli*. Provided that he abstains from the prohibited sciences: since he is not specifically prohibited to religious in certain sciences, *de ne cleri. non magno*, this seems to be granted to others as well, *extra de presump. none* — especially theology, to which all without distinction ought to be admitted, since it leads all toward the good, *extra c. sup specula*. Moreover, the salvation of souls and the common utility — which, as is said in the same place, ought to be preferred to private \[utility\] — also demand it. Hence in this above all a monk ought to obey his abbot, when this requires it, provided it is not the utility of his own monastery but of the universal Church, by argument from *16. q. i. monachi*. For instance, if a monk is more suitable than others by reason of life and learning, why should he not better be placed in the chair than a secular cleric who is less suitable? According to Chrysostom, by argument from *di. 40. multi sacerdotes*; and Ambrosius, *16. q. i. coactos* and the following chapter — especially when they teach freely; and thus in this case cathedral churches may be relieved, whence they are not to be prohibited, according to the Lateran Council and the General Council, *extra de magi. c. i.* and the penultimate \[chapter\]. Nor does it stand against this that, according to Jerome, a monk has the office not of teaching but of mourning, and ought not to feed but to be fed, *16. q. i. monachus* and in many following chapters.
