For he says that under the two chapters one must understand the case of someone publicly defamed: otherwise, in his view, the man is not to be suspended (C. 2 q. 5 *Presul* 7 §. *Si*). For such a man, since he is suspended by the law itself, as was said above, will then be suspended in fact by the judge. The same procedure, according to Hostiensis, applies even when the offense is hidden, provided there are sound indications of it. And it is in this sense that one can understand X eod. *Sicut simoniaca*. And if the accuser proves his charge, whether by confession or by witnesses (it does not matter which, so long as the matter is sufficiently established, that is, proved), the accused must be deposed. In this sense one can understand the chapters *Sicut simoniaca* (X eod.), C. 1 q. 1 *Presbyter*, and C. 1 q. 7 c. *Si*. If, however, the proceeding is by way of inquisition, then the man being investigated is either a secular or a regular cleric. If a secular obtained the benefice or order simoniacally, he either acquired it in ignorance \[of the simony\] — and then is forced to resign if he is not deposed (X eod. *Ex insinuatione*) — or knowingly, and then he is deposed (X *De accusationibus* *Inquisitionis*). Bernardus holds the same view in the same place. The same will hold if a regular is simoniacal in his order (argument from c. *Tuas* of the same title). But if it concerns a benefice and was done in ignorance, he is bound to resign; he then receives it anew, with the prior place changed and a lower one assigned to him, or else he is placed in another house of the same order (as in X eod. *De regularibus* at the end). Yet both givers and receivers are to be struck with the canonical penalty (X eod. *Sicut tuis* §. penult. and final). But what if he resigns because they hope to have him reinstated, and would not otherwise resign — is he then received back? Robertus answers that those received in this way may remain, provided no condition or pact intervened, and provided they repent of their corrupt intention. But if regulars have been knowingly promoted simoniacally into the regular community, they are punished with the penalty of deposition. Nevertheless, those received \[under such circumstances\] are, by decree of the General Council, to be removed from the monastery they obtained in this way and thrust into other monasteries of stricter rule; toward those received before the General Council, however, mercy is shown by way of dispensation, so that they may be placed in other houses of the same order, or — if this cannot be done because of their too-great number — they are permitted to remain in the same monastery after a free resignation, with their original seats changed and lower ones assigned them. Understand in this sense X eod. *De regularibus*, *Responsio* 1, and the General Council’s decree *Quoniam simoniaca*. So Hostiensis.
