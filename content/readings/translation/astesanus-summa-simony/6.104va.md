## Next, on the sixth point: namely, restitution to the simoniac receiver. Concerning which it must be known that one who receives a benefice simoniacally is bound to renounce and to restore all fruits received and all that could have been received — namely by a sufficiently diligent possessor, [14, q. 6, “Si res aliena”] and [Extra, de restitutione spoliatorum, “Gravis”]. This is true if simony was committed with his knowledge and consent, or if, as soon as he knew, he did not renounce, [Extra, eodem, “Sicut tuis,” near the end]. For if he were a possessor in good faith — because the simony was committed without his knowledge, and as soon as he knew he immediately renounced — he would not be bound for what was consumed but only for what remains, [C. de rei vendi., “Certum”]. For generally one possessing without title is bound for all advantages and gains received and to be received, [ff. si rei vendi., “Si navis,” § “Generaliter”]. And he calls “title” here a just beginning or just cause of possessing, such as purchase, sale, gift, daughter \[i.e., succession\]. The same applies if he has such a title that it is to be held as no title — for instance, if a sale has been forbidden against the laws, [C. de agri. et censi., l. “Quemadmodum”]. Understand this, however, of the fruits that remain after deducting expenses, which are for the sake of seeking, preserving, and gathering the fruits — for these are deducted from a possessor in good faith as well as from one in bad faith, [ff. de pet. her., “Si a patre,” § ult.]. Understand this also of the fruits which he did not spend in the service and utility of the Church, but for other uses, by the argument of [ff. de neg. ges., “Si pupilli,” § “Sed et si quis”]. But to whom should the money or possession simoniacally received be restored? I reply, according to Hostiensis, where the entire chapter chooses, that it ought to be restored to the one who gave it. Thus he understands [Extra c. “Veniens”]. But if one or two persons or some outsider \[gave it\], it ought to be given to the church to whose injury it was given. Thus he understands [Extra c. “Ex. B.”].
