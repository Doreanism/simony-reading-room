Others, however, taking simony according to the general custom they adhere to, say — and more probably, according to Raymundus — that there is then simony in an administration that has been bought only when it is such as has something spiritual annexed to it; otherwise not. But this is noted in the gloss on that chapter *Saluator*, and there Raymundus at the end of the title *De simonia*. And Hostiensis says in the same place that a bishop may give his rent-collectors or his land to farm under an annual rent, provided that no spiritual jurisdiction is annexed thereto. But he can receive nothing from anyone in order to appoint him *prepositus*, *iconomus*, or *vicedominus*. Nevertheless, bishops are accustomed to give to farm a *prepositura* or temporal bailiwick with temporal jurisdiction, just as secular princes do.

###There are some inquiries concerning certain spiritual matters: namely, whether it is lawful to give or receive money for spiritual acts, such as for prayer, preaching, the praise of God, and procession. I respond that it is not, for these and similar things are called spiritual inasmuch as they proceed from grace and dispose unto grace; yet they are exhibited through the ministry of men, who must be sustained by the people, according to that text of 1 Corinthians 9: “Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense?” And therefore to sell what is spiritual in these things is simoniacal; but to give or receive something for the sustenance of the ministers, according to the ordinance of the Church and approved custom, is lawful — provided, however, that the intention of buying and selling is absent, and that it is not exacted from the unwilling by withholding the spiritual things, since these are to be furnished. For this would have a certain appearance of selling grace; but, the spiritual works being lawfully exhibited, the established and customary offerings may rightly be exacted from the willing and able to pay, with the authority of the superior intervening.

###Concerning prayer, then, I say that those giving alms to the poor, that they may pray for them with the suffrages of their prayers, do not give as if intending to buy prayers, but as if to provoke them by gratuitous beneficence to pray for them out of charity.

###Concerning preaching, I say that to preachers temporal goods are owed for their sustenance, but not as a price for buying the word of preaching. Whence on that text of 1 Timothy 5, “those presbyters who rule well,” etc., the gloss says it is of necessity, that not even from Phoebe \[Phebe\] is the gospel a thing for sale, that the preacher should preach to them.

###Concerning the praise of God, I say that some give temporal goods to those praising him in the celebration of the ecclesiastical office, whether for the living or for the dead — not as the price of officiating the office, but as a stipend of sustenance.
