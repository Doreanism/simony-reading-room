Concerning the simoniacally promoted non-simoniac with respect to a benefice, Hostiensis in his *Summa*, and Raymundus, and Bernardus on *X de renunciatione [post translationem]* in gloss 2, distinguish thus: namely, that if the benefice was a simple one, then after his free resignation he can recover it by dispensation of the bishop, or of the chapter with the consent of the bishop. *X eodem [ex insinuatione]*; *X de electione [si alicuius]* at the end. Or it may be said, according to the gloss on that chapter *[ex insinuatione]*, that the chapter does not dispense, but elects such a one through the dispensation of the bishop, because the bishop dispenses with him on the counsel of the chapter. *C.1 q.5 [presentium]*. And Rod\[ericus\] says that this means a simple benefice, that is, one without prelatial dignity. For sometimes “simple benefice” is said of one which does not have the cure of souls annexed, as above in article 2, §. *circa autem munus a lingua*. Whence he says that concerning a church having such a cure of souls annexed without prelatial dignity, the bishop can dispense in this case. But what if a dignity is annexed to it? He says that he cannot. Argument from *X de prebendis c. ult.* But if there was a prelacy or dignity, distinguish there: namely, if he came to the bishop without any accusation and of his own motion renounced, humbly asking that he admit his renunciation, then once the renunciation has been made he can recover it by dispensation of his bishop, provided however that the utility or necessity of the church requires this. Argument from *C.1 q.5 [presentium]* and *c. [quicunque]* near the beginning, and argument from *X de electione [si alicuius]* a contrario sensu. But if he contended in court or was summoned, and out of fear of the proofs in court confessed, the bishop cannot dispense with him on that occasion or in that summons, but can on another occasion. As argument *X eodem [nobis] §.1*, and *X de electione [si alicuius]* at the beginning. And according to this understand that text *C.1 q.1 [erga]* and *C.1 q.5 [presentium]* and other similar texts. But Rod\[ericus\] also says that this is true when someone is elected to a prelacy or dignity by a college, but if he were elected by the king or by someone holding the right of patronage, he believes that the bishop could dispense so that after resignation he might be elected again. And the same he says if there were no proof of simony except by hidden confession made in the penitential forum before confirmation of the election. Or say more briefly, also according to Hostiensis, that in a simple benefice the bishop with the chapter dispenses with cause and without cause, provided however that merits demand it; thus they understand those chapters *[ex insinuatione]* and *[si alicuius]* at the end.
