It can therefore be said that the fruits of the church can be sold to a layman, but not such that he administer them, but rather another shall be appointed who will render \[them\] to him, argument from *X De locato, Nostra.* For although the fruits proceed from spiritual things, yet they are not spiritual; whence they may be sold, provided that in the sale or in this lease nothing spiritual is mixed in, *X De rerum permutatione, Ad questiones.* What if some prelate sells the fruits of his church up to the seventh year, and immediately receives the money for the whole seven-year term, and then shortly thereafter dies — is the successor bound to abide by the sale? Hostiensis answers no, because, since benefices are personal, contracts about them do not exceed the persons of the granters, argument *X De prebendis, Nisi.* For a cleric is compared to a usufructuary, who cannot lease the usufruct of a thing he has beyond his own life; whence if the lessee has made such expenses upon the thing itself, he must reckon them to himself, because he ought to have foreseen that the man could die, *Dig. Locati et conducti, Si quis dom.* Yet perhaps the expenses can be reclaimed from the heirs of the usufructuary, argument *X De rebus ecclesiasticis, Non alienandis, Ad audientiam.* Thus far Hostiensis. But according to Hostiensis \[i.e., the gloss\] and Bernard *X eo. Querelam,* if the money has been turned to the utility of the church, it is necessary either that he return the money or that the lease stand, argument *X De solutionibus c.* 1. Otherwise the contrary, *X De precariis c.* 1, and let him impute it to himself who gave the money — unless perhaps it had been obtained from the prince by special privilege, perhaps for the cause of theology or pilgrimage to the Holy Land, or some other cause, *X De magistris, Super specula.* But Innocent on *c. Querelam* says that if the lease is made in the name of the church, and usefully, the successor is bound, *X De solutionibus c.* 1; otherwise if uselessly. But if he leased it in his own name, not intending to obligate the church, the successor is not bound, nor even his heir. And this is true of the minister or prelate of the church, namely that the successor is bound to hold ratified everything that was done by his predecessor. A canon prebendary, however, can never lease his prebend beyond the time of his life.
