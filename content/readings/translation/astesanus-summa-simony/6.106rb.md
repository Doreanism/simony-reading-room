## There follows, in the seventh place, the special consideration of the simony of prelates, which they commit by granting their offices or churches for an annual fee. Concerning which it must be known that no prelate ought to grant or lease for a price his prelacy, jurisdiction, office, or his offices regarding spiritual things — that is, jurisdictions. The reason for this is threefold. The first is that this redounds to the burden of subjects and the subversion of judgments, *X c. Cum.* 2.2, since one who appoints a vicar entrusts to him the care of souls, *X De officio vicarii c. ult.* And therefore one who gives or receives money for a vicariate commits simony, *X De simonia, Ad nostram.* For spiritual things cannot be sold, as has been established above, and jurisdiction is spiritual, *C.* 1 *q.* 3 *Ex multis;* whence to sell a stewardship or an oeconomate is also simoniacal, *C.* 1 *q.* 1 *Si quis episcopus,* and *C.* 1 *q.* 3 *Salvator.* For all power and all jurisdiction is from God, and is seen to be spiritual. And this is the argument according to Hostiensis, that if anyone gives money to be promoted to emperor, or as a podestà or rector of any city, he commits simony. The second reason is that, since a prelate ought to judge gratis, *C.* 14 *q.* 5 *Non sane,* he establishes a venal judgment in which he offends with the law, *C.* 11 *q.* 3 *Qui recte.*

### But \[it should be noted\] that these two are equal as regards the commission of simony, namely to remit or to confer spiritual things for money, *X De simonia, Querelam,* and *De restitutione, Veniens.* And yet a bishop, with the consent of the chapter, can remit jurisdiction under an annual census, *X De religiosis domibus, Constitutus.* But a prelate granting a privilege of liberty for temporal goods, who remits the census, retains \[some right\] to himself, *X De censibus, Cum venerabilem;* indeed in the conveyance of his property he can retain what pleases him, *X De censibus, Quanto.* Furthermore, the imposition of a census does not introduce the vice of simony, because it is not imposed in order that jurisdiction be remitted — for that would be simony, *X De transactione, Preterea* — but in order to prove an ancient subjection, *X De censibus, Omnis anima,* and to manifest pristine liberty, *X De privilegiis, Recepimus.* From the foregoing it is clear that one who receives jurisdiction ought not give money for it.
