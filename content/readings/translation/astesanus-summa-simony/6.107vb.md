But \[the term\] would run if they had retained the fruits for a time, by argument from *extra ut beneficia ecclesiarum. ut nostrum*. What if no prebend is vacant. I respond, according to Bernardus: let one wait until one falls vacant, *extra de preben. dilectus. 2*. Or, according to Hostiensis: let everyone of the church give so much of his own as makes up one prebend, by argument from *extra de his que fi. a maiori parte ca. c. finali*. Nor does that stand against this at *extra de preben. ca. antepenultimo*: for this is understood of the case when some private person wished to be received, asking that a prebend be assigned to him which would be deducted from the prebends of others — so that in that gift a private utility was involved. Here, however, public \[utility\] is at stake, which is to be preferred to private. Moreover, it has been decreed that in a metropolitan church a theologian and a grammarian be appointed — unless it would be too burdensome to support both, in which case the theologian should be provided through the metropolitan church, and the grammarian through another church of the city or diocese. And such masters ought to teach freely the clerics of the same church and also poor strangers; but from other wealthy strangers they may levy a collection. Thus they understand that text at *extra eodem. c. primo* and *c. quia nonnullis*. Whence, according to Bernardus on the same passage: a master to whom a suitable benefice is assigned may seek a collection from the wealthy — namely from strangers. But according to Hostiensis, if the master is poor or under obligation, whether or not he receives a salary, he licitly takes a collection. For since the scholars are bound to assist anyone in necessity, much more the master. Therefore the necessity of the teacher is to be relieved, as at *extra de magistris. quia nonnullis*. And it is to be given to him with regard to his learning, eloquence, and labor, *extra de prebendis. de multa*, and with regard to the dignity and resources of the scholars, *ff. de administra. tu. cum plures. §. procurator*. But if no necessity presses upon the master, and he has a salary or even a benefice sufficient for food and clothing, he ought to exact nothing — yet he may accept what is freely offered, as at *extra de simonia. dilectus filius. in fine. 7. 1. q. 1. placuit*.
