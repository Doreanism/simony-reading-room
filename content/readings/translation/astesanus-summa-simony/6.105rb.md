1 [q.1] *[ordinationes]*. And the vice of theft is not purged unless the thing returns into the power of the owner. *C.14 q.6 [si res aliae]*. But it must be said that he can indeed dispense, because the vice of theft can be purged even while the thing remains with the thief. *Dig. de usuris et usucapionibus [si fur]*. Note this in the gloss on that chapter *[ordinationes]*. ### What if a church has been founded with simoniacal money? It is answered, *C.1 q.1 [non est putanda]* in the gloss, that it shall not be torn down. *C.24 q.3 [comperimus]*; *C.12 q.2 [aurum]*. ### What if someone buys a vineyard with simoniacal money: can he give alms from its fruit? The aforesaid gloss there responds that he cannot, because the fruit is judged by the same law as the money, since the fruit succeeds in the place of the money. Argument from *X de usuris [cum tu]* in fin. With one simoniacally ordained who is not a simoniac, however, the bishop can dispense. *C.1 q.1 [latorem]; [pristinum]*; *C.1 q.1 [pristinus]*; and according to *X [ex simoniaco]* in the text and gloss; and this according to Raymundus, Hostiensis, and Bernardus, in the same place—if, after he learns that he was simoniacally ordained, he first renounces simply and purely the execution of the order so received. For he is bound to this. *X de electione [si alicuius]; [eodem ex insinuatione]*; *C.1 q.5 [quicquid]* in the beginning. And from this fact, these laws and others speak of the benefice as well as the place in the order. If it be objected that such a one has no execution, and no one can renounce what he does not have, Hostiensis responds that he can renounce in so far as it is in him by offering to be perpetually deposed. *D.21 [nunc autem]*. But according to Hostiensis, with such a simoniacally ordained non-simoniac, the bishop can perhaps dispense if the crime be hidden. Argument from *X [eodem] de simonia*. And with one simoniacally promoted to some religious order who is not a simoniac, the bishop can likewise dispense, as with others. Argument from *X [ex eo] de regularibus*. According to others, however, one must abide indistinctly by *X [eodem quoniam]*. Of the first opinion seems to be Raymundus—indeed not only the bishop, but also another prelate of his, or the chapter, can well dispense with such a one, provided they themselves were not the principals of the simony; for then, since on this account he ought to be punished (*X [eodem dilectus]*), they ought not by the same act enjoy a privilege, but rather seem to have lost the power of electing, and others who have not sinned to have acquired it. *X de electione [bone memorie]*. ### But can the bishop dispense with one who, through another’s simony, entered religion or obtained a benefice or order—who did not renounce immediately when he knew, but ministered in the order received, and received the order otherwise? Roffredus believes that the bishop can do this, because such a one is not a simoniac, although he gravely transgressed in this matter; nevertheless he does not believe of the abbot or the chapter that they could dispense concerning the order, because he does not recall that this was granted to them.
