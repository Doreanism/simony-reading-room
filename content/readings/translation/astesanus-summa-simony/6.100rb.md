For it is exceedingly inhumane to be willing to receive from no one, but to receive everything and from everyone is most base, *ff. de offi. proconsulis, solent, § ultimo*. Therefore it is permitted to receive food and drink that can be consumed within three days, *ff. de offi. presidis, plebiscito*. If, however, with corrupt intention and without a pact they hold or receive gifts, however many, a small simony is committed, namely with respect to God, which nevertheless, without resignation of the exercise of the order or of the benefice conferred, is purged by penance alone. And according to this, that text in *c. nemo* can be understood, according to Hostiensis.

### It must therefore be known that nothing ought to be conferred or received

for burials or funeral rites of the dead, or for the blessings of those marrying, nor for chrism, nor for holy oil, *extra c. cum ecclesie, ea que ad nostram sententiam in tantum*. Nor for conferring or consecrating a church, or for installing an abbot or abbess in the seat. Nor for investiture of a church, nor for the consecrations of bishops, their enthronements, the blessings of abbots, the ordinations of clergy, even though by corrupt practice it has been fixed how much is to be paid to this one or that one, as in *c. in tantum*; just as nothing is to be sought or given for any sacrament of the church, nor even for fodder or pastry, nor for one’s voice, nor for one’s pen, that is, writing, and the like, *extra c. veniens*, and *c. 1*, and *1, q. 3, si quis obiecerit*. Nor does any custom avail to allow anything to be exacted for such things, *extra c. non satis*, and the following chapter.

### If it be objected that praiseworthy customs

ought to be observed, *1, q. 3, quesitum*, and *di. 18, de eulogiis*. It must be said, according to Hostiensis, that nothing ought to be received by pact under the pretext of any custom, but it can be received if it is offered freely, as the curia holds and as is proved from *c. dilectus filius*. And this will be discussed immediately. Or, also according to the same Hostiensis, better thus: Either clerics are exacting from laymen under the pretext of custom, and then the clerics ought not to retain a spiritual pledge, but ought to bury freely or give the blessing freely, and afterward, the truth being known through the bishop of the place, the laymen will be compelled to observe the praiseworthy custom, *ex c. ad apostolicam*. Or clerics are exacting from clerics, and these, if they seek something in which their proper and private utility is involved, it is not valid, as appears in the said *c. quesitum, de eulogiis*.

### Certain cases, however, are excepted by the law,

in which a gift of the hand may be received or can be received. One is if it is offered freely, *ex c. dilectus filius, in fine glo. a*.
