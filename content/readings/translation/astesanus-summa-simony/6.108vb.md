I answer, according to Alexander de Ales, that the blessing is not of the substance of matrimony but of the solemnity; nevertheless, since they have it from the constitution of the church, recourse must be had to the superior, who if he refuses to compel the priest, it is safer to abstain than to offer him an occasion of sin.

What of the priest, when two persons of different parishes are joined in matrimony, who refuses to give license to another priest to perform the blessing unless money is given or promised to him? Rodericus answers that if that priest does not intend to sell the license to perform the blessing but \[merely\] to provide for himself with respect to the offerings and oblations which would have come his way, it is not simony; but perhaps more is given or promised to him than he would have had if he himself blessed. If, however, he should demand more than the share coming to him would presumably be worth, it could rightly be presumed that he intended also to sell that license.

### What of the priest who refuses to give his Christian \[parishioner\] license to confess except for money? Rodericus answers that such a one commits simony.

### What if a priest, in danger of death, refuses to baptize except for money given to him: may it be given to him without the vice of simony? I answer as above in the tract on baptism, title 17, at the end.

Whether a notary may receive anything without simony for writing concerning someone’s ordination. I answer that he may not, *Extra* eodem c. 1 and *i. q. \[3\]* “Sicut episcopum.” But for one for whom no one is bound to make a benefice from his own. *io. q. \[3\]* “Precarie.” On this, note Bernardus on c. eodem c. 1 \[saying\] that this Bartholomeus and certain others commonly hold, where the notary receives a salary from the public. Laurentius, however, said that even if he receives nothing from the public, he ought to receive nothing for such writing, because he would commit simony; and this is special in odium of simony, lest fraud be procured. Bernardus, however, in the same place says that nothing may be taken by pact, but if anyone freely and spontaneously offers something, and he came to this from the beginning without any \[such\] intention, he does not believe that a lay notary would sin if he received it, by argument from *Extra* eodem “Cum sit Romana.” And it is express in c. “Sic episcopum.” But Bernardus and Bartholomeus on c. “Sicut episcopum” hold with Laurentius, saying that the notary may take nothing either for his labor or for the parchment or anything else; but the bishop should provide that he writes without acceptance of any gift.
