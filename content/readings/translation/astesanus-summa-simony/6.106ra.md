But can a bishop also dispense with one who has received a benefice simoniacally, after he has resigned, in the reception of orders, just as in the exercise of orders? Hostiensis answers no. For since he cannot dispense as to the receiving of a benefice — which is probable — neither \[can he dispense\] as to the receiving of orders, unless the man enters religion; for then, by favor of religion, the bishop could dispense, provided the simony were occult. But if it were manifest, or if he were defamed by it, he could not, because if he were defamed, then one must proceed to inquisition or to purgation upon the defamed party’s testimony.

But can a bishop dispense as to simony committed concerning the offices of the dead, the blessings of those being married, burials, chrism, the sale of sacred vessels or the blessing of vestments, and similar lesser species? Hostiensis answers yes, according to the masters, after penance has been performed, since it is not prohibited — argument from *De simonia, ex. Nuper.* Nor does that text *C.* 1 *q.* 1 *Erga* stand in the way, because it speaks of a simoniac in a dignity, as in *X De simonia, Nobis* at the end, according to Hostiensis. But what was said above, namely that the bishop can dispense, must be understood of one to whom the money was given not personally but to one of his associates without his knowledge: otherwise, if he himself had been at fault, he would be unworthy of the power to dispense, argument from *X De clerico coniugato, Diversis* at the end, and *C.* 1 *q.* 5 *Prelatum.* And then, as Hostiensis says, the pope would dispense, or the successor of that bishop, or some other ordinary judge of that cleric — for instance, if he has his domicile or benefice in the diocese of another bishop — or even the archbishop, according to Raymond, with discretion exercised in the forum of penance. And this must be understood, according to Hostiensis, when the archbishop has it by custom that he may hear the confessions of the subjects of his suffragans; otherwise not, because he is not their ordinary judge except in the cases expressed in law, among which this is not one.

On this note that, according to Raymond, there is a fourfold dispensation with a simoniac: namely, a small one, that he may have lay communion, *C.* 1 *q.* 1 *Data laycis,* at Easter or other times when it is customary to communicate. A great one, that after performing penance he may remain in his order in another church. A greater one, that he may remain even in the same church, and in the minor orders.
