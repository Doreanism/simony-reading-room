But where a spiritual benefice is assigned in such a way that he renounces by pact, it is simony, as was said above in c. *Ad audientiam*; nevertheless, since it was there a kind of permutation of spiritual for spiritual (*Extra*, de permu., *Ad questiones*), he is bound to it, and tacitly so, even though there is loss involved — namely, that if he receives one benefice he must renounce the other, since he cannot lawfully hold both at once; and *Extra*, de preben., *Cum non ignores*. According to Bernardus, what is said in c. *Ad audientiam* by *venditas* is to be understood as renounced by such a pact.

What if a bishop, in founding a feudal church, lays down that whoever shall give such-and-such an amount in annual rents to that church shall in perpetuity be received as a canon, and shall hold what he has given as his prebend. Raymundus answers that, if such an institution is made for a time, and this principally with the intention of increasing the worship of God — so that the candidate is received to spiritual things principally on account of his own devotion, and not on account of his \[gift\] — it can be tolerated, provided the one received is worthy; otherwise not. The same can be said of monasteries newly founded for the needy: namely, that whoever gives such-and-such an amount be received into that monastery, up to a certain number, argument from *Extra*, *Tua nos*.

Suppose someone wishing to be beneficed or ordained gives something to the doorkeeper or steward to let him in to the bishop in order to ask for a benefice or an order or the confirmation of an election: is such a man a simoniac. Raymundus answers that he is not, if he does not give with the intention of corrupting — that is, in order, by the intercession of him to whom he gives, to obtain his end. Likewise, if an abbot or another gives something to the bishop’s household so as not to seem mean, and so as to be more pleasing, and to gain easier access to the bishop, or to be praised for his liberality, it is not simony, provided the intention is not corrupt, as has been said — although such things may be done with sin, as if they are done for the sake of boasting or vainglory.

What if a cleric, on the condition or by the pact that he be afterward admitted as a canon and may keep \[his goods\] for his prebend, gives or offers his goods. The answer is in *Extra*, e[odem titulo], *Tua*: such an offering or reception cannot be made without simony, since in such matters every pact and every agreement must cease.
