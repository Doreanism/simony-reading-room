Argument expressly *X De officio ordinarii, Inter cetera; De appellationibus, Pervenit.* And such a one will have the care of souls and a delegated jurisdiction — but not an ordinary one — and for a time, not in perpetuity, when a vicar is appointed; or in perpetuity, when a perpetual coadjutor is given, as is clear from what has been said in the first \[part of\] the chapter. But may a prelate grant his church under an annual census? It seems not, by the fourth title of the fifth book of the Decretals which prohibits this, and by the third chapter of the same. But that he may, seems to follow from the last chapter of the same. To this Bernard answers in the same place that the name “church” is said in many ways. For it is called the assembly of the wicked: *Ps.* \[25:5\], “I have hated the church of the wicked.” It is also called the assembly of the faithful, *De cons. d.* 1 *Ecclesia.* It is also called the enclosure of the walls, *De cons. d.* 1 *De fabrica.* It is also called the body of Christ, *C.* 1 *q.* 1 *In ecclesia.* It is also called the prelacy of that church itself, *X eo. c.* 7 and in the aforesaid title; and in this sense the church is forbidden to be granted under an annual census, as appears there, and *X De simonia, Non satis,* because a church so received ought not even be entrusted to hired priests, *D.* 71 *q.* 2 *Precipimus.* The church is also called the offerings coming in by reason of the church, and so it is taken in *X eo. c. final;* and these can be sold by him who has the church entitled to him, or commended under a fixed census. Yet the agreement must be examined, as appears there — notwithstanding any contrary diocesan statute, as in *X De locato, Nostra* — and this for a fixed and modest term, not in perpetuity, nor for a non-modest term, provided he does not hand over the church under firma to a layman, *X Ar. ecclesiastica c.* 2, nor to a monk; therefore by contrary sense he can hand it over to a secular cleric, *X Ne clerici, Iuxta instituta;* but he can also \[grant\] it in the name of a monastery for a modest time, as is said there.
