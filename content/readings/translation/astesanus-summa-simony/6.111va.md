### Whether to bear witness for money is simoniacal.

I reply, according to Richardus, that it is so in a spiritual case, but not otherwise — although there is a grave sin even there. Or, according to Alexander \[of Hales\]: in ecclesiastical causes and on the part of ecclesiastical persons, one commits simony if he sells his testimony; in others, he sins gravely.

### Whether an advocate commits simony.

I reply that the case of the judge and the advocate is not the same. For the judge has rents assigned to him for his office, and is bound to render a just judgment; but the advocate has nothing assigned to him, and therefore for his labor — since it is corporal, and he is not bound to it by office — he can take a moderate fee. The same is to be said of the jurist who gives counsel.

But why is it not the same with the witness? Alexander \[of Hales\] replies that this is so because the truth of testimony belongs to the very case to be decided, whereas counsel, like advocacy, supports the case from outside. Hence witnesses are compelled to testify, but jurists are not compelled to give counsel. Some, however, distinguish further: namely, that if advocates and counselors sell their knowledge, they commit simony — and this greater or lesser according to their oratorical knowledge — but not if they sell their labor, since they are not bound to that. The judge and the witnesses, however, can take nothing for the labor of judging and testifying, since they are bound to it. Thus Alexander.

### Whether it is lawful to buy the right of primogeniture.

I reply that it is, because it is not now, as it was anciently, annexed to spiritual things. For, as is said in the gloss on Genesis 14, the Hebrews say that all the firstborn from Noah down to Aaron were pontiffs. Yet Iacob bought that right from Esau, Genesis 25, and is not reprehended for this, because that right was owed to Iacob himself by divine ordination; and therefore it should rather be said that he redeemed his unjust vexation than that he bought that right. Or it can be said that it was not in such a way among the things annexed to spiritual matters in the law of nature and the old law as it is in the new — because just as that priesthood ought rather to be called a figure of the priesthood of Christ than a priesthood, so it ought rather to be called a sign of a spiritual office than a spiritual office.

### Whether it is lawful to sell the right of patronage.

I reply that it is not, because in something spiritual it would be simony (*Extra, de iurepatronatus, “In re”*). Yet it does pass with a corporation or village sold cheaply, unless excepted (*eodem titulo, “Ex litteris”* and “*Cum primum*”), as the church likewise passes, as is said in the same place. And just as the right of patronage cannot be sold, so neither can it be given in fief. And if a village were sold for a higher price on account of the right of patronage, it would be simony, because such a sale would in some way reach the right of patronage itself.
