# Protocol of the Roman Synod of 1076

## Concerning the Synod

In the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1075, in the fourteenth indiction, lord Gregory the pope himself celebrated a synod at Rome in the church of the Lord Savior, called the Constantinian. There was present a great number of bishops, abbots, and clerics and laymen of various orders. There, among the other decrees enacted, he excommunicated Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, in the following terms: “Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, who has attempted to tear the bishops and abbots of the kingdom of the Germans away from the holy Roman Church, that is, from their spiritual mother, we suspend from all episcopal office by the judgment of the Holy Spirit and the authority of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, and we cut off from the communion of the body and blood of the Lord — unless perchance the peril of death should intervene — yet on this condition: that he be led by penitence to come to his senses. As for the others, who of their own accord subscribed to his schism by consenting to it and wish to persist in that iniquity, we likewise suspend from all episcopal office.
