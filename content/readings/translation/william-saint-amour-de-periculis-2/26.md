For if the Roman Pontiff should strive — which God forbid — to destroy what the Apostles and Prophets have taught, he would be convicted not of pronouncing sentence but rather of erring. The Apostle himself was unwilling to preach to a people not his own, for the reason already given, and lest he build upon another’s foundation, as is read in Romans 15: *Thus I have preached the Gospel, not where Christ was named, lest I should build upon another man’s foundation*. Gloss: *Lest I should preach to those already converted by others*. And 2 Corinthians 1: *Not glorying beyond measure in other men’s labors*. Gloss: *where another had laid the foundation of faith*. And below: *Not having hope to glory in another’s rule*. Gloss: *In matters committed to another’s governance*.

Likewise, if one person, or an unlimited number of persons, were to have the power of preaching everywhere uninvited — since this is the chief office of bishops — there would then be unlimited universal bishops, which is contrary to the laws, namely that anyone should be, or be said to be, a universal bishop; *dist. 99. cap. Nullus*, and *cap. Ecce*.

Likewise, it is established that to all those who preach by virtue of \[official\] power, procuration, that is, expenses, is owed, 1 Corinthians 9: *God hath ordained that they who preach the Gospel should live by the Gospel*. Therefore, if an unlimited number of persons had the power of preaching in any given church, every church would owe unlimited procurations, which is absurd to say. For the parish priests, who have their own Apostles, that is, bishops, and their helpers, namely the archdeacons and archpriests, to whom, when visiting them, they must furnish procurations according to the ecclesiastical decrees; and the lay faithful, who have their own priests, to whom they pay due stipends according to the ordinance of the Church, ought not to be further burdened; *10. q. 3. cap. Cauendum*. Hence, if bishops send preachers through their dioceses to preach, those bishops themselves — not the priests nor the lay faithful — ought to furnish them with the necessities of life; *Extra, De off. ordin. cap. Inter cætera*. Nor ought new Apostles to be established in the Church of God, since the Church herself, both as to Apostles, that is, bishops, and as to other ministers, was once, by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, divinely ordained by the Apostles and by the councils of the Holy Fathers, according to the pattern shown to Moses on Mount Sinai, as Blessed Dionysius says in *Ecclesiastica Hierarchia, cap. 5*.
