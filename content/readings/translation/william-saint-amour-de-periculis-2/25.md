But someone will say: all those can preach who have the authority of the Lord Pope or of the diocesan bishops, since it is said in *Extra, De Hæreticis*, cap. *Excommunicamus*, §. *quia vero*: *Let ALL who, being forbidden or not sent, presume to usurp the office of preaching, publicly or privately, apart from authority received from the Apostolic See or from the Catholic bishop of the place, be bound by the chain of excommunication*. From which it seems, by argument from the contrary sense, that by the authority of the Apostolic See or of the diocesan bishops, anyone whatsoever may preach.

We answer that we do not wish to dispute concerning the power of the Lord Pope or of the bishops; yet, since according to both divine and human laws there can be in one Church only one rector — otherwise the Church would not be a bride but a harlot, q. 2. cap. *Sicut in vnaquaque* — and in one Church there ought not to be many heads, lest it be a monster, *Extra, De officio Iudicis ordinarij*, cap. *Quoniam in plerisque*; and since in the governance of the Church the office of preaching exists as the chief office, just as the order of preachers is the chief order, *Extra, De Hæreticis*, cap. *Cum ex iniuncto*: if perhaps the Lord Pope grants to certain persons the power of preaching everywhere, it must be understood as wherever they shall have been invited for this purpose. For even bishops, unless they have been invited for this, ought not to enter beyond their diocese to dispose of any ecclesiastical matters, 9. q. 2. cap. *Non inuitati*. For a prince does not, through his mandates, will that prejudice be brought upon the jurisdiction of another, *Extra, De vsu pallij*, cap. *Ex tuarum tenore*; *Extra, De off. Iud. ord*. cap. *Licet*; ff. *Ne quis in loco publico*, lege 2. §. *si quis a principe*. Lest — which God forbid — injuries seem to arise from the very source from which laws arise, C. *Vnde Vi. meminerint cuncti*; and *Extra De accusationibus*, cap. *Qualiter & quando*. Indeed, the Apostolic \[Pope\] does injury to himself who disturbs the rights of his brethren, 25. q. 2. cap. *Quod vero dicitis*. And since, according to the Apostle, no one ought to glory in matters committed to another’s governance, 2 Corinthians 10: *We will not glory beyond measure, beyond the measure of the rule which God hath measured to us*. Gloss: *that is, beyond the people measured out to me by God, of whom I am the rule for directing*. It is not likely that the Lord Pope, contrary to the teaching of the Apostle Paul, would grant license to an unlimited number, or to many, to preach to peoples not their own, unless they have been invited by the parish priests; since the chapter *Sunt quidam*, 25. q. 1, says \[as much\].
