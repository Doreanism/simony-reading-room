Perils shall also press in through pseudo-preachers. Matthew 24: *Many pseudo-prophets shall arise, and shall seduce many*. Now pseudo-preachers are all those who preach without being sent, however learned and holy they may be, even if they should perform signs or miracles. Romans 10: *How shall they preach, except they be sent?* Gloss: *They are not true Apostles unless sent; for no signs of virtues bear witness to them*. And as Cyprianus says in dist. 50. can. *Si quis præpropere*: *Martyrs do indeed command that certain things be done; but if what they command is not written in the law of the Lord, we must first know whether they have obtained from God what they ask*. But none are sent except those who are rightly chosen by the Church; just as none are called by God except those who are rightly chosen. Hebrews 5: *Neither doth any man take the honor to himself, but he that is called by God, as Aaron was*. Gloss: *He is called by God who is rightly chosen*. Now by the Church the Bishops, who have succeeded the Apostles, are rightly chosen; and the parish Presbyters, who have succeeded the 72 Disciples and hold their place. Dist. 21. can. *In Nouo Testamento*. Whence on Luke 10 at the beginning the Gloss says, *As in the 12 Apostles is the figure of Bishops, so in the 72 Disciples is the figure of Presbyters*. Nor are there in the Church more grades established for governing the Church; whence the Chorepiscopi, because they usurped in some respect the office of Bishops, were abolished by the Church. *For since we have known there to be no more than two orders among the Disciples of the Lord — that is, of the 12 Apostles and the 72 Disciples — whence this third order proceeded we are utterly ignorant; and what lacks reason must be uprooted*. So one reads in dist. 68. c. *Chorepiscopi*.

But if anyone should object concerning Archdeacons, or concerning Vicars, who in the place of others exercise the cure of souls, we say that such men hold power in the Church as *opitulatores*, that is, as those bringing help to their superiors; whence the Apostle in 1 Corinthians 12: *God indeed hath set some in the Church; first Apostles, secondly Prophets, thirdly Doctors, after that Powers, then graces of healing, helpers* — that is, as the Gloss says, *those who bring help to their superiors, as Titus to the Apostle, or Archdeacons to Bishops*. No one, therefore, holds the government of souls except Bishops and parish Presbyters, or their helpers bringing help to them, or those legitimately instituted or called by them.
