Now, no Angelic Spirit is permitted to do anything except what has been ordained by God, as Dionysius says in *Celestial Hierarchy*, chapter 3; and it has been ordained by God that no inferior should exercise the office of a superior, nor exert influence upon him, but should be content with his own office, as is said in the same chapter. It follows, then, that the regulars (whom blessed Dionysius calls Monks), so long as they remain in the order of those being perfected, which is the inferior order, can in no way exercise the office of the superiors\—that is, of the perfecters\—which is to purge, to illuminate, and to consummate, as is said in the same chapter; that is, the office of teaching, of preaching, and of administering the sacraments. And therefore it is said in chapter 6 of the *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* that *the monastic order ought not to be set over others*; or, according to another translation, *the monastic order is not for leading others*, namely through preaching and teaching. Whence ecclesiastical constitutions have issued on this point: that monks, no more than laymen, however much they may glory in the name of learning, cannot preach publicly. *16. q. 1. cap. Adijcimus*, with its concordances; although they may, through canonical election, be raised to the office of preaching, that is, to the episcopate or the priesthood, as is said in *16. q. 1. cap. Sic Viue*. And Gratian determines this sufficiently in *16. q. 1. §. Ecce sufficienter*. Nor is it likely that a mortal man is permitted to alter the aforesaid most sacred hierarchy; especially since Pope Leo says, *25. q. 1. cap. Quæ ad perpetuam*, that *those things which are generally instituted for perpetual usefulness ought to be varied by no change*. And so the Roman Pontiff cannot destroy what has been decreed by the Apostles and Prophets; otherwise he would be convicted of erring, as Pope Vrbanus says, *25. q. 1. cap. sunt quidam*. But how shall any mortal be able to alter, or even to revoke, the most sacred Ecclesiastical Hierarchy divinely ordained, since power has been given to none of the Prelates for destruction, but for edification? 2 Corinthians 10.
