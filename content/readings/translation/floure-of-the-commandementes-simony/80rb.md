We read in Matthew 10 how our Lord said, *Gratias date* (“Give freely”), which is expounded in two ways: either when a man sets no condition of sale, or when a man has regard in giving to nothing but God alone, or to grace alone. And therefore he who gives to his kinsman does not give in such a manner, even though God has said, *Gratis date*. Likewise, whoever would give to his kinsman without simony must see that the person be worthy of it, and that the gift be made purely for the love of God, as it is said, *Ut intret per ostium: id est Christum in ovile ovium* (“that he may enter by the door, that is, by Christ, into the sheepfold”). Our Lord Jesus Christ gave the dignity and government of the Church to Saint Peter, and not to Saint John nor to his other kinsmen, giving us the example that we should do likewise. So too, when the apostles chose Matthias and Joseph the Just, so that one of the two might be apostle in the place of Judas, *Sors cecidit super Mathiam* (“the lot fell upon Matthias”)—for Joseph was a kinsman of Jesus. We read in the second book of Kings, *Quia infidelis est episcopus qui nepotulo suo confert prelationem cum sciat enim inhabilem et sciat totum pondus prelii versum super prelatum.* That is to say: the bishop is unfaithful who confers prelacy or a dignity upon his little nephew, when it is so that he knows him to be too weak to fight, and that he knows that all the country and the brunt of the battle fall upon the prelate.
