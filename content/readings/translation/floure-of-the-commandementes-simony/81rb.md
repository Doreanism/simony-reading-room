A question, in order to understand whether an advocate may sell the knowledge by which he handles the just cause. The answer: he should not sell the knowledge, but he may sell the labor and toil of his body that he undertakes in studying, in pleading, and in defending the just cause. For God says in the gospel that the workman is worthy of his food and of his wages. Matthew 10: *Dignus est enim operarius cibo suo.* And Luke 10: *Dignus est enim operarius mercede sua.* But if the advocate were to sell the knowledge by way of sale, or had it in his mind with the intention to sell it, he would be a simoniac before God. It is written, Proverbs 23: *Veritatem eme et noli vendere sapientiam et doctrinam et intelligentiam, etc.* Buy the truth, and do not sell wisdom, doctrine, and understanding.

Likewise, knowledge that is sold does harm, as has been said; and if it is given, it grows and also multiplies; and if it is hidden, it serves no purpose. Hence: *Unde scientia quae data est crescit: crescere nescit si taceatur.* And one reads in Ecclesiasticus 20: *Sapientia absconsa est et thesaurus invisus: quae utilitas in utrisque. Sequitur: Melior est qui celat insipientiam suam homo qui abscondit sapientiam suam.* Wisdom hidden is a treasure in which a man does not see what profit there is in the one or in the other. Better is he who hides his folly than the man who hides his wisdom.
