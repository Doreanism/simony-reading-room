And Augustinus says: *Non debemus evangelizare ut vivam, sed vivere ut evangelizem.* We should not preach the gospel in order to live, but rather live in order to preach. Likewise we should preach so that the reward of our preaching is not the food we crave and desire, but the necessary thing that follows from it—that is, the maintenance in temporal goods that a preacher requires. As Augustinus also says: *Evangelizare debemus ut non sit merces predicationis cibus qui appetitur, sed necessarium quod sequitur, id est sustentatio in temporalibus que necessaria est predicatori.* So too those clerics who refrain from preaching, from teaching, from rebuking, and from correcting vices out of any favoritism, or out of false affection, or out of fear, or for any other useless reason—and who likewise keep silent about what they see and hear—the prophet Isaiah calls them dogs that will not bark, that is, that will not preach. As Isaiah says: *Canes muti non valentes latrare. Sequitur: Omnes in viam suam declinaverunt, unusquisque ad avaritiam suam, a summo usque ad novissimum.* A dog that does not bark to defend its master is no good to him; no more is a preacher to God who is mute in defending the truth. And therefore he is called a dog. And one reads in Jeremiah 4: *Sapientes sunt ut faciant mala, bene autem facere nescierunt.* They are wise to do evil, but not to do good. There is an example in the gospel of the man who hid his talent: he was called a wicked and slothful servant, and it was commanded that the talent be taken away from him.
