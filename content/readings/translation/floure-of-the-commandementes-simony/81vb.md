Hence Paul, Romans 1: *Non solum qui faciunt peccata: sed qui consentiunt facientibus digni sunt morte.* Not only those who commit the sins, but also those who consent to the doers, are worthy of death. These sin mortally; yet one should understand that the said advocates also commit simony, etc.

An example of how the devil carried away in body and in soul a cursed, covetous, thieving advocate. *Quere lxxxi.c.* Another example, of an insatiable advocate who pleaded a case for a man who did not bring him money to his liking: he wronged him, took the documents, tore up the papers, and trampled on them in contempt, saying, “Villain, there is your lawsuit, you who brought me nothing but this.” The good man, weeping, excused himself by his poverty. But, to be brief, soon afterward the advocate died wretchedly. Likewise, the master of a school should not sell knowledge, but only the labor that he expends with his body; and the same goes for others of his kind. *Quia omnis operarius dignus est mercede sua: ut dictum est.* Likewise, preachers should not sell the knowledge they utter in preaching with the ultimate aim of gaining temporal profit, or in like manner to win human favor or for vainglory and not for charity and the reconciliation of poor sinners; for in doing so they commit simony. But inasmuch as they have taken bodily pains to study, to preach, and to travel, they may accept what men and women give them for the support of their living. *Quia deus ordinavit his qui evangelium annuntiant de evangelio vivere, ut dicitur. Corinth.*
