He ought also to keep and defend the just cause so that it does not come to harm. And afterward he may take a fee for the labor of his body, as has been said, without exceeding the bounds of reason. Many do exceed them, demanding and taking salaries and fees outrageously, more than they ought, and more than they have earned. And therefore they ought to dread damnation, for they are not just and do not fulfill justice within themselves when they desire to have things they have not earned and which do not belong to them.

They ought also to be afraid if they have hated and broken justice, judgment, the good cause, and the good right in order to receive gifts; for they sin mortally and incur a curse. Hence, Isaiah 5: *Ve qui iustificatis impium pro muneribus: et iustitiam iusti aufertis ab eo.* They ought also to be afraid if they have no will to plead, to counsel, and to defend the causes of the poor as they do those of the rich, because the poor have no goods to give them. It is written in Ecclesiasticus: *In iudicando esto pupillis misericors ut pater et eris tu velut altissimi filius obediens et miserebitur tibi.* In judgment be merciful to orphans, like a father, and you shall be obedient; and as the son of the most high God, he shall have mercy on you. Likewise, if these advocates have made bad causes appear good through their tricks, deceits, and subtleties, and cause the good right to be lost, they sin and are unjust and corrupters of justice.
