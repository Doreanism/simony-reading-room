## Of the Manners of Simony That God Forbids

It is written in Acts 8: *Pecunia tua tecum sit in perditionem.* Saint Peter said to Simon Magus, “May your money perish with you.” These words may here be taken and likewise understood of all simoniacs, as has been said above; but one should understand that the sin of simony takes many forms according to the variety of people who improperly meddle in spiritual matters, as some particular cases will be set out hereafter.

First, a man who receives holy orders, or who enters into any benefice of the Church by giving or promising, whether by himself or through another, gold or silver or anything else, is suspended by written law, *Extra de simonia, Tanta, t.i.; ca. i, q., Reperiuntur*. And if in such a condition he then officiates at the divine mysteries, he is irregular and may not be absolved unless he resigns the benefice, or unless he has a dispensation from \[the pope\].
