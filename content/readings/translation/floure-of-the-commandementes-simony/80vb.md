It is also written: administer to the people, taking nothing for it, the Eucharist, that is, the sacrament of the altar; hear confession, taking nothing; baptize, taking nothing; give to all people for nothing, after the Apostle. *Unde augustinus dicit. Gratis eucharistiam plebi ministrate: gratis confitemini: gratis baptisate secundum apostolum: cunctis gratis date.*

When any priest sells or trades in masses, or sings a mass for money, and the final cause of his delight in it is that he may receive the said money, it is simony, and he sins mortally. *Unde augustinus. Qui precise unam missas vel plures per pecunijs celebrat damnationem sibi eternam preparat.* He who sings a mass, or many, for money prepares for himself eternal damnation. And it is to be noted here that temporal things should never be the end of spiritual things, though they may well be motivating causes. *Unde hec est falsa.* The priest is bound to give spiritual things so that he may obtain temporal things. *Sed hec est vera.* The layman is bound to give temporal things in return for spiritual things, not buying them—for they should not be sold as a man sells meat upon the stall. The grace of God that is in the sacrament is beyond price and does not fall under any price, for a man has nothing with which he might buy it.
