(x) *How is it relaxed?* etc. He holds the common opinion concerning absolution from non-reserved excommunication, drawn from ch. *Nuper* etc. *De sententia excommunicationis*. But he says the proof from that chapter is not conclusive as regards the force of the inference, because it does not simply follow: “the framer of the canon does not reserve, therefore he concedes.” It does not follow, I say, by the laws themselves, on the basis of the rule: “What is not granted is deemed to be prohibited.” And it is clear that the aforesaid reasoning does not hold universally in matters of jurisdiction, neither from the nature of the matter nor universally in law — which is what is required for the force of the inference. Not from the nature of the matter, because an inferior cannot dispense in the law of a superior, as the parish priest cannot in an episcopal statute, nor the bishop in a pontifical law; nor consequently can he by his own authority remove the effect of a law, or a penalty which exists by law, on his own authority. Nor does the same inference follow universally according to the laws, as is plain in the case of dispensations from laws and from irregularities; otherwise it would follow that a bishop could dispense in the prohibited degrees, for instance, for contracting matrimony, simply by the fact that dispensation is not prohibited.
