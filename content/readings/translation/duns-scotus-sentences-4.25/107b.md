And when it seems expedient to the Church that the prelates among them, on coming over to the Catholic communion, should not exercise their honors there, the very Sacraments of ordination are nevertheless not taken away from them, but remain upon them; and therefore hands are not laid upon them, lest injury be done not to the man but to the Sacrament itself. As in Baptism there is the right that it may be conferred through them, so in ordination there is the right of conferring — each indeed to their own ruin. But it is one thing not to have, another to have unto ruin, and another to have unto salvation.” The same: “Concerning those who are separated from the unity of the Church, there is now no question that they both have and can confer \[the Sacraments\]; but they have them unto ruin and confer them unto ruin, because they are outside the bond of peace. Injury must be done to neither Sacrament. As one does not rightly possess it because he has withdrawn from unity, yet he does possess it, and therefore it is not restored to him when he returns; so too one who has withdrawn from unity does not rightly confer it, yet he does confer it; and therefore, whoever receives from him, when he comes to unity, is not reordained.” The same: “It is one thing not to have something, another not to have it rightly, or to usurp it unlawfully. The Sacraments of Christ and of the Church are therefore not on that account not Sacraments, because not only heretics but even all the wicked use them unlawfully; but those persons are to be corrected and punished, while the Sacraments themselves are to be acknowledged and venerated.” Likewise Gregorius: “What you say, that one who has been ordained should be ordained again, is quite absurd.
