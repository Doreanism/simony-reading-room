### Concerning those who are ordained by force by heretics or simoniacs.

Likewise, when Pope Alexander decrees that “simoniacs are altogether to be condemned and deposed,” one must understand: unless someone has been compelled by force. For concerning such persons, and those ordained under violence by any heretics whatsoever, Innocentius says that they can have some color of excuse, provided they at once withdraw from them and renounce their detestable conventicle.

### Concerning the age of those to be ordained.

On the age of those to be ordained, Pope Nicolaus decreed thus: “The sacred Canons have established that a subdeacon should not be ordained before fourteen years of age, nor a deacon before twenty-five, nor a presbyter before thirty. Thereafter, if he be worthy, he may be elected to the episcopate.” We likewise command this to be observed in the same way. So too Fabianus: “If anyone has not completed his thirtieth year, let him on no account be ordained a presbyter, even though he be highly worthy”—because the Lord himself was baptized at thirty years of age, and so began to teach.

(End of the Master’s text.)
