(z) *It is relaxed by him who imposed the interdict, etc.* Here he also adduces the moderation of Boniface VIII in the matter of interdict, which is found in ch. *Alma mater* etc. *De sententia excommunicationis* in book 6. First, as regards the administration of the Sacrament of Penance during the time of interdict. Second, as regards the divine offices and Masses to be celebrated with closed doors, in a low voice, without the ringing of bells, in the place of the interdict. Third, concerning the suspension of the interdict on the feasts of the Lord’s Nativity, Easter, Pentecost, and the Assumption of the Most Blessed Virgin, as is contained in the constitution; and this is what the Doctor intends by the relaxation of the interdict in certain cases.

The penalty of those who violate the interdict, if they are clerics, is found in ch. *Is cui De sententia excommunicationis* in book 6, and ch. 1 of the same title, and ch. 1 *De sententia et re judicata*, etc. Whence the doctors gather that they incur irregularity if they celebrate Masses or the divine offices with solemnity, except in the cases granted, and if they administer the sacraments which are prohibited.

(a) *The sixth penalty is suspension, etc.* He defines suspension as a prohibition from something otherwise fitting, and this for a time, etc. It therefore does not take away the power, but the use of it, and this, he says, *for a time*, etc. By these words he signifies the distinction of this penalty from the others.
