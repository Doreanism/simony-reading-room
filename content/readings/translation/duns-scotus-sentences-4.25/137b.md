### Scholium.

That children and those lacking reason are dishonorably ordained; that children who have the use of reason are licitly ordained to minor orders but not to sacred orders; and that in both cases the ordination is valid, even if the recipient lacks the use of reason. D. Thomas, here *q. 1. art. 1. q. 2.* Bonaventure, *art. 2. q. 2.* Richard, *art. 4. q. 2.* Paludanus, *q. 3.* Gabriel, *q. 1. q. 2.* Sotus, *q. 1. art. 2. conc. 3.* Sylvester, *v. Ordo 4. q. 1.* Durandus here *q. 2.* holds that children before the use of reason are incapable of ordination.

Here (a) I say briefly that to be excluded from an order, or from the reception of orders, or to be unable to receive orders, can be understood in three ways: namely, not to be able to receive them duly and honorably; not to be able to receive them licitly, because it is contrary to a precept; or not to be able to receive them in any way at all, even in fact, so that if the attempt is made, nothing comes of it. In the first way, a child and one without discretion cannot receive orders, because he cannot receive with due reverence such a rank as is conferred in an order. In the second way, a child cannot receive a sacred order, although he can in that way receive the lower orders.
