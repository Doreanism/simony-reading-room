The Apostle suggests a twofold reason why a woman ought to remain silent in the Church: first, because she is subject to the man from creation, since she was made as a helper to him; and second, in punishment for sin, as appears in Genesis 3: “You shall be under the power of your husband, and he shall rule over you.” Augustine, in book 11 of *On Genesis according to the Letter*, chapter 37, \[says that\] by divine institution the woman is subject to the man, and therefore she cannot be capable of an eminent rank in the Church in which she would preside over a man, or publicly teach by virtue of office — from which passage Epiphanius also rightly attacks the aforesaid heresy. So too another passage which he cites, 1 Corinthians 14, proves the same: “Let women keep silent in the churches, for it is not permitted them to speak, but to be subject, as the law also says” (supply Genesis 3); “but if they wish to learn anything, let them ask their husbands at home.” See Ambrose and Anselm \[on this\]: what \[the Apostle\] said above, “I do not permit,” he here states as “it is not permitted,” to show that this is a matter of law. Now by law a priest, a deacon, and a bishop teach in the Church by virtue of office; therefore, since women cannot do these things by divine law, it follows that they are in no way capable of this eminence in the Church, either that by which they would preside over others, or that by which they could and ought to teach by law.

The twofold reason for this assigned by the Doctor is most fitting: \[first,\] because the entire sex could not be excluded from this rank without injury, if it were capable of it; and \[second,\] because such a rank in the law of the Gospel was conferred upon no woman, not even the most holy Mother, nor in the law of nature, nor under the Mosaic law, was there in women any power of offering sacrifice.
