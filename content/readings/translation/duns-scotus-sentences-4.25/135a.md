One suspended from a minor order is suspended from a major order, but not the reverse — Hostiensis on *cap. Dilectus filius, de tempor. ordin.*, with the common opinion. One suspended from minor orders cannot ascend to major orders; one suspended from conferring minor orders cannot confer major ones. Conversely, if suspended only from conferring major orders, he can still confer minor ones (the Abbot on *cap. final. de Apostat.* and others).

One suspended from entry into a church cannot exercise his order in it, nor hear divine offices in it (John Andreas, Geminianus, and the Ancharanus on *c. Is cui, de sentent. excommunic. in 6.*); he cannot be buried in it or in its cemetery unless absolved as a penitent. He may, however, receive all sacraments outside it. For more on these matters, consult the Doctors and the Summists.

### Scholium.

The last five penalties — namely, infamy, irregularity, excommunication, interdict, and suspension — do not invalidate the conferral of orders. As to degradation, if it concerns a bishop, the matter is doubtful, according to what was said in the preceding distinction, n. 6. For if one holds that \[the episcopate\] is not an order, it must consequently be said that the conferral of orders by a degraded bishop is null. The Doctor does not hold this view, however, as I noted there, because \[the episcopate\] is much more an order than are the porter and the lector.

Concerning (c) *the second principal article*, it is certain with respect to the last five penalties that they do not prohibit the conferral or reception of orders in fact, as is proved by Augustine, *1. q. 1. Quod quidam*. The reason is that with these penalties the character of minister and recipient, intention, and the other necessary conditions on either side can stand together.
