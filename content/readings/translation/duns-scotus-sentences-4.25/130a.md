## Commentary.

(u) *The fourth penalty is excommunication, etc.* See the Scholium, which summarizes the foundation of this teaching; for the number \[of censures\] see Toletus in his *Summa*, book 1, ch. 14 and following; Suárez, vol. 5, *On Censures*; Reginaldus, *In praxi*, book 9; Sayrus, *On Censures*, book 3; and Navarrus in his *Summa*, ch. 27; the Summists under the word *Excommunicatio*. It is inflicted on account of a mortal and grievous sin to which contumacy is joined, and the same holds for interdict and suspension. Council of Trent, sess. 25, ch. 3 *De reformatione*; ch. *Praesenti*, C. 5 q. 2; *Veritatis* etc. *De dolo et contumacia*; ch. 2 *De testibus cogendis*; ch. *Honoratus*, dist. 75; Augustine, *De correptione et gratia*, ch. 15; ch. *Nullus*, C. 11 q. 3. It is the common opinion: Abbas on the last chapter *De cohabitatione clericorum et mulierum*; Felinus on ch. *Apostolicae* etc. *De exceptione*. Therefore it is not to be inflicted for every sin, even mortal, but for a grave one to which contumacy is joined, as the Doctor says. And the reason he assigns is conclusive, because this power given to the Church is set forth in that passage to which the Doctor alludes, Matthew 18, and its exercise is declared to be directed against those who do not hear the Church, who are contumacious, as St. Thomas teaches in *Sentences* IV, dist. 18, q. 2, art. 1, quaestiuncula 3. Our Castro, *On Penal Law*, book 2, conclusion 4; Covarruvias on ch. *Alma mater*; Navarrus, *Summa*, ch. 27, nn. 9 and 11; Soto, *In* IV, dist. 22, q. 1, art. 2, and *On Justice and Right*, book 5, q. 6, art. 2; Suárez, disp. 4, sect. 5.
