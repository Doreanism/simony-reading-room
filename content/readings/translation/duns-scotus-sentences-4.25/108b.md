For after they have been cut off and condemned by the judgment of the Church, they assert that the right of ordaining and consecrating is taken away from them, just as from those who have been degraded or excommunicated. Others, however, say that the Sacraments celebrated by heretics and by those cut off according to the form of the Church are true and valid, because in withdrawing from the Church they have not lost the right of conferring and consecrating; and those who are ordained in this way by heretics, when they return, are not to be ordained again. But those things which are done by heretics otherwise than in the Church are false and empty; and those who appear to be ordained by such persons receive not a gift but a wound. And it is according to this distinction that the Doctors speak variously about these matters. Some, however, say that the same Sacraments can be celebrated by heretics who have been cut off as by Catholics, if the form of the Church is observed by them, and that the Sacraments celebrated by them are true and valid in themselves, but as to their effect are false and empty, both in those who handle them wrongly and in those who wrongly receive them; therefore they are called vain and false, because they do not bestow what they promise and are believed to confer; they are also called damnable, because for those who unlawfully give or receive them they are unto judgment; they are also called polluted, not in themselves, but on account of the unworthy handling by the heretics. Hence Gregorius calls the communion of Arius an execration, and Innocentius calls the ordination of Bonosius a damnation, not that they are so in themselves, but because they make those who wrongly give or receive them such; just as Hieronymus also calls their sacrifices the bread of mourning,
