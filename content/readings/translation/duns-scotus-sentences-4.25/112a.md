How is this (d) punishment inflicted? I reply: never by the law itself, but precisely as inflicted by a judge. The form, however, according to which it is to be inflicted, is found in *Extra, de poenis*, c. *Degradatio*, and is today in the sixth book. The causes (e) on account of which it is to be inflicted are enormous crimes, such as heresy, schism, and revealing the confessional. On the first, see *Extra, de haereticis, Ad abolendam*; on the second, *Extra, de schismaticis, Ad scindendos*, in the sixth book; on the third, *Extra, de poenitentiis et remissionibus, Omnis utriusque sexus*.

In what way (f) is this punishment remitted? I reply: only by restitution to the whole, by him who can restore—which is set down as the Pope alone—although one legitimately deposed is not, as a rule, read of as afterwards restored.

The second (g) punishment is infamy, which is a state of injured dignity as to life and morals, just as good reputation (*fama*) is a state of unimpaired dignity as to life and morals. This injury is incurred in different ways. Where it must be observed that to be infamous (*infamis*) and to be defamed (*infamatus*) are not the same. The defamed person is one who is publicly accused of some crime.
