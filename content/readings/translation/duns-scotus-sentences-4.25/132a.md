(a) *The sixth penalty is suspension*, which is a prohibition from something otherwise fitting, and this for a time; in this respect this penalty is specifically distinguished from the others. (b) This penalty, moreover, is manifold, because one is a suspension from office; another from benefice; another from entering the church; and further, it is sometimes incurred *ipso jure*, as is found *Extra, De sententia et re judicata: Cum aeterni*, and *Extra, De sententia excommunicationis: Cum medicinalis*. And also in receiving orders from a bishop who has renounced such a place and dignity, because he who does not have the exercise \[of office\] does not confer the exercise; and so if he confers the order, nevertheless the recipient does not have the exercise, and therefore he is suspended. On this see C. 1 q. 7 *Daibertum*, and *Extra, De ordinatis ab episcopo qui renuntiavit*, ch. 1, in the gloss. Likewise concerning one knowingly ordained simoniacally, as has been said above; frequently, however, it is inflicted by a judge, now one form, now another. It is removed by the relaxation of such a suspension made by him who imposed it, or by his superior.

## Commentary.

(y) *The fifth penalty is interdict, etc.* He defines interdict as a restriction from exercising certain ecclesiastical acts, or from attending those acts. It is called a restriction, or prohibition, or privation, which is common to all penalties.
