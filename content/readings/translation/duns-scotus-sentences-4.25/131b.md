## Scholium.

He defines interdict, divides it, and posits its efficient and removing cause. For it is sometimes imposed and removed by law, sometimes by a human authority. He likewise defines and divides, and posits in the same way, the effective and destructive causes of suspension. All things are clear and settled by express laws. See the Summists, under *Interdictum* and under *Suspensio*.

(y) *The fifth penalty is interdict*, which is a restriction from exercising certain ecclesiastical acts, or from attending certain such acts. And although this penalty can sometimes be inflicted on a place and not on a person, sometimes on a person and not on a place, sometimes on both; and just as it is inflicted, so is it to be observed — nevertheless, insofar as it is a penalty distinct from the others, it is more frequently inflicted on a place and not on persons, except while they are in the place: namely, that in such a place it is not lawful to celebrate the divine offices solemnly, nor for others to be present.

And this penalty of interdict is sometimes contracted from the law itself, sometimes from a judge. An example from the law itself is found concerning those who harbor foreign usurers, *Extra, De usurariis: Usurarum voraginem*, and is today in book 6.
