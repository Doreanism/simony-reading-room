A case is brought to the public forum by denunciation and accusation with judicial process instituted; but when the crime cannot be proved, it falls back to the same state in which it was before, with respect to the faculty of dispensing, because his state is not changed, as some would have it, especially when full and semi-full proof is lacking.

The Doctor speaks of voluntary homicide in this sense when he says that voluntary homicide is never wiped away except by Baptism, because below at dist. 33, q. 2 he supposes that it is removed by dispensation, just as bigamy also remains after Baptism, as he teaches there. Indeed even with regard to the Supreme Pontiff this dispensation is reckoned impossible in the sense that it is exceedingly difficult to grant, and not to be granted except for the most weighty causes. Whence the Council of Trent cited wished to signify this very thing in session 14, ch. 7, saying: “he may at no time be promoted to sacred Orders, etc.; he is perpetually deprived of every Order, etc.” See *cap. Miror*, dist. 50; *cap. Inquisitionis* etc., *De accusationibus*; *cap. Non ab homine* etc., *De iudiciis*.

To voluntary homicide in this way is reduced homicide which is sometimes indirectly willed by presumption of the forum: for example, if someone wounded or struck another, not intending to kill or mutilate him, but death or mutilation followed, he incurs the irregularity of voluntary homicide by presumption of the forum, which is founded on the deed; nevertheless, in conscience he is not a voluntary homicide in this way, because the truth of the matter according to conscience is what is regarded — and the will of the one striking, which can be inferred from circumstances and from his own confession,
