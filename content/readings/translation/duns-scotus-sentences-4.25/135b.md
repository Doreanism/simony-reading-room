As for the first penalty, namely degradation — especially if it concerns a bishop — it is doubtful whether it precludes the conferral of orders in fact; but the arguments for and against are given in the preceding question, article 2.

*To the arguments.* To the first, the concession may be granted as to the first five penalties; but if one holds, with respect to the sixth penalty, that a bishop who has been simply deposed is not a bishop, then in that case the parallel in the inference must be denied, because the character of the priesthood cannot be taken away, and consequently neither can the priesthood, as the episcopate can.

To the other: intention does not suffice unless the minister is competent, which a deposed minister is not according to that opinion.

To the third: it is not parallel, because penance is a general sacrament, and so no one is excluded from it, either in fact or by law. The other sacraments, which include something of excellence, may rightly be denied to someone on account of an offense that calls for it.

As to the argument from heresy, it is true that heresy does not exclude in fact, but it does exclude by law, even if such a person is not condemned.
