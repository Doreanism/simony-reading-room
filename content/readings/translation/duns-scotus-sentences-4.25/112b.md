But sometimes he has not been convicted of this in court, and then he is infamous from the Law only, and not from a judge; sometimes he has been convicted and punished by the judge with such a penalty, and then he is infamous in both ways, both from the Law and from a judge. From these things the second point is plain, namely how infamy is contracted: the first kind of infamy is contracted from the imputation of a defamatory crime; the second is incurred from the Law or from a judge punishing such a crime with such a penalty.

But (h) what is the cause on account of which infamy is incurred in the second way? I reply: C. 6, q. 1, *Infames*: “We say that all are infamous whom the secular laws call infamous”; therefore those secular laws which punish a crime with infamy are canonical; and those laws are found in *Digest, de his qui notantur infamia*, throughout.

What (i) is the remedy against this punishment? I reply: against infamy in the first way—when someone is defamed—it is canonical purgation, as is held in *Extra, de purgatione canonica, Ex tuarum*; for that purgation wholly removes that infamy or defamation. Against infamy in the second or third way, which are nearly the same, though from different sources—the Law and a judge—there is no remedy except restitution to the whole by him who can so restore, *Extra, de testibus, Testimonium*, in the gloss *Arcetur*.
