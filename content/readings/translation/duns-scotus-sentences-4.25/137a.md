## Question 2

*Whether female sex or childhood age impedes the reception of orders?*

D. Thomas, *in addit. q. 39. art. 1. et 2.*, and here *q. 1. art. 2.* D. Bonaventure, *art. 2. per totum*. Richard, *art. 3. et 4. per totum*. Vasquez, *3. part. d. 245. c. 2.* and *disp. 246. cap. 1.* Bellarmine, *de Sacram. Ordin.*, last chapter. Coccius, *tom. 2.*

Secondly I ask whether female sex or childhood age impedes the reception of orders. That it does not: at Galatians 3, *In Christ Jesus there is neither slave nor free, neither male nor female*; therefore there is no difference between woman and man in the law of Christ, just as there is none between slave and free. Therefore the sacrament of the evangelical law that a man can receive, a woman can also receive, just as slave and free can.

Furthermore, at distinction 32, *Presbyter*, mention is made of a presbyteress, and at *27 q. 1*, that a deaconess ought not be ordained before forty years of age.

As to childhood age, it is argued likewise: it does not prohibit the reception of other sacraments, such as Baptism and Confirmation. And it seems most to the point with respect to Confirmation, since that sacrament is given for worthily confessing the faith of Christ — an act that cannot belong save to an adult, just as neither can the act of ordination. Therefore, etc.
