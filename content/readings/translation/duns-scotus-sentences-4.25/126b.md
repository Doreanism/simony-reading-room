## Commentary.

(f) *There follow also two further cases, etc.* He treats of the irregularity arising from homicide and bigamy; he says, moreover, that it occurs sometimes from fault, sometimes without fault. The distinction of this category from the preceding rests on this: although mutilation of oneself can occur with fault, as he supposed above, yet because it rarely occurs, and in moral matters account is taken of what occurs frequently, he therefore in the general division reduces it to the irregularity arising from indecency and deformity, from which it primarily results, although fault itself is also punished in the mutilation of oneself in part or in a whole member.

But this irregularity, which arises from a defect of the Sacrament or of its signification, as in bigamy, or from a defect of leniency, as in homicide, is frequently accompanied by sin; sometimes also without sin — which is enough for the present distinction, which is accidental and not substantial.
