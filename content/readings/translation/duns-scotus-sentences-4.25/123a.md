A simoniac (p), however, is not punished so gravely in respect of a benefice; yet from the moment it is established that he obtained it simoniacally, he is bound to resign it, and for the whole time during which he holds it, he holds it unjustly, and he is bound to make restitution of everything he has received. These points are proved from various chapters *de Simonia*.

Concerning theft in an order, I distinguish: for either it is forbidden under excommunication that anyone approach unless he be of that bishopric, and unless he has first been lawfully examined and received; or there is no such excommunication or prohibition. In either case, one who furtively intrudes himself is irregular, but in the first case indispensably, except by the Pope; in the second case dispensably by the bishop. This is proved by *Extra, de eo qui furtive Ordines suscepit*, in the first and second chapters, in the text and in the gloss.

Concerning the third crime, that irregularity follows upon it, this is had in *Extra, de Clerico non ordinato ministrante*, throughout. Concerning the fourth, this is had in *Extra, de Clerico excommunicato vel deposito ministrante*, throughout. And understand this of major excommunication, because, as is had in the same place, chapter *Si licet*, one who celebrates while under minor excommunication sins gravely, yet incurs no mark of irregularity. Understand also: after he has known himself to be excommunicated.
