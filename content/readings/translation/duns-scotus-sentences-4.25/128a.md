Although therefore by presumption of the forum he is held to be a voluntary homicide from deliberate purpose — because the will is presumed from the deed itself — yet in the forum of conscience it is to be judged otherwise. Although he is irregular by reason of the offense in both forums, nevertheless he is not a homicide from deliberate purpose or intention, which is required for voluntary homicide, provided he employed restraint conformable to his intention; nor could or ought he, from the nature of the action, have foreseen the death or mutilation that would follow. Otherwise in both forums he would be a voluntary homicide, because that intention would be feigned when sufficient caution is not adjoined, or the manner whereby, from the nature of the action, the effect of death or mutilation which follows from the case is to be guarded against.

The Doctor further adds that voluntary homicide in a qualified sense induces irregularity, as when it is done in self-defense (*inculpata tutela*). This was true at the time when he wrote, under the old law, but afterwards this irregularity was abolished by the unique Clementine, *De homicidio*, etc. This homicide is called by the canonists “homicide from necessity”: the Gloss on *cap. De his*, dist. 50; the Archdeacon and the Provost there; the Gloss on *cap. Sicut dignum*, *De homicidio*; the Council of Trent, sess. 14, ch. 7. They divide this necessity into avoidable, which does not excuse from irregularity, and unavoidable, which does excuse — for example, if one could escape by maiming, then by killing he would become irregular and would sin; likewise if the defense passes over into aggression, he is not excused, because in that excess there is no longer defense, nor is it necessary for escaping death; nevertheless one who kills even in that case is not reckoned a voluntary homicide in the first mode. Whence the irregularity which is incurred from such homicide is reduced by some to that which arises from accidental homicide.
