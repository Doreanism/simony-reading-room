But before that papal declaration, no special prohibition in that case is recorded, nor any express reservation of the power, beyond that alone which arose by force of the law as emanating from a higher authority. Similar is that of Trent, sess. 7, *De sacramentis in genere*, canon 13, where it is denied that bishops or other prelates of the Church have the power to change or innovate the common rites of the Church for administering the sacraments. See the doctors on ch. *Et si Clerici* etc. *De judiciis*, etc., with the Gloss. The Pope’s reasoning therefore in ch. *Nuper, De sententia excommunicationis* is to be understood with reference to absolution from excommunication, because this is granted, or presumed to be granted, from the usage and practice of the Church, whose best fittingness is that which the Doctor hints at below, in the section *Comparando* etc., concerning the ease of remission or absolution from excommunication: therefore, he says, it is more easily remitted, because it is the most serious and excludes from more things; on account, therefore, of the peril of souls, and because absolution ought not to be delayed when the offender makes satisfaction and disposes himself (ch. *Qua fronte* etc. *De appellationibus*), it is therefore by the intention of the Church to be removed; and this power flows to the ordinary prelates, not from their own authority, but from the indulgence of the Church and of the Pope. The power of dispensing in a law or in other penalties has a different rationale from this.
