But that such a person illicitly receives the order — unless ignorance excuses him — is certain from the common opinion of the theologians: he sins mortally, since he transgresses a prohibition of the Church in a most grave matter and sacrilegiously receives a sacrament. This is also evident from the severity of the penalty and of the canons. The Doctor further adds that it is doubtful whether a degraded bishop validly confers orders, on account of the controversy we explained above (preceding distinction, sole question, article 1), where we defended the affirmative side, and elsewhere concerning the minister of the order, in explaining the definition in article 2. I said the same of the episcopate as of the other orders, with the exception of first tonsure — though some hold that even this is validly conferred by a degraded bishop, on the ground that the Church is not found to have annulled his conferral of it. But on the view that tonsure is not an order, and that the power of conferring it belongs to the Church, it must rather be said, looking to the rigor of the law, that the conferral is altogether invalid, since one who is degraded loses the office and the jurisdiction of conferring it. But if a case can be presented in which such a tonsure is not repeated by the Church, it must be said that it becomes valid by ratification, which retroacts upon the act, and not otherwise by virtue of the first conferral by the degraded \[bishop\].

By this the response to the first and second arguments is clear. The response to the third is clear from the text. The response to the fourth is clear from what has been said: that ordination is valid even if performed by a bishop who has been cut off and condemned. The remaining responses are clear from the text and from what has been said in reply to the last argument, where he takes the law of nature broadly so as to comprise its perfection, whether natural or supernatural, and the mode of action of the First Cause impressing the character as indelible — a character that is not subject to moral corruption nor to natural corruption by a created cause.
