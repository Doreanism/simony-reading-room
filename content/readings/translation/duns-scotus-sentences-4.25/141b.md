### Commentary.

(c) *This “cannot” in the third sense, however*, etc. The Kataphryges or Montanists, the Quintillianists, the Pepuzians—who are the same in Epiphanius, *Haer.* 49—used to ordain women as deaconesses of the altar, indeed even as priests, as Augustine reports in his book *On Heresies*, c. 27. As for the Pepuzians, who are so called from the village in which Montanus, Quintilla, and Priscilla are said to have been born, false-prophetesses, they are also called Quintillianists and Priscillianists. The Kataphryges \[are named\] from Phrygia; the Artotyrites from the bread and cheese which they used in their mysteries.

The conclusion proposed is of faith; hence the Fathers cited hold it as of faith with respect to the order of the diaconate, the presbyterate, and the episcopate, condemning the contrary as heresy. The universal tradition of the Church teaches the same, never extending this power to women.
