He also says *from certain acts*, etc., because certain acts are excepted, such as conferring baptism and chrism, penance, viaticum; extreme unction, burial are prohibited, and according to some, matrimony, and orders if they are to be conferred on laymen, and the divine offices. This penalty differs from excommunication as regards the acts of which it deprives, in that excommunication is a privation of these by reason of communion. Interdict, on the other hand, deprives directly of the use of those acts, and *per se* insofar as they are sacred things, and independently of the communion of the members, which excommunication regards. It differs from suspension in that suspension deprives of the active use, and with respect to particular persons, namely ecclesiastical ones. Interdict comprises both uses, and also in common, that is, the use of those things which are common to people and clergy.

He divides interdict into local, personal, and mixed. If it is directed at a place, it will be local; if at a person, personal; either can be general or particular, whose difference and nature can be gathered from the form of the interdict. And because it belongs to the nature of a penalty that it should not include any except those to whom it is directed, then attention must be paid to the propriety of the words, and to what is signified by the subject to which the penalty is directed; for if the clergy is interdicted, the people are not included, nor if the people, is the clergy included, nor even others who are not members of the people, but the penalty includes only all and each of that body.
