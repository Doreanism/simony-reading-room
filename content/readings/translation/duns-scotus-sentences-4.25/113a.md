The third (l) punishment is irregularity; and this is an unfitness for the reception and execution of the acts of Orders. This (m) is frequently incurred from the Law, although it can sometimes be inflicted by a judge in accordance with the Laws. It is incurred (n) by the Law from certain crimes, and from certain non-crimes, and from certain things which sometimes can be crimes and sometimes not. From crimes (o), four pertaining to Orders or to their acts: one of these is simony; another is theft committed in an Order; the third is exercising ministry in an Order not received; the fourth is contumacy in not observing ecclesiastical penalties.

Simony can occur either in an Order or in a benefice. If in an Order and knowingly—that is, such that the ordained person knows himself to be ordained simoniacally—then the Pope alone dispenses: *Extra, de simonia, Nobis*, and C. 1, q. 1, as was argued for the opposite side; for since the legislator does not altogether annul their consecration, to him alone does it belong to release. But if he is ignorant by invincible ignorance—as, for instance, when someone else, of whom he is wholly unaware, has given a price that he might be ordained—he does not become irregular, unless, after the matter has been made evident to him, he exercises the act of the Order so received. Hence after he becomes aware of the Order so received, he is suspended from the act of that Order, until he is freed from such suspension.
