As for the third species, which is accidental homicide, the doctrine which the Doctor sets forth on this is the common one. See *cap. Dilectae*, etc., with the three following, *cap. Joannes*, *cap. ultimo*, *De homicidio*, where Doctors and Theologians treat it in this distinction and on 1a 2ae, q. 73, and 2a 2ae, q. 64, art. 8; Covarruvias, on the Clementine *Si furiosus*, part 2; Navarrus, ch. 27. Here is chiefly blamed the negligence in avoiding homicide and its danger. Concerning the irregularity arising from homicide in these three modes, general rules and particular cases are framed by the Doctors in detail, which occur throughout in the Summists; I therefore pass them over here for the sake of brevity, since it is enough to say some things in general for the clarification of the text, since an entire treatise cannot be set out without prolixity. There is, moreover, another irregularity of homicide which is not from offense, but from a defect of leniency or of the signification which is called sacramental — as when a judge kills evildoers according to the penalty of the law. On all these matters see Suárez, *De censuris*, disp. 44 and following, where he treats this material at length. As for general rules and particular cases concerning the irregularity arising from bigamy, the Doctor treats this below at dist. 33, q. 2, which we shall briefly explain there.

## Scholium.

Excommunication is by Law and by man, but it cannot be inflicted except for a grave mortal sin accompanied by contumacy. This is the common view, and the first part is found at C. 11, q. 3, *cap. Nemo Episcoporum*, and Leo teaches it in epistle 89. The same applies to suspension and interdict, as Suárez holds, vol. 5, disp. 4, sect. 4, against Navarrus, ch. 27, n. 165, and others. The second part, concerning contumacy, is held by Thomas Aquinas, *In 4 Sent.*, dist. 18, q. 2, art. 1, quaestiuncula 3; Soto, book 5, *De iustitia*, q. 6, art. 2; Navarrus, ch. 27, nn. 9 and 11; Covarruvias, on *cap.
