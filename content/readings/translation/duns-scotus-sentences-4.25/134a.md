But if perpetual suspension is imposed, it has more the character of deposition than of suspension; or at any rate it does not differ greatly from deposition or from the other perpetual penalties. And if someone wishes to allow perpetual suspension along with the others, then the Doctor must be understood as noting a difference that for the most part belongs to suspension, and to it alone — which is sufficient in a moral matter. For the other penalties do not limit their time by their nature: excommunication and interdict last as long as their cause lasts, and are not lifted except by absolution or relaxation upon satisfaction being made. Irregularity and infamy by judicial sentence are likewise perpetual by their nature, as are deposition and degradation, and they do not cease of themselves but by dispensation and restoration.
