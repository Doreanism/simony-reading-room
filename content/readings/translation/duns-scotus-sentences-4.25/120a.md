Likewise in causa 41, question 3, chapter *Si quis Episcopus*, where the penalty of irregularity is decreed against a bishop, presbyter, or deacon who, while excommunicate, has presumed to make oblation, or the morning and evening sacrifice, or to act in his office as before: *”It shall not be lawful for him to have hope of reconciliation in council, nor to be gathered back further,”* etc. In these words is not understood reconciliation from excommunication, since this is not denied to one who repents, but dispensation from irregularity, as the common opinion with the Gloss holds; the same is canon 4 of Antioch and in the council of Pope Martin, canon 37. Likewise the chapter *Theagualdum*, under the same title, where in a council Pope Nicholas, having deposed the bishops of Cologne and Trier from every ecclesiastical dignity, established the same penalty: *”We define,”* he says, *”that if, in accordance with their previous custom, they dare as bishops to touch anything of the sacred mystery, in no way shall it be lawful for them to have, in any other synod, hope of restitution or place for satisfaction.”* This sentence, although here pronounced by a judge according to the canons, is also understood of irregularity.
