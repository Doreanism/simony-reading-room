This number is required only for the pronouncing of sentence; once it is pronounced, the bishop or metropolitan can proceed to execution by real degradation, as Panormitanus teaches in chapter *Qualiter* 3, *de Accusationibus*. See c. *Si quis*, c. *Felix*, c. *Si autem*, C. 15 q. 7.

Most recently, the Council of Trent, session 13, *de reformatione*, c. 4, decreed that for the degradation of a presbyter and of the others of lower rank, other persons may be substituted, when the number of bishops prescribed by the canons cannot be had. To verbal deposition the bishop’s vicar general may proceed; but to real deposition the bishop alone, as is also decreed there.

(e) *The causes, however, on account of which it is to be inflicted*, etc. He enumerates certain causes for which it is to be inflicted by the canons; but in the third case real degradation from the clerical state is not understood, because the text does not include this, since it says that one deposed from the priestly rank is to be thrust into a monastery to do perpetual penance. Penalties are to be restricted; he intends, therefore, degradation from order, and verbal \[degradation\], as is simple and perpetual deposition, not relative to actual and real degradation.
