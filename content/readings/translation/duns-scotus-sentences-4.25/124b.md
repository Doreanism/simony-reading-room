### Commentary.

(q) *There follow also irregularities from three non-crimes*, etc. From the condition of servitude, as opposed to freedom, irregularity arises, so long as the slave is bound to a master, that is, subject to servitude. This is clear from distinction 54, from the whole title *de servis non ordinandis*, from the *Authentica Si servus, C. de Episcopis et Clericis*, and from the common opinion of theologians on this distinction. The reason which the Doctor assigns is from the first epistle of Pope Leo, chapter 16, found at chapter *Generalis*, and chapter 1. One ordained without his master’s knowledge and against his will, if he is reclaimed, must be returned, unless he is a priest, chapter *Ex antiquis*; but he must restore his peculium. This irregularity is removed by manumission, or by whatever means the slave is restored to freedom.

(r) *There follows also illegitimacy*, etc. This irregularity is clear from the text cited, and in the *Sext*, chapter *Consuluit*, etc., according to the gloss, *de servis non ordinandis*; chapter *Per venerabilem*, etc., *Qui filii sint legitimi*; and distinction 56, through many texts at the beginning.
