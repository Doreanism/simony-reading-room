Likewise, grave slander against a bishop, c. *Si quis Sacerdotum*, C. 11 q. 1. Although the gloss on c. *Ad abolendam* says that, beyond the three cases of heresy, falsification of papal letters, and reviling of a bishop, \[clerics\] are not usually handed over to the secular court, Panormitanus, on c. *Novimus*, says that for a heinous and pernicious crime degradation can take place; and this seems to be presupposed by Trent and by practice. Akin to this penalty of degradation, as regards the effect of deprivation of clerical privilege, is the penalty of a *diffidatus* (outlawed) cleric, in c. *Pro humani, de homicidio*, in the Sixth, as Geminianus teaches on the same passage; for such a one is outside all ecclesiastical protection, and \[the penalty\] has the same effect that degradation and handing over of the person to secular judgment have — indeed more, since one who is *diffidatus* can sometimes be killed even by a private person.

(f) *In what manner is this penalty remitted?* etc. This resolution is common, and is evident from D. 50 cc. 1 and 2. \[The deposed person\] is restored by the solemn handing back of all the instruments and other things which were taken away by degradation. The degraded man, moreover, is not relieved of the burdens of sacred orders, since he remains unfit for marriage, and is bound to the divine office, because the penalty takes away honors but not burdens.
