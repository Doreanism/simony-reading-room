*To the arguments on the opposite side.* To the first, I concede that the ordination of a simoniac is invalid as to its exercise; nevertheless he does receive the order, and therefore he needs only that his suspension be lifted.

To the second: the inference does not hold, because it has been given to the minister of the Church to be a minister in conferring, not in taking away.

To the third — it will be touched on below — how the Church can make persons illegitimate and thereby disqualify them, so that they are not competent to contract marriage. But here, once the character has been conferred, there is no comparable power to take it away from them. And when it is argued that the former \[marriage\] is more according to the law of nature than the latter, this is true insofar as a marriage once contracted is valid, but not insofar as any and all persons, no impediment standing in the way, may contract that marriage. For it is more in accord with the law of nature that what God impresses upon nature, where it is not opposed to sin either formally or as a demeritorious cause, should remain perpetually in the soul, than that any and all persons should be capable of contracting marriage. Therefore, some disqualification of persons for this act is not so contrary to the law of nature as it would be for a character once impressed upon someone to be erased.
