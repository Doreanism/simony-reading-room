Cyprianus too says that whatever heretics do is carnal, vain, and sacrilegious, and that their “altars are false, their priesthoods unlawful, and their sacrifices sacrilegious,” who “after the manner of apes, which, though they are not men, imitate the human form, lay claim to the face and authority of the Catholic Church, although they themselves are not in the Church”; and although they are sacrilegious, they administer the priesthood and set up an altar, although no oblation can there be sacrificed where the Holy Spirit is not present; nor can the Lord profit anyone through the prayers and entreaties of one who has violated God himself. Hieronymus likewise asserts that “everything offered by heretics is defiled in the sight of the Lord; for although these things may appear holy in their outward form, yet because they have been touched by one who is polluted, all are polluted.” Again: “God hates the sacrifices of heretics and casts them away from himself; and as often as they gather together in his name, he detests their stench and closes his nostrils.” Again: “They offer sacrilegious bread,” and so forth.
