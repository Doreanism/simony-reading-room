Third, he says that one who kills by accident, if he is engaged in some unlawful act, becomes irregular. Understand this to mean: if the act in which he is engaged is forbidden because of the danger of homicide, but not otherwise. So Thomas Aquinas, 2a 2ae, q. 64, art. 8, where Cajetan, Sylvester under the word *homicidium*, 2, § 2; Angelus, n. 1; Suárez, vol. 5, disp. 45, sect. 6, citing many; Covarruvias, *De homicidio*, part 2, § 4, n. 9-10; Herrera, *In 2 Sent.*, dist. 32, q. 15; Emanuel, *Summa*, ch. 166; Soto, *De iustitia*, q. 1, art. 9. Of these, Thomas and others hold this teaching without any limitation; and it is found in *cap. Dilectus*, *cap. Joannes*, *cap. fin., De homicidio*; likewise *cap. Continebatur*, in the same title. The opposite opinion is highly probable: Henríquez, book 4, ch. 11, § 11, citing many; Salón on 2a 2ae, q. 64, art. 8; Báñez, and others. Fourth, he says that the irregularity arising from voluntary homicide is not removed except by Baptism — understand: unless a dispensation supervenes, as he explains below at dist. 33, q. 2 at the end. For certain other irregularities are removed in other ways, as when an infirmity ceases, or childhood, or ignorance.

There follow (f) also two further cases, which sometimes can occur with fault and sometimes without fault, namely homicide and bigamy. Under homicide is included the active and serious mutilation of another human being; on this matter see the *Extra* title *De homicidio voluntario et involuntario*, and dist. 55, *cap. ultim.*; dist. 50, *cap. Miror*, and this on account of the horror of shed blood. Whence God also did not allow David to build a house for his name, because much blood had been shed (2 Samuel 7). But on this matter distinguish between voluntary and involuntary homicide.
