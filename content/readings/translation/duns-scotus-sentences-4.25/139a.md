The conclusion is clear against Durandus and Paludanus by the Doctor’s reasoning, because for receiving the power of an order it is not required that one be able to exercise that act at once, or that an act of the power accompany the power itself. For an order is a grade disposing one to ministry; therefore the grade can be present and communicated before someone is fit to exercise the act. By a similar reason, in *cap. Majores, de Baptismo*, the Pontiff teaches, with the common view, that the virtues are infused in baptism into infants as to habit, though not as to use until adult age; therefore in the same way the power of an order can be present as a habit, though its use is impeded during the state of infancy, because in this there is no repugnance on the side of the thing. It is also confirmed that this happens with respect to the divine institution, because no consent is required, as is plain in the minor orders, in which too there is handed over a power ordered to an act to be exercised. And it is proved *a posteriori* in *cap. de Clerico per saltum promoto*, etc., where Innocent III commands that someone who received minor orders before the years of discretion, and was afterwards ordained a priest without first having been a deacon, be ordained deacon. He commands, I say, that he be ordained deacon; he does not command that the minor orders be conferred on him again; therefore he presupposes that such ordination was valid before the years of discretion.

Second, the consent of an infant is required for the effect of ordination neither as matter, nor as form, nor as a condition—even speaking of consent in himself or of consent residing in the will of the parents; therefore he is validly ordained.
