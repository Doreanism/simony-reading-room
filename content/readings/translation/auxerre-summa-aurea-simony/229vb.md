Again, if that chancellor gives it gratis, the one who receives it gratis ought to receive it gratis; but what is received gratis ought to be given gratis, and he commits simony if he accepts money from the boys whom he teaches. To this certain masters say that there is simony here, and although knowledge is not properly a spiritual thing, nevertheless it is in some way spiritual: for it is a perfection of the soul, which is spiritual, and is a gift of God, and is necessary for having the virtues; nor is it permitted to accept anything from boys for the teaching, but for the labor — just as a hired priest accepts payment for his labor in order to have a livelihood. But against this is what was said above, that a spiritual thing is that by which one has the Holy Spirit; yet when one has obtained the license in the liberal arts, or has been granted the power of teaching, the Holy Spirit is not had — this is self-evident; therefore such knowledge, or the power of teaching such knowledge, is neither a spiritual thing nor annexed to a spiritual thing; therefore to sell or buy it is not simoniacal. Again, Proverbs 23 \[Prov. 23:23\]: “Buy truth, and do not sell wisdom.” Here the sale of wisdom is forbidden, and not of a spiritual thing — for a spiritual thing is rather to be sold and put out at interest \[cf. Matt. 25:27\]. Solomon therefore is speaking in this authoritative text of material sale; therefore when he says, “Buy truth,” etc., by this the buying of truth is enjoined as corresponding to the sale that is forbidden; therefore it is permitted to buy truth, or the knowledge of truth, by a material purchase; and thus what it is permitted to buy it is permitted to sell, and to buy or sell the knowledge of truth is not simoniacal. But on the contrary: he says in that authoritative text, “Do not sell wisdom and instruction and understanding” — wisdom, which concerns the liberal arts; understanding, that is, prudence concerning matters open to consideration; therefore it is a sin to sell the teaching of grammar, logic, and so on for the rest — but it is only the sin of simony; and to sell knowledge of this kind is simoniacal. Again, Augustinus in his book *On the Master* \[*De magistro*\] says that other masters, distinct from the one Master, propose signs (that is, propositions); and once the meanings of these signs are known, the hearers consult the inner Master (that is, reason) as to whether the proposition put forward is true or false, and \[reason\] judges according to what it sees there. It is plain therefore that the knowledge of truth comes from the inner Master; for the inner Master has the knowledge of truth immediately from the truest Master, who is God. And this is what he says in the Gospel: “The Holy Spirit will teach you all truth” \[John 16:13\]. The knowledge of truth therefore is immediately from the gift of God; and this is plain from what he says in Exodus 25 about Bezalel: “Whatever a craftsman can devise, God has placed in his heart” \[cf. Exod. 31:3, 35:31\]. And the Interlinear Gloss says that it is a gift of God; for if the craftsman’s knowledge, which is mechanical, is a gift of God, much more is the knowledge of the liberal arts a gift of God. But it was on this account that Simon sinned, because he thought the gift of God might be possessed with money, as blessed Peter says in Acts 8 \[Acts 8:20\]; and the sin of simony is to sell or buy such teaching. Again, if it is not simoniacal, it is asked why not: when such knowledge is had, the Holy Spirit is not had. He proves that this is no obstacle: for the power of working miracles is had without the Holy Spirit, and the power of prophesying, as appeared in Balaam, and the power of speaking with tongues. For it is better to prophesy than to speak with tongues \[cf. 1 Cor. 14:5\]; yet it is simoniacal to buy or sell powers of this kind, and this because such a power is immediately a gift of God. And since the knowledge of the liberal arts is immediately a gift of God, it is simony to buy or sell it. Again, it is not permitted to a judge to sell his judgment, nor to a witness his testimony; yet it is permitted to an advocate to sell his just advocacy. But why?
