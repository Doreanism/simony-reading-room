Likewise, it is permitted to give temporal things to this end, that one may have the Holy Spirit. This is proved by what Gregorius says: “Meanwhile, do whatever good you can, that God may illuminate your heart unto penitence.” Therefore it is permitted to give temporal goods to the poor, that God may illuminate one’s heart by infusing the Holy Spirit. Therefore it is permitted to give temporal things for the Holy Spirit; but nothing is so spiritual as the Holy Spirit, nor more beyond price. Therefore, much more strongly is it permitted to give temporal goods for the spiritual goods which the priest gives, and thus to buy the sacraments of the Church. Solution. To the first we say that this argument does not hold: “It is permitted to buy spiritual goods that are eternal; therefore it is permitted to buy the spiritual goods that the priest gives.” And this is because eternal goods fall under merit, but the first grace, which is given through the sacraments of the Church, does not fall under merit \[since\] otherwise it would not be grace unless it were given without merit. The kingdom of heaven, however, is for sale, and it is bought with God’s coin, namely by the works of God informed by charity. But the first grace cannot be bought, since one who does not have the first grace has no coin of God’s, but only the coin of the devil, namely the coin of sin; or if he has some good works of a sort, these are purely temporal and do not reach to grace, which is above time. To the second objection we say that this proposition is false: “It is permitted to give temporal things to religious men, that they may pray for us.” For prayer cannot be the immediate end of the giving of temporal things; but when they give temporal things to certain persons for God’s sake, they incite the recipients to friendship, and from friendship they are moved to pray. Hence it is clear that prayer is not the immediate end of the giving of temporal things, but rather friendship, or the inciting to friendship, when the giving of temporal things is rightly done. For Cicero says that friendship properly exists only among the good, since in the wicked, insofar as they are such, there is nothing lovable. Hence the Lord says in the Gospel of Luke, “Make for yourselves friends of the mammon of iniquity”\[Luke 16:9\]—that is, give to the poor for God’s sake, so that they may be incited to friendship, and out of friendship be bound to pray for you. It is clear, therefore, that prayer is not the immediate end of the giving of temporal things; indeed, just as the giving of spiritual things ought to be gratuitous, so the giving of temporal things ought to be gratuitous. Hence Augustinus says concerning the Apostles: “They received gratuitous bread from those to whom they offered gratuitous preaching.” For such ought to be the account of giving and receiving, of which the Apostle speaks to the Philippians, saying: “No church communicated with me in the account of giving and receiving, except you alone”\[Phil. 4:15\]. To the third objection we say that the case is not similar, since to give temporal things to God in order to have the Holy Spirit is to incite God to friendship, because this is to give temporal things to the poor for God’s sake; but to give temporal things to a man taken in himself is not to incite him to friendship. From what has been said it is clear that it is not permitted to give temporal things for trental Masses, or for thirty Masses to be sung. Hence it is forbidden in the synods that priests receive anything before they have sung those Masses. But however much giving is giving, and however much receiving is receiving; therefore, just as it is unlawful to receive temporal things before the Masses are sung, so also after. By the same reason that it is forbidden to receive beforehand, by the same reason it ought to be forbidden afterwards as well. Likewise, it is permitted to give temporal things to religious men that they may pray for us, because it is permitted to “make for ourselves friends of the mammon of iniquity,” as has been said. Therefore it is permitted to give to priests even before they sing. Likewise, as has been said, it is permitted to give certain revenues to some church for the celebration of an anniversary. Therefore it is permitted to give temporal things to priests, not only for Masses to be sung, or after Masses have been sung, but also for Masses to be sung, and even before they sing. Solution. To the first we say that neither before nor after is it permitted to receive temporal things for Masses. Hence temporal things are not to be given to priests even after Masses have been sung for those very Masses. But because “the laborer is worthy of his hire”\[Luke 10:7\], so that there may be food in the Lord’s house, it is forbidden to receive beforehand, but not afterwards, on account of the appearance of evil and to avoid scandal.
