But on the contrary: an advocate is said to be for both parties, since it is not lawful to mix anything false into his pleading; therefore he is for both parties, and yet it is lawful for him to sell his advocacy. It seems therefore by this reasoning that it is lawful for a witness to sell his testimony. \[But on the contrary,\] it is not lawful to sell known truth, nor is it likewise lawful for a judge to sell his understanding, even though such knowledge is merely about vineyards and meadows. If therefore it is simoniacal to sell such knowledge, much more is it simoniacal to buy or sell knowledge of the liberal arts. Again, the soul is better than infinite worlds, if there were such; therefore its perfection too, namely knowledge, is better than infinite worlds, if there were such; therefore it is beyond price; therefore he who sells or buys it places what is beyond price under a price; therefore he commits simony. And this we concede on account of the aforesaid reasons. To the first objection we reply that knowledge is spiritual, and when it is possessed, the Holy Spirit is possessed. But the Holy Spirit is possessed in two ways: in one way as illuminating the intellect, and he is possessed when knowledge is possessed; in another way as inflaming the affections, and thus he is possessed through the sacraments of the Church. To the second objection we reply that truth or the knowledge of truth is to be bought — not with money, but in the way which Solomon teaches in Proverbs 4 \[Prov. 4:7\], saying, “The beginning of wisdom, get wisdom, and with all thy possession purchase prudence.” As if he were to say: if you wish to have wisdom, take it; for sometimes it offers itself. Hence in Wisdom 6 \[Wisd. 6:15\]: “He shall find her sitting at his doors”; and when you have her in your possession, acquire her more and more, for she is bought and acquired through wisdom itself. Hence in the Gospel \[Matt. 13:46\]: “Having found one precious pearl, he sold all that he had and bought it.” Hence in Proverbs \[Prov. 17:16\]: “What doth it profit a fool to have riches, seeing he cannot buy wisdom?” It is clear that he does not speak of the buying that is done with money, but of the spiritual buying by which a fool, remaining in his folly, cannot buy wisdom — and this is so because he does not have it. We therefore concede that to buy or sell any kind of knowledge is simoniacal; but according to the diverse degrees of the nobility of the sciences, the simony is lesser or greater. We say therefore that it is not lawful for an advocate to sell his knowledge or his counsel. Likewise a judge and a witness, if they travel to other regions, may receive their expenses; but for their judgment and testimony nothing may be received.

## Whether the tithe can be simoniacal.

## Another principal question.

It is asked next whether ecclesiastical tithes can be the matter of simony. It seems that they cannot, because the right of receiving tithes is purely earthly, since that which underlies it is by nature purely earthly. For the right of receiving the produce of some house is purely earthly. Since therefore the right of receiving tithes is purely earthly, it can be priced under an earthly price; therefore if laymen buy the right of receiving tithes, or clerics sell it to laymen, no simony is committed. Again, knights have the right of receiving a *particampus* \[a share of the field\]; and the *particampus* is sometimes a tithe of the sheaves, namely either one in eleven or one in twelve, according to the diverse customs of diverse regions; and that right is purely earthly, since only what is earthly underlies it. By the same reasoning the right of receiving the tithed sheaf is purely earthly, and so it is not simoniacal for laymen to buy tithes. But on the contrary: the right of receiving tithes is annexed to a spiritual thing, that is, to holy orders, since only clerics have the right of receiving tithes; but simony is the will to buy or sell a spiritual thing, or one annexed to a spiritual thing; therefore it is simoniacal for laymen to sell or buy tithes.
