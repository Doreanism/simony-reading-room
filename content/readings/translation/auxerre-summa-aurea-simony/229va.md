For many indiscreet priests are scandalized by it and commit simony. Just as it is simony to sell the burial of the dead, so it is simony to sell blessed ground dedicated for the burial of the dead. Hence Ephron is censured, who sold to Abraham the field in which lay the double sepulchre in which the dead were buried; and as a sign of this censure his name was changed. Hence, whereas at first he was called Ephron, afterwards he was called Ephraim, by which, as Hieronymus says, it is signified that one who could sell the memorials of the dead was not of perfect virtue. It seems therefore that he committed the sin of simony. But on the contrary: everything he sold is temporal; it is therefore neither spiritual nor annexed to a spiritual thing; therefore he did not commit simony. Again, if he committed simony, then he sold what should not be sold; therefore Abraham bought what should not be bought, and committed simony in buying, just as Ephron did in selling. Again, Abraham seems to have sinned more than Ephron, because Ephron wished to give him the field gratis for burying his dead, and Abraham was unwilling to accept it except for a price; therefore he scandalized him, and so seems to have sinned more than Ephron. To this some say that Abraham bought a certain large field in which lay that great cave in which the dead were buried. Hence he did not sin, because that cave passed with the whole property — just as when someone buys an estate to which is annexed the right of patronage of some church, he does not commit simony, because the right of patronage passes with the whole property. But by the same reasoning Ephron did not sin in selling the field etc., and with it the cave passed. Why then was his name changed? To this Prepositinus used to say that one does not read that this field was consecrated for burial, nor was that cave blessed or consecrated for this purpose; but the ancient fathers had themselves buried in that double cave hoping that they would rise again with the Son of God. And that cave was called “double” because it had two chambers — one upper, in which the men were buried, and another lower, in which the women. Hence that ground was not blessed, nor did Abraham sin by buying nor Ephron by selling. Yet his name was changed because he descended from a state of perfection to a state of imperfection: for at first he wished to give what belongs to the perfect, and afterwards he sold it at the highest price. Hence at first he was called Ephron, that is, “consecrated and perfect,” and afterwards Ephraim, that is, “infirm and imperfect.” The same is the reason for the change of name according to another solution, which says that the cave passed with the whole property. Now just as it is simony to sell blessed ground — not on account of the ground, but on account of the blessing which is spiritual — so simony is found in certain very small matters. Hence it is simony to sell blessed bread and blessed water, for these are spiritual and wipe away many venial sins.

## On the sale of knowledge.

## Another principal question.

But concerning other very small matters there is the question whether it is simony, as when a chancellor sells the schools, or the power of teaching. It seems that it is. The chancellorship is an ecclesiastical dignity, hence spiritual, and the power of teaching is annexed to it; and if a bishop were to sell that chancellorship he would commit simony. Therefore the chancellor received the power of teaching gratis, and he ought to give it gratis; therefore if he sells it he commits simony.
