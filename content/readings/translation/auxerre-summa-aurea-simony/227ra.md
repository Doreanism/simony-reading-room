Therefore the Apostle’s preaching, which was undertaken for the sake of temporal goods, is spiritual; and therefore no preaching is earthly. Again: “the words that I speak are spirit and life.” Therefore, on this account, all preaching is spiritual and none is earthly. Perhaps someone will say that it is not lawful to preach principally for the sake of a temporal good, but it is lawful to do so secondarily. Therefore, since preaching is more spiritual than the giving of a prebend, it is lawful to give a prebend for a temporal good not principally but secondarily — as when a bishop gives some prebend to a good man principally so that he may serve that church, and secondarily gives it to him so that he himself may give a hundred pounds to some monastery, from which the monks who serve God there may be supported. Therefore to give a prebend in this way is not a sin. Again, the point is proved by Augustinus, who says that different wills have different ends; but if those wills are referred to that End which is God, then those wills and actions are upright. Now in this kind of action — that is, in this giving of a prebend — all the ends are referred to God, for it is good that the man himself serve in that church for God’s sake. Again, it is good that he should give a hundred pounds for the support of poor monks, so that they may serve God more freely and have leisure for contemplation. Therefore whatever is in that giving is referred to the supreme End; therefore that giving is good. Therefore to give a prebend for a temporal good is not only lawful but even good — which is manifestly false. Solution. One must not preach for the sake of a temporal good, because this preposition “for” denotes a first, principal, and immediate cause. For Augustinus does not say “we ought to evangelize in order that we may eat,” but “to eat in order that we may evangelize,” so that food is not the first thing sought, but is added to that which is principally sought. For he who ploughs ought, as the Apostle says, to plough in hope, not for hope, as the gloss says. The principal end of preaching ought in no way to be temporal things; but the consequent or secondary end may be. For someone may preach principally in order to move men to works of piety; and from this kind of end there follows the direction and, upon request, the giving of temporal goods. Hence it is permitted to a preacher preaching in this way to have regard for temporal goods as for a consequent end. And in this way it is permitted to the preachers of the hospital to preach about temporal goods — so that by this preposition “for” a consequent and secondary cause is denoted. To the first objection, then, we say that the case of preaching is not like that of prayer, for some prayer is earthly, but no preaching is earthly. To the second objection we say that the Apostle did not preach for a temporal good in such a way that a principal, immediate, and proper cause was denoted; but he preached for a temporal good in such a way that a consequent and secondary end was denoted — one that moved to piety, in which the likeness of God consists. The case of the giving of a prebend, however, is not the same, because from the works of piety to which men are moved by preaching there follows directly and properly the giving of temporal goods. Hence it is in no way lawful to give a prebend for a temporal good, since a temporal good cannot enter into the giving of a prebend either as the principal end or as a consequent and secondary end. If, therefore, it enters in there as a price, then it is placed under that pact which is attached to a spiritual thing, and so simony is committed. Nor is it true that all those acts of will which are at work in the giving of a prebend on the condition that the recipient should give a hundred marks to some monastery are referred to God; for charity and every virtue shrinks from a prebend given for a temporal good as for a price. Hence this cannot be done with a good end. Hence the referral of actions of which Augustinus teaches cannot take place here. And thus the solution to all the objections is plain.
