And through the gift of prophecy and the power of working miracles the Holy Spirit is had for doing certain excellent works. Therefore only these four are properly speaking spiritual: virtue, sacrament, the gift of prophecy, and the power of working miracles. But the right of receiving tithes is not properly spiritual but attached to the spiritual, namely to the sacrament of orders: for a bishop, insofar as he is a bishop, has the right of receiving tithes.

To the first, then, we say that a secular judge cannot commit simony by selling, but he can by buying: for he cannot give the Holy Spirit, since he can give neither virtues nor sacraments, and thus neither can he sell. The solution to that argument is therefore clear: for the justice which the secular judge dispenses is not a spiritual thing — to restore my cloak to me is not a spiritual thing.

To the second we say that there is a thing unsalable by law but not in fact, and there is something unsalable both by law and in fact. Unsalable both by law and in fact is virtue, which ought not to be sold; and thus it is unsalable in fact, for God, whose part it is to give it, cannot sell it. Unsalable by law but not in fact is the sacrament: for it ought not to be sold, yet it can be sold, because God gives the sacrament through the ministry of the priest; whence the priest can sell it in fact but not by right. And in fact there are the chalice and such things, and these are neither spiritual nor attached to the spiritual: for what is attached to the spiritual, properly speaking, is what is in that in which the spiritual is. And there are certain sacred possessions, and on this account, if it should be necessary to sell them to laymen, they ought to be brought back into the common holdings of the church, or they can be sold under the same form.

To the third objection we say that, although he redeems his own right, he nevertheless does not buy anything, because he is not making something his own out of what is not his own. To the last objection, the solution is the common one: that it is lawful for an advocate to sell the right of pleading on account of the labor attached to it, because he must run through the case and plead it. It is not so, however, with a judge, and for this reason the cases are not parallel.

## In what things simony has its being. The second chapter

Concerning the second chapter, it is asked about the spiritual things in which simony has its being. And this is divided into three. For there is something which is spiritual only. There is something which is only attached to the spiritual. Both spiritual and attached to the spiritual is preaching: for preaching is spiritual, because through it the Holy Spirit is given, and it is attached to the spiritual — for it is attached to orders, since it belongs properly to priests to preach, or at least to those in orders. Spiritual things are the sacraments of the church, which it is not lawful to buy or sell. Attached only to the spiritual is the right of receiving tithes, the funeral rites of the dead, prebends, and similar things.

Concerning the first case, he lays down the rule that it is not lawful to preach for the sake of temporal gain, vainglory, or human favor. For there is a threefold gift, as blessed Gregorius says, namely the gift of the tongue, the gift of the hand, and the gift of the heart. If anyone preaches for the sake of any of these, he commits simony.

But it seems that it is lawful to preach for temporal things, because it is lawful to pray for temporal things: for the Church prays thus, “vouchsafe to give and preserve the fruits of the earth,” etc. Therefore it is lawful to preach for temporal things, for prayer is spiritual, as is preaching. Likewise, the Apostle preached to the Corinthians for temporal things, namely that he might take up collections for the holy poor who were in Jerusalem; therefore it is lawful to preach for temporal things. But if so, then it is lawful to give a prebend for temporal things, for preaching is more than giving a prebend, since through preaching the Holy Spirit is given, but through the giving of a prebend He is not given.

Perhaps one will say to this that preaching is twofold and prayer is twofold: for there is a certain spiritual prayer by which we ask that the name of God be sanctified in us, and other spiritual things; and a certain earthly prayer by which we ask for temporal things. Similarly, there is a certain spiritual preaching, and this it is not lawful to perform for temporal things; and a certain earthly preaching, which it is lawful to perform for temporal things.
