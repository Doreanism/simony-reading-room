But if a priest had nothing on which to live, it would be permitted for him to receive temporal things given for God’s sake even before he sang those Masses; and thus he would be incited to friendship, and thus bound to celebrate. To the second objection we say that the case of religious men and that of priests is not similar, since in the former there is no appearance of evil; and besides, religious men know better the account of giving and receiving than priests do. To the third we say that it is the general custom of the Church that before the faithful die they make their testament and their bequest, and in that bequest some assign revenues to some church for the celebration of the anniversary day of their death; and this is not an appearance of evil. And for this reason the case is not similar to that of what is given to priests before they sing Masses.

## Whether it is permitted to give anything for baptism

## The third principal question.

It is asked afterwards whether it is permitted to buy or to give temporal things for baptism and the nuptial blessing. It seems that it is. For let it be the case that some women bring a little child laboring in extremis to some priest that he may baptize him, and he is unwilling to baptize him unless they give him money. It seems that in this case they ought to give it to him, because unless they give it the child will die and be damned. Likewise, it is permitted to each person to redeem his own right; therefore it is permitted to give money in that case, that the child may be baptized. If it be said that a simoniac priest is to be reckoned as a madman, and thus as nothing, and that therefore the women themselves in that case ought to baptize that little child, since they have no priest—On the contrary: suppose that they cannot obtain water from a priest; it seems that then they ought to give money to the priest for the baptism. The same objection arises concerning those whose marriage has been so far promulgated, and nothing remains except that they should have the nuptial blessing, and the priest is unwilling to give it unless they give him money. It seems that it is permitted for them to give, since they themselves cannot bless. But on the contrary: if they give money for the sacraments, they buy the sacraments; therefore they commit simony. Solution. To the first case, concerning baptism, we say that in that case the women can baptize; but if they do not have water, it is permitted for them to give money to the priest, not for the baptism, but for the removal of the madness or obstacle on account of which they do not have what is owed to the child. To the second case we say that one must have recourse to a superior; and if the superior is unwilling to bless them without money, two ways remain: one, that they abstain, and this is the counsel of the perfect; the second, that if they are unwilling to abstain, they give money to the priest not for the blessing, but for the removal of the obstacle on account of which they do not have what is owed to them. But according to this it seems that if there are three good clerics, religious men, in some diocese, and their bishop is unwilling to give them prebends without money, it would be permitted to them to give money in order to remove the obstacle on account of which they do not have what is theirs; for the prebends of a diocese are owed to good men who are of that same diocese. To this we say that the case is not similar, because the prebend is not owed determinately to this man or that, but baptism is owed to this one, and the nuptial blessing is owed to this one. Yet where the custom is that one who ministers spiritual things may receive temporal things, after the spiritual things have been given the priest can ask for temporal things, because “the laborer is worthy of his hire”\[Luke 10:7\].
