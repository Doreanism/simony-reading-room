To sing as a layman who has expertise from outside and yet sings for vainglory is foolish, or without savor and fullness, and he understands not one thing — chiefly, by grace, that the song is in savor when one sings out of charity, with understanding bowed down to its meaning. As I have just expounded: “He who sings does not understand,” that is, his understanding is not directed by anything to the holy concord — except by him who says “to sing wisely is to sing in holiness,” namely “I will sing, sing, sing in conscience” — that is, first, he who sings does not understand: he does not open his heart to understanding; and so by their action they are like one who has not yet come to understanding, who knows not the signified \[meaning\] but trembles in unsteady thought. “Understand,” that is, choose in your heart; and he who chooses in his heart \[over\] singing-as-a-fool does not equally exult — rather, one ought to choose to sing as one subject, not by grace from the perfect Lord. So, even if “if one does not understand,” that is, “if one does not choose in heart” — by which one does the work of God for reason and for evil rather than for grace.

## Whether One Who Does the Work of God Fraudulently Falls Under the Curse

It is asked, then, whether one who does the work of God fraudulently — bodily or purely spiritually — performs it, or whether nonetheless what he does is not divine but a human work. Or rather: it is the work of God by grace, yet nonetheless the work of God may be done fraudulently, and in that case — in common parlance — \[such a man\] is to be cursed. Likewise to do \[so\], or to be in such a habit of indignation by grace, both at the beginning and end have, in confession, simple supposition; or to do the work of God carnally, by man, or with bodily food, is by grace honest in our regard — namely that what is done is done by grace, that is, by reason; or every truly simple supposition, on account of what perishes by grace, is well done — namely, this is to do the work. But when it is said “nonetheless the work of God and the work of God” — and this by grace is supposed thus, since what most often is done as the work of God fraudulently — \[such a one\] does not give what he ought to give, or does fraud against his lord, who forbids that what is owed to be referred to the use of his lord be turned to a venal use, and \[so\] he uses God’s benefice fraudulently. Thus, if by grace he turns the good which he owes to refer to the lord aside to his own uses, this is held to be transferred to a present indignation; and \[fraud is committed\] from the heart, from the hand, and from the body — in four ways one does the work of God so that it is well thought of, with praise, or to be shrunk from, or well spoken of: from the hand, when he does not contrive an unjust profit — that is, when he does the work of God for gain, doing the work of God fraudulently. Indeed they say all things \[are not\] by which you go through, and the good works of God are not — namely in avarice it frees nature greatly by grace. From those things written by them: of the other two transactions it is well said by your own that “good,” for such a profit, in that one celebrates for emolument the firstfruits of the earth to be given, and so on. And by the same one finds what the Lord commanded — that the priests give of God, not the sons — \[namely\] that the godly should pray for the godly by grace, and they vow to prophesy.
