It is Saint Luder’s rents, Saint Alban’s lands, Saint Edmund’s right, Saint Peter’s patrimony, say they, and none of ours. Likewise, if a man die in another man’s parish, besides that he must pay at home a mortuary for forgotten tithes, he must there pay also the best possession he has there—whether it be a horse worth twenty pounds, or however good he may be, or a chain of gold worth a hundred marks, or five hundred pounds, if it so happen. It is very much, truly, for so little labor taken in confession and in administering the sacraments. The beadrolls \[lists of the dead to be prayed for\]. Likewise the chrisom \[fee for the cloth used at baptism\], churchings \[fees for the rite purifying women after childbirth\], banns, weddings, offerings at weddings, offerings at burials, offerings to images, offerings of wax and lights—all come to their profit, besides the superstitious waste of wax in torches and tapers throughout the land. Then there are brotherhoods and pardoners. What do they also get by confession? Yes, and many enjoin penance: to give a certain sum to have so many masses said, and to desire to provide a chaplain for themselves. Soul masses, diriges \[the Office for the Dead\], month minds, year minds \[commemorations a month and a year after death\], All Souls’ Day, and trentals \[sets of thirty masses for the dead\]. The mother church and the high altar must have something in every testament.
