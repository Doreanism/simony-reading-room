Offerings at priests’ first masses. Likewise, when a man is professed into any religious order whatsoever, he must bring something. The hallowing—or rather conjuring—of churches, chapels, altars, super-altars, chalices, vestments, and bells. Then book, bell, candlestick, organs, chalice, vestments, copes, altar cloths, surplices, towels, basins, ewers, ship \[the incense boat\], censer, and every kind of ornament must be provided for them free of charge; they will not give a mite toward it. Last of all, what swarms of begging friars there are. The parson shears, the vicar shaves, the parish priest polls, the friar scrapes, and the pardoner pares; we lack only a butcher to flay off the skin.

What do they gain in their spiritual law (as they call it) in a year, at the Arches \[the Court of Arches\] and in every diocese? What do the commissaries and officials, with their summoners and apparitors, gain by their bawdry in a year? Will you not find plenty of curates who, to flatter the commissaries and officials with everything they can—so that they may get off scot-free themselves—will open up to them the confessions of the richest in their parishes? These men they cite privately and charge in secret. If the accused wish to know who their accusers are, “No,” they say, “the matter is well enough known—and to more people than you are aware of.
