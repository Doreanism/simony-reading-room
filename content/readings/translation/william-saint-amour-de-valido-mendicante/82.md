Likewise, Chrysostomus on the Epistle to the Hebrews: *”If anyone asks for sustenance, do not examine him on these matters. If Abraham had been a scrutinizer of the poor, he would never have received the angels as guests, but along with the rest would have driven these away also.”* *Dist. 42, Can. Quiescamus*; therefore alms are to be denied to no one.

Likewise, *1 q. 2 cap. Quam pio*, Bonifacius says: *”We nowhere read that the Lord’s disciples, or those converted through their ministry, prompted anyone to the worship of God by the intervention of any gift; unless perhaps someone awkwardly raises the matter of the food given to the poor, to none of whom, of whatever profession, were victuals denied.”* Therefore even to the able-bodied mendicant alms are not to be denied.

Likewise, *11 q. 3 cap. Quoniam multos*, Gregorius says: *”If anyone wishes to give something to the excommunicated, not in support of their pride, but for the sake of humility, we do not forbid it.”* Therefore it will also be lawful to give to the able-bodied mendicant.

Likewise, Hieronymus, *Ad Paulinum Monachum*, where he has: *”Whomever you perceive to be speaking always, or frequently, about trifles (except for almsgiving, which can be given indifferently to all), regard him rather as a peddler than as a monk.”* Therefore almsgiving can be given indifferently to all; therefore also to the able-bodied.

Likewise, Augustinus, in his book on the Psalms (referred to in *23 q. 4 cap. Duo ista*), where he says: *”When we speak these two names, MAN, SINNER, they are not spoken in vain; because he is a SINNER, rebuke him; because he is a MAN, have mercy on him.”* And so almsgiving is not to be despised, which is bestowed upon any poor person by way of human kindness.

*On the contrary:* Luke 16 \[*recte* Luke 14\]: *”When you make a dinner or a supper, do not call your friends, nor your brothers, nor your kinsmen, nor your rich neighbors, lest perhaps they also invite you in return, and a recompense be made to you; but when you make a feast, call the poor, the weak, the lame, the blind, and you shall be blessed, because they have nothing to repay you.”* Therefore, when from a poor able-bodied person, or from one belonging to the household of the powerful, you can expect something in the present, alms are not to be given to him.
