*As to the objection* concerning those poor who were in Jerusalem, for whom collections were made: we reply that it is not said there that they themselves asked, and they are to be understood either as those who were Disciples, or Prelates, and had sent their preachers to the Gentiles; or as those who, beyond what they could obtain by labor, suffered want and need. 2 Thess. 3: *But you, brethren, be not weary in well-doing* — Gloss: to the poor — because, even if they work, they may yet stand in need of some things; for he is not held blameworthy who is humane in giving; but rather this one, who, though able to bear labor, wishes to lead a life in idleness. Or perhaps all those were preaching, and could not obtain support from their fellow tribesmen to whom they were preaching; indeed, those rather persecuted them — just as Paul, while preaching to the Corinthians, received from other Churches on account of the Corinthians’ avarice. 2 Cor. 11: *I have stripped other Churches, receiving wages of them for your ministry* — Gloss: that I might preach to you, and I received nothing from any of you, even when I was in need.

*As to that:* *Let him that is instructed in the word communicate to him that instructeth him* — it is to be said that this is true when the one catechizing has Apostolic authority.

*As to that:* alms must be given to an excommunicate — it is true, in extreme necessity.

*As to what is asked* concerning preachers, whether they may ask: I believe not, lest it seem to be an occasion of avarice (1 Thess. 2), and lest it seem to be gain-seeking (2 Cor. 4: *Nor adulterating the word of God* — Gloss: preaching for gain); or lest they be compelled to flatter, as was said; or even to disparage (Rom. ult.: *Such serve not Christ the Lord, but their own belly* — Gloss: they flatter some, they disparage others, that they may fill their belly); or lest they seem to commit simony, if they receive under the pretext of preaching — especially those who are not Apostles, nor hold their place — most of all since one must abstain not only from evil, but from *every appearance of evil*. 1 Thess. 5.
