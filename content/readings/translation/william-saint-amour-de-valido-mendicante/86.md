Therefore, if a Friar of the one who preached, even after the preaching, asks something of those to whom he preached, he commits simony; therefore nothing should be given to him.

Likewise, according to human law, the able-bodied mendicant, if he is of servile condition, becomes the slave of the one who exposes him; if he is free, he becomes his perpetual tenant. *Cod. De mendicantibus validis, Lege vnica*. Therefore, since it is certain that what is the more severely punished is a grave offense (24. q. 1. ca. Non afferamus stateras dolosas), it follows that the able-bodied mendicant grievously offends; therefore he who knowingly places himself in such a state seems not to be in the state of those to be saved.

*I answer without prejudice:* *To the first, distinguish:* Of those who ask alms, some are known, some unknown; some ask as if by right, such as preachers and prelates; others ask for the sustenance of life. If he asks by right and as it were by reason of his office, then if his office or authority is established, he is to be received; if he is unknown, he is to be examined as to whether it is so, as Chrysostomus says on the Epistle to the Hebrews, and as is found in dist. 42. can. Quiescamus. If you ask only for nourishment, then it should be given indiscriminately, unless, being secure in his food, he neglects justice, as Augustinus says at 5. q. 5. cap. Non omnis — for then nothing should be given to him, unless he is already dying of hunger; dist. 86. can. 21. *Feed him who is dying of hunger, for if you have not fed him, you have killed those whom by feeding you could have saved.* But he who begs when he can live by the labor of his body neglects justice, because he acts against the doctrine of the Apostle, and ought to be excommunicated, as was said above. Therefore nothing should be given to such a person.

All those authorities, therefore, which say that something should be given to every poor person who asks, understand thus: it is true — either the thing asked for, or something better, namely, correction, if he asks unjustly. And this the Gloss says on Matt. 5: *Give to him who asks of thee* — even if not what you ask, yet something better, when you have corrected the one asking unjustly. But it is clear that he asks unjustly and contrary to the precept of the Apostle who can have his living elsewhere without sin, as was proved above; therefore nothing should be given to him, but rather he should be corrected.
