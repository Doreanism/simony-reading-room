Likewise, Ephesians 1 \[*recte* 4\]: *Let each one labor with his own hands*, not in the wicked works of gain, but by doing what is good; *that he may have*, not only the means by which to live, but also the means *by which to give to him who suffers need*. Therefore one must labor, not beg.

Likewise, Hieronymus, *To Rusticus the Monk*: *If the apostles, having the power to live from the Gospel, nevertheless labored with their own hands so as not to burden anyone, and offered refreshments to others from whom they ought to have reaped carnal things in return for spiritual things, why do you not prepare anything to provide for your own needs? Either weave a little basket of rushes, or plait a hamper of pliant withies; let the soil be hoed, let the little beds be divided by an even boundary, in which, when the seeds of vegetables have been sown, or the plants set in order, water may be led in to irrigate them.* And a little later: *Let nets also be woven for catching fish, let books be copied out, so that both the hand may earn its food and the mind may be filled with reading; every idle man is full of desires*. *De consecratione, dist. 5, c. Numquam*. Therefore every member of a religious order ought to labor, especially if he has no other means of living; therefore if he does not, he acts unjustly.

Likewise, blessed Benedictus, in the *Rule*, *c. De opere manuum quotidiano*: *Idleness is the enemy of the soul, and therefore at fixed hours the brethren ought to be occupied in manual labor, and at other fixed hours in divine reading*. Therefore a member of a religious order ought to work; therefore if he does not, he acts unjustly.

Likewise, in the same place: *Then are they truly monks, if they live by the labor of their hands, as did our fathers and the apostles*. Therefore if they do not do this, they are not true monks; therefore nothing ought to be given to them.

Likewise, from the Fourth Council of Carthage, *can. 51*: *A cleric, however learned in the word of God, should seek his livelihood by a craft*, as is contained in *dist. 91, c. Clericus*. Therefore if he does not do so, he acts unjustly.

Likewise, in the same place: *All clerics who are able-bodied for work should learn both crafts and letters*. Therefore if they do not, they act unjustly; therefore nothing ought to be given to them.

Likewise, John 11 \[*recte* John 21\]: *Simon Peter says to them, I am going fishing*. Gloss: *After Christ was restored alive, after they had received the breath of his inspiration, after his words,* As the Father has sent me, even so I send you, *the apostles suddenly became what they had been before, namely fishermen — not of men, but of fish. But it must be noted that they were not forbidden to seek their livelihood by their lawful craft, the integrity of their apostolate being preserved, since they had nothing by which to live*.
