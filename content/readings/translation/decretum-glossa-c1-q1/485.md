*(Continuing canon C. XXXIX from p. 484.)*

> [the remission of sins] which one obtains through baptism is not given by an evil minister, but [is given] by the merit of the Church — for the greedy a were not bestowing it; rather it is the prayers b of the saints, that is, it is given through the groanings of the dove, whoever it may be that baptizes, if those to whom it is given belong to her peace. For the Lord would not say to robbers and usurers: [If you forgive anyone his sins, they are forgiven him; if you retain anyone’s, they are retained.] Outside c, indeed, nothing can be either bound or loosed, where there is no one who is able either to bind or to loose: but he is loosed who is not at peace with the dove, whether he be openly d outside, or seem to be within.

> §. If, therefore, the sacraments e cannot be defiled by unclean ministers as light cannot be defiled, and if, like a pure stream, they pass through stone channels to fertile garden-plots, it is clear that simoniacs hold the sacrament of anointing to their own disadvantage and ruin, while they administer the same anointing to others usefully and salutarily. Just, then, as the sacraments of heretics are true as regards their form, so they are true and not empty f as regards their effect.

> 6. But it must be noted g that of the sacraments some belong to necessity, others to dignity h. For since necessity has no law, it makes a law for itself: those sacraments which are necessary for salvation, since they cannot be repeated, ought not to be taken away (since they are true), but with penitence are allowed to be ratified. But those sacraments i which belong to dignity, unless they are administered worthily — so that the worthy are worthily promoted by worthy ministers — cease to be dignities: not so that the truth of the sacrament is diminished, but so that the office of administering ceases, either by place, or by time, or by promotion.

*Of place, as Leo says to Rusticus, bishop of Narbonne, letter 90 [al. 92] c. 1.*

*¶On those who are ordained by pseudo-bishops.*

## C. XL. On the same

> If any clerics by these pseudo-bishops k have been or-

**a. ¶The greedy.** That is, idolaters, that is, any evil-doers. *28. q. 1. idololatria.*

**b. ¶The greedy do not give.** That is, they are unworthy to give. *De consecr.* below.

**c. ¶Outside.** Understand this thus at the chapter *De toleratis*: take “outside” to mean outside the Church which contains only the grain. But if it concerns those cut off, take “outside” to mean outside the Church militant, which contains both grain and chaff. *7. q. 1. §. cum vero.*

**d. ¶Openly.** When being a Christian one openly attacks the Church, or secretly disturbs the Church. *32. q. 4. ne currat. v. si.*

**¶If therefore.** CASE. Gratian infers from the aforementioned chapter *si iustus fuerit* that the sacraments of simoniacs are useful to others, even if they do not profit the simoniacs themselves, by the similitude of water running through channels, and of light, which is not defiled by unclean things. But he raises an objection concerning the sacraments of heretics, which are true as to form and as to effect; and he resolves it by saying that the sacraments of necessity confer their effect in full force, but the sacraments of dignity [are true] only as to form.

**e. ¶Sacraments.** This refers back to the chapter *si iustus fuerit*. Below, same title.

**f. ¶And not empty.** This is true of a sacrament of necessity. *54. q. 1. si quem fortè.*

**BUT IT MUST BE NOTED.** This is the 6th part of the question, in which he draws a distinction between sacraments of necessity and sacraments that are voluntary. For sacraments of necessity, without which no one is saved (such as baptism), can be received from anyone. But voluntary sacraments (such as orders), unless duly given, are not given: but cease either by place, or by order, or by promotion: concerning which he gives many examples, which are proven through 6 chapters. Joannes de Fantutiis.

**g. ¶But it must be noted.** That is, Gratian responds to the simoniacs by the following distinction added to his conclusion. For a sacrament wrongly received lacks effect in two ways: either because in the sacrament so administered grace is not received — and this is charged to the malice of the recipient: and this is common to all sacraments — or because execution is denied, which is peculiar to sacraments of dignity.

**h. ¶Of necessity.** Sacraments of necessity are five: baptism, confirmation, penance, extreme unction, the body of Christ. In baptism, however, there is this special feature, that in extreme necessity it can be received from anyone. *32. dist. §. verum. v. finem.* And likewise concerning penance.

*\*See Blessed Thomas, 4. dist. 17. q. 3. a. 4. q. 1 and 2; and dist. 20. q. 1. a. 1. q.*

**i. ¶Those [sacraments].** Here he assigns one difference between baptism and the other sacraments: he sets out another below in the same [section] *§. ecce si clericus*; another in that *§. ecce cum honoris*.

**¶If any clerics.** CASE. It is said in this chapter that if anyone is promoted by common consent from one who was wrongly elected but tolerated, he can ministerially serve in the church to which he was ordained, but not in another. The following *§. planus*. and everything up to *§. ecce*.
