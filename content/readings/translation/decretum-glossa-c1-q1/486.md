*(Continuing canon C. XL from p. 485.)*

> [...or]dained to those things that pertained to their proper bishops, and their ordination was made with the consent and judgment of those presiding, it may be held ratified, so that they may continue in those very churches a: but otherwise the ordination is to be deemed void, since it is neither founded in [a proper] place nor armed with [proper] authority.

*¶This chapter has been corrected and supplemented from the original letter itself, and is also given above at dist. 62. c. 1. § si qui.*

*Here, then, is someone ordained by pseudo-bishops: if he changes the place of his ordination, such an ordination is void and will be without administration.*

*Of time, as Innocent says, letter 22 c. 5, on those ordained by Bonosus.*

## C. XLI. Things granted on account of necessity will cease when the necessity ceases

> What has been established on account of the necessity of the time b, when the necessity ceases, that which c was pressing† must in all cases likewise cease: for there is one thing that is the legitimate order, and another that is the usurpation which the present time forces to come about.

*α. ¶Which to the present. These words are added from Innocent, whose own words in this chapter are reduced to a summary; but below, q. 7 c. quod pro remedio, they are more fully set forth.*

*Therefore one ordained at another time by one who was like Bonosus shall cease from the administration which was permitted because of the necessity of that time.*

*Likewise of promotion, as Leo says, letter 3 to Julian †of Aquileia.*

## C. XLII. Those who return from heretics may be received in their own orders without hope of promotion

> If anyone d has stained β himself with the contagion of heretical communion, let him hold it a great favor if, having renounced all hope of promotion e, he remains in the order in which he is found f.

*β. ¶Has stained. Here in Blessed Leo other words are interposed, and so c. saluberrimum below q. 7. For this chapter is, as it were, a kind of summary, just as is c. omnis below in the same [question], although that one is somewhat fuller.*

*Here, g if a cleric communicates with heretics whether by ordination or by any sacrament, ceasing from promotion he scarcely remains in his own order. For this sacrament is not like the others. For the rest are either given to wash away faults, as baptism and penance, or are denied because of faults — not eternally but for a time — as the Eucharist, which is granted to a penitent in the article of death even in the case of heinous sins. This one alone is forbidden not only on account of fault, but also on account of infamy h†, and on account of the prerogative of cleanness i that should be his — whether external or internal — of perfection: as it is denied to bigamists, to the husbands of a widow, to the illiterate, or to those defective in any part of the body — to whom, nevertheless, baptism and the Eucharist are not denied. Such a one can also administer the other sacraments; only this one k he is unable to confer.*

**a. ¶Churches.** Below, in the same question, *in ecclesia*: one who obtained [his office] by manifest simony is not permitted to minister. Here on the contrary, that is on account of scandal, since the offense is hidden, in the church to which he was assigned [he may minister], and not in another, [if] he is received. Or [the distinction is]: this when he bought that order, that when [he bought] a dignity or benefice. Below in the same, *erga*.

**b. ¶What has been established on account of the necessity of the time, etc.** When the necessity ceases, the dispensation too ought to cease.

**c. ¶That which.** That is, [the necessity] which urges the dispensation; or [it is in the accusative], that is, [referring to] what the necessity was urging — that is, [what it was] inducing. Argument to the contrary below at 19. q. 2. *dux*, where it speaks of “on account of criminals.” Bonus solves: there [it is] of impelling cause, here of final cause. ff. de postn. l. 1.

**d. ¶If anyone.** This chapter does not speak of strict right.

**e. ¶Of promotion.** *See 34. dist. praecipimus.* C. depositi, *si depositi*. For he must show gratitude for this, as there.

**f. ¶Is found.** Except in the cases noted. 9. q. 1. *in summa* 1.

**HERE. CASE.** Here Gratian shows the difference between the sacrament of orders or dignity, which is not conferred with effect by heretics, and a sacrament of necessity, which is conferred with effect even by a heretic — such as baptism, penance, and the Eucharist: because the sacrament of orders is forbidden not only on account of fault, but also on account of irregularity; whence one who cannot confer this latter [sacrament] can nevertheless confer the sacraments of necessity.

**g. ¶Here.** The Master here shows the difference between the sacraments of dignity and the others, and the cause of the difference.

**h. ¶Infamy.** Which is incurred even without fault. 6. quaest. 1. §. *verum*.
