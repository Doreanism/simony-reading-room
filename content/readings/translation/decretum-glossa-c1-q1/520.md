## C. CVIII. Concerning those who are ordained not simoniacally by simoniacs

> If any e have been ordained not simoniacally by simoniacs — if indeed they shall be able to prove f that, when they were being ordained, they did not know them to be simoniacs, and if at that time α g they were held in the church as Catholics — we mercifully tolerate the ordinations of such persons, provided that a praiseworthy life commends them. But those who knowingly have permitted themselves to be consecrated (nay, execrated) by simoniacs, we altogether decree their consecration to be void —

*α. ¶*Et si tunc*.\] Previously it read* & quod tunc*, as if the burden of proving it lay on the one wishing to purge himself. It has been emended from two manuscript exemplars of that council.*

*The same Nicholas the Younger. The same Alexander II.*

*The words of this and the following chapter regarding simoniacs are in the epistle of Nicholas II, in which he reports the council held by him; and Alexander II repeated them in his epistle directed to all the Catholic bishops, which is preserved in Rome in three very old manuscript codices.*

***\[He\] adds what the law is when they are thus ordained.** \[?\]

**a. ¶Simoniacos autem non.\]** Those persons are called simoniacs in word only, not in real guilt. *Extra de si. de simoniacè* — namely, on account of the ordination performed by simoniacs, just as a usurer is sometimes called one who pays usury. *ff. de vsur. debitor.* To this —

**b. ¶Misericorditer.\]** And so it is here said that they did not receive the exercise \[of orders\].

**c. ¶Permanere.\]** In execution.

**SED HOC. CASE.** Gratian says that this last clause is to be understood of those who are ordained not simoniacally by simoniacs whom they did not know to be simoniacs, whom their own guilt does not \[?\] make simoniacs, but the ordination by a simoniac.

**d. ¶Sed hoc.\]** It looks back to the last part of the preceding chapter, namely *simoniacs not by guilt, but on account of the ordination*, in the next chapter below.

**¶Si qui à simoniacis.\] CASE.** It is here said that if anyone has been ordained without payment by a bishop, but \[ordained\] by his \[?\] simoniacal \[ordainer\]: if he has proved that at that time he \[the ordainer\] was tolerated by the Church, and that he did not know him to be a simoniac, dispensation may mercifully be given to him. But if he knew him to be a simoniac, he must be deposed. The following chapter is plain, which is to be noted.

**e. ¶Si qui.\]** H. understands this chapter of those cut off \[from the Church\], who however were not known to be cut off by those being ordained \[?\]. For his opinion is that, if it is hidden that the ordainer is a hidden simoniac, who is tolerated, the ordained receives the exercise, as *15. q. 7. c. ult.* — unless it is notorious, as *32. dist. §. veri \[?\]*; or unless this can be proved by witnesses, as *above the same place, quisquis*. And therefore, in order that what follows here may have its place — namely *mercifully* etc. — he must necessarily read it of those cut off. We, however, who think otherwise (argument *j. ca. quibusdā* and *3. c. reperitur*), read this chapter of secret simoniacs. For however secret they may be, even if unknown, such a one does not obtain the exercise. And this in a simoniac in orders; and although it happens without the fault of the one ordained, he nevertheless needs dispensation, both in detestation of the crime, and therefore he says *mercifully* — and although this is the case with one in ignorance, it is otherwise with one who was coerced, as below the same place, *constat*. For the latter is by right to be admitted to the exercise: and the reason is that the will of one coerced is more clearly known to us than that of one in ignorance. Nor are you to understand that chapter *de bap. maiores*, because however \[?\] —

*Whether one ordained by a hidden simoniac receives the exercise of orders.*

**f. ¶Probare.\]** By oath. *Extra de sent. excō. si vero.* Or it is proved by the very fact that it is not proved 5 \[?\]. *C. milli \[?\] no possu\[m\] per seruis. lib. 12.* But suppose the prelate is a simoniac, and wishes to ordain me. If I can prove it \[?\], I ought not to receive orders from him; if I cannot \[?\] —

*If a simoniac \[bishop\] wishes to ordain his subordinate against his will, what the law is.*

**¶Probare.\]** Hence the argument that one who alleges ignorance must prove it. 1. And since proof is made in three ways — namely, directly, when that is proved from which what is intended necessarily or probably follows — yet for ignorance a probable proof suffices; which appears from what is said, *at that time they were held as Catholics*. From the following words also, *if a more praiseworthy life commends them*, it can be argued that prior merits avail to obtain pardon for an offense afterwards committed. *Arg. ff. de re mili. non omnis.* Io.
