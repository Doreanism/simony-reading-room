> does not appease the wrath of the mighty God, but provokes it. Hence again it is written: \[Honor the Lord with your just labors.\] He therefore who wickedly takes, that as it were he may give well, no doubt does not honor the Lord. Hence also Solomon says: \[He who offers a sacrifice from the substance of the poor is as one who slays the son in the sight of the father.\] Now how great is the father’s grief, let us weigh, if in his sight the son be slain. And from this we easily recognize how great a grief is exasperated before God, when a sacrifice from rapine is offered to him. Therefore, dearest brethren, it is most strongly to be avoided that under the pretext of alms, we perpetrate the sins of the simoniacal heresy. For it is one thing to do alms on account of sins α, another to commit sins on account of alms †. *And after a few \[lines\].* ¶Whoever, therefore, desires to obtain the priesthood not for the pomp of exaltation, but for usefulness, let him first measure his own strength ᵇ against this burden which he is about to undergo, so that if he is unequal, he may abstain ᶜ; and to that which † he judges himself sufficient, let him approach with fear.

## C. XXVIII. A simoniac pastor offers no medicine to the sheep

> The simoniacal ᵈ † heresy is reported (which crept in by diabolical implantation as the first against the Church of God, and at its very rise ᵉ was struck and condemned by the zeal † of apostolic vengeance) to dominate in the borders of your kingdom: although in priests, faith should be chosen together with life. For if life is lacking, faith ᶠ has no merit, blessed James testifying, who says: \[Faith without works ᵍ is dead ʰ.\] For what can be the works of a priest, who is found to obtain the honor of so great a sacrament through reward? *And after a few \[lines\].* ¶Hence therefore a lethal wound is inflicted not only on the soul of the one ordaining and of the one ordained, but also the kingdom † of your excellence is weighed down ⁱ by the fault of the bishops (by whose intercessions it ought rather to have been helped *) *. For if he is judged worthy of the priesthood for whom not merits of action, but abundance of rewards…

*† al. simoniacam haeresim.*

*† al. scio.*

*† al. the rule of your bishops.*

***So he infers, since Theodoric was beheaded at Cologne and Theobert was poisoned by Brunichilda. The crime of one harms the innocent. 3 On this, no. B. extra de pe. q\[ua\] bus\[?\]dam.***

**a. ¶Facere.** But it is objected: “Make for yourself friends from the mammon of iniquity”; what \[does this mean\]? — that is, from wealth, anxieties, and labors acquired: or what you have taken away, restore to those it belongs to 1, or to their parents: or if those are dead, offer to the priests: and there all who have been made your friends will pray for you to the Lord. For although it has been said, “What you have acquired for evil, expend for good,” nevertheless this will not patronize \[the matter\] for this purpose. H. To acquire wickedly for the sake of alms, and to expend it for good, is nothing other than to slay the son in the sight of the father. 14. q. 6. *insolantis*. But what is otherwise acquired, e.g. out of love for one’s children, and is expended for good, that will patronize you toward reward. Huguccio.

*“To make friends from the mammon of iniquity” — what is this?*

*1 Here you may see what is held at 14. q. 5. Sed hic. There the aforesaid authority is expounded.*

**b. ¶First his own strength.** “Take, you who write, a subject equal to your strength; and weigh long what your shoulders refuse to bear, what they can bear.” argument. ff. *de excu. tutoris*. *paupertas*. The same the law says in *muliones* ad legem Aquil. l. *idem iuris*. §. 1. & l. *qua actione*. §. 2.

*Horace, in the Art of Poetry.*

**c. ¶Let him abstain.** Here he presumes against an unworthy prelate \[?\] that he was badly elected, argument from a 3rio sensu, 61. dist. *miramur*. vers\[icle\] *nec putandus est honor*.

**¶Reported.** THE CASE. Gregory rebukes here the kings of France, because in their borders they allow simony to flourish. And he shows that this is a loss to the king, because simoniacs harm his rule by their unjust prayers, by whose intercessions he ought to have been aided. For who can confide in the prayers of those whom he knows inwardly to be wounded? and what kind of fruit is produced from such an infected root?

**d. ¶Simoniacal.** Gregory notes first concerning the negligence of those kings of France, in that simony dominates in their kingdom. Second, he says that by the fault of these prelates their rule is twisted back \[?\]. Third, one must fear concerning those who have such intercessors with God, not provoking God, whom they ought to placate.

*Simony dominates in France.*

**h. ¶Is dead.** If time is given or place \[for it\], \[turn\] to penance.
