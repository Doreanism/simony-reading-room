*(Continuing the Gratian dictum from p. 512.)*

> a — to this opinion b of Augustine: the power of conferring baptism, the right of consecrating the Lord’s body, and the authority of bestowing sacred orders differ greatly among themselves. For when a priest is suspended or deposed, no power of sacrificing is left to him. The sacrament of baptism, however, is not violated by any repetition, whether ministered by a deposed priest, by a Catholic layman, or even by a heretic or pagan c; but no reason allows those to be reckoned among priests who receive the oil of sacred (nay, execrable) unction at the hands of a layman or a pagan. It is not, therefore, consistently d to be concluded that, if the right of baptizing remains with those who have departed from the faith, the power e also of conferring sacred orders remains, although both \[powers\] come from consecration. For a degraded bishop does not have the power f of bestowing g sacred orders, yet he has not lost h the faculty of baptizing. ¶But a, lest i we utterly disapprove of Augustine in this opinion, let us understand that the power of conferring sacred orders is one thing, and the exercise of that power is another. For those who, established within the unity k of the catholic Church, receive priestly or episcopal unction, obtain the office and the exercise of their office from the consecration. But those who depart from the integrity of the faith retain the power they have received so far as the sacrament l is concerned, but they are utterly deprived of its effect; just as married persons departing from one another do not dissolve m the marriage once entered, yet they are found alien from the conjugal work. ¶Augustine, then, is speaking of those who, having received priestly power, depart from the unity of the catholic Church: not of those who, set in schism or heresy, receive priestly unction. Otherwise he would contradict the Council of Chalcedon n, in which those ordained by simoniacs are judged to profit in nothing, and Innocent o, who testifies that those ordained by other heretics, through the wicked imposition of hands, obtain only damnation and a wound to their head. ¶Although p it may be said in general that the sacraments which are celebrated among the heretics no otherwise than in the Church of God are true and valid as far as they are in themselves, but false and empty as to their effect — both in those by whom they are wickedly handled, and in those by whom they are ill received. Nor is it any wonder. For our very salvation himself gave the morsel to Judas: at once with the morsel, not a good but an evil spirit entered him. Why? Not because the good \[giver\] did not give a good thing, but because the evil one received a good thing badly: and so the good thing did not have a good effect, because, where it would do this, it did not find \[a fit recipient\]. ¶Likewise, the Lord, when truly present in his own country, accomplished nothing — not through an evil minister, but through himself — because he did not find faith \[there\];

*his own \[solution\], which extends down to §. \[*Quamuis*\] etc.; there he sets forth another, which is more common, and to prove it he adduces a fourfold example: the first at \[*Ipsa enim*\], the second at \[*Item Dominus in patria*, etc.\], the third at \[*Quod vt plane*\], the fourth concerning the ten virgins, which begins at \[*Sic et omnes*, etc.\]. Finally he concludes from that place: \[*Sciendum vero*\] etc.; to prove his conclusion he adduces the chapter* per Esaiam*.*

**a. ¶Opponitur.** In this section he proceeds thus: first he sets forth his own objection against Augustine, which he treats down to §. \[*Sed ne*\]. There he offers a certain solution of his own,

**b. ¶Sententiae.** Of the preceding chapter, where it is said that to each \[orders and baptism\] is given a certain consecration; and thus he seems to put orders and baptism on the same footing.

**c. ¶Pagano.** Above, the same question, *si quis per ignorantiam.* Above in the same place, §. *ecce quando.* Below, *de consecr. distinct. 4. quos à paganis.* Below, *de consec. distinctio 4. à quodam.*

**d. ¶Consequenter.** Do not here rebuke the master, for he says this by way of objection, as is plain from the solutions that follow, especially from the second.

**e. ¶Potestas.** That is, the exercise of power. And in this sense he speaks truly; otherwise he speaks falsely. For the power of sacrificing — that is, the priestly order — remains in him.

**f. ¶Potestatem.** He argues poorly: for if by *power* he means the priestly order or the sacrament, he has lost the power neither in this case nor in that; but if he means the exercise, then he has lost it in both.

**g. ¶Largiédi.** But since a heretic can first confer both baptism and orders, what will be peculiar to baptism? I say that in a case of necessity the one baptizing does not sin — which is never the case in the conferral of orders.

**h. ¶Non amisit.** \[Namely\] in necessity, and with the solemnities. *50. distinctio. qui sub gradu.*

**i. ¶Sed ne.** Gratian says this as if Augustine had meant: *they have the right of giving* — that is, the exercise. And so he resolves \[the difficulty\].

**K. ¶Vnitatem.** Here Gratian touches on the opinion of Ioan. de Fant., who says that one who receives the final imposition of hands in the Church, etc. This opinion seems expressly confirmed at *extra de schitma. c. 1.*, but is expressly rejected at *q. quaestio. 1. ordinationes.*

**l. ¶Sacramento tenus.** That is, because if they ordain, they confer orders, although they ought not. And this is what follows: *without effect*, etc.

**o. ¶Et Innocentio.** *Above, the same \[chapter\]* *veniunt*.
