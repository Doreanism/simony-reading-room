## C. XVIII. A simoniac, through depraved imposition of hands, bestows not blessing but damnation

> We have come a to the third question, which on account of its difficulty† b requires a longer discussion. While we say that one ordained by heretics has been wounded c through that imposition of hands and bears a wound on the head d, where the wound e is inflicted, medicine f must be applied by which he can regain his health†. The healing that follows the wound cannot be g without a scar; and where the remedy of penance is necessary, there we judge that the honor of ordination can have h no place. For if, as it is read, “whatever an unclean person shall touch shall be unclean,” how shall there be granted to him i what cleanness and purity are accustomed to receive? ¶But on the other hand it is asserted that he who has lost the honor cannot give the honor, nor did that man receive k anything, since there was nothing in the giver that the other could receive. We acquiesce, and it is certainly true, because what he did not have, he could not give. But the damnation he did have, he gave through the depraved imposition of hands; and he who has become a partaker with the damned man, how should he \[be saved?\] ...

*(Continuation of the gloss on §3 from p. 469, beginning of left margin.)* \[...\] said, the position to be held is that any bishop, whether catholic or heretic, whether in the church or outside it, who confers the order — provided that he confers it according to the form of the church — the one ordained receives the order, although he does not receive the exercise of the order. Likewise that those so ordained ordain others, and so on to infinity, as at *J. ea. quod quidam. 9. q. 1. ordinationes.* I say the same about consecrating the body of Christ: *J. ea. dominus declarauit. § c. non nocet. infra de confe. distin. 4. non in nobis.* So the beginning of the chapter speaks of those who received the order in the form of the church. The second part \[speaks\] of those \[who received it\] outside the form: those receive nothing, nor do they confer anything, since no one can give \[what he does not have\], etc., as above ch. 1 and below at *q. 7. Daibertum.*

**Addition.** Laurentius says that many lies have been written about heretics of this sort, and uselessly. Joh. de Faventia and Goffredus say that a heretic, who receives the last imposition of hands outside the church, does not confer it, but confounds them. *J. ead. q. 7. conuenientibus.* Archid.

**¶We have come.** *Casus*. The Macedonian bishops put three questions to Innocent. The first was whether one who married a widow could be promoted, or if already promoted, ought to be deposed. The second was whether one should be called a bigamist who, before baptism, had one wife and, after baptism, another; the explication of which is given above at *34. dist. si quis viduam* and *26. dist. deinde opponitur.* He comes in this chapter to the third, which is namely whether those returning from the heretics, who were ordained among them, are to be admitted to orders among us. And he responds that they are not: because in reconciliation a penance is imposed upon them, after which a certain scar remains, that is, irregularity, which bars them from promotion. But this is objected to in two ways: if a heretic ordained someone — \[a heretic\] who had lost the honor — he conferred nothing on the one ordained; therefore \[it is the same as if\] he were ordained by catholics, just as if he had never been ordained. But Innocent replies that this is false, because he received condemnation, and that does not impede him from being promoted. Secondly, it is objected: if he incurs any stain from heretical ordination, that is wiped away by canonical ordination. And he replies that this is false: for if it were so, he would do penance for nothing, but would simply proceed to ordination, where all crimes are wiped away. From the custom of the Roman church, therefore, those coming from the heretics are admitted only to lay communion and not to the clergy. Three questions are here set, one principal and two incidental, and to all he answers sufficiently.

**¶We have come.** For three Macedonian bishops, to whom Innocent writes, had among other things asked questions. The first was whether one who married a widow could be promoted; or if already made a cleric, ought to be deposed on that account? Its determination is given at *s. dist. 34. si quis viduam.* The second was whether one should be called a bigamist who had one wife before baptism and another after baptism — its determination is given at `[?]`

*†al.* Pro sui difficultate.

**c. ¶Wounded.** That is, with the capital and sacramental dignity wounded along with the damnation received.

**d. ¶Head.** That is, the soul wounded by the stain of guilt — and “head” stands for the man, *de confec. dist. 5. de tales.*

**e. ¶Wound.** ¶Where the evil falls, there let him die — *and q. 4. peccaveris.*

**f. ¶Medicine.** This seems to be expounded thus: that is, the sacrament in such persons must be set aside. *J. ea. q. vij. per illicitum* — for this seems to be understood when this \[remedy\] has been omitted. *Arg. 1. q. vij. Daibertum*, where this is introduced.

**g. ¶Be a scar.** *J. q[ua]e[?]* — and even if they do penance, nevertheless the irregularity remains, which is not abolished by penance, as at *25. dist. primum.* and *50. dist. illud.* and *1. dist. 7. si quis omnino habere. J. infra 23. q. 4. ipsa pietas* contra. But you may distinguish: \[the strict rule applies\] to catholics who could fall into heresy; here \[we speak\] of those who received the imposition of hands from heretics `[?]`. Or this \[is by\] strictness, that \[other\] by mercy.

**h. ¶How shall it be granted to him.** Argument that a wife is not bound to render the marital debt to a leprous husband. *Arg. s. 12. q. 7. omnes.* and *q. 5. si quis uxorem.* See on whose word one is to stand: *extra de coniug. lepro. cap. 1. et 2.*

**i. ¶Received.** Otherwise *to receive*, and supply: and therefore not to hurt him as regards being ordained, because what is nothing can also constitute an impediment, as at *extra de iure pa. qui in fi.* But this is false: *extra de spon. c. ad audiendum.* — *Damnation. J. ea. ea.* damnation: that which he had to his hurt, he gave to the hurt of another by giving; and so was made a partaker of the damnation. *J. ea. q. 7. si quis. J. ead. q. 4. § cum venita de confe. dist. 4. ab antiquo.*
