*(Continuing canon C. XX from p. 471.)*

> is the servitude of idols a, whoever does not vigilantly guard against it — and most of all in conferring ecclesiastical honors — is subjected b to the perdition of infidelity c, even if he appears to hold the faith which he neglects α.

*α. ¶Which he neglects. So it is amended from the manuscript copies and the original. For in the printed editions it was read:* fidem verbis, quam negat facto, videatur *\[“he appears to hold the faith in words which he denies in deed”\].*

*From the letter of Tarasius, bishop of Constantinople, sent to Pope Hadrian.*

## C. XXI. The heresy of simoniacs is shown to be more damnable than other heresies

> Those who have imposed hands for money, or have received the imposition of hands \[for money\], Peter the divine d Apostle (whose chair perhaps you usurp `[?]`) deposes as a Simon Magus. *And further below.* ¶For the impious heresy of Macedonius and those e who are around him, the impugners of the Holy Spirit, is more tolerable. For they, in their madness, declare the Holy Spirit to be a creature and a servant of God the Father and the Son: but these make f the same Spirit their own servant. For every master sells, if he wishes g, what he has — whether a servant or any other of his possessions. Likewise too the one who buys, wishing to be master of what he has bought, acquires it by paying a price in money h. So too those who perform this iniquitous action drag down i the Holy Spirit, sinning on a par with those who blasphemed, saying that Christ cast out demons by Beelzebub; or to speak more truly, they are to be compared k to Judas the traitor, who sold Christ to the Jews, the murderers of God. Since therefore the Holy Spirit is consubstantial with Christ, they will be of altogether the same portion. And without doubt the grace of the Holy Spirit — that is, the holiness of the priesthood — is not in them. For what they did not receive, they do not have. For Peter said to Simon: \[*“You have no part nor lot in this word, because you thought the gift of God could be possessed for money.”*\] *And further below.* ¶For the twenty-ninth canon of the holy l Apostles, and their Acts, and the third m and fourth n book of Kings, declare him to be wholly alien from the priesthood who shall at any time have given or received money, whether before the imposition of hands, or at the very imposition

**a. ¶Of idols.** *Arg. 6. q. 1. quaero.*

**b. ¶Perdition.** And so he is a heretic, according to Gratian’s argument.

**c. ¶Is subjected.** He is rather so called than is so in fact. *J. 24. q. 2. sane. Et infra 22. q. 5. cavere. 8. q. 1. sciendum. Infra 23. q. 4. forte.* Joh.

**Those.** Casus. Just as Blessed Peter condemned Simon, so he not undeservedly condemns and deposes all simoniacs; which is proved by six reasons. The first is that they are worse than the Macedonians, who say that the Holy Spirit is a servant of the Father and the Son `[?]`: these make him a servant, because they sell him. The second, that a master sells what he has, if he wishes. But God does not have the Holy Spirit `[?]`, but is himself the Holy Spirit. The third, that they detract from the Holy Spirit, just as the Jews said that the works which Christ did were done by Beelzebub. The fourth, that they are compared with Judas, and are worse than he, because Judas gave over Christ without beginning him `[?]`, but these the Holy Spirit, who is consubstantial with Christ. The fifth, because they do not have the Holy Spirit, as is plain from the authority of Peter: *“You have no part,”* &c. The sixth, that the twenty-ninth canon of the holy Apostles, and the third and fourth book of Kings, forbid simoniacs to remain in the priesthood.

**d. ¶Divine.** Taken in a good sense as referring to deity, or from divinity, as above at *38. distinct. omnes. distinct. omnes* — not from divination, as below in the same, *nunquam.*

**e. ¶And those.** That is, his followers.

**f. ¶They make.** Namely, so far as in them lies, just as is settled concerning buying and selling, below in the same cause, *q. 3. salvator.* —B.

**Addition.** *49. distin. c. ult. de consec. dist. 4. c. quaris. Inf. ead. iudices.* Arch.

**g. ¶If he wishes.** Nothing is so consonant, etc. *insti. de re[rum] div. § per traditionem. C. de contrah. emp. dudum.* at the end — unless the law forbids the sale. *extra de iure patrona[tus]. de iure. C. de reb. alien. l. ulti.* Indeed, sometimes one is even compelled to sell. *C. de communi ser. ma. l. 1. ff. de reli. si quis sepulchrum.* and *ff. communi divi. l. 1. § ult. emere autem non compellitur quis. C. de iure delib. nec eme[re]. arg. C. de don. sancimus.*

**h. ¶Money.** For the price consists in counted money, as at *institu. de emp. et ven. § item pretium.* *arg. extra de re. permu. ad quaestiones.* For where one thing is given for another in kind, it is barter. *ff. de contrah. emp. l. 1.* But here “purchase” is taken broadly, and likewise “money,” as below at *q. 3. per totum.*

**i. ¶Drag down.** For just as the Jews attributed to the devil the working of miracles which were done by the Holy Spirit, so these acquire for themselves through the devil the orders, which are gifts of the Holy Spirit.

**k. ¶Since therefore.** Better to say *“for since,”* &c., according to what follows in the text.

**l. ¶Canon of the holy.** Which runs thus: If any bishop, priest, or deacon obtains his dignity through money, both he and his ordainer are in every way to be cut off from communion, like Simon Magus from Peter. This bears on the matter at *infra. quaest. 3. si quis episcopus*, and at the final chapter.

**m. ¶And the third.** Where Jeroboam is spoken of, who filled the hand of whomever wished, and he became a priest of the high places; and for this cause the house of Jeroboam sinned, and was overturned and wiped from the face of the earth.

**n. ¶And the fourth.** Where Giezi is treated.
