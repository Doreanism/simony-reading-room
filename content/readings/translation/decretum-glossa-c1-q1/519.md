## C. CV. Nothing ought to be exacted for the reception of baptism or chrism

> It has been said that in certain places coins are accustomed to be given for the reception of chrism, and likewise for baptism and communion. This seed of the simoniacal heresy the holy synod has detested and anathematized; and it has decreed that henceforth nothing whatsoever shall be exacted, neither for ordination, nor for chrism, nor for baptism, nor for balsam, nor for burial, nor for communion; but let the free gifts of Christ be given by free dispensation.

*Likewise from the Council of Chalon, in the time of Charlemagne, ch. 17.*

## C. CVI. Nothing ought to be exacted for the dedication of basilicas, or for conferring the other sacraments of the Church

> We decree that, just as nothing is to be accepted for the dedication of basilicas or for the conferring of orders, so likewise priests who are about to receive chrism shall give nothing for balsam or for buying lights b. The bishops therefore shall buy the balsam from the resources of the church, and shall each provide for the lights to be used in their own churches.

Just as c, then, these sacraments d, even when ministered simoniacally, nevertheless confer on the recipient the effect of the blessing, so likewise priestly unction, even when ministered simoniacally, ought not to lack its effect. But, as has been said above, those sacraments are of necessity, these are of dignity; and therefore the privileges of those cannot generate a common law for these. Simoniacs therefore have a wounded head through the wicked imposition of the simoniacal hand. This, however, is to be understood of those whom neither ignorance nor the violence of compulsion excuses; who are ordained by those whom they undoubtedly know to be simoniacs, whether they are ordained by them simoniacally or not simoniacally. For as Pope Nicholas distinguishes: some are ordained simoniacally by simoniacs; others, simoniacally by non-simoniacs; others, not simoniacally by simoniacs.

> We establish e the decree concerning simoniacs of the threefold heresy: that is, concerning the simoniacs who simoniacally ordain or have been ordained; and concerning simoniacs ordained simoniacally by non-simoniacs; and concerning simoniacs \[ordained\] not simoniacally by simoniacs. ¶The simoniacs simoniacally ordained, or the simoniac ordainers, according to the ecclesiastical \[canons\] —

**¶Statuimus.\] CASE.** This chapter is divided into two parts. In the first part the same thing is said which was said in the distinction of Nicholas. In the second part, which begins at \[*Simoniaci*\], it says that simoniacs simoniacally ordained, together with their ordainers, must be deposed. Simoniacs, however, simoniacally ordained by non-simoniacs shall be removed from the ill-acquired office, but the ordainers shall not be deposed. With those not simoniacally ordained by simoniacs, dispensation may be given on grounds of necessity, that they remain in their office.

**¶Sicut ergo.\] CASE.** Gratian infers that just as these sacraments — namely, baptism and chrism — have effect even when ministered simoniacally, so also priestly unction. But there is this difference between those and these: that those are sacraments of necessity, but these of dignity. Simoniacs, then, have a wounded head, whom ignorance or another cause does not excuse. Hence Nicholas distinguishes: some are ordained simoniacally by simoniacs; some simoniacally by non-simoniacs; some not simoniacally by simoniacs.
