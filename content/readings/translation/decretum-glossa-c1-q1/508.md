*(Continuing C. XC from p. 507.)*

> [And a priest in whom there shall be a blemish m, shall not approach to offer offerings to the Lord.] H[uguccio] however [says] that this authority is to be understood of a bodily blemish, as *49 dist. hinc etiam*; and so it is sophistically adduced [in] §. *non accedat*. For so long as a strange fire that consumes and does not heal burns in the breast of the one praying, he cannot worthily approach the altar. *De poen. dist. 3. c. inter haec hircum.* Whence it is noted here that any priest is suspended for the blemish of mortal sin, with respect to himself; but not with respect to the recipients, except as is noted at *32 dist. §. verum.* &c. *nullus*.

*Likewise Alexander, epist. 2.*

¶So much more easily are priests heard, as they are more worthy before God.

## C. XCI. The priests themselves [intercede] for the people.

> THe priests themselves intercede for the people, and blot out and consume the sins of the people a: who, the worthier they are, the more easily they are heard for the necessities for which they cry out.

*Likewise Gelasius to Bishop Elpidius.*

¶The Holy Spirit is not present in the sacraments which are ministered by criminals.

## C. XCII. The Holy Spirit is not present in the sacraments.

> THe most sacred b religion, which contains catholic discipline, claims for itself such great reverence that none may dare approach it except with a pure conscience. For how shall the heavenly spirit, invoked [?], come to the consecration c of the divine mystery, if the priest, and the one who beseeches him to be present, is rejected as full of criminal [?] actions?

*Likewise Gregory in the register, book 1, epist. 24.*

¶An unclean hand defiles the things it touches; it does not cleanse them.

## C. XCIII. An unclean hand.

> IT is necessary that the hand which seeks to wash away filth be studious to be clean: lest, having touched things, it more grievously defile them, if it holds the mud of filth [?]. For it is written: [Be ye clean, you that carry the vessels c of the Lord.] For the vessels of the Lord are the souls of his servants, taken up to be led inward [?] by the example of their conversation.

*Likewise Augustine, book 50 of the homilies, homily 26.*

¶The word of Christ is to be preserved equally with the body of Christ.

## C. XCIIII. The word of Christ is to be preserved equally with the body of Christ.

> I ask you, brothers and sisters: tell me, which seems to you to be more, the word of God, or the body of Christ? If you wish to answer truly, this surely is what you must say: that the word of God is no less than the body of Christ. And therefore, with as much care as we observe, when the body of Christ is administered to us, that nothing of it from our hands fall to the ground, with as much care [?] let us observe that the word of God, which is bestowed upon us, while we think or say something else, perish not from our heart: because he will be no less guilty who has heard the word of God g negligently, than he who has by his negligence permitted the body of Christ to fall h to the ground.

*The same, book 4 on the Trinity, c. 14.*

¶I ask. CASE. It is said here that the word of Christ must be heard with equal diligence, lest it slip from the heart, just as...

**¶Any priest is suspended for mortal sin as regards himself.**

**a. ¶The priests themselves.** The priests eat the sins of the people. Thus *1. q. 1. quia.*

**a. ¶The priests themselves.** ¶They eat. That is, [they eat] the things offered for sins. 1. Or they are said to eat the sins of the people, because they blot them out, and so they eat the sins of the people by their ordinations. Or perhaps because by the example of good operation and by the word of preaching they correct men, and thus as far as in them lies they blot [them] out. *36. dist. §. ut sane [?].* Likewise. As *s. e. q. ut evidenter.* Or [we say that] God blots out the sin, that is, the guilt of sin: 2. the priest mitigates by his prayers the punishment to be inflicted for the sin: 3. or [we say] that the sin is said to be blotted out [as in] *13. q. 2. animae.* H. Or those are said to eat the sins of the people who can support themselves from their own [means], and yet take from the goods of the Church [?].

**b. ¶Most sacred.** That is, the religious reverence of the sacraments. Or that is, the Eucharist, or any other sacrament whatever. *Below same q. ventum.*

**c. ¶Consecration.** Because [it is] a divine ministry that has performed it, not the administration of the minister, as *below de consecr. distinct. [?].*

**¶Word.** That is, what is signified by the word, or what is conceived through the word.

**h. ¶Fall.** *De consecr. dist. 2. qui bene.*
