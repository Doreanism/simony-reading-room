*(Continuation of the gloss on K Saulem from p. 476.)* — and they went to the prophet: and Saul was anointed king by Samuel at the Lord’s command.

**a. ¶But suppose.** That is, posit. And so according to the canons a party may posit a falsehood. *De consecratione*, distinction 4, *san\[...\]ctum*.

**b. ¶Five \[?\]**

> and the servant replied: \[Behold, there is found in my hand a quarter shekel of money, and I will give it to the man of God, and he will declare to us our \[concerning our\] way.\] Now it is not written that Samuel received it, or that they offered it: rather, they are fed by the prophet, and invited to dinner. But suppose ª they had received it: alms for the tabernacle are to be reckoned of greater worth than gifts for prophecy: for a shekel has twenty oboli; and a quarter shekel is five oboli ᵇ. Therefore our priests too, if they wish to sell prophecy and to set up doves in their chairs (which the Lord overthrew with a scourge), let them be content with a fee of just five α oboli: would that ᶜ they took not the price of estates, but five oboli. ¶This same lesson is sounded also in the third book of Kingdoms, when the wife ᵈ of Jeroboam, with her son ailing, goes to Ahijah the man of God ᵉ, and brings to him ten loaves β, and a cake, and a vessel of honey; for γ these things are rather to be reckoned as expenses for the prophet ᶠ than as a payment for prophecy. Yet those who were accustomed to go to soothsayers (since there were many diviners and soothsayers in Israel) might, by a bad custom, have judged \[matters\] also with regard to him among the prophets, and have wished to offer to holy men what they had been accustomed to offer to diviners. ¶Whence § in the fourth book of Kingdoms it is read that \[Elisha ᵍ came to Damascus, and Benadad king of Syria was ill, and they announced it to him, saying: The man of God has come hither: and the king said to Hazael ʰ: Take with you gifts, and go to meet the man of God, and consult the Lord through him, saying, whether I shall be able to escape this illness. So Hazael went to meet him, having with him gifts, and every good thing ⁱ of Damascus, the loads of forty camels.\] But it is not to be believed that he received them: just as it is read of the same Elisha, that Naaman, cleansed from leprosy, offered ᵏ him gifts, saying: \[I beg, that you accept a blessing from your servant. But he replied: As the Lord lives ˡ, before whom I stand, I will not accept it. And though he urged him ᵐ, he by no means consented.\]

*α. ¶Five only the price of oboli let them be content, who.\] All these things are absent from the more recent editions of blessed Jerome, and even from the older ones, except the word “who.” But they are not to be expunged, because they do not produce a \[different\] sentence.*

**¶This chapter in one old exemplar is joined to the previous one; and in place of the first word, “Judges,” is read, “Leaders,” just as it is in blessed Jerome’s version of the LXX.**

**b. ¶Are five oboli.** Argument that in a delict the quantity of the thing is to be regarded. Below, 14. q. ult. cap. ult. Below, 17. q. 4. *quicunque*. q. ult. *qui ferri sunt*.

**c. ¶Would that.** And read this as a negative, that is, would that they did not take more. You have a similar passage at 23. q. 4. *si ea* and *episcopi*; and therefore perhaps he says this, because if they did not take more, no one would be willing to commit simony. Or perhaps for this reason, because the less one takes, the less one sins: according to which it is said that one is punished less for a pig or sheep than for \[?\] ff. *de abigeis* l. 1. j. *responsum*. Or it could be understood about \[him\] in the decretal *Extra de simo. Et si quaestiones*. Argument to the contrary: below, 14. q. ult. cap. ult. 17. q. 4. *quicunque*. cap.

**d. ¶That the wife.** History. We read in the third book of Kings that when the first-born son of Jeroboam was sick, he said to his wife: “Take with you gifts, and with changed dress go to the prophet Ahijah, that he may declare to you what shall come of the boy.” When she had come to the prophet, although on account of the dimness of his eyes he could not see, he nevertheless said: “Enter, wife of Jeroboam; why do you feign to be another? I am sent to you as a hard messenger.” And when he had announced many things to come upon the house of Jeroboam, he added: “Go to your house, at the entry of your foot into the city the child shall die: who alone of the seed of Jeroboam shall be borne into the sepulchres of his fathers.” The history of the prophet Elisha is plain in the text.

**e. ¶Ahijah the man of God.** Who foretold to him the death of his son Abia: and who reproved him for his sins, and predicted his vengeance, as below in the same place *si quis inquit*. He says: 23. q. ultim. cap. ult.

**f. ¶For expense.** For it is permitted to receive \[gifts\] for expenses and sustenance. Extra de sim. *cum sit*. 23. q. 5. *dicat*. ff. *de officio proconsulis*, *solent*. ff. *de officio praesidis*, *plebiscito*. And thus a priest, “if he is poor, may receive for the celebration of Mass. Extra de praebendis, *significatum*. Unless he is the proper priest. De consecratione, distinctio 1. *sufficit*.

ADDITION. As noted at 21. q. 7. cap. ult.

**i. ¶Good things.** That is, some of all the good things, that is, of every kind of goods. ff. *de regula iuris* aliud. §. *refertur*.

**K. ¶Offered.** Argument that in the same deed, according to a difference of intention, one may sin and another not, as in 32. q. 5. *propositio*.

**l. ¶Lives.** This was an oath in the Old Testament. 22. q. 1. *et iurabunt*.

**m. ¶He urged.** Argument against the Catalans.
