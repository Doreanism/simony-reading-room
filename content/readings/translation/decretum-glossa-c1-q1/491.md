**¶If anyone takes refuge. CASE.** It was decreed in the Council of Nicaea that if anyone of the Paulianists or Cataphrygians comes to the Catholic Church, he is to be rebaptized. But if they were clerics among them, and unblemished and irreprehensible, let them likewise be ordained by the bishop of the Catholic Church. But if, upon examination, they are found less fit, they ought to be deposed. The same form will be observed concerning deacons and all others who are found in the same clergy.

*Ambrose has spoken, saying: [The baptism of the perfidious does not heal, &c.] But also that they be rebaptized and reordained was established in the Nicene Council, c. 19.*

¶Let those be rebaptized and reordained who return to the Church from the Paulianists and Cataphrygians.

## C. LII. Let those be rebaptized and reordained who return to the Church from the Paulianists and Cataphrygians

> If anyone takes refuge in the Catholic Church from the Paulianists a and Cataphrygians α b, it has been altogether established that [he must be baptized]. But if any were clerics among them, if they are unblemished and irreprehensible, they are to be rebaptized, and again c also ordained by the bishop of the Catholic Church. But if upon examination they prove less fit, they ought to be deposed d. Likewise also concerning deacons β, and concerning all who are found in the same clergy, the same form will be observed.

*α. ¶Cataphrygians. This word is read in the old version, which is found in various printed and manuscript exemplars of the Isidorian collection; but it is not in the Greek canon, nor in the version of Dionysius, nor in others more recent. That these too are to be baptized is taught in the Council of Laodicea, c. 8, in the seventh or second Nicene synod, c. 7, and below de consec. dist. 4. c. hi vero, which is from Gregory.*

*β. ¶Deacons. The Greek is, περὶ τῶν διακονισσῶν, καὶ ὅλως περὶ τῶν ἐν τῷ κανόνι ἐξεταζομένων, that is, Concerning deaconesses, and concerning all who are altogether enrolled in the canon.*

*Likewise Innocent to the bishops of Macedonia, letter 22 c. 5.*

¶On the same. C. LIII.

## C. LIII. On the same

> The Paulianists e by no means baptized in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; the Novatians do baptize with those same fearful and venerable names; nor among the latter was any question ever raised concerning the unity of the divine power, that is, of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

> *Others f keep the form of the Church in baptizing. But, when they are baptized by those who in heresy or schism communicate with them, they indeed receive from them the sacrament of baptism, but they do not obtain its power, which no one can obtain without faith. Whence it is said of the Lord that in his own country g he could h not work signs because of their unbelief.*

*Whence Augustine tract. 80 on c. 15 of John, and sermon On the Cataclysm, c. 5, at the beginning.*

¶When the word approaches the element, the sacrament is made.

## C. LIIII. When the word approaches the element, the sacrament is made

> Take away i the word — and what is the water, but water? Add—

**a. ¶Paulianists.** Who used to say that Christ had not always existed, but had his beginning from Mary, as below *24. q. 3. quidam. §. Paulianistae*.

**b. ¶Cataphrygians.** The Cataphrygians say that the Holy Spirit is a little man, &c. as in the said *c. quidam. v. Cataphrygae*.

**c. ¶Again.** Argument that one not baptized receives no order. *Extra de presbyt. non bapti. c. si quis presbyter*. Below in the same title, *c. si quis per ignorantia. §. ea. cum Paulus*.

**d. ¶Deposed.** In fact, or “deposed” — that is, not admitted.

**e. ¶Paulianists.** These did not baptize in the form of the Church; but the Novatians did; but they rebaptize those already baptized. But those who have been baptized by them in the form of the Church, if they communicate with them in heresy, then they do not receive the effect of the sacrament, which no one without faith can obtain.

**f. ¶Others.** This §. refers back to the §. set above, *sed notandum*.

**g. ¶In his own country.** Because no prophet is accepted in his own country.

**h. ¶Could not.** That is, would not, or because it did not befit his power. Argument *32. q. 5. si Paulus*, because they themselves were unbelieving. But could you not [also] cause that they not be unbelieving, and so work miracles? For the rule of law says that he who can cause that he can [do something] is said to be able, as *ff. de regu. iur. qui potest*. But that rule is understood of one who is bound by necessity, and is constrained to do something.

**¶Take away the word. CASE.** Here it is shown that if the evangelical word be added to water in the proper form, a sacrament is made: in which there is so great a power, that it touches the body and washes the heart — not, however, because the word is said, but because faith is given to it. Following *§. pater*.
