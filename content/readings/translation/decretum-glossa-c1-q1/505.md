¶We should consider not the life of priests but what they teach and minister.

## C. LXXXV. You have moreover signified that many.

> You have moreover signified that many in the very ecclesiastical honors are observed not to live congruously a with the sermons and sacraments which through them are ministered to the people. O wretched men, who, looking at these, forget Christ — who long before foretold that the law of God should rather be obeyed than that those should appear as objects of imitation who do not do the things they say. And tolerating his betrayer until the end, he sent him even to preach the Gospel with the others. For the Apostles did not have such a custom, nor did they teach it to be held b.

*Likewise Pope Nicholas to the Emperor Michael, in the epistle which begins, [I had proposed].*

¶On the same.

## C. LXXXVI. Not what kind the priests of the Lord may be.

> Not what kind the priests of the Lord may be, but what they speak concerning the Lord, must be the chief object of your foresight. Nor in the vicars of blessed Peter the Apostle should you attend to who they are, but what they busy themselves about for the correction of the churches and for your salvation. For c surely you will not say these are inferior to the scribes and Pharisees sitting on the seat of Moses, of whom the Lord commanded, saying: [Whatever they shall say to you, observe and do; but according to their works do not act.] Therefore, Emperor, consider: if he said that those who sat on the seat of Moses ought to be heard, how much more do you think one ought to listen to those who reside on the seat of Peter?

*Likewise Augustine, against the letters of Petilianus, book 2, c. 22.*

¶The uncleanness of priests does not pollute the sacraments.

## C. LXXXVII. The Lord declared in one assembly of men taking the same sacraments.

> The Lord declared d in one assembly e of men taking the same sacraments, that the uncleanness of some cannot harm the clean. *And below.* Petilianus f said: Or if anyone holds the songs of a priest by memory, is he a priest because he publishes the song with a sacrilegious mouth...

**¶You have signified. CASE.** Here he proves by a twofold example of Christ that bad priests must be tolerated: both because he taught that the Pharisees and Scribes were to be heard but not to be imitated in evil works; and because he sent Judas his betrayer to preach the Gospel with the other disciples, and tolerated him until the end. Nor did even the Apostles despise the sacraments of such men.

**a. ¶Not congruously.** Therefore they were avoiding their sacred rites.

**b. ¶They taught.** Therefore neither should we presume or invent [it], as *above dist. 37. qui de mensa, below 2. q. 5. consuluisti. below 31. q. 1. q. si, above dist. 55. si euangelica, above dist. 45. cap. episcopum. 7. q. 1. c. 1.*

**¶Not what kind. CASE.** Some despised the sacraments of bad priests, but Nicholas proves this must not be done: because if bad priests sitting on the seat of Moses are to be heard, much more those governing the seat of Peter are to be heard.

**c. ¶For [you will] not [say these].** Note the manner of arguing from bad to good. *De consec. dist. 1. c. 1. J. 11. q. 1. sacerdotibus, above dist. 21. submittitur 1.*

¶From this some elicit that Peter is greater than Moses. Argument, etc.

**¶The Lord declared. CASE.** Petilianus said that through a wicked priest true sacraments cannot be...

**d. ¶The Lord declared.** In the parables, [namely] about separating the tares from the wheat. *11. q. 3. nolite.* And about the goats, as *23. q. 6. c. 1.* Or he declared by enduring Judas. *2. q. 1. j. unus ex uobis.* ¶Whether tares are to be separated from the wheat. Or the Church, where there are good and bad. *32. q. 4. recurrat.* ADDITION. *De consec. dist. 4. est unitas ecclesiae.* Archd.

**e. ¶Assembly.** That is, the gathering of the Apostles.

**f. ¶Petilianus said.** By way of objection. For he said that a bad priest could not consecrate, because he is not a true priest — just as a layman cannot.

¶Errors of Petilianus. Note that Augustine in this chapter disproves two opinions of Petilianus, who said that the uncleanness of the wicked harms the clean; he also said that bad priests did not confer or consecrate true sacraments. Therefore he says in the first part that the uncleanness of the wicked does not harm the good and clean. In the second he sets forth a certain objection of Petilianus which does not touch the issue, and he resolves it. In the third he shows that the sacraments, if they be conferred in the form of the Church, are true [when conferred] by anyone; and this he shows by a triple example: of Caiaphas, of the Apostle, and of David. In the fourth he shows that what kind those are who receive the sacraments, such are the sacraments themselves to them. In the fifth he infers a twofold conclusion against the twofold opinion of Petilianus, giving thanks to God that Petilianus has acquiesced in his arguments. John.

¶By the error of Petilianus, you would not know a wicked priest of God [?]
