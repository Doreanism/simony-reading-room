# Causa 1, Question 1

*(Continuing canon C. CXXV from p. 527.)*

> or who has attempted to snatch anything thence: and likewise those who act contrary to right and the laws, so that contributions may not be paid, or who hinder, or attempt to hinder them — we order them to be excluded from every honor, dignity, and military service, and to be infamous.

*From the Code,* de iudicibus, l. iubemus.

## C. CXXVI. We command that all judges may be accused

> We command that all judges may be accused whom our highness has placed in charge of any administration: so that, the imperial commands being observed, they may more easily be restrained. We exhort accusers, however, that they should make those guilty of proven crimes defendants not by personal injury, but with their dignity taken from them.

*See same title of the Code,* l. cum venalem, *and* ff. ad legem Iul. repet., *and* ad SC. Turpil., *and* ad l. Iul. de ambitu.

*Likewise from the Code,* de assess.

## C. CXXVII. On the same

> To the administrators, or to whomsoever discharges any offices, we give license to accuse those who in their administrations have stained themselves with venal sentences: nor under the pretext of honor or dignity shall their crimes be left unpunished.

*See same title of the Code, & ff. ad l. Iul. de ambitu. & ff. de his qui not. inf.*

*Likewise from the same title,* de assess., *bk. 3.*

## C. CXXVIII. On the same

> If you think that you have any jurisdiction against a *magister militiae* \[master of the soldiery\]: do not be prohibited from summoning him to court in the customary manner: nor should their officials be prohibited from defending their own defenders.

*See same title of the Code,* l. 1. *and* l. seq.

**a. ¶We command.** If according to this law all judges can be accused, much more ecclesiastical judges: or not, because it is a lesser dignity, as *infra eod. q. omnes.*

**¶We command all.** CASUS. The Emperor commands that all judges, even of the higher dignity, can be accused: and this that the rumor of their wickedness may come to them. Yet he does not wish that such be deprived of their honor `[?]`.

**b. ¶Whom.** He says this for this reason, that even higher judges might be accused. Argument *de off. praet. de praef. praet. ff. de adv. de divers. iudic. ff. de offic. praef. praet. l. 1. & ext. de cohaer.*

**c. ¶We exhort.** Perhaps he tempers his speech, lest he seem to order someone unwilling to accuse. *C. ut nemo invitus. in rubro & nigro.*

**d. ¶Administrators.** If he is such a one whom in jurisdiction `[?]`.

**¶As *extra de immu. ecc. iter.* and *c. ult. 17. q. 4. omnes.* and the following chapter. Arch.**

**¶We command. CASUS.** It is said in this chapter that if any holder, without administration, who is a *decurio* or a colonist of the republic, has been shaken down by some judge — that is, burdened in money — likewise if the judge has rendered a venal sentence: or has imposed or remitted a penalty for a price: or has been found wicked by some reason — before the administration or after, he can be accused: and if convicted, he will be removed.

**a. ¶We command.** To this end he introduces these laws, that he might show that those are to be punished with an equal penalty, who, induced by a price, hinder what by law ought to be done, and those who do what by law ought not to be done.

**b. ¶We exhort.** Perhaps he tempers his speech, lest he seem to order someone unwilling to accuse. *C. ut nemo invitus. in rubro & nigro.*

**c. ¶Administrators.** If he is such a one whom it is lawful to summon in jurisdiction. But to the contrary *2. q. 1. prohibetur.* Sol.: that of the higher magistrates; this of the lower. Argument *ff. de iniur. nec magistratib. & ff. de in ius voc. l. 2.* Or this of the perpetual judge. Argument *ff. de re. cred. praesidis. & l. praecedenti. in auth. ut differentes iudices. §. si tamen. col. 9.* And this lest his crime remain unavenged. That of the temporal. Or here, when he has sinned in his administration itself `[?]` committed in administration. *auth. ut iud. sine quoquo suff.*

**d. ¶About to bring back.** To bring back, *infra l. prox. 12. q. 2. cum dec.*

**¶In whom. CASUS.** He who by calumny calumniates someone in a case, when he was bound by law to act, \[shall pay\] fourfold; afterwards in similar `[?]`.

**e. ¶Useful.** From the beginning: afterwards it is done for a year.

**f. ¶Fourfold.** To the extent that the triple alone may be. *§. si.* And this is true when so much would be given on his behalf, then he who gave the whole claims back the money. If anyone has received such a sum from someone, it shall be given to the treasury: but half to the accuser. *Ad hoc C. de assess. l. 3.*

**g. ¶Pertains.** Not to him who `[?]`.
