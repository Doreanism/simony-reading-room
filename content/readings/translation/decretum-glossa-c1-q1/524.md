## C. CXVI. On the same

> We have forbidden under strict interdict to accept anything for ordinations. It is therefore necessary that, neither for advantage, nor through favor, nor through certain persons’ supplication, you should consent or permit anyone to be brought to holy orders.

*Likewise to all the bishops constituted throughout the province of Hellas, same book, epist. 56 or c. 100.*

*¶Let none approach holy orders by price, by entreaty, by favor, or by supplication.*

## C. CXVII. On the same

> From the report of certain persons I have learned that in those parts no one comes to a holy order without the giving of an advantage. If this is so, weeping I say, groaning I announce, that since the sacerdotal order has fallen within, it will not be able to stand long outside either. ¶For we know from the Gospel what our Redeemer did by himself: that, entering the temple, he overturned the seats of those selling doves. To sell doves indeed is to receive temporal advantage for the Holy Spirit (whom God Almighty, consubstantial with himself, bestows upon men through the laying on of hands). From which, as I have said, it is suggested what follows: that those who presumed to sell doves in the temple of God — God being judge — their seats have fallen. ¶Which error indeed is propagated with increase among subjects. For he himself, who is brought to a holy order, already corrupted in the very root of his advancement, is the more ready to sell to others what he bought. **a. ¶Love.** From this gather that every man is a simoniac who, led by love, confers a benefice on someone unworthy: argument *extra de simo. nemo.* For when the favor of a third party introduces too great a favor, much more that of the very person who is being ordained. Or take this in this sense, that this understanding ceases — “by the unjust love of his heart,” that is, by cupidity and ambition.

**b. ¶Attendance.** That is, service.

**c. ¶Fraudulent.** A gift is fraudulent when one says that he had wished to give this to him otherwise, as *extra ead. & si quaestiones.* Or when one party says he had incurred expenses, and asks that these be repaid from the benefice, as *extra de pact. cum pridem.*

**d. ¶Not voluntarily.** The same applies to any crime: but this is said for the sake of terror. And note that he says “voluntarily”: because if he were compelled to leave the order of law, then unless \[done\] voluntarily, it does not avail for salvation.

**e. ¶Death.** From this (as I said) in any crime penance does not avail for salvation, unless he perseveres in mind to the end. *de pe. distin. 1. § his autem. ver. item illud.* Joannes. And though for all other crimes one may do penance while retaining the order, as *82. dist. presbyter. 50. dist. de his.* — here not, unless he renounces the order. *extra de si. tanta.* Whence it does not seem that admonition or denunciation has any place here. Argument to the contrary *7. q. ult. c. patet. dic ut extra de accusatio.* upon these.

**¶On ordinations.** ¶Supplication.\] *7. e. latorem.* Contra: where the Pope, at the supplication of certain persons, ordains someone. But distinguish: either someone asks for himself, or another for him, as *7. ea. ordinationes. 8. q. 1. § hi.* Or say: there \[the case is\] of carnal \[entreaty\], here of spiritual.
