¶On those who, through ignorance, are ordained before being baptized.

## C. LIX. On those who, through ignorance, are ordained before being baptized

> If anyone e is ordained through ignorance before he is baptized, those baptized by him must be baptized again, and he himself shall not be ordained further f. But the Roman Pontiff judges not the human being who baptizes, but the Spirit of God to administer the grace of baptism, even if he who baptizes be a pagan.

*Burchard and Ivo cite this chapter from the sayings of Isidore: from which form of citation it is customarily indicated that these are not the proper words of the author. It seems, moreover, that in the first part either a question is posed, or someone’s false opinion is recited, to which then in the latter part the true view is opposed. “But the Roman,” etc., which is also cited below in* de consec. dist. 4. cap. Romanus *from Isidore. And this is more clearly explained in book 5 of the* Capitularia, c. 4 *in these words: If anyone has been baptized by an unbaptized presbyter, and the holy Trinity was invoked in that baptism, he has been baptized, as Pope Sergius said: he does, however, need the imposition of the hand. Gregory, the Roman bishop, and John the Sacellarius held this view.*

## C. LX. Palea

> If a presbyter, once ordained, discovers that he has not been baptized, let him be baptized, and let him be ordained again a.

*Although this Palea is so designated, it is cited from the* tom. collectio *which is in the volumes of councils after the decrees of Alexander III, and chapter* de presbytero non baptizato *from the council at Compiègne.*

*α. ¶Again. There followed: “and all whom he previously baptized”: which has been struck out, because it is not contained in the said c. 1.* de presbytero non bapt., *nor in c.* veniens *of the same title, where it is reported, although it is contained in the other places indicated.*

## C. LXI. Things offered by heretics are not pleasing to God

> Such is the people, and such is the nation of all heretics in my sight, says the Lord; whatever they do, in that the sacrament of baptism received from a heretic in necessity or in ignorance is valid h, yet the other sacraments cannot be administered by them: and if performed by them, they are empty, and this is understood by the following chapters on the sacraments of heretics, in which the form of the church is omitted, or when they are received from them apart from necessity, and knowingly.

**a. ¶Baptism.** Argument that a sacrament is not to be repeated, even if it is not perfect.

**b. ¶Should not.** Argument that dissimulation ought not to prejudice anyone. *l. 5. dist. 22. quod interrogatur. J. 21. q. 5. eccle. inf. de consec. dist. 4. solet quaeri,* against. *J. ca. 12. q. 2. quod ait. ff. de act. & oblig. obligationum.*

**c. ¶But completed.** Argument *23. dist. quorum:* and in *l. dam. 7. q. 1. nihil.*

**d. ¶These things which.** That is, that they should anoint on the crown and breast and shoulders, *extra de sac. non ite. c. 1. in fin.* John.

**¶Arg. on “if anyone.”** Someone, before he was baptized, was ordained a presbyter, and before baptism he baptized many. Now the question arises whether those baptized by him ought to be baptized again, and whether he himself ought to be repelled from promotion. And the Roman Pontiff replies that they ought not to be baptized again: but from promotion [?] he must cease. And he reads the text interrogatively up to that point, “But the Roman Pontiff.”

**e. ¶If anyone.** Some present the case thus. Two questions are put here to Isidore. First, whether those baptized by one ordained before baptism are to be rebaptized. Second, whether he himself ought to be ordained to the presbyterate. And it must be said that he replies to the first question from that point, “But [the] hand.” To the second question he does not reply, but it can be decided through the chapter above in the same title, *si quis confugerit.* And according to this, the whole is read under interrogation up to that point, “but Roman.” But according to this you will supply in the text, “he shall not be ordained further: but,” that is, “because,” etc. And thus he answers one question, not the other. Arg. *extra de praesump. nonne. instit. de inutil. stip. §. quoties. C. de dier. rescrip. l. 1.* and this is true, since they are connected. Others read the chapter *plane* and say that the baptized by him ought to be rebaptized. But they speak wrongly; whence “But the Roman” is corrective. “And he himself shall not be ordained further,” and this is corrected above in the same title, *si quis confugerit.* Or here he speaks of strict law according to Isidore, according to the case which Isidore poses, according to the false opinion of certain ones, namely that such are to be rebaptized, whence he immediately corrects himself there, “but Roman.” As if to say, so others say, but Roman, etc.

**f. ¶Shall not be ordained.** Of strict law, otherwise it holds. *extra de cler. non ordi. ministran. c. 1.*

**g. ¶Pagan.** *Sup. dist. 32. §. verum. inf. de cons. dist. 4. quos à paganis.* Against, but there they were baptized apart from the form of the church; here, however, it treats of that baptism which is administered in the form of the church.

**¶QVOD AVTEM. CASE.** It was said above that the sacrament of baptism received from a heretic in necessity or in ignorance is valid, but the other sacraments cannot be administered by them [?].

**h. ¶Cannot.** With effect freely. Note that what is done freely harms. Likewise, note that it is to be considered not what one does, but on whose authority one does it.

**¶Such is the people. CASE.** He says in this chapter that the offerings of heretics or their vows, whether for peace or for sins or for the church [?], their fasts, alms, and songs, are all contaminated in the sight of God. The following chapters up to chapter *si quis inquit* are plain: because they speak of the same matter, and the same thing is repeated, and you will see for yourself.
