*(Continuing canon C. LXI from p. 494.)*

> they have offered to me, whether vows a, or for welfare b, or peace-offerings c, or for sin, or for transgression, or as a holocaust d, whether alms, or fasts, or continence of food and chastity of body, they shall be contaminated in my sight e. For although the things offered by such people may appear holy in their outward form f, yet, because they have been touched by one who is polluted in soul, all things shall be polluted.

*α. ¶Such is the people. In the original it reads, “Such is this people, and such is that nation,” namely of the Jews, and of the Gentiles, and of all heretics, etc.*

*β. ¶Vows. In the same place it has, “or vows for welfare,” but on account of the gloss it has not been altered.*

*Likewise on the prophet Amos, c. 5.*

¶On the same.

## C. LXII. On the same

> God hates the sacrifices of heretics, and casts them from himself; and as often as they are gathered together under the name of the Lord, he detests their stench, and closes his nostrils g.

*The same, on Hosea, c. 6.*

¶On the same.

## C. LXIII. On the same

> They offer sacrilegious bread, and give alms, and seem to follow humility. Such things, if they are done truly h, I interpret as holocausts. But since they have abandoned the knowledge of God, in vain do they boast that they have the other members of faith when the head i has been cut off.

*The same on the Epistle to the Galatians, c. 1.*

¶Without faith and charity neither the Gospel is had, nor do any good works profit.

## C. LXIIII. Without faith and charity neither the Gospel is had, nor do any good works profit

> Marcion k, and Basilides l, and the other plagues of heretics do not have the Gospel of God; because they do not have the Holy Spirit, without which the Gospel that is taught becomes merely human m, n. Nor should we think that the Gospel consists in the words of the Scriptures, but in their meaning o; not on the surface, but in the marrow; not in the leaves of words, but in the root of reason.

*The same, on c. 5 of the same epistle.*

¶On the same.

## C. LXV. On the same

> *Such things we suffer.* See how great a good charity is. If we have so suffered martyrdom that we wish our relics to be honored by men; if, following the opinion of the crowd, we have fearlessly poured out our blood, and have given our substance even to our own destitution; to such a work p, not so much a reward as a punishment is owed, and the torments of perfidy rather than the crown q of victory.

*Likewise from the Council of Laodicea, c. 32.*

¶The blessing of heretics is a curse.

## C. LXVI. The blessing of heretics is a curse

> One ought not to receive the blessings of heretics: since they are curses rather than blessings.

**a. ¶Or vows.** This is general, and afterward he subdivides it and the parts.

**b. ¶Or for welfare.** He says “for welfare” and “for sin” as though they were names of sacrifices: of which one was offered for preserving welfare, the other for purging sins. Some manuscripts do not have the second, according to H.

**c. ¶Or peace-offerings.** Thus was called the sacrifice that was offered for peace either obtained or to be obtained.

**d. ¶Or as a holocaust.** *Holon* (whole), *cauma* (burning). *50. dist. ponderet. versic. vna.*

**e. ¶In my sight.** For ecclesiastical dignity does not make Christians, as in *5. 1. q. 7. non omnes episcopi.*

**f. ¶In their outward form.** As to the surface.

**g. ¶Closes his nostrils.** In a similar way *24. q. 1. odi.*

**h. ¶Truly.** That is, out of charity and faith. *de poe. dist. 4. c. penul. & vlt.*

**i. ¶With the head cut off.** That is, with faith, which is the foundation of all good things. *sup. ead. cum Paulus.*

**k. ¶Marcion.** This Marcion said that there are two principles, one of higher things, the other of lower things. *24. q. 3. quidam. versi. & Marcionitae.*

**l. ¶Basilides.** This man denied that Christ was [?] baptized [?] *24. quaest. 3. cap. quidam.*

**m. ¶Human.** That is, not divine, not conferring grace.

**n. ¶Gospel.** In words. Argument that words serve things, not things words; for intention is preferred to words. arg. *13. q. 1. c. 2. verb. verba.* arg. *38. dist. sedulo.* arg. *22. q. 5. humanae.* and *q. 2. ne quis.* arg. *23. q. 1. paratus.* and *q. 8. occidit.* arg. *29. dist. regulae.*

**o. ¶But in the meaning.** arg. *11. q. 3. antecessor. &c. quod praedecessor.* arg. *ff. de leg. & senatusc. scire leges.* arg. *extra de verb. sig. propterea. & c. intelligentia.* arg. *22. q. 2. is qui.*

**p. ¶Work.** See how much it is a sin to give alms for vainglory. arg. *23. q. 1. militare. 24. q. 1. prodi.* [?]

**q. ¶Than the crown.** *supra dist. 90. neque, in fi. & c. si quis autem infra de poe. dist. 3. §. pe.* against. arg. Augustine says that if anyone who lacks charity suffers martyrdom, even if it does not profit him unto eternal life, it nevertheless profits him at least toward bearing a lesser punishment. Solution: there he is dealing with those who, with good zeal but not in accord with conscience, undergo martyrdom; here, with those who do so arrogantly, as is hinted here. John.

**¶One ought not. Blessings.** That is, sacraments, or doctrines: or he says this ironically, blessings [?]

*Editorial marginal notes: Martyrdom does not always profit toward eternal salvation. Error of the heretic Basilides: that Christ was not baptized [?] in the name.*

*Decret. Grat. 2. ps. III*
