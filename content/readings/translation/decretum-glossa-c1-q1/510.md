> ...sacraments of dignity conferred by heretics do not have effect. Against this he alleges the authority of Anastasius, which he shows to be frivolous by the authority of Gelasius and Felix.

**a. ¶In the treatise.** From this it is proved that Gratian did not divide this work by distinctions. Argument *11. q. 3. §. evidenter.*

**ITEM OBIICITVR. CASE.** It was said above in [the chapter] *Arrianos*, and above in §. *cum ergo Innocentius*, that baptism alone [?] is true among heretics. Against this an objection is now raised in this chapter.

**b. ¶Likewise objected.** Namely, against what was said above [in the] same. §. *cum ergo Innocentius*. &c. *Arrianos*.

*Likewise that [text] of Augustine, book 2 against the epistle of Parmenianus, c. 13, is objected b, [in which Anastasius] approves the iteration, asserting that even those whom he [Acacius] baptized or ordained after his condemnation [were unharmed]. But that he did this illicitly and not canonically — indeed, contrary to the decrees of his predecessors — is proved by Felix and Gelasius, who excommunicated Acacius before Anastasius, and by Hormisdas, who, the third [in line] from this same Anastasius, condemned the same Acacius. Whence also he [Anastasius] is repudiated by the Roman church, and is read to have been smitten by God in the deeds of the Roman pontiffs in this manner: [Anastasius the Second, etc.] Look back in the treatise a of the decretal epistles.*

> ¶He who departs from the Church loses neither baptism nor the right of giving [it].

## C. XCVII. What some say.

> WHat some c say — namely, that he who departs from the Church does not lose the baptism which he received, but does lose the right of giving d [it] e — appears in many ways to be said in vain and emptily. ¶First, because no reason is shown why he who cannot lose his baptism f can lose the right of giving [it] g. For each is a sacrament; and for what reason one is lost, [for that same reason] the other [is lost], since each is given h to man i — the one when he is baptized, the other when he is ordained — and for this reason in the Catholic Church neither is permitted to be repeated. ¶For if at any time, from that same side k, even leaders of schism, coming over, have been received in error, although it seemed needful that they discharge the same offices that they had been discharging, they are not to be reordained †; but just as baptism remained intact in them, so also their ordination, because the fault had been in the separation (which has been corrected †), not in the sacraments l, which wherever they are m, are themselves true. And although it appears expedient for the Church that their leaders, coming to catholic society, should not administer their honors there...

[*† otherwise: “corrected” † otherwise: “ordained” † otherwise: “peace of unity”*]

**c. ¶What some [say].** CASE. It is the opinion of some that a priest or bishop departing from the Church to the heretics does not lose the sacrament of baptism, but does lose the sacrament of orders. Augustine refutes this in many ways. First, thus: since each is a sacrament, no reason can be assigned why one should be lost rather than the other. Secondly, thus: because the custom of the Church is that, when such men return, they are not accustomed to be reordained, if the Church wishes to tolerate them. Thirdly, he says that just as one departing from the Church retains both, so it seems that he can confer both upon others outside the Church, though he confers wrongly; and when someone receives a sacrament outside the Church, if he returns to the Church, the sacrament is not to be repeated in him. And this he proves.

**d. ¶Of giving.** Namely, [holy] orders.

**e. ¶Loses.** For the character he cannot lose, although it seems to be said otherwise *11. q. 3. episcopus*.

**f. ¶To lose.** For “where there is the same reason, there is the same law.” *ff. ad leg. Aquil. illud.* Whether however this be true, is noted *28. q. 1. sic enim. 32. q. 7. Apostolus. above ea. q. §. cum ergo*.

**g. ¶Right of giving.** Namely, of orders. As shown at *de consec. distinct. 4.* Or the power of conferring baptism, *below 32. q. 7. licite.*

**h. ¶Each.** Namely, baptism and orders.

**i. ¶To man.** This [refers] to the baptism of a priest, in whom the power of baptizing is a sacrament; in a layman it is a kind of license [?], not a sacrament. 2. Argument that the knowledge of a heretic [?] is valid, and binding and loosing, since he received the right of binding, judging, and loosing from the time of consecration. *S. dist. 48. 3. ca. 1.*

**k. ¶Side.** For an assembly of heretics is most rightly called a “side” [*pars*], from “partitioning,” that is, dividing.

**l. ¶Not in the sacraments.** [The vice] was [in the separation, that is].

**m. ¶They are.** Because by whomever they are given, they should not be repeated.
