# Causa 1, Question 1

*(Continuing C. XXI from p. 474; Tarasius’s letter to Pope Hadrian in the 16th-century editors’ Latin translation. Content rendered:)*

> \[For those who lay on hands are\] ministers, by the voice of the Lord, having received this very freedom and power — so that if anyone is convicted of having bought it with gold, \[the canons\] pronounce him fallen from the priestly order. Though he has obtained the name of “priest,” it is proved by the very thing itself that this title is falsely conferred upon him. For no one can serve God and mammon, as we have learned from the Gospel. And since we have heard God crying out through the mouth of the prophet, \[“Priests, speak to the heart of Jerusalem,”\] and again threatening and saying, \[“If the watchman sees the sword coming and does not sound the trumpet, and the people do not guard themselves, and the sword comes and takes a soul from them; he himself is indeed caught in his iniquity, but his blood I will require from the watchman’s hand,”\] from the fear of this condemnation which threatens through silence, we make announcement to all the heads of the churches that are among us; so that, with confidence imitating the divine Apostle, we may say: “We are clean of the blood” of those who transgress the canonical constitutions, and far more of those who have ordained or have been ordained for money — since Peter the divine Apostle, whose chair your brotherly sanctity also has obtained, deposed these as a Simon Magus. Wherefore we do not shrink from proclaiming the truth, observing and holding fast those things which have been canonically handed down by the holy and ever-blessed Apostles and by our illustrious fathers. And if any of these things has been violated by anyone, we turn away from it. Your brotherly sanctity, then, fitting to the highest priesthood, having lawfully and aptly, according to the will of God, ministered priestly sanctification, has won celebrated glory. For Christ, the prior and great Pontiff, said through the prophet, \[“As I live, I will glorify those who glorify me.”\] For you know, O man whose desires are of the Spirit, that the heresy of Macedonius and of those who follow him, impugners of the Holy Spirit, is far more tolerable. For while they prattle that the Holy Spirit is a creature and servant of God the Father, these — so far as they suppose — make him their own servant. For every master sells whatever he has when he wishes, whether a slave or any other of his possessions. Likewise too the buyer, wishing to become the lord of the thing bought, acquires it for a price of silver. Thus those who admit this nefarious deed press down the Holy Spirit, sinning equally with those who said with blasphemous mouth that Christ cast out demons by Beelzebub; or, to speak more truly, they are like Judas the traitor, who sold the Lord to the Jews, the killers of God, for a price of silver. Since therefore the Holy Spirit is consubstantial with Christ God, he is also to be counted with him, as we have shown. And since he is not sold — as he certainly is not — it is most fitting also that the grace of the Holy Spirit, as also the sanctification of the priesthood, is not in these; and what they did not receive, they do not have. Let them remember the holy Peter, who said this to the one who attempted it: \[“You have no part nor lot in this word.”\] For if the dignity of the priesthood is sold, then an honorable institution of life and the cultivation of purity and virtue are superfluous among them; superfluous among them too is Paul the divine Apostle, teaching that a bishop must be without crime, temperate, modest, apt to teach, continent, sober, holding fast to the faithful word according to the doctrine, that he may be able both to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict. All these things, therefore, perish from the fact that the priesthood is sold and bought. The authorities cited below, then, gathered from the saints, pronounce wholly alien from the priesthood the man who at any time has given or received \[money\], whether before the ordination, at the ordination, or after the ordination. For to receive is to receive at any time. They also command that all ecclesiastical promotions, in which money has intervened, be void.

*From the Council of Braga, ch. 3.*

## C. XXII. On the same

> Let him who gives and him who receives, in order that sacred orders be conferred, be anathema.

## C. XXIII. \[On the same\]

> It is resolved, concerning the a ordination of clerics, that bishops accept no gifts: but, as it is written, what they receive freely from the giving God, freely let them give. The grace of God and the imposition of hands are not to be sold for any price: because the ancient definition of the fathers thus decreed concerning ecclesiastical ordinations, saying: \[“Anathema to the giver and to the receiver.”\] Therefore, since certain men, weighed down by many crimes `[?]`, while ministering unworthily at the holy altar, obtain this \[ministry\] not by the testimony of good deeds nor by the lavishness of gifts; clerics must b be ordained not by the favor of gifts, but first through diligent examination, and then through the testimony of many c.

**a. ¶It is resolved that concerning.** Argument that this prohibition was instituted on account of the unworthy: yet it extends to all. *J. eadem q. 3. pervenit. Infra 19. q. 2. duae.*

**b. ¶Must.** *J. Supra dist. 83. cap. 1.*

**c. ¶By the testimony of many.** *Supra distinct. 67. reliqui*, at the end, contra. *Sup. 23. distin. lector*, contra; and *20. quaest. 2. oportet*; *Si sup. distin. 75. cap. 5.* Solution: here “many” means two or three. Or understand this when the clerics are unknown.
