> plague. *16. quaest. 1. cap. nec mirum.*

*¶48. §. 9.*

> The Apostle raised up a dead man, [yet] Simon Magus performed many [wonders]. There were many Christians there who could do neither what Peter did, nor what Simon did. But why did they rejoice? Because their names were written in heaven.

*Likewise Leo to bishop Nicetas, letter 77 [al. 79] c. 7.*

¶Baptism once handed down is not to be repeated.

## C. LVII. Baptism once handed down is not to be repeated

> This a rule, as you know, we preach to be observed in all the churches: that the laver once begun be violated by no repetition, the Apostle saying: [One Lord, one faith, one baptism.] Whose washing is to be profaned by no repetition; but, as we have said, only the sanctification of the Holy Spirit is to be invoked, so that what no one b receives from heretics may be obtained from catholic priests.

> *10. By “no one” b is to be understood [no one] established in heresy or schism. But if with integrity of faith and purity of soul anyone has received baptism from the hand of heretics in the form c of the Church: then is fulfilled that saying of Augustine: “Through a stone channel d, water also passes to the garden-plots.” And again: [The spiritual virtue of the sacrament, &c.]*

*The same, sermon 20 on the words of the Lord, on blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.*

¶Even through evil men the good are gathered.

## C. LVIII. Even through evil men the good are gathered

> The Holy Spirit in the holy Church, even at this time, when † the threshing-floor [orig. how] is threshed as grain with chaff, so works that he despises no one’s true confession, is deceived e by no one’s pretense, and shuns the reprobate f in such a way that even through their ministry he gathers the upright g.

> *¶Here is when † baptism from heretics is received with its virtue: which administration is so necessary, that even what is given by pagans h cannot be repeated. Whence in the ecclesiastical history it is read that Alexander, bishop of Alexandria, when he was celebrating the solemnities of the martyr Peter, after the sacraments of the Masses were completed, saw on the seashore the play of boys i imitating, as is wont to be done, a bishop, and performing those things which are customarily done in the church. He at once ordered them to be brought before him, and inquired what they had done. They unfolded the order of the matter, confessing that certain catechumens had been baptized by them through Athanasius k, a boy who had played the bishop among them. When from their responses he saw that everything was in accordance with the rite of our religion, he decreed that those over whom water had been poured with complete questions l and responses should [not] be repeated*

**a. ¶This.** This is part of that c. 3 in the same [title], *hi qui*. And he repeats here the end of that chapter so as to expound it in the following §.

**NO ONE.** This is the tenth part of the question, in which it is said that baptism can be given by anyone: and this up to §. *hoc ergo*. Joan. de Fant[utiis].

**b. ¶No one.** Argument bearing on the question of the intruder, whether through him a cleric can be presented to a church, and whether a just sentence may be given through a heretic. *De consec. dist. 2*. And Lord Joan. said that both are false, because the intruder is not tolerated, and the heretic is excommunicated.

**c. ¶Form.** Namely, in necessity, or in ignorance.

**d. ¶Through a stone channel.** *Ca. si iustus*.

**¶The Holy Spirit. CASE.** It is said here that the Holy Spirit is deceived by no one’s pretense, yet uses the faithful in such a way that through their ministry he gathers the good.

**e. ¶Is deceived.** For we may lie to God, but we cannot deceive him. *De consec. dist. 2. panem*. For all things are certain to him. *ff. de aedil*. But also f. *retentatur*. §. *vlti*. Argument to the contrary *81. dist. oportet. 16. q. 1. revertimini*. *F. ea. c. si iustus*.

**f. ¶The reprobate.** Argument that the religion of another should not prejudice anyone in his own cause; for one should not be the more believed because he works signs and miracles, since these are common to good and to evil [men], as above ca. *multa*.

**g. ¶The upright.** Because they receive with that heart [of faith]. *Infr. eo. non nocet*.

**HERE.** This [is from] Hu[guccio], and the history set down in it is plain from the text.

**h. ¶By pagans.** If given in the form of the Church. *De consec. distinct. 4. à quodam*.

**i. ¶Of boys.** Capable of deceit. Otherwise they would do nothing — for such [children] are compared to the insane. *ff. de acquiren. posses. l. prima, §. primo. 1 c. quaest. 1. illud*. But who is capable of deceit, the law tells, namely, he who is near to puberty. *ff. de regu. iur. pupillum*. But the ignorant acquires something. *ff. quemadmo. ser. amit. si partem. §. 2*.

**k. ¶Through Athanasius.** A proper name.

**l. ¶With complete questions.** By the fact that he says “complete,” it is clear that the deed was not done in jest: and so that question is not resolved here. Argument *de conse. dis. 4. solet quaeri*. Although some say it is resolved here. But I say also that, even though he receives the form in play, provided that he intends to baptize, he baptizes. But if all things are done in jest, it was not baptism. Argument *22. q. 2. c. quod ait. ff. de actio. & oblig. obligationum* [?]

*Editorial marginal note: One baptized in jest is not baptized.*

*Decret. Grat. ps. ii. II*
