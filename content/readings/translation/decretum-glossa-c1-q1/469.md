> \[is prolonged\] by the greed of possessing — \[which sentence\] is condemned by prophetic authority (especially a since our Lord Jesus Christ has by the laver b of regeneration given to all the remission of sins) — unless you understand “seed” to mean rather \[the seed\] of vices c than of birth. For just as those who are sons of the promise are reckoned as good seed, so too those who are sons of error are reckoned as evil seed. For the Jews too are from the devil their father — not, of course, by succession of the flesh, but of crime. Therefore all the covetous, all the greedy, possess Giezi’s leprosy along with their wealth, and from their ill-gotten gain have gathered up not so much a patrimony of property as a treasury of crimes, for eternal punishment and a brief enjoyment.

*α. ¶Unto the fourth. Earlier it was read “to the third and fourth”: but the word “third” is absent both from Blessed Ambrose and from one ancient codex of Gratian. Now in Deuteronomy 23, the sons of the Edomites and the Egyptians are indeed permitted to enter the church of the Lord in the third generation, but of the Moabites it is written thus: “The Ammonite and the Moabite shall not enter the church of the Lord, even after the tenth generation, forever.”*

*β. ¶Of sins. There followed: “Why then is the seed of Giezi condemned forever?” — which is not found in Blessed Ambrose.*

> § 2. Therefore since by the authority of Pope Leo simoniacs *receive nothing but the spirit of lying*, and according to Gregory among *simoniacs the priesthood does not subsist* γ, *their blessing is turned into a curse*, with them remains the anathema of giver and receiver — since they are not holy, nor are they set within the body of Christ, since they are accursed; they cannot sanctify others, nor hand on or receive the body of Christ, nor have power to bless others; and according to †Ambrose they are *condemned with the reproach of anathema* and are *cut off from participation in the body and blood of Christ*, since gold is received and leprosy given; since *the guilt of the sold mystery is inexpiable*†. According to the Council of Chalcedon they are pronounced to gain nothing by such ordination; according to Gelasius *they are entangled in the condemnation of Simon*; according to Gregory Nazianzen *they cannot by any means remain in sacred orders, or be reordained: by Elisha they are sprinkled with leprosy, by Peter condemned unto perdition, by Christ cast out of the temple* — what else can a simoniac in his ordination confer upon a simoniac, except what Innocent testifies concerning other heretics, saying, in letter eighteen to Alexander, c. 3?

*γ. ¶Does not subsist. Simoniacs do indeed receive the character, but they are suspended ipso facto. Thus in respect of its use it may truly be said that their priesthood does not subsist; and accordingly Blessed Gregory, in bk. 10, ep. 33, writing to Bishop Victor, very rightly calls a simoniacal ordination unlawful and lacking effect.*

## C. XVII. Those who do not have the perfection of the Spirit cannot give it

> Those who have lost the perfection of the Spirit which they had received cannot give the fullness d of it (which operates most of all in ordinations) which they have lost through their faithlessness. *And again.* He who did not have the honor could not give the honor; nor did he receive anything, since there was nothing in \[him by which he could\]

**a. ¶Especially.** It refers to what had been said \[in the word\] *harsher*: or the line begins there, and refers to the following text (namely, *why then in the name of Christ*) — why then *by the laver* `[?]`.

**b. ¶By the laver.** Without which no one has eternal life, unless one has been baptized by blood for the name of Christ, as at *15. q. 1. c. firmissime* and *de confec. dist. 4. cap. firmissime.*

**c. ¶Unless of vices.** He argues sophistically: for the same holds in the case of idolatry; but in the preceding he considers only the literal sense.

**Therefore.** This is the second part of the question, in which he covertly raises the case of those ordained by heretics (that is, outside the form of the church) and says that such are not received except unto damnation. Joh. de Faventia `[?]`. *Casus*. Gratian now concludes from nearly all the foregoing chapters, saying that simoniacs, according to Leo, receive nothing but the spirit of lying; and since according to Gregory *among them the priesthood does not subsist*, and since *their blessing is turned into a curse*, etc., a simoniac can confer nothing else upon a simoniac `[?]` \[than\] a heretic upon a heretic.

**¶Those who the perfection.** *Casus*. This is understood of those who were ordained in the catholic church but afterward lapse into heresy: and so they lost the grace of the Holy Spirit which they had received (which operates most of all in orders). Since therefore they do not have it, they cannot give it; because he who does not have the honor cannot give it to another: but he will give the damnation which he has along with the true order.

**d. ¶Fullness.** That is, the exercise of the order. Here the Master inserts \[a treatment\] on the orders of heretics, no. 1. But whatever Joh. de Faventia `[?]` and others said on this matter `[?]`.
