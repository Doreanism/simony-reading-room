*(Continuing canon C. XXVIII from p. 480.)*

> *…vouches a; what remains, but that gravity defend nothing for itself in ecclesiastical honors, industry defend nothing; but that the profane love of gold obtain everything. And while vices are rewarded with honor, in the place of the avenger* b *, he who perhaps ought to have been avenged* c *is brought in: and from this priests are judged not to profit, but rather to perish. For if the shepherd is wounded, who shall apply medicine to heal the sheep? or how shall he protect the people with the shield of prayer, who exposes himself to be struck by the enemy’s darts? or what kind of fruit will he produce of himself, whose root is infected with grave pestilence? A greater calamity, therefore, is to be feared as coming upon those places, where such intercessors are brought to the place of rule, who provoke* d *the wrath of God upon themselves more, than they ought to have appeased it through themselves on behalf of the peoples.*

> *Since therefore, according to Leo, simony is not brought to a good outcome, according to Gregory no building up of a good work follows, nor is it to be considered alms, what is paid out to the poor from the price of simony; \[since\] when the shepherd is wounded, no medicine is applied to heal the sheep; from an infected root no fruit is produced; and a greater calamity is to be feared in the future; from such a sinister beginning what happy outcome is to be hoped?*

*Hence also Augustine says.*

## C. XXIX. The sacraments of simoniacs lack the effect of grace

> Just as he was a eunuch e, who bought Joseph; so he who buys grace, has no living seed f, his genitals being dried up. So also the fire of sacrifice, which had lived under water g through the seventy years of the Babylonian captivity, was extinguished, with Antiochus h selling the priesthood to Jason: which signified that the fire of the Holy Spirit does not shine i in the sacraments of simoniacs†.

*† al. simoniacorum \[of the simoniacs\].*

> *What did blessed Augustine wish to note in these comparisons, except that the sacraments of simoniacs (as also of other heretics), although they are true as to form, are nevertheless empty and false as to effect, since they cannot beget or kindle heavenly grace in the hearts of men.*

*(Continuation of gloss i. Verumetiam excellentiae from p. 480:)* \[...\] that a thing transacted among others does not harm others generally — for neither does it harm one as to another. Yet in special cases it fails, which are expressly found in the law. Extra *de re iudicata*, cap. *quamuis*. & 6. q. 1. §. *verum*. and in other similar cases, as s. q. 6. §. *diffinitiua*.

**a. ¶Vouches.** For the law says: “Let it be estimated how each one merits, not how much he is sufficient to give.” Codex, *de episcopis et clericis*, *si quemquam*. But it is asked why the pope writes to the king, since he has no power over these matters. 11. q. 1. *sacerdotibus*? Because, since they were participants: or because they were incorrigible, as J. ea. q. ult. c. ult. 17. dist. *nec licuit*. extra *de iud*. *cum non ab homine*.

**b. ¶Avenger.** That is, the prelate, who ought to avenge the vices of others.

**c. ¶Ought to have been avenged.** That is, ought to be punished on account of \[?\] sins.

**d. ¶Provoke.** Upon themselves, not upon those for whom they pray. And so understand: 3. q. 7. *in gravibus*.

**THEREFORE.** Gratian concludes from all that has been said above, that from a bad beginning a good outcome is not to be hoped.

**¶Just as.** THE CASE. It is said in this chapter that just as he was a eunuch who bought Joseph, so they are eunuchs — that is, without the moisture of grace — who commit simony: and in them the fire of the Holy Spirit does not flourish. Which is proved by the example of Antiochus, when he was selling the priesthood, the fire which had lived under water for seventy years, was extinguished. And note two histories in this chapter: namely of Joseph, and of the fire. For we read in Genesis that Joseph was sold by his brothers to the Ishmaelites who were going into Egypt: who later sold him to Potiphar the eunuch of Pharaoh the king: whom I think to have been miraculously made a eunuch, since he had bought Joseph in order to abuse him, when previously it is established that he had a wife and a daughter. Likewise we read in the book of Maccabees that when the children of Israel had been led into perfidy, Jeremiah with certain others hid the fire of sacrifice in a valley where there was a concealed well: but after many years, at the command of the king of Persia, that fire was sought: and when it was found, they drew water with it, with which water the wood that had been arranged was sprinkled, and immediately it burned. For which miracle the king built a temple over that well. But in the course of time, when king Antiochus had received the kingdom and was reigning in Judea, Jason, brother of the high priest, approached the king and offered him 40 talents: and received from him the principate of the high priesthood: and immediately the fire of sacrifice was extinguished. The following two § are plain.

**e. ¶Just as a eunuch.** Potiphar, who first had a wife, daughter of Pharaoh: when he was afterwards ordained priest of Heliopolis, he was castrated. Or as some say, the Lord dried up his genitals, because he had bought Joseph for shameful uses.

**f. ¶Living seed.** Namely, of a good work.

**g. ¶Under water.** Argument: dist. 81. c. *si qui*. Argument however to the contrary: 28. dist. cap. ult. & below, in the same place, *Dominus*. Johannes.

**h. ¶Antiochus.** This Antiochus was a Greek: and he greatly oppressed the Jews, and sold the priesthood of the temple. And it is argument here that in the Old Testament it was simony to buy an order.

**i. ¶Does not shine.** The fire was sent by Jeremiah into the well: which afterwards was never found: but a thick water, placed under stone, would burn it. Concerning this fire you have *de peni*. dist. 3. *inter haec hircum*. And the well was in the Valley of Jehoshaphat: where the king of the Persians had a temple built, and was called by Jeremiah *nepthar*, which is interpreted as “purification.” Johannes.
