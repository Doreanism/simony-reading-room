*(Continuing C. LXX from p. 497.)*

> Moses to Eleazar son of Aaron, that he should take their censers and beat them into plates, and affix them to the altar, that they might be a sign to the sons of Israel, lest anyone dare to approach and offer incense to the Lord, except of the seed of Aaron. The Holy Spirit, following also through the prophet Hosea, testifies and says: [Their sacrifices are as the bread of mourning: all who eat them shall be defiled,] teaching, namely, and showing that absolutely all who are contaminated by their sin are joined in punishment with the authors.

*¶This chapter has been collected from various passages of the letters of blessed Cyprian, and of the council held by him, sometimes altered and reduced to a summary; which is indicated in the margin.*

**a. ¶Thus also through Hosea.** As Hosea 9.

**b. ¶Sin.** That is, by consent to their sin. *8. q. 1. §. cum autem.* and *23. q. 4. ita plane.* and c. *si quis à catholica.*

**¶Censure.** In the epistle itself it is contained thus: [In vain do they contend that one can be baptized and sanctified there by salutary baptism, where it is agreed that the one baptizing does not have the authority to baptize. And so that it might be the more understood, against such audacity what the divine censure is, we shall find that in such a wickedness not only the leaders and authors, but also the participants are appointed to penalties, etc.]

*Likewise Augustine in his book of sentences [collected by Prosper of Aquitaine, bishop of Riez, c. 15.]*

## C. LXXI. True sacrifices are celebrated only in the faith of the church

> Outside the Catholic Church there is no place for true sacrifice.

*Likewise Gregory in his 3rd book of dialogues, c. 31, says of Hermigild son of Leovigild †.*

*† var. Linigild*

## C. LXXII. Communion is not to be received from the hand of heretics

> When the day of the paschal festival came on, in the deep silence of the night his perfidious father sent an Arian bishop to him, that from his hand he might receive the communion of sacrilegious consecration, and through this might merit to return to his father’s favor. But the man devoted to God reproached the Arian bishop as he came, as was fitting, and drove away his perfidy from him with worthy rebukes.

**c. ¶Outside the Catholic Church.** That is, outside the form, i.e., of the church.

**d. ¶True.** By reason of effect.

**¶When the day came on.** CASE. The father of Hermigild sent to his son the Arian heretical bishop, that he might receive communion from his hand, which because the son refused to accept, the father killed him.

**e. ¶Of the night.** There are four parts of the night: *conticinium, intempestum, gallicinium, antelucanum.*

**f. ¶He might receive.** Inquire whether in case of necessity children are to receive baptism from a heretic but not the Eucharist, I have given the reason *24. q. ult. si quem.*

*Likewise Innocent to bishop Alexander, letter 18, c. 3.*

## C. LXXIII. The priests of heretics are not held worthy of the honors of Christ

> The Arians, moreover, and other such plagues, since we receive their lay converts to the Lord, under the image of penance, and by the sanctification of the Holy Spirit, by the imposition of hands; it does not seem that their clerics ought to be received with the dignity of any priesthood or ministry. Since we permit only their baptism to be valid (which indeed is performed in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit), we do not consider that they have the Holy Spirit from that baptism, and from those mysteries.

**¶Arians.** CASE. This chapter is continuous with the said chapter of the same age [?]. For here it is said that heretical laymen, who are held by heretics, are received by the imposition of hands, and reconciled as public penitents: nevertheless clerics who return from them are not received with the dignity of clericate or priesthood; this is because only baptism is valid among them: nor can they be reconciled except under the image of public penance. The following §. *scilicet,* likewise is read, with the following [it] is clear.

**g. ¶Arians.** Priests unworthy of the honors of Christ.

**h. ¶Under the image of penance.** He calls “image of penance” the public reconciliation which is done by imposition of hands.

**i. ¶From baptism.** As *q. si quis contra.*

**k. ¶We consider.** Understand: it must be said. For not [?], etc., because their laymen, etc. As if to say, since this must otherwise be true of clerics: for clerics, etc., because their laymen, etc., as though it were of laymen: rightly so must it be of clerics: and therefore it is clear that they are worthy [or not worthy] of ecclesiastical honors, and they will be received in similar form: and so it is clear of ecclesiastical honors, and this chapter is continuous with the end of that one. Or say: because of theirs, that is, although-as-if to say, although their laymen [not, however] their clerics are to be received in honor.
