# Causa 1, Question 1

*(Continuing the preceding canon, from p. 466.)*

> Judas sold the Redeemer of all, and soon, hanged by a noose, he did not obtain that same grace of the Redeemer — and rightly so, since no one can keep what he has sold. Simon Magus too, when he wished to buy the same grace of the Holy Spirit, heard the sentence of damnation from Peter: “Let your money be with you unto perdition, because you thought the gift of God could be possessed for money.” Likewise the Lord, in order to show that buyers and sellers of this grace have no share in it, by grace let go all the sinners in the temple, but singling out the sellers and buyers for rebuke, drove them from the temple with whips — by which it is clear that his sellers and buyers were destitute and empty of the grace of the Holy Spirit. For whom Christ cast out of the temple, will the Spirit of Christ make for himself a temple?

*Among the works of Blessed Gregory Nazianzen, many of which are extant in Greek and Latin, no such passage is found. Similar passages, however, occur in the letter of Tarasius to Hadrian.*

**a. Judas.** But did Judas commit simony? Certainly. And indeed he committed *plagium* \[man-stealing\] in selling a free man — *ff. ad leg. Flav. de plagi. l. 1.* He also committed simony in that he sold a sacred thing. H. says that the Lord cannot sell his own servant `[?]`.

**b. Sold.** Note that it says “sold”; and yet at the time this man \[Giezi\] demanded gifts, the other had already obtained the grace. And this is an argument against those who do not demand \[payment\] before the conferral of a benefice, but afterwards. *Arg. 7. q. 1. eos*; *and q. 6. c. 3. si quis obiecerit. ff. de calum. l. 1. § qui autem.* Yet some say that Giezi was condemned not for the sale but for the lie — which does not please me.

**c. Your money.** This is said in detestation of the crime. *1. q. 3. §. contra.*

**d. He let go.** An argument that anyone, on account of mortal sin, was barred from entering the church. *Ar. ne poe. dist. 5. qui sanctus sacerdos*; *31. q. 4. vir cum propria.* For the Lord cast out from the body of the church only the simoniacs, leaving the other sinners. Yet understand this of those who commit simony in an order they have received: though the authority appears to speak generally. Whence, say with Baz. and M. that neither such a simoniac nor a public fornicator ought to enter the church before doing penance. I noted on this matter at *33. q. 4. ut cum propria.* Joh., however, says (as he has often answered me) that any sinner, even one excommunicated, may pray, provided he is not present at divine offices, since this is not found to be prohibited — *extra de sen. exc. cum nuper.* —H.

## C. XII. Simoniacs cannot be priests

*From the same Gregory.*

> Whoever sell or buy holy orders cannot be priests. Whence it is written: “Anathema to the giver, anathema to the receiver; this is the simoniacal heresy.” How then, if they are anathema, and are not holy, can they sanctify others? And since they are not in the body of Christ, how can they hand on or receive the body of Christ? How can he who is cursed bless?

*Deusdedit in his treatise* *Contra Symoniacos* *reports this from the letter of Pope Paschal II to the Milanese.*

**e. Whoever.** *Casus*. It is said here that simoniacs cannot be promoted, whether giving or receiving. For both givers and receivers are simoniacs; and since they are accursed, they cannot bless others; for they are reconciled \[only\] through penance. Though one acquired \[a benefice\] for money, he nevertheless was unwilling to bestow anything by means of money, and \[suffer\] his penalty in effect `[?]`.

**f. Whence it is written.** He who has received an order for a price, and the order receives a price in order to be ordained, receives something which it does not call for `[?]`. When he receives the price, and *§ ca. et post tantum.*

**g. Heresy.** Thus Christ said: simony is heresy. —H.

## C. XIII. On the same

*The same Gregory to Syagrius and other bishops, bk. 7, ep. 110.*

> Since it is clear that this heresy crept in before all others from a pestilential root, and was at its very origin condemned by apostolic anathema; why is it not guarded against? Why is it not weighed that the blessing turns into a curse for him who is promoted in order to become a heretic?

*α. All. Earlier the reading was “all things” (omnia), but the reading “all” (omnes) seems more connected — it has been restored from some manuscripts and from the original itself.*

**i. Before all.** Contra: *de cons. dist.* `[?]` — this is true after the resurrection, as for all other things.
