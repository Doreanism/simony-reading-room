*The same Ambrose,* *De ministeriis*† *or* *De dignitate sacerdotali, ch. 5.*

## C. XIIII. He who ordains for a price confers leprosy, not office

> When the bishop was being ordained, what he gave was gold; what he lost was his soul: when he ordained another, what he received was money; what he gave was leprosy a.

*The same, in the same work.*

## C. XV. On the same

> When you were being ordained, you did not receive grace; because you did not earn it gratuitously b.

*The same, bk. 4, on Luke ch. 4.*

## C. XVI. On the same

> Quickly does leprosy follow the shameful reward, and money ill-gotten defiles the body and the mind. *And a little later.* You see how the heir’s succession is condemned by the author’s deed. For the guilt of the sold mystery† is inexpiable c, and the heavenly vengeance of grace passes to posterity. Finally the Moabite d and the rest shall not enter the church until the third e and fourth generation; that is to say (to interpret more simply), so long, until the succession of multiple f generations should wipe out the guilt of the authors. But since those who fell into the error of idolatry against God seem to have been punished only to the fourth g α generation, the sentence by which the seed of Giezi is prolonged unto eternity appears even harsher h.

**a. When he was being ordained. It was leprosy.** That is, he rendered himself infected with the wound of sin.

**b. Grace. You did not earn.** *Ar. 21. q. 4. c. 1.*

**Quickly.** *Casus*. There is touched on here what is said in the fourth book of Kings: that Naaman the Syrian, after being cleansed of leprosy by Elisha, departed; but Giezi, Elisha’s servant, seeing that the Lord \[Elisha\] had taken nothing from him, secretly followed him saying that two sons of the prophets had come to his lord, and that Elisha commanded him to send him a talent of silver and two changes of garments; which when he had sent them, Giezi received them and hid them. Then, while he later stood before his lord, Elisha said to him: “Whence comest thou?” He replied, “Thy servant went nowhere.” But the other said, “My heart was present. Now you have received the silver and the garments, and the leprosy of Naaman shall cleave to you and to your seed forever,” and he went out from him a leper as white as snow.

**c. Is inexpiable.** As regards the exercise of the order: or say that it is said in detestation of the crime, and as the guilt of discord, which perhaps is not purged even by suffering — as above, *dist. 90. neq; ad Cain*: or because he repents up to the moment of death, below in the same, *si quis*.

*† al. ministerii.*

**Finally.** *Casus*. It is said also that the Ammonites and Moabites, who descended from the sons of Lot, withstood the children of Israel coming out of Egypt, because of the idolatry into which they led them, and therefore were forbidden to enter the church until the third and fourth generation. The point of this canon, then, tends to prove in summary that the crime of simony is graver than the crime of idolatry. For the crime of idolatry is punished only to the third and fourth generation; though the crime of idolatry `[?]` is forbidden to enter the church in the third and fourth generation, the seed of simoniacs is punished forever. “Seed” is mystically understood in the succession of vices, and not in the succession of birth, as is taken there: *“You are in part born of the devil.”* And simoniacs and Giezites are here broadly understood to mean all the covetous and all the greedy. And you may first set out the case and then narrate the history, or vice versa.

**d. ¶Moabites.** These were Moabites and Ammonites; and when the children of Israel were returning from the Egyptian captivity and these people knew they were threatening the church, they went out to meet them with bread without pores \[i.e., without yeast\], bringing along with them Balaam the soothsayer to curse them; on account of which sin the Lord commanded that they should not be made proselytes `[?]` nor be received into the church of God until the seventh generation — although by another translation it is said until the tenth. But they do not contradict each other: for those descending in the male line were not received until the seventh; in the female line, until the tenth. But the Hebrew said that to receive them into the church of God meant nothing other than to give them their daughters in marriage. The history of those Moabites is at *35. dist. capitu. sexto die.*

**e. ¶Unto the third.** That is, unless they have the faith of the Trinity and the four kinds of virtues, or \[the faith\] of the four Gospels.

**f. ¶Multiple.** Argument that guilt or fraud is purged by the passage of time, as at *2. q. 1. in primis. 35. q. 8. de gradibus. ff. de hae. uel ac. uen. l. venditor. ff. de ac. et ob. is qui. extra de toto. extra qui ma. acq. pos. insuper.* Argument to the contrary: *j. ead. centum. ff. depo. l. 1. § si tem. ff. de cond. fur. l. qui furtum.* and *l. ulti.* But the truth of the matter is that, although it cannot be purged by passage of time as regards the present case, it is purged through the dispensation of the church, as at *23. q. 4. ipsa pietas. uersi. est enim.* Whence the verses: *“Composito lacte foetenti quaeso cibare, / Qui putat exclusum crimen per temporis usum”* \[“I beg you, feed with mixed milk him who stinks, who thinks a crime is dismissed by the lapse of time”\]. *De consue. cap. quanto. in gl. solam consuetudinem.*

**g. ¶Unto the fourth.** Here Ambrose is not following \[the standard\] interpretation, but that of the Seventy translators. Or say that he is referring here to the mystical sense, that is, until they have the faith of the Trinity and of the four Gospels.

**h. ¶Harsher.** And who doubts that what is more harshly avenged was more wickedly committed, as below *24. q. 1. non affe* `[?]` *ramus in si. quod perpetuum est* — although also the killing of one’s own wife is punished more gravely (as at *J. 33. q. 2. admonere*) than matricide, *33. q. 2. latorem.* Yet that is a special provision made on account of a frequent occurrence, and to curb the husband’s ready malice against his wife. Such things (since they are special) ought not to be drawn into consequence. Joh., as at *27. q. 1. nuptiarum* and *dist. 27. si vir.*
