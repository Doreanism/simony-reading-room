## C. LXXIIII. Imposition of hands is not the repetition of a sacrament

> The imposition of hands cannot, like baptism, be repeated [yes, can be repeated]. For what else is it, except prayer over a person?

*Since therefore Innocent permits only baptism to be valid among heretics; since Gregory calls the consecration of the Arians sacrilegious; since Cyprian declares whatever is done by heretics to be carnal, empty, and false; since Jerome asserts that all things offered by them are contaminated in the sight of God; since Leo testifies that outside the church there are neither valid priesthoods nor true sacrifices: it is clear that the ecclesiastical sacraments, except baptism, as was said above, cannot be administered by heretics.*

*Likewise that of Jerome is brought forward, against the Luciferians.*

## C. LXXV. He who is holy in baptism shall not be a sinner at the altar

> Just as Christ is the one who ba[ptizes, ...]

**b. ¶Perfection.** That is, holiness, and the execution of office. *ADDITION. Here see what is read and noted, the same q., who [discusses] perfection.*

**c. ¶That theirs.** That is, because theirs, so that it is relative, cause, and so through this the preceding reading does not seem to be approved, that is, therefore it does not seem so, because, etc.

**d. ¶Imperfect.** Because they did not receive that baptism.

**e. ¶Whoever touches.** By touch of the mind. *extra de sen. exco. nuper.*

**f. ¶Condemned.** That is, of condemnation, or of one already condemned, etc. 1.

**i. ¶Is commanded.** As often as he returns from heresy, above same, hi q. baptismo, etc. 2.

**¶The imposition of hands.** CASE. It is said here (although obscurely) that the reconciling imposition of hands is also repeated, even though baptism cannot be repeated, and it is a similitude through contrast.

**k. ¶Hand.** The sense is: the imposition of hands can be repeated, as below *de cons. dist. 4. quomodo baptismus.* Note same dist. *sicut.*

**l. ¶Imposition.** Consecratory; but not confirmatory. Likewise not ordinatory, but the curative is rightly repeated, of which it is said: *they shall lay hands upon the sick,* etc., as *96. dist. §. sed illud.* Reconciling likewise, as here, for the reconciling hand is imposed on heretics who have returned, as above *de consecra. dist. 4. quomodo,* and on the excommunicated, as *9. q. 4. cum aliquis,* and on penitents, as *50. distinctione in capite.* And in heretics or the excommunicated it is not a sacrament: and therefore it is repeated. In penitents, if it is public, it is a sacrament, and therefore it is not repeated, and it pertains only to bishops, as *17. q. 6. ca. 1.* and *50. dist. in capite.* If it is private, it is not a sacrament, and is repeated, and it pertains also to presbyters, as *26. q. 6. c. presbyteri. § vece.*

**m. ¶Not as.** That is, although.

**n. ¶Repeated.** Below same q. *quod quidam.* Against. Solution: but there the imposition of hands [?].

*Note that because the public is a sacrament, the private is not, therefore that one is not repeated, this is repeated: but on account of those cases by which the reason is brought forward, however much. on penance, dist. m. 3. See also Turrecremata there.*
