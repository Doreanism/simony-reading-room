## C. XLIII. Of bishops who consecrate one whom it is not lawful to consecrate

> If any bishops have consecrated a a priest who b is not lawful [to be consecrated], even if in some way c they escape the loss of their own honor, nevertheless they shall not henceforth have the right of ordination d e; nor shall those f ever take part in the sacraments which, neglecting the divine judgment, they undeservedly conferred.

> *Here h, although they escape the danger of [losing their] honor, so that they are permitted to administer the rest of the sacraments as priests, yet from this one [sacrament] they are removed — not only on account of heresy or any greater fault, but even on account of negligence. In which it must be carefully noted that the sacrament of priestly promotion is to be given or received with more care and worthiness than all the rest: for unless it be conferred thus, it ceases to be ratified to the extent that it was not duly perfected. The other sacraments are given to each one for his own sake, and become such for each as the heart or conscience with which they are received. This one alone is given not for the recipient’s own sake, but for the sake of others: and therefore it is necessary that it be received with a true heart and clean conscience, as regards the recipient himself, since for the sake of others it [must be received] truly — not only without any fault, but also without any infamy, on account of the scandal of the brethren: for whose benefit the priesthood is given, not only that they may preside, but also that they may profit them.*

*Whence Jerome, on c. 3 of Micah.*

## C. XLIIII. Simoniacs are guilty of the infidelity of those who are scandalized by them

> These men, whomsoever i they ordain as clerics from among their followers, and by exposing their life as a scandal k to the people, are guilty of the infidelity of those who are scandalized.

> *For indeed those who are chosen for this — that they may preside over the rest — just as they are foreordained in dignity, so they ought to be preeminent in holiness. Otherwise, why are they preferred to the rest, when they are not taken from the rest by any grace of merit? Hence also Symmachus to Laurence, bishop of Milan, after the sixth Roman synod:*
