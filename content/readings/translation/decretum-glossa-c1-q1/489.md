## C. XLVII. As I seem to be pressed

> Just as I seem to be pressed when it is said to me, “Therefore a heretic forgives a sins?” so I too b press [in turn] when I say, “Therefore one who does not keep the heavenly commandments — the greedy, the rapacious, the usurer, the envious, [those who] renounce the world in words and not in deeds — does he forgive sins?” If by virtue of the sacrament of God, then both [the heretic and the greedy man]; if by his own merit, then neither. For that sacrament is known to be Christ’s even in evil men. But in the body of the one dove c, incorrupt, holy, chaste, “which has no spot or wrinkle,” neither is found. [Just as if someone’s † hand is withered, it would indeed be a true member, although bloodless, and would be ineffectual and dead: so also faith, although it be true, yet without works is dead.]

> *Likewise a little after.* ¶So therefore one baptized in heresy in the name of the holy Trinity is nevertheless not a temple of God, if he does not depart from heresy; just as one baptized in the same name while in greed is not a temple of God, if he does not depart from greed, which is the service of idols.

> *β. ¶Just as if someone’s. These words up to “Likewise” are not read in this place in the published codices of Blessed Augustine, nor among the other collectors.*

> *§ 8. From these words, then, it appears that baptism which is administered by heretics confers, by its own virtue, the remission of sins.*

*But the same Augustine testifies to the contrary, in book 6 on baptism against the Donatists, c. 1, saying:*

¶The baptism of Christ, even if not violated by any perversity, nevertheless lacks effect among heretics.

## C. XLVII. Even those slower of heart

> Even d those slower of heart, as I judge, understand that the baptism of Christ can be violated by no perversity of man, whether of the giver or of the receiver. But since e the sacrament is one thing, and the effect of the sacrament another: since it is certain that those who in the very unity of the Church live wickedly can neither give nor have remission of sins — for it was said not to the wicked but to the good sons: [If you forgive anyone his sins, they are forgiven him; if you retain anyone’s, they shall be retained] — it is clear, then, that heretics f only give or receive the sacrament of baptism, and do not give the remission of sins.

> *γ. ¶But since one [thing]. From here to the end the words of Blessed Augustine are reduced into a summary, but the sense remains intact. The whole passage is found at de consec. dist. 4. Quomodo. §. Non ob aliud.*

*The same, in sermon 11 on the words of the Lord, on blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.*

**a. ¶Forgives sins.** Above respond contrariwise: to give an instance is to say, for what reason not. You will arrange the argument thus: the heretic baptizes justly; therefore he forgives sin — which I deny. And I press [the objection]: the greedy man baptizes justly; therefore he forgives sin — which they themselves would not concede. But say the instance is invalid; because it is concluded according to their own opinion. So *27. q. 1. n. puiarum.*

**¶Of the one dove.** Which embraces grain without chaff. *32. q. 4. recurrat §. contra remissionem*, and in the final gloss.

**FROM THESE.** This is the 8th part of the question, in which it is said that baptism given in the form of the Church by anyone is valid: but one who is baptized [while] cut off is not saved, just as a member cut off from the body does not have the spirit of life. Joan. de Fant.

**b. ¶Even. CASE.** From this place [Augustine] proves the contrary; namely, that the baptism of heretics does not have effect. The case, then, is this. It is said in this chapter that even the slow [of heart] understand slowly that even a wicked man gives and receives true baptism, but does not give the remission of sins, which is the effect of the sacrament: because it was said only to the good: *To whomever you forgive* sins, &c.

**c. ¶Even.** From that place the Master adduces chapters by which he means to prove that even though heretics can confer the sacrament, the effect of the sacrament [is not theirs to give]. Sometimes, however, he adduces [authorities] that they can confer the effect; and under this alternation [he proceeds] up to the end of the question with unwearied brow.

**e. ¶But since.** Just as the element is one thing and the consecration another, as *de consecra. distin. 4. suo fuit Cyprianus*; for not below in the same, *si quis inquit*.
