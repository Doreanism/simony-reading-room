# Causa 1, Question 1

*[Continuation of Tarasius’s letter to Pope Hadrian, begun on p. 473. The Greek original and a 16th-century Latin translation appear together; rendered once in English here.]*

> ... \[the truth\], keeping and holding fast what was canonically handed down by the holy and all-praiseworthy Apostles and by our renowned fathers. And if any of these things has been violated by anyone, we detest it. Your brotherly holiness, then, fitting to the supreme priesthood — having lawfully and rightly, according to the will of God, presided over the priestly sanctity — has attained celebrated glory. For Christ our God, the first and great High Priest, said through the prophet, \[“As I live, those who glorify me, I will glorify.”\] For you know well, O man whose desires are of the Spirit, that the impious heresy of Macedonius and those of his party, the impugners of the Holy Spirit, is far more tolerable. For while they in their nonsense declare the Holy Spirit a creature and a servant of God the Father, these — as they think — make him their own servant. For every master, whatever he possesses, sells if he wishes, whether a slave or any other of his possessions. Likewise too the buyer, wishing to be lord of what he buys, acquires it through a price of silver. Thus those who perform this lawless deed drag down the Holy Spirit, sinning equally with those who blasphemously said that Christ cast out demons by Beelzebub; or, to speak more truly, they are like Judas the traitor, who sold the Lord to the God-slaying Jews for a price of silver. Since therefore the Holy Spirit is consubstantial with Christ our God, it is altogether clear that they will be of the same portion, as has been shown. But if \[the Spirit\] is not sold — as plainly he is not — undoubtedly the grace of the Holy Spirit, that is, the holiness of the priesthood, is not in them; and what they did not receive, they do not have. Let them recall the holy Peter saying thus to him who attempted this `[?]`: \[“You have no part nor lot in the word.”\] For if the dignity of the priesthood is sold, then for them the noble manner of life and the conduct in purity and virtue are superfluous; for them too the divine Apostle Paul is superfluous, who teaches that the bishop must be without reproach, temperate, modest, apt to teach, continent, sober, holding fast to the faithful word according to the doctrine, that he may be able both to exhort in sound teaching and to refute those who contradict. All these things, then, are lost from the seller and the buyer of the priesthood. The holy authorities cited below, therefore, declare wholly alien from the priesthood the one who has at any time given or received \[money\] — whether before the ordination, in the ordination, or after the ordination. For to receive is to receive at any time. They also reject all ecclesiastical promotions † \[in which\] money has been given.
