*14. Since therefore simony is noted in this chapter in a threefold way, it must be seen in how many ways the price in this heresy is given. Blessed Gregory expounds this in his treatise on the Gospels, homily 4, at the end, saying:*

*¶On the manifold kinds of simoniacs.*

## C. CXIV. On the manifold kinds of simoniacs

> There are some who indeed do not accept money-rewards for ordination, and yet bestow holy orders for human favor, and from that same largesse seek only the recompense of praise. ¶These indeed do not bestow freely what has been freely received: because for the holy office bestowed they demand the coin of favor. Whence rightly, when the prophet was describing the just man, he says: \[Who shakes his hands from every gift\]. For he does not say “who shakes his hands from a gift,” but added “from every”: because the gift from service is one thing, the gift from the hand another, the gift from the tongue another. The gift from service indeed is subjection improperly bestowed. The gift from the hand is money. The gift from the tongue is favor. He, therefore, who bestows holy orders, then shakes his hands from every gift, when in divine matters he requires not only no money, but not even human favor.

*The same, in the register.*

*¶He shall find eternal damnation, who by any gift whatever approaches holy orders.*

**a. ¶Intention.** Therefore without that intention there is no simony: otherwise, however `[?]`.

**b. ¶Thieves.** Since therefore a simoniac is compared to a thief, it seems that no one can dispense with a simoniac, unless he has wrongly received \[the thing\], as *7. ead. si quis neq[ue]. & 7. ead. erga. & extra ea. nobis.* If “si quia” — nor will I have purged the vice of theft — let it return into the Lord’s power, as *14. q. 6. si res aliena.* This could be sufficiently conceded: and it could be said that a simoniac would not even receive the character: because a simoniac must renounce the thing he has obtained by vice. It is clear that he would not renounce the execution, because he never had the execution; and to the character also he cannot renounce. Whence it seems sufficiently true that he did not receive the character: unless you wish to say that he received possession of the character, just as a thief is said to have a stolen thing — that is, possession of the thing. Yet it is more credible that one say that he has the character, and that the Pope can dispense with him even though he does not renounce, for even the final \[stage\] of theft can be purged by the thing in the thief’s possession being lost — *si fur. in prin.* the same seems to hold in a violent detainer, as *ff. de preca. certe. § Iulianus 1.*

**c. ¶They accept.** *Extra de si. & si quaestiones.*

**d. ¶Was describing.** That which follows is not in Isaiah, but in the psalm: “Judge me, O Lord,” to himself, “in whose hands.”

**e. ¶From service.** Thus it seems that I should not serve for the sake of a benefice, as here *and 7. par. ar. contra. 12. q. 2. quicunque suffragio. & c. charitate. & de cle. non resi. de cetero.* Yet one may also hope for or aspire to dignities, as *74. dist. consuluit. 61. dist. quod proderit. & extra de pac. cum pridem.* Sol.: H. says, if he directly has his mind on this when he serves, he is a simoniac, as *8. q. 1. sunt quidam.* But you should say that if he is worthy, and renders worthy service, he is not a simoniac, as here and *extra de cle. non re. si de cetero*: otherwise, if he is unworthy, or unworthy \[and\] serves worthily, he is a simoniac: and this not only when undue service is rendered, and although a worthy man may serve, yet this cannot be reduced to an agreement: for that is simoniacal, *extra de pac. pactiones.* He shall therefore give no gift except such as he would have given otherwise on account of some necessity, as *extra ea. & si quaestiones,* or unless he gives some present, as *ff. de of. procon. l. solent. § vel.* or unless he gives it as alms at the temple’s offering box, as *sup. ead. iudices.*

**f. ¶From every.** That he might include all particulars and singulars. *5. 19. dist. si Romanorum. § 7. 14. q. 3. putat. inf. e. q. 3. salvator. 3. 31. q. 1. qui si dormierit 2.* Note that he says “undue”: for if it is duly bestowed, it is not so, as *7. ea. q. 3. salvator.* And Bazianus expounds “undue”: either an undue worthy man, or a duly unworthy man, serves for the benefice — otherwise not, argument *insti. de societare. § de illa.*

**g. ¶Favor.** *7. 24. dist. quando.*

**IF ANYONE.** *CASUS*. If anyone has received an order or an ecclesiastical dignity through simoniac depravity, unless he leaves it in life and finally repents, he will perish forever. The following chapter is plain and self-evident.
