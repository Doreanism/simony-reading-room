# Cause I, Question I

*(Continuing canon C. LXXXIIII from p. 503.)*

> ...sanctifies, both by blessing and that he may bless. A “mystery,” α therefore, brethren, is so called for this reason: because it has a secret and hidden dispensation b. A “sacrifice” [sacrificium], as if “sacred deed” [sacrum factum], because it is consecrated for us by a mystical prayer c in memory of the Lord’s passion. *And below.* A “sacrament,” however, exists in a certain celebration, when a deed is so performed that it is understood to signify something which must be received in a holy manner. ¶. Now the sacraments are: baptism, chrism, the body and blood of Christ — which are called “sacraments” for this reason, because under the veil of corporeal things the divine power more secretly works the salvation of those same sacraments. Whence they are called “sacraments” from secret powers, or from sacred things. They are performed fruitfully within the Church for this reason, because the Holy Spirit remaining in her latently works the effect of those same sacraments. The sacrament of this bread and chalice is called in Greek “eucharist,” and in Latin is interpreted “good grace.” And what is better than the body d and blood of Christ? Whence, whether it be dispensed by good or by bad ministers within the Church of God, it is nevertheless a sacrament; because the Holy Spirit mystically vivifies it, who once in the apostolic time appeared in visible works. Nor is it amplified by the merits of good dispensers, nor diminished e by those of bad ones, because “neither he that planteth is anything, nor he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase.” This must be understood and held concerning the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, this likewise concerning baptism and chrism: because the divine power works more secretly in them, and this is solely a divine power or potency, not one of human efficacy.

*Thus far Isidore.*

*We pollute β therefore the bread, that is, the body of Christ, when we approach the altar unworthily; and being defiled, we drink the pure blood. And below.* *Jerome on Malachi, ch. 1.*

*“I will curse, γ” he says, “your blessings,” that is, those things which you now possess by my blessings; or whatever is blessed by you, I will curse; or because δ they do not bless the holy from a true heart; or because those who act iniquitously are blessed by them, and they flatter sinners, provided that they are rich.*

*The same on ch. 2.*

*¶This chapter is collected partly from the sentences of blessed Gregory, partly from the very words of blessed Isidore and Jerome.*

*¶“A mystery therefore.” This passage as far as the verse “this concerning the body” is found in Isidore, though not in exactly the same order.*

*¶“We pollute.” Taken from blessed Jerome in his commentaries on chapter 1 of Malachi at the words “and you say, In what have we polluted?” etc.*

*¶“I will curse.” In the same author on chapter 2 it is given thus: “And I will curse (he says) your blessings, that is, those which you now possess by my blessings; or whatever is blessed by you shall be cursed by me; and I will curse them (supply: your blessings), because you have refused to understand what is spoken — for this is what is said: [you have not laid it to heart.]”*

*¶“Because.” Taken from the same place a little further on, where these things are read: “Or certainly, because the command is given properly to priests, their blessings are turned into a curse when they do not bless the saints from a true affection of the heart, as Isaac blessed Jacob, and Jacob the patriarchs, and Moses the twelve tribes; but by sweet speeches and blessings they deceive the hearts of the innocent; and those who act iniquitously are blessed by them; and they flatter sinners provided that they are rich, and they fawn upon their vices.”*

**a. ¶Therefore.** And because [it works] secretly and invisibly. *Such [is the effect of his] sanctifying.*

**b. ¶Dispensation.** That is, the power and grace dispensed and conferred by God.

**c. ¶Because by a prayer.** The sacrifices of the Old Testament did not accomplish what they prefigured. Argument *de consec. dist. 2. sacrificium.* And as for what he says, that it is consecrated by a mystical prayer, refer this to the species of the sacred according to H., because neither is the body of Christ consecrated, since it was already the most sacred body, nor is the bread, because at that point it immediately ceases to be.

**d. ¶What is better than the body.** Certainly the soul of Christ — whence understand this statement only by way of amplification. Similar *below 32. q. 6. nihil, and q. 7. quid in omnibus.* Or say: this is greatest among the ecclesiastical sacraments as regards holiness and reverence, as *below de consecrat. dist. 2. nihil, and ca.* The body of Christ exceeds other sacraments as to reverence. *Qui manducat, and 25 dist. ecce.* Or [understand it of] many of the same kind, namely among corporeal things. Or understand “of the body” as meaning the body which is Christ. Argument *below same q. interrogo.* Contra. But there expound “the word” [verbum], that is, the Son. And so it is not contra. *De poe. di. 1. verbum.*

**e. ¶Diminished.** But contra *5. 81. dist. hi qui sunt presbyteri.*
