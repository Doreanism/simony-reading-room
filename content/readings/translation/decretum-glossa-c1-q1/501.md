## C. LXXVIII. Although all sacraments are harmful to those who handle them unworthily, they nevertheless profit those who receive them worthily through them

> All sacraments, although they are harmful to those who handle them unworthily, nevertheless profit those who receive them worthily through them: just as also the word b of God. Whence it is said c. [What they say, do: but what they do, do not do.]

*Likewise on Psalm 103, verse [Qui tegis.]*

¶On those who use holy things badly.

## C. LXXIX. Even Saul, an evil king, prophesied

> Even Saul d, an evil king, prophesied when he was persecuting holy David. *And below.* ¶Therefore let those not boast who happen to have this holy gift of God without charity: but let them see what account they will have to render to God, who use holy things unholily. Among these will be those who shall say: [In your name we have prophesied.] To them it will not be said, “you are lying”: but it will be said, [I do not know e you, etc.]

*¶In tractate 7 on the Epistle of John there are some things which can be referred to the meaning of this chapter, and from there it was previously cited. But these very words are altogether [from] the place indicated.*

*They must therefore be said to have had the spirit of prophecy, not merit.*

*Likewise Jerome on c. 1 of Haggai.*

¶Anyone is not so much sanctified by the offerings as polluted on account of sin.

## C. LXXX. On the same

> O Prophet f, you who, with only the altar built up, and my house torn down g, offer me victims at the altar: you think yourself sanctified by its victims; know that the offering on the altar is indeed holy: but you are not so much sanctified by the victims as you are polluted by dead works.

*In blessed Jerome it is read thus: O people, you who, with only the altar built up, and my house torn down, offer me victims at the altar, and think yourself sanctified by its victims and flesh; know that you are not so much sanctified by the victims, which with the temple torn down cannot profit you,*

**a. ¶Recreated.** As to the sustenance of the body, *de consec. dist. 2. non iste panis 1.* Namely after the soul, as *de consec. dist. 2. non iste panis 1.*

**b. ¶All sacraments. And the word.** That is, preaching: which if it is done by an unworthy person, profits others: but harms him. arg. *dist. 53. §. quanquam.* arg. *15. q. ult. c. ult.*

**c. ¶It is said.** Such people instruct God how he ought to punish them. *40. dist. multi.*

**¶He prophesied. CASE.** It is proved in this chapter that an evil priest can minister: because Saul was evil, and he prophesied. For it is read in the first book of Kings that when Saul was persecuting David, he himself, fleeing, came into Ramatha to Samuel: which when it was announced to Saul, he sent officers to seize him: who, when they had seen the company of prophets, and Samuel standing over them, the spirit of the Lord came upon them: and they themselves also began to prophesy: which when it was announced to Saul, he sent other messengers: and they also prophesied; again he sent a third group, and they also prophesied: and Saul, angry, departed, and the spirit of the Lord came upon him: and he prophesied with the rest before Samuel, and sang naked all day and night, whence came the proverb, “Saul among the prophets.”

**d. ¶He prophesied.** Here he proves that an evil priest can confect [the Eucharist]: because Saul was evil, and he prophesied: indeed, the evil have all the gifts of the Holy Spirit except charity. *supra c. multae.* And the history will here serve for the case.

**e. ¶I do not know.** That is, I have not approved. So say some scholars who do not belong with certain ones in the schools, when they see them in their own country, they say, “I do not know you.” Or when they are rich, they say to the poor a, “I do not know you.” Or (what is worse) they refuse to speak with them.

**¶O Prophet. CASE.** In this chapter evil priests are rebuked, who although they confect the true body of Christ on the altar, are not sanctified by this: but rather are polluted by their evil works.

**f. ¶O Prophet.** Literally it is understood of the Babylonian captivity. For when Cyrus gave permission to return from the Babylonian captivity, the people of Israel built up the altar, and cast down [i.e., laid] the foundation of the temple: but by letters of Cambyses, commander of the army of Cyrus himself, they were forbidden to proceed with the building until the second year of Darius: in which they were ordered to build the temple begun by the prophet Haggai, who was exhorting the people to this. Jerome therefore speaks of the time in which they were sacrificing on the altar: but they were neglecting to rebuild the house of God, namely the temple, when they were rebuilding their own houses: which appears more fully in Haggai himself.

**g. ¶My house torn down.** That is, the building up of virtues, [?]
