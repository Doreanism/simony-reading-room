*(Continuing canon C. CXXVIII from p. 528.)*

> — that our clemency may recognize you as a defender of the innocent: the offices of the masters too ought to assist in defending their ministers.

*See the title ff. de iurisdictione omnium iudicum.*

## C. CXXIX. On the same

*PALEA.*

> If anyone has received anything for an accusation or for a judgment: as a sacrilegious and infamous person, he ought to be deprived of all dignities, honors, and military service.

## C. CXXX. To a judge who without a price justly judges, no competition for honor is forbidden

*Likewise from the same title of the Code.*

> Between those who on account of calumny in judgment are condemned and infamous, and those who without a price justly judging labor under no mark of calumny, there is a great difference: since this one is innocent, that one guilty: this one without crime, that one judged a defendant: \[for this one\] is not forbidden either to accuse, or to be judged, or to seek to be a judge.

*β. ¶In the Pandects there follows the verse, “By this law the condemned are found at the end of this chapter.”*

**a. ¶Of the innocent.** That is, of those who are without fault: or also of those who are accused. Argument *infra eod. q. omnes.*

**¶PALEA.**

**¶If anyone for an accusation.** *CASUS*. Here it is said that whoever accuses or judges for a price is infamous and sacrilegious, and deprived of every honor.

**b. ¶For a judgment.** Or for an accusation: and then it is said of a calumniator: or for a judgment, and then it is said of a judge who has accepted a price. *ff. de iudic. l. 1. & l. 2. & ff. de calumn. & ff. de his qui not. inf.*

**¶Among them.** *CASUS*. There is a great difference between those who are condemned for calumny, and those who without a price justly judge: for these are without any crime, but those are judged defendants. And therefore those who without a price have justly judged can accuse, and be promoted to every dignity.

**c. ¶Among them.** Here the Emperor shows the difference between judges: because some are condemned for calumny; others justly judge: and these indeed can accuse, be judged, and be judges.

**d. ¶Who.** Namely judges: because, as has been said above, *those who have judged for a price are infamous.*

**e. ¶Whether to accuse.** That is, to lawfully perform all judicial acts. *ff. de iudic.*
