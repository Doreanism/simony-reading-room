## C. LXVII. It is not permitted to pray with heretics or schismatics

*Likewise from the council of Pope Martin, c. 70.*

> It shall not be lawful for clerics or laymen to receive eulogies from heretics; because they are curses rather than blessings: nor shall it be lawful to pray either with heretics or with schismatics.

**a. ¶It shall not. ¶Schismatics.** *J. de consec. dist. 1. eps nullum.* Against. Solution: that was of old, this is strengthened by the custom of the church. H.

**b. ¶Nor true.** As regards having the substance of the sacrament, and lawful execution.

**c. ¶In the property of our [nature].** That is, by the true faith of the incarnation, by which we believe that Christians are reconciled to God by his being born, dying, and rising again, etc. *23. dist. qui episcopus.*

*ADDITION. Or in his own proper human nature. As if to say, they are not valid and true with respect to the effect of execution, or of grace and salvation, without the faith of the mediator, that is, unless one believes that Christ is true God, and has reconciled us to the Father. Without this faith, nothing avails for those who offer, as regards life and salvation, and likewise for those who receive. Pe.*

## C. LXVIII. Outside the church there are no true sacrifices

*Likewise Leo to Anatolius, bishop of Constantinople, ep. 38, c. 2.*

> In the Church of God, which is the body of Christ, otherwise neither are priesthoods valid, nor sacrifices true, unless in the property of our nature the true Pontiff reconciles us, the true blood of the immaculate Lamb cleanses.

## C. LXIX. The divine sacraments withdraw themselves from the hands of the impious

*The same to Leo Augustus, ep. 73.*

> It is manifest that through the most cruel and most insane savagery † in the see of Alexandria all the light of the heavenly sacraments has been extinguished. The offering of the sacrifice has been intercepted; the sanctification of chrism has failed; and all the mysteries have withdrawn themselves from the parricidal hands of the impious.

*† var. madness*

**d. ¶It is manifest through the most cruel.** Namely, the Arian.

**e. ¶Extinguished.** As *24. q. 3. resecandae.*

**f. ¶Intercepted.** That is, utterly consumed as regards effect [?]. And so Ovid says in the sixth book of the Metamorphoses, in the fable of the frogs: *Their necks seem intercepted.*

## C. LXX. The sacraments can be administered outside the church, but they cannot confer salvation

*Likewise the blessed Martyr Cyprian in his letter against the heretics.*

> If anyone, he says, has gone forth from the church through heretical presumption, he is condemned by himself. With such a person, according to the Apostle, we ought not even to take food. This is shown in the book of Kings, where, when the man of God had been sent to Jeroboam, who was to reproach him for his sins [...]

**¶If anyone.** CASE. This chapter is divided into three parts. In the first, Cyprian says that if anyone follows a heresy already condemned, he seems to condemn himself, and we ought not to take food or drink with him: as the Apostle testifies, and this is also proved by the history of Abdo [?] the prophet. In the second it is said that the sacraments are not to be received from them, because they are false, since not even temporal food is to be taken with them: and that they are false, he proves in many ways. In the third part he says that whoever defends them are punished, and not only the leaders and authors, but also the participants. And this is proved by the authority of Moses and the prophet Hosea. The second part begins there, [*An debet*]. The third there, [*Quare qui*]. John also set out a case in his apparatus, and you will see it.

CASE. Cyprian intends to prove that we ought not to use the sacraments of heretics. First, by the authority of the Apostle, who forbids taking bodily food with them. Second, by the authority of the Lord, who forbade the prophet Abdo to take food with them. Third, because heretics have only the image of the sacraments, as an ape, but they do not have true sacraments. Fourth, because if the evil who are in the church, after a threefold admonition, are compared

**g. ¶Has gone forth.** That is, acted, on account of which he deserves to go forth and be condemned. arg. *24. q. 3. si quis non recto.*

**h. ¶By himself.** That is, by his own act, namely by falling into a heresy already condemned, as below *4. q. 5. c. 1.* below *24. q. 1. c. 1. 2. & 3.*

**i. ¶He is condemned.** That is, he has cast himself into excommunication. *4. 7. q. 1. denique.*

*4. Or say: he is condemned, that is, he himself has done that for which he is justly condemned. fi. 11. q. 3. placuit universo, &c. & c. ipse ligandi. Ar.*

**k. ¶To Jeroboam.** History. It is read in the third book of Kings that when the prophet Abdo was sent to Jeroboam to rebuke him for idolatry, it was said to him that he should neither eat nor drink in Samaria, and that when the business was completed he should return, [but] a prophet deceived him and led him into Bethel, that he might eat with him, and when he had eaten, the prophet spoke to him in the spirit, saying: because [?] you have eaten and drunk the word of God in a forbidden place, your corpse shall not be brought into the sepulchers of your fathers, and as he was returning seated on an ass, a lion attacked and killed him.

**¶To Jeroboam.** As *7. q. 1. denique,* who had made two idols, and had set up one in Bethel, another in Dan.
