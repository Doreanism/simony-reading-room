# Cause I, Question I

*(Continuing the preceding canon, from p. 510.)*

> ...have, and therefore they themselves exist — that is, they are firm and true and good. Yet the sacraments of ordination are not stripped from them, but remain upon them. Therefore the hand a is not laid upon them in [the assembly of] the people, lest injury be done not to the man but to the sacrament itself. *And below.* §. As they have in baptism that which can be given through them, so [they have] in ordination the right of giving b: each, indeed, to their own destruction, so long as they do not have the charity of unity. But yet it is one thing c to not have at all; another, to have perniciously; another d, to have salutarily. Whatever is not had must be given when there is need for it to be given; but what is had perniciously, once the destruction has been driven out through correction, must be made to be had salutarily. Although, even if a layman, in the necessity d of one perishing, has given what, when he himself received it, he learned must be given, I do not know e whether anyone could piously say it must be repeated. For if it is done with no necessity compelling, it is a usurpation of another’s office. But if necessity presses, it is either no fault or a venial fault f. And even if it is usurped under no necessity, and given by anyone whatever to anyone whatever, what has been given cannot be said not to have been given; although it can rightly be said to have been illicitly given. Therefore the affection of one remembering and repenting corrects the illicit usurpation. But if he does not correct [it], what has been given will remain to the penalty of the usurper, either of him who gave illicitly, or of him who received illicitly; nevertheless it will not be held g as not given. Nor in any way will the royal sign be violated through a devoted soldier, [merely] because it has been usurped by private persons. For if some have stealthily and extraordinarily, not at the public mints h, struck or signed gold or silver, when this has been detected, will not the recognized royal sign, once they have been punished i or freed by pardon, be required to be brought into the royal treasures? *And a little later.* §. If perchance someone, not actually serving, has in a cowardly manner shrunk back k from that mark of military service in his own body, and has taken refuge in the clemency of the emperor, and, his prayer having been poured forth and pardon obtained, has begun to serve as a soldier — when the man has been freed and corrected, is that character to be repeated? Or rather, does not [the emperor], recognizing it, approve [it]? Or perhaps the Christian sacraments cling less than this corporeal mark, when we see that even apostates do not lack baptism — though it surely is not restored to them when they return through penance, and for this reason they are judged unable to be admitted? *And a little later.* §. Concerning those l who are separated from the unity of the Church, there is now no question that they both have, and can give, and that they have perniciously, and perniciously hand on, [the sacraments] outside the bond of peace. *And below.* Injury must be done to neither sacrament. *Below.* §. Just as those who depart from unity do not have [the sacraments] rightly, but nevertheless have them — and therefore [the sacrament] is not restored to one returning — so also one who departs from unity does not give rightly; but nevertheless gives. And therefore what was received from him is not iterated for the one coming to unity. *And below.* §. Just as to one returning is not restored what he had even outside, so to one coming what he received even outside is not to be repeated. Whence consequently it is understood that the perversity of men is to be corrected...

*† otherwise: “to someone perishing”*

**a. ¶Hand.** *Above ca. q. c. manus impositio.* Contra. But this concerns the consecratory [imposition].

**b. ¶Of giving.** That is, of consecrating, or of giving the ordination itself.

**c. ¶One thing.** Argument against that general [statement] which is *extra de translatio. inter corporalia.* and *7. q. 1. factus. ff. si quis*; contra. *cau. quoties.* Argument *63. dist. Salonitanae. 45. dist. de Iudaeis. 7. q. 1. episcopis.*

**d. ¶Necessity.** Here it is plainly gathered that laymen do not have the right of baptizing, unless “right” or “power” be taken broadly, that is, as permission, as *j. de consec. dist. 4. constat.* and *c. à quodam.* and *30. q. 1. ad limina.*

**e. ¶I do not know.** That is, I do not approve, if anyone should say it must be repeated.

**f. ¶It is a venial offense.** Here is a philosophical tempering, for in truth it is none. And it is similar to *22. q. 2. in quibus.*

**g. ¶Will be held.** Argument contra *extra de iurepatro. quod autem.* at the end.

**h. ¶At the mints.** That is, the [official] signs, seals, coinages, or instruments of striking coin.

**i. ¶Punished.** These are punished with various penalties. *C. de fal. mo. p totum.* Spared, however, is he who formed [the coin], if before he impresses the sign he repents. *ff. ad leg. Corn. de fa. qui falsam,* etc.

**k. ¶Shrunk back.** Who perhaps fled from public war, and so became infamous *1. 6. q. 1. infames.* And this is understood of those soldiers who swore that they would not avoid death on behalf of the republic. And these had a stigma, or other signs of some color, of which it is said: *We wish our soldiers to know arms, not laws. C. de iure deli. sancimus.* Or the sense of this text is: that if someone [?] has by the authority of the prince obtained the belt of military service, and afterwards is reconciled to the prince himself — is the character to be repeated in him? As if to say, no. *ADDITION.* And in this reading say “shrunk back” — namely because he had not received [the belt] from the prince. And note here that the character is imprinted on soldiers. *Jo. de Fant.*
