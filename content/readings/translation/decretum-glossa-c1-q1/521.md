## C. CIX. On him who is ordained by one whom he knows to be a simoniac

> Furthermore we decree that if anyone hereafter permits himself to be consecrated by one whom he does not doubt to be a simoniac, both the consecrator and the consecrated shall undergo no unequal sentence of damnation: but both, deposed, shall do penance, and shall remain deprived of their proper dignity.

## C. CX. To simoniacs no mercy is to be shown by preserving them in their dignity

> Concerning simoniacs we decree that no mercy is to be shown by preserving them in their dignity; but, according to the sanctions of the canons and the decrees of the holy Fathers, we wholly condemn them, and by apostolic authority we ordain that they be deposed.

*This must be taken with the understanding “unless they have been drawn in by force.” For concerning these, and those ordained by force by any heretics whatsoever, Innocent speaks to the bishops of Macedonia, epist. 22, c. 5.*

## C. CXI. On those who are unwillingly drawn into ordination by heretics

> It is clear that many have suffered violence, and unwilling, drawn in, and resisting, have been ordained by heretics. But if such a one, after such an ordination, was not present when they confected the sacraments, if he did not participate in their communion, if, as soon as they had departed, he renounced their most wicked conventicle and returned to the Church — such a one may have some color of excuse. As for those, however, who returned after a month or more, when they considered themselves ordained by heretics — although they received nothing from them — they are guilty of usurped dignity.

**¶It is clear \[Constat\].** CASUS. It is said here that if any have been ordained by heretics through coercion: if they did not consent to their communion, but immediately, when the violence ceased, returned to the Church, they are sufficiently excused; but those who return after a month are then guilty of an accepted dignity.

**e. ¶If immediately.** Argument: if a woman is handed over to someone unwilling, and she resists with all her strength, if she flees immediately after the handing over, that marriage does not hold, which is presumed from the word at *Cum statim. Especially if she never consented.* H. And so by what follows the preceding are grasped *2. 12. q. 2. quod ait. De poeni. dist. 3. Iudas.*

**f. ¶Returned.** With this intention, that he not return to them again: as the law says, he who flees from enemies with the intention of returning to them again does not have the right of postliminium. *ff. de cap.* And c. 1. pen. for it is not enough that he has returned home, if he is alienated in mind, as *ff. de offic. praef. l. 1. divus.* and *39. q. indicas.*

**g. ¶Of excuse.** An argument that someone in doubt is excused from punishment because of coercion in orders. Thus *50. dist. presbyteros. 15. q. 6. c. 1. 20. q. 3. praesens.* Argument to the contrary, *15. q. 1. merito.* And *infra de conse. dist. 4. eos, quos.*

**h. ¶After a month.** Argument: if a woman is handed over to someone unwilling, and she remains in the house with him for some time, and when she could she did not flee; if she afterwards leaves the man, she can be recalled by him, because by such a consent of time the marriage was ratified. Argument *31. q. 2. de neptis. 63. dist. quanto. 50. dist. presbyteros. & dist. 45. de Iudaeis. & extra de spon. ad id quod.*

**¶Furthermore \[De cetero\].** \[CASUS.\] Does not doubt. Because it is notorious: or by sentence. H. But the letter contradicts.

**b. ¶Let them undergo sentence.** Understand this concerning him whom he could prove to be a simoniac, who is to be ordained. Better understand it according to what I said in the gloss on the immediately preceding chapter, on the gloss *per sacramentum*, at the word *hoc teneas*. A.

**¶Toward simoniacs.** CASUS. He says in this chapter that simoniacs promoted to some dignity must be deposed: and no dispensation can be made with them. The following § makes it clear, where he excepts those drawn by force: whether they were drawn by simoniacs or by heretics.

**c. ¶No \[mercy\].** *Infra eod. si quis neque. Infra ead. q. 7. requiritis. Infra ead. q. 5. c. contra.* Sol.: here simony is in both the promoted and the promoter.

**i. ¶Or more.** Argument for proving that consent was present in marriage, and \[that one should\] not at once turn away while one is able. *16. q. 1. revertimini.*

**¶Such persons \[Tales\].** CASUS. It is said in this *tales*, namely those drawn by force by heretics: whether they returned immediately or not, they are sometimes tolerated out of mercy: but no dispensation is made with simoniacs: nor can they perform authentic — that is, fruitful — penance, unless they renounce that which they have simoniacally obtained: and then a dispensation is made with them, just as a dispensation is made with the lesser heretics. The following chapter makes it clear, in which it is shown how a dispensation is made with a heretic.
