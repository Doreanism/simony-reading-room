## C. XLV. Just as in honor, so also in learning ought each one to excel

> Most vile † a is he to be counted, unless he excels b in learning and holiness, who is more eminent in honor.

> *§ 7. It is therefore clear c that that saying of Augustine — namely, that the sacraments of Christ ministered through heretics do not lack their effect — is not to be understood generally of all the sacraments, but of the sacrament of baptism.*

*Whence the same Augustine says, tract. 5 on John.*

## C. XLVI. Baptism, whether ministered by a good or by an evil minister, ought not to be repeated

> Judas gave d baptism, and after Judas no one was baptized [anew]; John gave e f, and after John there was baptizing g; because that which was given by Judas was the baptism of Christ h, but that which was given by John was John’s own. We rightly prefer not Judas to John, but the baptism of Christ given even through the hands of Judas, to the baptism of John given even through John’s own hands. *And a little after.* ¶Those whom John baptized, John baptized; but those whom Judas baptized, Christ baptized. So, then, those whom a drunkard i baptized, those whom a murderer baptized, those whom an adulterer baptized — if it was the baptism of Christ, Christ baptized them. *Again, after a few words.* ¶This sacrament is so holy, that it is not polluted even by a murderer ministering it.

*†al. To be compared to the vilest.*

**a. ¶Most vile.** [?] or in learning he excels in learning &c.

**Let the prelate’s learning be such [?] sufficient.**

**Honesty and diligence is required in a prelate.**

**IT IS THEREFORE CLEAR.** This is the 7th part of this question, in which it is said that a sacrament conferred in the form of the Church by an evil catholic, [if he is] tolerated, is ratified. Joannes de Fant.

**c. ¶It is therefore clear.** This refers back to that chapter *ex catholica*, *and ca. iustus*.

**Gave. ¶ CASE.** In baptizing a catechumen the merit of the giver is not regarded, as is proved by the example of Judas: for those whom he baptized were not afterwards baptized [again], because Christ baptized them. But those who were well baptized by John were afterwards baptized [again]: because John[’s] was [John’s own] baptism. So, then, those whom a drunkard, an adulterer, or a murderer baptizes, Christ baptizes.

**d. ¶Gave.** Because baptism was a certain sacramental [rite] by which one was cleansed. *De confe. dist. 4. baptismus et [?].*

**e. ¶Gave.** In the name of Christ, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; and therefore *distin. 4. aliud*.

**f. ¶John gave.** In the name of John.

**g. ¶And there was baptizing.** Below, *de generabantur*.

**h. ¶It was the baptism of Christ.** In the name of the one to come, &c., and therefore it was read. Here it is to be considered that some were baptized with the baptism of Christ by John; it must be known in what state John was. To those reply that they, not knowing the Holy Spirit in his baptism, were afterwards baptized with the baptism of Christ. But the baptism of John was in the name of the one to come; whence Jerome on [?]: He who says he believes in Christ, and does not believe in the Holy Spirit, does not yet have clear eyes. Hence those baptized by John in the name of the one to come — that is, of our Lord Jesus Christ — said: “We have not so much as heard whether there is a Holy Spirit,” but neither [did they hear of] the Holy Spirit explicitly in the name of Christ. They were rebaptized in the name of Christ — that is, after Christ was baptized in the baptism of John — they had both the Father and the Son; but by the laying on of hands of the apostles upon them they received the Holy Spirit. Others who did not so believe were baptized with the baptism of Christ, as has been said. Whence Jerome: Those who did not know the Holy Spirit when they received baptism from John were baptized again, lest anyone from among the Jews or Gentiles [?].
