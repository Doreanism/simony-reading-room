## C. LXXXVIII. What hath Christ done to you.

> WHat hath Christ done a to you, who endured his betrayer with such patience that he handed over to him the first b Eucharist — consecrated, as to the rest c [of the apostles], and entrusted by his own [hand] [?] and by his own mouth [?], just as to the other Apostles? What hath Christ done to you, who sent that same betrayer of his, whom he called a devil e — who before the Lord’s betrayal could not even practice fraud on the Lord’s purses f — to preach the kingdom of heaven with the other disciples, in order to demonstrate that the gifts of God reach those who receive them with faith, even if the one through whom they receive be such as Judas was?

*Likewise Gregory.*

¶The malice of the bishop does not harm the sacraments.

## C. LXXXIX. The malice of the bishop does not harm the sacraments.

> THe malice of the bishop does not harm either the baptism of the infant or the consecration g of the church h: because baptism is given by God, it does not come from man; so too the Eucharist, and all things whatsoever that are placed on the altar receive their sanctification not from man but from God.

## C. XC. Not the words but the merits of priests.

> PRiests who serve at the Eucharist, and divide the blood of the Lord among his people, act impiously against the law of Christ, thinking that the Eucharist is made by the words k of one who invokes, not by his life l; and that only the solemn prayer is necessary, and not the merits of the priests, of whom it is said: [And a priest in whom there shall be a blemish m, shall not approach to offer offerings to the Lord.]

**a. ¶What hath Christ done.** Who showed us signs of patience.

**b. ¶First.** That is, he gave the first consecrated Eucharist. For there is only one [Eucharist], and therefore it is not first or last. Or thus: “first,” that is, the only one, and not many.

**c. ¶[By his] mouth.** When he said: “This is my body.” *Below de consecr. dist. 2. accipite.*

**d. ¶To the rest.** *Above 23. q. 4. tu bonus*, and *c. quam magnum. Below de cons. dist. 2. cum omne.* Contra. Solution: but there [it speaks] of the morsel of bread which he gave only to Judas; here, of the body of Christ, which he first delivered to himself, and afterwards gave to all.

**e. ¶He called.** Where, I do not know, but I believe it was where it is said: “He that dippeth his hand with me,” etc. Argument in *Inst. de exhered. lib. Si quis filium [?] sed faemininum [?].*

**f. ¶Purses.** Argument against the unfaithful servants [?], that they are like devils. *De poeni. dist. 1. §. hoc idem [?].*

**¶It does not harm. ¶To the [consecration] of the church.** *Below de consecr. distinct. 2. ecclesijs.* Contra. But that is when the form has not been preserved.

**h. ¶Consecration.** Provided, however, that all [?] or some greater impropriety [?] ceases; otherwise it must rather be called execrated than consecrated. As *J. ea. q. 4. ecclesia.* But this in detestation of the one consecrating, for in truth it is consecrated.

**SED OBIICITVR. CASE.** Now Gratian raises an objection against the foregoing, and so he proceeds in this question, alleging now on one side, now on the other in turn, and drawing solutions.

**i. ¶But it is objected.** This refers to §. *si ergo*, set forth just above. For he adduces these six chapters, some directly, some by inference from the contrary sense, to prove that a bad priest cannot consecrate the sacraments; but he responds there in the following §. *cum vita*.

**¶Priests. CASE.** It is said in this chapter that not only the words but also the merits of priests must concur, because it is written: “Let him not approach to sacrifice having a blemish.” The three following chapters are plain, in which it is said that priests who intercede for others ought to be clean, because they are more easily heard.

**k. ¶Of one who invokes.** That is, of one calling [upon God] through prayers. H.

**l. ¶Not by his life.** As to the matter of the sacrifice itself, namely that they may consecrate more worthily; otherwise life is not required in order for them to consecrate.

*ADDITION. Supply: but that they may profit the one offering. Below same. cum vita. Archdeacon.*

**m. ¶Blemish.** Of mortal sin; argument *de consecr. dist. 2. quotidie.* Or of venial, and then it is a counsel. Argument *dist. 6. testamentum.*
