> *§. But it is objected a, that \[passage\] of Augustine against the heretics, treatise 5 on the Gospel of John.*

> *¶Whether the minister be good or bad, through either the effect of grace is conferred.*

## C. XXX. On the same

> If the minister be just b, I count him with Paul, who does not seek his own glory, saying: *I planted c, Apollos watered d, but God gave the increase.* But whoever shall have been a proud minister, is counted with the devil: but the gift of Christ is not contaminated, because through him the river runs pure to the pure, and comes to the fertile land. I know that a stone cannot bear fruit from water: and if water passes through stone channels e to the little plots, in the stone channel nothing is generated; but nevertheless the garden bears much fruit. For the spiritual power of the sacrament is received like pure light by those to be enlightened; but passing through unclean ones it is not polluted.

*¶From the beginning of this chapter up to the word “the gift of Christ,” Alger, book 3, c. 5, cites it from blessed Augustine, whose own words are nevertheless found below at de confec. dist. 4. c. aliud. §. si fuerit.*

*The same to Vincent the Donatist, epistle 48.*

## C. XXXI. On the same

> All f the Lord’s sacraments come from the catholic g Church: which you so have and give, as they were had h and given even before you went out from it.

*The same on baptism against the Donatists, book 5, c. 19.*

## C. XXXII. On the same

> But thus God i is absent from † his own sacraments, and his own words k,

*† al. Si \[If\]*

**a. ¶But it is objected.** In defense of the simoniacs, by proving that the sacraments which they confer have effect. Johannes.

**¶If just.** THE CASE. Here it is shown, that if the priest be just, he is a good minister of the sacraments, and not the agent \[author\]: which is proved by the example of Paul. If, however, he is bad, his malice does not hinder the sacraments, because just as one is not sterile, although it flows through a sterile channel: so the spiritual power, even if it passes through unclean ones, is nevertheless received by those to be enlightened. And note that from this above-cited chapter up to the chapter *etiam corde*, it is proved that the sacraments of bad priests are true: and are truly conferred by them, provided they are conferred in the form of the Church.

**b. ¶If just.** From this chapter up to that one, *et corde*, the master shows that the sacraments of heretics are true, and are truly conferred by them, if they are conferred in the form of the Church: not however as to power: with a distinction however: because sacraments of necessity, unless one receives them from a heretic in necessity, or also if one receives them unknowingly, one indeed receives both the power and truth of the sacrament. But if one does not receive them out of necessity or knowingly, one indeed receives the truth of the sacrament, not the power: if however one receives feignedly, and afterwards does penance, one will have both. *De consecratione*, dist. 4. *tunc valere*. & §. *sed notandum* 2. below, in the same place. But in a sacrament of dignity, as in order, distinguish according to that distinction at 11. q. 1. *ordinationes*. In the sacrament of the Eucharist we say that from the hand of a heretic one is not to receive. 24. q. 1. cap. ult. & 32. dist. §. *verum*. If however it be received, it is the true body, if it is confected in the form of the Church: although some say that they themselves do not consecrate. 24. q. 1. *schisma*.

**ADDITION.** Argument: *de consecratione*, dist. 2. *quid sit*\[?\]. Archidiaconus.

**c. ¶I planted.** By preaching.

**d. ¶Watered.** By baptizing.

**e. ¶Passes through.** Below, in the same place, *sic populus contra*. Solution: this concerns sacraments of necessity, which always have effect unless the fault of the recipient impedes; and there \[it is\] concerning sacraments of dignity, or here concerning those feignedly ordained: there concerning those whom the Church tolerates. Or say that they are placed as regards those, as 49. dist. cap. ult. 15. q. ult. c. *si*.

**Ex catholica.** THE CASE. Augustine says that from the catholic Church the heretics received true sacraments, who so have and confer them as they had \[them\] before they went out of the Church. The following three chapters are plain, and self-evident.

**f. ¶From the catholic.** In this and in the two following chapters, it is said that the sacraments of heretics are true, which are celebrated in the form of the Church.

**g. ¶All.** That is, the truth and form of all the sacraments.

**h. ¶Were had.** Argument to the contrary: J. c. *extra catholicam*. But there explain: that is, outside the form of the catholic Church.

**i. ¶But thus God.** God is said to be present at such sacraments, that the truth may subsist, and that the power may be granted to him who receives unknowingly. Above, *si iustus*.

**k. ¶And his own words.** And so the words are necessary: argument below, in the same place, *detrahe*.
