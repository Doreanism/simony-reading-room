*(Continuing the Gratian dictum from p. 513.)*

> for, by reason of the unbelief of the rest, he lacked nothing of his own truth, but \[only of\] the effect of good through power. ¶And that he might plainly demonstrate this in all the sacraments, he was twice a transfigured: in one transfiguration he appeared like the sun b, that the faith of believers might be increased toward contemplating the heavenly glory raised above himself; in the other, he feigned to be a stranger and unrecognizable to those doubting him \[?\] and to be going further away, and held their eyes — for what other purpose, except that, because by faith he was not in their hearts, he might show himself to be receding further from them, and, because they did not believe him to be that which he was, they should see him as another d than he was? Behold, in each vision his presence and his truth were the same: but in the one faith was exercised, so that they should believe more than they did; in the other unbelief was held back, so that they might not even recognize what they saw. Therefore the sacrament of his body and blood is to some \[a means of salvation\] \[?\], to others \[a means\] of judgment. Faith too, though without works, is dead. So likewise all the ten virgins were equally virgins: but of these, five were prudent and five \[foolish\]. Their virginity was equal, but the intention of that same virginity was not identical in all. So also martyrdom or alms-giving, when charity does not acknowledge it, is sounding brass e or a cymbal f. If indeed the blind g should hold a lantern of truth for the blind, neither yet on that account illumines his own blindness any the more. So also an evil minister administers true sacraments to an evil receiver, but not spiritual gifts on that account; because by their evil deserts the Holy Spirit is prevented from working in them h what is his own. Hence Innocent i, when he grants that the baptism of heretics is valid, does not yet grant that the Holy Spirit is had from that baptism; \[and\] for those who have received the form of baptism but are not made holy and truly so when the giver is acting unlawfully, but he commands that the Holy Spirit, which no one receives from heretics, be obtained by his invocation and the imposition of hands by Catholic priests. It must be known, however, that the sacraments of heretics are said to be invalid, or even to be condemned, false, and empty, not so far as they are in themselves — since they are holy and true even when celebrated by heretics — but because, since they are unto judgment for the unfaithful who give them unlawfully, they do not confer the Holy Spirit on those who unlawfully receive them. They are called invalid and not true because what they promise, and what is believed to be conferred, they do not bestow; and therefore they are to be condemned, so that the giving or receiving of them by heretics may not be approved but forbidden. For so far as they are in themselves they are not polluted, although they are said to be polluted by the heretics. Hence Gregory calls the communion of Arius an execration, and Innocent calls the ordination of Bonosus a condemnation: not that they are so in themselves, but that they fail those who give or receive them ill i. So too Jerome on Hosea calls their sacrifices k the bread of mourning, not as to themselves, but as to their effect — which he himself shows by adding: \[*Whoever shall eat of it shall be defiled, because he takes it unto judgment for himself*.\]

*Hence also Augustine, book 2 against the Epistle, chapter 5.*

*α.* Sed ne Augustinum.*\] Here Gratian seems to speak of St. Augustine as if his opinion on this matter were doubtful or uncertain:*

**a. ¶Bis.** Concerning the first \[transfiguration\], or concerning the second, as it appeared to the two disciples going to the castle of Emmaus. But why does he say *in the flesh*, since even now he is in the flesh? But say that some say *in the flesh*, namely in such-and-such flesh, but wrongly. For in mortal flesh, according to mortal flesh, he was transfigured only once; after the passion indeed only once. And therefore it cannot be said *when he was in the flesh*, that is, among the carnal, where he assumed flesh. Or it can be understood *in the flesh*, *scilicet* mortal, and so the punctuation will be placed there: *he was twice transfigured when he was in mortal flesh* — that is, in the transfiguration on Mount Tabor, and after the resurrection appearing in the guise of a pilgrim. \[?\]

*Christ was twice transfigured in the flesh.*

**b. ¶Sol.** That is, Christ. And this is the first example.

**c. ¶Apparuit mirabilis.** Before James, Peter, and John on Mount Tabor.

**d. ¶Alium.** That is, of another sort.

**e. ¶Sonans.** Because it confines itself, lending sound to another.

**f. ¶Charitas.** *7. q. vide.*

**g. ¶Caeco.** *If a blind man tries to lead a blind man along: the leader will first fall into the pit, then the follower.*

*A blind man ought not to undertake to lead a blind man.*

**h. ¶Ne in eis.** While they are in heresy, or as regards the effect, except in case of necessity or in ignorance. *s. ea. perueniente.*

**i. ¶Innocentius.** *s. ea. q. qui perfectionem. j. ea. q. 7. c. 5. §. item.* nec. and *c. quod pro remedio.*

**K. ¶Sacrificia.** As is said. *above c. q. §. sed illi.* and *c. Dominus.* verse *nos dicimus.* and *c. si quis in \[?\].*

**l. ¶Non quantum.** *dist. 49. sacerdotes. j. ea. q. 3. saluator.*
