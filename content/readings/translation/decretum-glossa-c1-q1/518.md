## C. CII. Nothing is to be exacted from priests for the balsam which is placed in the chrism

> It has been resolved that no bishop shall further exact anything for the small amount of balsam a which, blessed, is given for the sacrament of baptism throughout the churches; since some are accustomed † to exact a tremissis b for it: lest by chance, just as Simon Magus wished to buy the gift of God with money, so we, selling what is consecrated for the salvation of souls through the invocation of the Holy Spirit, should ourselves damnably be sold.

¶Nothing is to be exacted from those who bring their infants for baptism.

## C. CIII. Nothing is to be exacted from those who bring their infants for baptism

> It has been resolved that each bishop shall command throughout his churches that those who offer their infants for baptism — if they offer anything voluntarily of their own vow, it is to be received from them. But if through the constraint of poverty they have nothing to offer, no pledge is to be violently taken from them by the clerics. For many of the poor, fearing this, hold back their sons from baptism: and if perhaps, while \[baptism\] is deferred, they depart this life without the grace of baptism, their c perdition d must necessarily be required e from those whose despoilings they feared, on account of which they withdrew themselves from the grace of baptism.

*Likewise from the Council of Elvira, ch. 48.*

**¶Placuit, vt nullus.\] CASE.** It is here laid down that no bishop shall exact a tremissis for chrism, as bishops were once accustomed to do, lest they, in selling \[it\] — as Simon Magus wished to buy \[the gift\] — be themselves damnably sold.

**a. ¶Balsami.\]** That is, of chrism, or chrism. Therefore chrism cannot be sold, even in necessity, since it is a sacrament. *1. q. 1. multi.* and *1. q. 3. si quis praebendas* and *above, the same place, quidquid.*

*Chrism cannot be sold.*

**b. ¶Tremissem.\]** A tremissis is the third part of a solidus. Concerning this tremissis you have *C. de veste \[?\] milit. fortissimis. lib. 12.* And it is therefore called *tremissis* according to Isidore, as it were “thrice put together \[*ter missus*\] it makes a solidus.”

*A tremissis is the third part of a solidus.*

**¶Placuit.\] CASE.** It is laid down in this chapter that for the conferring of baptism nothing is to be exacted; however, what is offered spontaneously may be received; and if anyone, through fear of extortion, has withdrawn himself from baptism, the soul of the one in peril shall be required from such a priest.

**c. ¶Eorum.\]** Argument that he is in the same perdition who gives the cause or occasion of damage, and he who is seen to have caused the damage. *J. \[?\] 23. q. 5. de occidendis* and *c. cum homo*. Contrary: *26. q. 6. si presbyter. J. 27. q. 2. si tu abstines. S. dist. 55. si quis absciderit. J. 17. q. 4. diffiniuit. saepe. J. 16. q. 1. decimae \[?\]. 50. dist. studeat.* and *c. Jo. Sol.* This is to be understood of a proximate cause, especially when they give the cause or occasion with evil zeal. H.

*He is seen to have caused the damage who gives occasion for the damage.*

**d. ¶Perditio.\]** For there is no difference whether one kills or provides the cause of death. *ff. ad l. Cor. de sic. nihil.* Hence if, after the oath of impunity has been given, the priest does not return runaway slaves who have fled to the church, and they afterward flee, their flight is to be sought from the priest, as below *17. q. 4. diffiniuit*. For he who gives the occasion of damage, etc. *ff. ad l. Aquil. qui occidit. §. ult.*

**e. ¶Requiratur.\]** Note that for time and place things become unlawful which are simply lawful, as *27. q. 1. neque uiduas.*

**¶Emendari.\] CASE.** It is here said that for baptism nothing should be received, nor are the feet of those to be baptized to be washed. The next two chapters are clear, in which it is said that for the sacraments, or for orders, burial, chrism, communion, or lights, nothing ought to be exacted.

*1 Hence he adds,* ne sacerdos *etc.*

**f. ¶Non mittant.\]** For simony is presumed. 1. A similar presumption arises from time or place. *J. 31. q. 1. relatu. speciem mali. Arg. extra de transact. constitutus. ff. ad leg. Iul. de ambitu. l. una*, where it is said that the accused ought not to enter the house of the judge. Here argue that a deed in itself praiseworthy is reproved by reason of time, lest a malign \[?\] occasion be introduced. For although to throw coins into the basin is a work of piety, yet at the time of receiving baptism it introduces a suspicion of simony. *Arg. is ff. ad l. Iul. de ambitu. l. vna. in fin.* — so much so that if even under some pretext it is given at a forbidden time, the presumption of simony does not vanish. *Arg. 57. dist. si quis in aegritudine 2. 8. q. 3. c. primo.*

*A work in itself praiseworthy is reproved by reason of time.*

*2 And note* j. 18. q. 2. diff\[i\]nimus.

**g. ¶Ne sacerdos.\]** Argument that one must abstain not only from evils but also from every appearance of evil. *Arg. above 54. dist. generalis.*

*One must abstain from every appearance of evil.*
