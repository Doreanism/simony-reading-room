# Causa 1, Question 1

*(Continuing canon from p. 477, gloss tail.)*

> ...when she had brought those things: and yet the prophet is not written to have received them: since indeed he both rebuked her, and foretold the coming mourning; and although, &c.

*β. ¶Ten loaves.\] So has the Latin Vulgate of blessed Jerome: loaves, and raisins, and a small vessel of honey. In the Greek vulgate, however, “cake” and “raisin” are mentioned — ἄρτους, he says, καὶ κολλύρια τοῖς τέκνοις αὐτοῦ καὶ καψύλας \[loaves, and small cakes for her children, and small vessels\].*

*γ. ¶For these things rather.\] These words up to “Although,” are not found here in blessed Jerome, but in their place here is: “For it might be said, what she should have brought with her: and yet the prophet is not written to have received it: since indeed he both rebuked her, and foretold the coming mourning; and although, &c.”*

*δ. ¶Whence in the fourth.\] These words up to the end are not found in blessed Jerome.*

## C. XXIIII. That divination is not taken in a good sense

> Never ª is divination ᵇ taken in scripture in a good sense. \[There is no augury in Jacob, nor divination in Israel.\] They seemed to themselves indeed to be prophets: but because they took money, their prophecy was turned into divination.

*¶This chapter also is, in some old \[copies\], joined to the preceding one, and is read in the original as “For never.”*

> Since therefore ᶜ, according to Jerome, the good prophets did not receive a payment for prophecy, but alms for the tabernacle, but for the wicked, who received \[a payment\], prophecy — which was the gift of God — was turned into divination, which is diabolical: what else is to be gathered, except that (as was said above) no spiritual grace cooperates with simoniacs? For since *(in the sacraments neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything: but God who gives the increase, who works in them, if the Lord* does not shine, but is idle. Likewise, what is said concerning Christ, that just as Christ when sold performed miracles, so the Holy Spirit can be sold, and even in the very selling can work his grace: this similarity does not hold. For the Holy Spirit does not work spiritually in the perfidious traffickings of avarice that which Christ did corporally for the sake of a certain mystery, as an example of patience. For if the comparison is total, since it is believed that he can be sold as Christ was, it would also be believed that the Holy Spirit could be crucified, which is impossible. It can also be understood *(so that we do not depart from the comparison)* that, just as Christ when sold did not confer the gifts of his grace upon the seller or buyer, but upon those who were standing by in ignorance — for whom also he prayed on the cross — so also the Holy Spirit is proved to bestow the effect of his grace not upon the seller or buyer, but upon those who, led by ignorance, receive the Lord’s sacraments from their hands. Likewise it is objected ᵈ, that although it is bad to buy ecclesiastical things, nevertheless evil beginnings often come to happy endings. As Jacob came to the blessing by supplanting ᵉ, as a crop that is sown by a leprous hand is nevertheless reaped clean, as from adulterous intercourse a beautiful offspring is born: so also money that is acquired through simony has a healthy outcome, when it is distributed for the benefit of the poor. But none of these things defends the simoniacs.

*For Leo the bishop says to the Moorish bishops, ep. 85. al. 87.*

**¶Never.** THE CASE. It is said here that divination is rarely taken in a good sense. Hence those who took money for declaring future things were not true prophets, but diviners, although they believed themselves to be prophets.

**a. ¶Never.** That is, rarely \[and\] said for the most part. Otherwise it is false. As above, in the same place, *eos qui*.

**b. ¶Divination.** For it is impossible that divination should be prophecy. So explain thus: “was turned into divination,” that is, into the divination of hidden things which was believed to be prophecy. For their prophecy was declared not to be prophecy.

ADDITION. That you may better understand this, know that prophecy is the manifestation of latent things — past, present, or future — by divine inspiration, according to Papias.

**¶THEREFORE.** THE CASE. Gratian concludes from the foregoing that the grace of the Holy Spirit is void in those who take money: notwithstanding the example of Peter, who when sold performed miracles, since he did this for the sake of a mystery, as an example of patience, or that he might benefit others, and not the buyer or seller: just as simoniacs may benefit those who are ignorant that they are simoniacs: and notwithstanding the fact that good ends often come from things begun with a bad beginning.

**c. ¶According to.** And thus the master teaches us to resist. Argument from a similar case: 17. q. 1. *nuptia*. ruff. de condi. indeb. *si reus*. 12. q. 7. *Apostoli*. Moreover, a response can be made through what is noted above, in the same place, *eas* 2.

**d. ¶Likewise it is objected.** In defense of the simoniacs, others — from §. *sed nihil*, etc. — to impugn them. \[?\]

**e. ¶By supplanting.** Yet \[it was\] done at the Lord’s prompting. 7. q. 1. *quam periculosum*. Beginnings.
