*(Continuing from p. 505.)*

> ...Augustine responded: You say this as if we were now asking who is a true priest, and not what is true baptism. For in order that anyone may be a true priest, it is necessary that he be clothed not only with the sacrament b, but also with justice; as it is written: [Let thy priests be clothed with justice.] But he who is a priest by the sacrament alone — as was the high priest Caiaphas, persecutor of the one and most true priest — although he himself is not truthful, what he gives is nevertheless true, provided he does not give what is his own c, but God’s. *Likewise d a little below.*

> ¶One of themselves, a prophet, said: Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies; this testimony is true. If, therefore e, the Apostle attested the testimony of some foreigner (I know not whom), because he found it to be true, why, when we find with anyone something which is of Christ, and is true, even though the one with whom it is found is perverse and deceitful, do we not distinguish the vice which the man has f from the truth which he has not as his own but as God’s? And [why do we not] say: this sacrament is true, just as he says, this testimony is true? Do we therefore say, “this man too is truthful,” because we say, “this sacrament is true”? *And a little later.*

> ¶But you must always be in error, so long as you either believe that on account of the vices of men the sacraments of God must be violated h, or think that we, on account of the sacraments of God which we refuse to violate in ourselves, must take on the sacrilege i of your schism. *Likewise.*

> ¶The purity of baptism is altogether distinct from the purity or uncleanness of the conscience of either the one giving or the one receiving. *Likewise.*

> ¶Remember therefore that the morals of bad men in no way harm the sacraments of God so as to make them either not exist at all or to be less holy. *Likewise.*

> ¶I ask, if Saul k had not the holiness of the sacrament, what did David venerate in him? If, however, he had innocence, why did David persecute the innocent? For on account of the sacrosanct anointing, he both honored him while living and avenged him when slain. And because he had cut off a small piece l from his garment, his heart trembled when struck. Behold, Saul did not have innocence; and yet he had holiness, not of his own life α (for no one can have this without innocence), but of the sacrament of God, which is holy even in bad men. *Likewise.*

> ¶We say that the sacrifice is to each one such as he who comes to offer it, and such as he who comes to receive it; and that those eat of the sacrifices of such men who approach them being such as they themselves are. Thus, if a bad man offers to God, and a good man receives from there, [the sacrifice is to each one such] as each one has been; for it is also written: [All things are clean to the clean m.] By this true and catholic statement, *Optatus n*, neither were you polluted p, if his deeds...

**a. ¶Baptism.** As if [to say], this is what we ask, and not that; and therefore the objection is not valid, etc.

**b. ¶Sacrament.** By rank and office, without justice. Argument contra *40. distinct. sicut viri.* But there the matter concerns the essence of the priest; here the quality.

**c. ¶If he does not give what is his own.** That is, if [he gives] not his own [things] but God’s; namely in the form of the Church.

**d. ¶Likewise.** The words are those of Epimenides the Theban priest, who was bad; and the Apostle takes the same words in testimony in the epistle to Titus 1.

**e. ¶If therefore.** And it is true (supply); and therefore let us approve it.

**f. ¶He has.** Argument in support of the testimony of someone who is not good, that his testimony should be believed in which he speaks truly, even if he lies in other matters. *Above dist. 37. turbat, and cap. si quid;* but contra *3. q. 6. pura.*

**g. ¶True.** As if to say: no. And thus not everyone who speaks the truth is truthful. *22. q. 2. homines.*

**h. ¶You violate.** That is, you believe must be violated.

**i. ¶Sacrilege.** As if we should be seen to approve your schism [?] from the fact that we do not reject your sacraments.

**k. ¶If Saul did not have.** Whom the Lord had chosen and made king — that is, had commanded to be made — as *below 8. q. 1. audacter.*

**l. ¶Small piece.** Who cut off [a part] of the cloak of his garment, as objected at *2. q. 7. c. plerumque. §. de his*, and *§. item opponitur.*

**m. ¶All things are clean to the clean.** According to the saying: He who [has] dirt on his hands; let him first wash the dirt off his works. Cleanse yourself within, because to the clean all things are clean.

**n. ¶Optatus.** This is a proper name, and he was a bad Catholic, or rather a heretic — that is, a hidden one.

**o. ¶Sacrifice.** Here it is clear that one who is cut off [from the Church] consecrates, as below *c. non nocet*, and *c. dictum*, and *de consecr. distinct. 4. non in vobis.* Moreover, what would be the reason why one damned by God could consecrate so long as he is tolerated by the Church, but one damned by men could not? Therefore say that an excommunicated, deposed heretic can administer this sacrament and the other sacraments, according to the chapters I have cited. Some, however, say the contrary. 2. according to that which is in *24. quaest.*

**p. ¶Polluted.** Argument that the sin of another is not imputed to a companion, if it displeases him, provided that he admonishes him to come to his senses.

*ADDITION. Understand all these last cited [authorities] as to the effect of the sacrament, or when they do not preserve the form of the Church, according to Raymond, who notes sufficiently on this in his summa de haeret. §. item nunquid, etc.*
