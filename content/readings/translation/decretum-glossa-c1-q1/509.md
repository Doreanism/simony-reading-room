¶On the same.

## C. XCV. Neither can that be rightly offered.

> NEither a can that be rightly b offered, except by a just and holy priest; nor [can it be offered] unless it be received by those for whom it is offered c; and that without fault d, so that it may be offered for the cleansing e of those defiled f. This indeed all desire, who wish that the sacrifice be offered to God for themselves.

*With the life b of priests the Eucharist is impaired [?]; this is to be understood not of the consecration but of the effect [?]. For it does not confer the effect of salvation upon him to whom rather pertains [?] the saying of the Apostle: [He who eats the body of the Lord and drinks his blood unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself.] Therefore against those who think that they can be cleansed by frequent offering of the sacrifice while not ceasing to act impiously against the law of God, that saying of Jerome is to be understood. But that a priest, even if he be bad, nevertheless for those offering [?] transfuses grace to men, Augustine testifies in his book of questions on the Old Testament, c. 11.*

¶Priests invoke the name of the Lord, but the Lord bestows the blessing.

## C. XCVI. It was said by God.

> IT was said h by God in Numbers to Moses and Aaron the priests: [You shall put i my name upon the children of Israel: I the Lord will bless them], so that he may transfuse the grace of [his] transmission through the ministry of the one ordaining onto men: nor can the will of the priest obstruct or avail k, but [only] the merit of the one asking the blessing. ¶How great is the dignity of the priestly order, let us perceive from this. For it was said of the most wicked Caiaphas, the slayer of the Savior, among other things: [Now this he said not of himself, but being the high priest of that year, he prophesied.] From this it is shown that the spirit of graces does not follow a person, whether worthy or unworthy, but the ordination of [the] transmission; so that, although someone be of good merit, he nevertheless cannot bless unless he has been ordained to exercise the office of ministry. But it is God’s [task] to grant the effect of the blessing.

*But the assertion that the sacraments of dignity ministered through heretics are said to lack effect is refuted by the authority of [Pope] Anastasius, who [approved] the ordination of Acacius, although a heretic...*

**¶Neither. Note that three things are said here: namely that the sacraments of the Lord are not rightly or properly conferred unless they are conferred by worthy persons, and received by worthy persons, and in the form of the Church.** The first is noted at: [Except by a priest]. The second at: [Nor unless]. The third at: [And that, etc.]. Some divide the chapter into two parts. In the first mention is made of the sacrifice, which is received by the one for whom it is offered, and this up to that place [And that]. In the second mention is made of the sacrament, which is not received by the one for whom it is offered. And say: “without fault,” that is, without sin.

**b. ¶Rightly.** With respect to the one offering and the one receiving.

**c. ¶For whom it is offered.** Supply: nor can it be rightly offered except by them.

**d. ¶Without fault.** That is, [the sacrament] is not aside from the form of the Church, but in the form of the Church itself.

**e. ¶That for the defiled.** Expound thus: so that it may be rightly offered for the defiled, that is, for those having venial [?] [sins]. But understand generally; from this it is gathered that although the will of the priest is not for it to profit, it nevertheless profits, *below de cons. dist. 4. non illud te*. But what if he sings Mass that God may destroy certain persons? He will indeed consecrate, but he must be punished. Argument *26. quaes. 5. quicunque.*

**f. ¶For the cleansing.** That is, for those having the blemish of venial sin.

**CVM VITA. CASE.** This §. refers to that chapter *sacerdotes*, where it is said that the good life of the priest is necessary for consecrating the body of Christ. Now [Gratian] resolves [it] and says that this is to be understood not of the consecration — because in truth even a bad priest consecrates — but of the effect of the consecration, because it is of no profit if it is taken by an unworthy one. And that a bad priest confers grace on those who receive worthily is proved by the following chapter.

**g. ¶With the life.** This refers to the chapter *sacerdotes*. And he resolves it well, and to confirm his solution he adduces the following chapter.

**DICTVM EST. CASE.** Here first it is proved by the words of the Lord, and by the example of Caiaphas, that among bad priests the sacraments are true, and that by [virtue of] their office they transfuse grace to men. Secondly, [it is proved] that so great is the force of the priestly dignity that without it there is not, even among good men, the authority to bless.

**h. ¶It was said.** Augustine proves that to bless requires not only merit, but also [holy] orders. In the first part, therefore, he sets forth the authority of the Lord, up to: [How great]. In the second he proves his intention both by authority and by the example of Caiaphas.

**QVOD VERO. CASE.** It was said above in §. *si ergo sacramenta*, that the sacraments of dignity conferred by heretics do not have effect. Against this Gratian alleges the authority of Anastasius, which he shows to be frivolous by the authority of Gelasius and Felix, who together with Hormisdas excommunicated Acacius before they had excommunicated Anastasius. But H. expounds “before [they had excommunicated] Anastasius,” that is, “before Anastasius was pope”: because Acacius died before Anastasius, and Anastasius succeeded him. And thus since [Anastasius] succeeded Gelasius, he was not excommunicated by him, nor by his predecessors.
