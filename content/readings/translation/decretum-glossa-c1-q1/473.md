*(Continuing canon C. XXI from p. 472.)*

> of hands, or after. For to receive a is to receive at any time whatsoever.

*¶This chapter is part of the letter of Tarasius to Hadrian. But in some places the order of the words has been altered, and a certain summarizing applied — as is also the case in Anselm. The complete text is preserved in manuscript after the seventh synod in the Vatican library, and in Greek codices of that synod, and in Latin in the version of Anastasius Bibliothecarius. It is also extant in print in Latin in Balsamon. But in that version — whether the interpreter’s, or the exemplar he followed — Gratian inserted a scandalous error. For where Tarasius wonderfully praises Hadrian, the translator introduces him as rebuking with these words,* fraterna ergo vestra sacerdotalis sanctitas, quae non iure, nec ex Dei voluntate pontificale ministrat, magna laborat infamia *\[“Therefore your brotherly priestly sanctity, which ministers pontifically not by right nor by the will of God, labors under great infamy”\]; which is manifestly at odds with many other words that immediately follow, in which he calls him a holy man of the Spirit and uses other illustrious titles. The Greek copies of the Vatican, of the seventh synod, and of Balsamon himself have the correct text. It therefore seemed necessary to present the earlier part of the letter in Greek with a new version. The rest may be sought from the printed Balsamon.*

*[The Greek original of Tarasius’s letter to Pope Hadrian, English content rendered:]*

> To the most holy and most blessed brother and fellow-minister in all things, the lord Hadrian, Pope of elder Rome.
>
> Tarasius, unworthy bishop of Constantinople, in the Lord, greeting.
>
> In many parts and in many ways we are taught — by the Gospel, by the Apostles, and by the Fathers — in the sanctity of the priesthood neither to take gold or silver nor to claim anything for ourselves at the ordination of any priestly man, as we shall show in the citations set forth in order from divine Scriptures and from the teachings of the Fathers. For those who lay on hands are ministers of the Spirit, not vendors `[?]` of the Spirit. For having received the grace of the Spirit freely, they have declared from the word of the Lord that it must be given freely to those who receive it from them, having received this liberty from his very voice. But if anyone be convicted of having bought this for gold, \[the canons\] proclaim such a person cast out of the priestly order †; and even though in name he has obtained the priesthood, the word does not lie `[?]` as to the reality. For no one can serve God and mammon, as we have learned from the Gospel. And since we have heard God prophetically crying out, \[“Priests, speak to the heart of Jerusalem,”\] and again threatening `[?]` and saying, \[“If the watchman sees the sword coming and does not sound the trumpet, and the people do not guard themselves, and the sword comes and takes a soul from them, the blood I will require from the watchman’s hand,”\] from fear of the condemnation that hangs over silence, we declare to all the heads of churches under us — so that with boldness we may say after the divine Apostle, “We are clean from the blood” of those who transgress the canonical decrees, and much more of those who have ordained or been ordained for money — since Peter the divine Apostle, whose chair has fallen to your brotherly holiness, deposed such as a Simon Magus. For this reason we do not shrink from declaring \[the truth\] ...

*[Marginal text-critical note: † τάξεως.]*

**a. ¶To receive.** Argument that in elections and alienations, in order for them to be firm, \[they should\] be made by consent following `[?]`, just as by prior \[consent\]. *Infra 29. q. 1. § his ita. Infra 16. q. 1. c. ulti.* and *7. q. \[1.\] cap. decimas*, etc., and *cap. ecclesiae, extra de iurepatura pastoralis.* Argument to the contrary at *24. distin. cap. ult.*

**a. ¶To receive.** Likewise also “to hinder” — and it is “to hinder at any time” `[?]`, as below in the same, *q. 3. Salvator* and *ca. si quis episcopus praebendas*, and *c. si quis obiecerit*, and *ff. de calum. l. 1. § quod autem.* And therefore he is a simoniac who, on account of an order or a benefice conferred, gives any gift, as I said of Giezi, above in the same, *qui studet.* Joh.
