*(Continuing canon C. CXXIII from p. 526.)*

> — that we will receive absolutely nothing for judging from those whose cases we judge; neither from the parties, nor from those who rejoice in their own administration: neither taking ourselves, nor through an interposed person, nor by any contrivance: content with what is given to us by our sacred treasury, receiving sufficiently for our labor: so that none of our subjects, whose cases we have examined, may sustain any burden on the occasion of judgment: but so acting let us spend the present life as those about to bring back the sentence of a greater Judge.

*See same title, l. cum venalem and §. tantum. l. iurare. ff. de albo scrib. l. 1.*

> §. 15. By this law also Justinian forbids that anything be received for judgment by judges: but on this account the crime of extortion is incurred, and the accused must purge himself of the suspicion of this kind. He establishes almost the same in the following chapter, in which he forbids that anything be received for the power of judging.

*From Justinian’s Code, de iudiciis, l. nullus.*

## C. CXXIV. Judges whom the crime of extortion attaches are infamous, and to be struck with anathema

> Let no judge whatsoever demand or take from litigants a price or reward by occasion of his jurisdiction. Since the license of judging ought to be not venal, but gratuitous: let him know that whoever acts against this is to be perpetually condemned with ecclesiastical or secular infamy.

*See same title of the Code, l. quicunque, and the following law, and in the Authent. de mandatis Principum. §. illud.*

*Likewise from the Code, de calumniatoribus, l. ne calumniis.*

> But he who does the aforesaid things on account of calumnies or false testimonies is a sacrilegious person: for he incurs the crime of falsehood: and all those convicted of such a crime fall from the grades of their dignities and honors.

## C. CXXV. Sacrilegious, and deprived of every honor, are those who by calumnies and flatteries or for a price oppress the justly condemned or the innocent

*The same Emperor, in the Code, de poena iudicum qui male iudicaverunt.*

> Those are properly sacrilegious who, imitating the false flattery or calumny of the populace, or in any other way, oppress against the law those justly condemned: or strike down the innocent by treachery: and likewise those who by usage and law of the people are prohibited from hearing laws, or are prohibited from being a judge or from acting as advocate, and nevertheless judging, advocating, being approached \[as advocate\], we order to be infamous.

*See l. 1. ff. de proxim. sacri scrin. & l. nemo iudicum. C. de poena iudicum male iudic. & ff. de calumn. & ff. de his qui not. inf.*

**a. ¶We judge.** Today, however, something is lawful to accept, such as fees, food, and the like — a moderate amount indeed.

**¶Whose cases we judge.** Argument that salaries for cases being judged are given from the public treasury, not from private litigants, as here. And *infra de iudic. l. cum venalem.*

**b. ¶By occasion of judgment.** That is, from some exaction made by the judge: as because he compelled litigants to give something in order that he might judge. *ff. ad l. Iul. repet. l. 1.*

**c. ¶About to bring back.** To bring back is to refer to one’s account: that is, to hold to one’s loss. *infra l. prox. 12. q. 2. cum dec.*

**¶In whom.** *CASUS*. He who, on account of calumny or false testimony, calumniates someone in his own case, or gives something to his own judge, so that — when those rightly judging the debt are infamous — he will also give fourfold what he received, and afterwards in similar cases cannot be usefully heard.

**d. ¶About to bring back.** From the beginning, after this he swore, and it is done for a year.

**e. ¶Venal.** Likewise he swears for a year: and this is done by fees and clerks, as `[?]`.

**f. ¶Fourfold.** To the extent that the triple alone may be, and \[along with\] the principal sum itself. *infra ead. q. si.* And this is true when so much is given: but if one were to give it on his behalf, then he will give the whole, as `[?]` he who gave it claims back the money. If anyone has received such a sum from someone, it shall be given to the treasury: but half to the accuser.

**g. ¶Sacrilegious.** Not to those who act against this, but to those for whom the Emperor enacted against this, and so upon those judging the penalty of infamy and sacrilege is imposed.

**g. ¶From usage.** That is, from long custom, or by reason of office `[?]`.

**f. ¶But he who.** As if he said: these are the sacrilegious who calumniously oppress some in criminal or civil cases, as `[?]`.

**g. ¶Infamous.** See *ff. de his qui notantur. l. 1.*

**¶They are.** *CASUS*. He who by calumny or flattery liberates the justly condemned, or oppresses the innocent, is sacrilegious: and one who by age, or sex, or infamy, is prohibited from hearing laws, and nevertheless judges, or pleads, or sets himself up as a defender `[?]` is infamous.

**¶Sacrilegious.** A sacrilegious person is one who removes sacred things, or violates what is sacred: which is done also if he accepts a price for judgment, because he takes away from judgment itself, which is sacred. *ff. ad l. Iul. pec. l. 1.*
