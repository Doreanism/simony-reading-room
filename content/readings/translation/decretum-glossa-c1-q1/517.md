# Causa I, Question I

**¶Quidquid.\] CASE.** Say here that for the sacraments or for the conferring of orders nothing ought to be accepted, except what has been offered spontaneously; and if a cleric does otherwise, let him be deposed. And if, with the bishop knowing and negligent to correct, anything has been exacted, let him be deprived of communion for two months. But if without his knowledge the clerics have accepted anything, they shall be punished according to the quality of persons and orders by the penalties here laid down, and shall nonetheless be deposed.

## C. CI. The gifts of invisible grace are not to be compared to traffickings

> Whatever is bestowed by the consolation ϐ b of invisible a grace ought never to be sold for traffickings γ c or any kind of payments, since the Lord says: \[*What you have freely received, freely give.*\] And therefore, whoever henceforth, being constituted in ecclesiastical order, has received for such ambition any prices or rewards (except what δ has been voluntarily offered) for the baptizing and confirming of the faithful, or for the conferral of chrism, or for promotion of grades — if such a thing has been done by his subordinates with the bishop of the place knowing it, that same bishop shall lie under excommunication d for two months, for the reason that he covered known ε evils and did not apply the necessary correction. But if one of his \[clergy\], without his knowledge, has thought he should accept anything for the aforesaid items e: if he is a presbyter f, let him be struck with four months’ excommunication g; if a deacon, with three; but a subdeacon h or cleric serving these greeds is to be struck with a fitting ζ penalty i and with due excommunication.
