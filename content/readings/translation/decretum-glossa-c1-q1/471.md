## C. XX. A simoniac is proved to be unfaithful

> Since all greed o \[is the servitude of idols\]

**a. Cannot.** By strict law: for by dispensation I can. *Infra ea. q. ult. convenientibus.*

**b. But it is said.** That is, “it is objected.” And this was the second objection that was put to Innocent. The first was at *Sed contra.*

**c. Blessing.** Which is given when one returning from heretics is reconciled to the church. Or, that \[blessing\] which according to those against whom Innocent here argues is given when those returning to the church are ordained again, or whenever anyone is ordained. —H.

**d. But of our.** Namely, of the Western. This is the third reason.

**e. Law.** That is, “custom.” *C. de man. li. l. 1.* Elsewhere it is put for “rule”: *J. ea. q. 7. c. 2.* Elsewhere for “principle”: *j. dist. consuetudo.* Elsewhere for “canon”: *5. dist. 31. Nicaena 1.*

**f. By the imposition of hands.** That is to say, not only those who were ordained by heretics cannot have honor in the church, but also those who were only baptized among them, in such a way that they have not been ordained, as at *J. ead. q. 7. qui in qualibet.*

**g. Even the least.** It is otherwise in the eastern church, which reforms them by the unction of chrism, as at *J. de cons. dist. 4. ab antiqua. J. ca. q. 4. placuit*, contra. But there \[the discussion is\] of infants; here of adults and persons of discretion, *de consec. dist. 4. c. sicut 2.*

**But it is objected.** This is the third part of the question, in which it is said that simoniacs do not seem to be in the same case as heretics: and he replies that they are the same, since simoniacs are infidels: and he proves this by four authorities. Joh. de Faventia. Casus. It was said above in *§. quia* and in the following chapter that simoniacs are compared with heretics; Gratian now objects, saying: heretics do not have the faith, but the simoniacs do — even though they consider the grace of the Holy Spirit a thing for sale; and what is said in the chapter *qui perfectionem* cannot be understood of heretics. To this he answers, saying that simoniacs, although they appear to have the faith, are nonetheless subject to infidelity, which is proved by the following chapter.

**h. To these.** \[Things\] said by him. For he is putting an objection to himself on account of the comparison between simoniacs and heretics in *§. quia* at the end. And so by alternation he proceeds as far as *§. constat ergo.*

**Peter.** Casus. It appears from the history, which is this. It is read in the Acts of the Apostles that when Simon Magus, recently converted to the faith, saw the sick being healed by Peter’s shadow, in order to sell this gift to others at a higher price, he wished to buy it from Peter, and offered him money — whom Peter condemned saying: *“Your money be with you unto perdition, because you have thought,”* &c. And therefore, since his heart was not right with God, he condemned him. The following chapter is plain.

**i. Depraved.** Similarly the chapter says of Saul, that depraved by the cause of his sin, he resorted to that for which he was condemned. *26. quaestio. 5. nec mirum*, past the middle.

**k. By the imposition.** Namely, so that on whomsoever he imposed his hand, he should receive the Holy Spirit, and the Holy Spirit should be infused into him.

**l. In this faith.** Namely, our catholic \[faith\]: but in the Acts of the Apostles it is written *“in this word,”* as at *J. ead. eos qui.* Whence it can be said *“in your faith,”* that is, in what you believe yourself to obtain — namely, that for money the Holy Spirit be infused `[?]`. This is to Gratian’s purpose: for he says, *“you have no part in this faith,”* and so \[he is\] an infidel, therefore a heretic. And thus understand the following chapter, &c.

**m. Pure conscience.** Which has also passed into act. So understand *de pe. dist. 1. cap. si cui etiam non contingat.*

**n. Of faith.** And in this it bears on his point.

**o. Since all greed.** Greed is called specially “servitude of idols” because the greedy man pays reverence to money as if to God. *J. 11. q. 3. pauper.*
