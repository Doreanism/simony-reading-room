# Causa 1, Question 1

**FROM THAT \[AUTHORITY\]. THE CASE.** Gratian concludes from the two chapters set forth above — namely the chapter *Petrus* and the chapter *Cum omnis* — that simoniacs, like heretics, deviate from the faith.

**LIKEWISE IT IS OBJECTED.** This is the fourth part of the question, in which he wishes to prove sophistically that to give something for ecclesiastical things is not a sin. Johannes de Faventia.

> From that authority of blessed Ambrose and Gregory it is plain that simoniacs (just as also other heretics) stray from the faith, and therefore what is decreed concerning the others is, by consequence, understood as applying to them.

> § 4. ¶Likewise it is objected ª that prophecy is a gift of the Holy Spirit; yet this α was accustomed to be sold in the Old Testament by the holy prophets. Hence Saul did not presume to go to Samuel to seek a prophecy except with a gift. The wife of Jeroboam likewise did not approach the man of God except with a gift. Likewise Christ, sold by Judas ᵇ, healed the servant’s ear. Therefore by the example of Saul and of the wife of Jeroboam, it is plain that to buy or sell a gift of the Holy Spirit is not a sin. By the example of Christ it is clear that the Holy Spirit, although unwillingly considered by simoniacs to be for sale, nevertheless does not forget his own power, nor does he cease, even when sold, to provide the effect of his grace. But concerning prophecy, blessed Jerome in his commentary on the third chapter of Micah resolves this fittingly, declaring that only the wicked, not the good, received \[payment\].

## C. XXIII. Wicked prophets, not good ones, sold prophecy.

> Her judges ᶜ, he says, ᵈ judged for bribes, and her priests answered for hire, and her prophets divined for money, and rested upon the Lord ᵉ, saying: Is not the Lord in our midst? evils shall not come ᶠ upon us ᵍ. Therefore on account of you Zion shall be plowed like a field ʰ, and Jerusalem shall be as the watch-hut of an orchard ⁱ. No one doubts that Jerusalem was destroyed on account of the crimes described in this chapter. *And below.* ¶Indeed Peter could have sold to Simon Magus what he was seeking; indeed, he could have pretended to sell (for the Holy Spirit can neither be sold nor bought): but he condemned the money offered together with the one offering it. *And a little later.* ¶Nor let anyone be disturbed by what we read in the first book of Kings β, that Saul ᵏ, wishing to go to Samuel, said to his servant that he could not go to him, since he had no price to offer for the prophecy;

**a. ¶Likewise it is objected.** Namely against that \[?\] which is set forth principally, that is, that to sell spiritual things is a sin: or that an order which has been sold does not have the grace of the Holy Spirit.

**b. ¶Judas.** The solution of this objection is given below in §. *cum ergo* in the chapter *numquam*.

**¶Judges.** THE CASE. Certain persons, when they were rebuked for simony, excused themselves by the example of certain figures in the Old Testament: but Jerome insists that simoniacs are to be condemned. First, through the words of the prophet Micah, who shows that on this account Jerusalem was destroyed. Second, through Peter’s condemnation of Simon Magus. Third, he excuses the prophet Samuel. Fourth, Ahijah, saying that they did not receive the money offered, or if they could have received it, \[they would have received it\] not as a gift for prophecy, but on the journey \[?\] for the time \[?\], and they received it as necessary expenses. Fifth, he excuses Elisha, who is not to be believed to have received anything from Hazael: for it is established that he refused the gifts offered by Naaman the Syrian. The second part begins at \[Poterat\]. The third at \[Nec quemquam moveat\]. The fourth at \[Hoc ipsum\]. The fifth at \[Vide in quarto\].

**c. ¶Judges.] Anastasius \[?\], Leaders.** The words of the prophet Micah, and such is the case. Certain persons, when they were rebuked for simony, excused themselves by the example of certain figures in the Old Testament, whom Jerome sets forth in this chapter. First then, he sets forth the words of the prophet Micah, and expounds them, showing that on account of such simony Jerusalem was destroyed. Second at \[Poterat Petrus\], he sets forth Peter’s words condemning. Third, he excuses the offering of money simonaically condemning \[?\].

**d. ¶Of her.** Of the Jewish people. Johannes.

**e. ¶Rested.** That person is said to rest upon the Lord who, on the strength of the priesthood or prophecy, does not live luxuriously: and explain “rested,” that is, they had his grace with them, in their own opinion.

**f. ¶Shall not come.** As if he were promising himself impunity.

**g. ¶Evils.** Which the prophets are threatening against us.

**h. ¶Shall be plowed.** With a hostile plow, that is, it shall be devastated and shall cease to exist: argument *ff. q.b. mod. usus\[fructus\] amit\[titur\]*, *si ultra fructus*. And this was done by the Romans.

ADDITION. *76 dist. ieiunium.* Archidiaconus.

**i. ¶Shall be as the watch-hut of an orchard.** For the watch-hut of an orchard is abandoned once the fruit has been gathered. And this is why he says this.
