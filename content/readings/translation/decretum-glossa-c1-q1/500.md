Wherefore I beg you, either to grant the license of sacrificing to him whose baptism you approve; or to reject the baptism of him whom you do not think to be a priest. For it cannot come about that he who is holy in baptism should be a sinner at the altar.

*¶The beginning of this chapter up to the words “I beg you” is not in that place in blessed Jerome. It is, however, in Alger, book 3, c. 10, where also some of the words that follow are had in the same way as in Gratian, although in blessed Jerome they read otherwise.*

*But this is to be understood only of a Catholic sinner a, not of a heretic. For whatever he does, by the cooperation of divine grace, with the Catholic faith, according to his office, although unworthy, is believed to be valid. Otherwise, if it were understood of heretics, he would be self-contradictory, since he says on Amos c. 5: [Their sacrifices, as the Lord ...] and elsewhere in the person of the Lord.*

**a. ¶Sinner.** *Blessed Jerome argues from the place, as the Dialecticians are wont to say,* *ad hominem*; *nor does he do this in order to establish and sanction something, but only to render the adversary more moderate. For after he had led him so far that, if no longer against him, he would at least profess himself a disciple, then he set forth the truest reason: why the church receives bishops who in the council of Ariminum imprudently consented to heretics.*

## C. LXXVII.

> Within the Catholic Church, in the mystery † of the body and blood of the Lord, nothing is performed greater by a good priest, nothing less by an evil one, because it is perfected not in the merit of the consecrator, but in the word of the Creator, and the power of the Holy Spirit. *And below.* ¶For if it were in the merit of the priest, it would in no way pertain to Christ. But now, just as he is the one who ba[ptizes...]

*† var. ministry*

**¶Baptizes.** Meanwhile.

**b. ¶Wherefore I beg you.** That man thought that one who had been a homicide or adulterer was cleansed in baptism, not however so as to be worthy of the priesthood. Gratian, however, understands this of a hidden sinner. For a certain one said about such a person, and wrongly, that although he could give baptism, he could not nevertheless confect [the Eucharist]. And such people are rebuked here. And according to this it is an argument against. *50. distin. qui sub gradu.* But there it concerns one deposed, with whom dispensation is given that he might baptize; here, of one tolerated.

**c. ¶To be a priest.** That is, to be able to confect the sacraments.

**d. ¶Can come about.** Argument that of two things, of which something is granted to someone, the other is also granted, since when something is granted to someone, what coheres with it is granted, as *7. q. 9. 6. hac quippe,* and *16. q. 1. si monachus,* and *q. 4. c. 1. supra 8. dist. quo iure.* H. BUT THIS. CASE. Gratian says the aforementioned chapter is to be understood of an evil Catholic: and not of a heretic; otherwise the same Jerome would be self-contradictory, as is proved by the following chapter, and §. following: which are clear in themselves.

**e. ¶Cooperating.** This “cooperating” is false as to the giver or the doer, not as to the receiver. *infra ea. Dominus.*

**f. ¶I will curse.** Another exposition of this word you have below same. *multi saecularium.*

HOC ERGO. This is the 11th part of this question, in which he proves that the wickedness of the priest does not harm others in the sacraments, although it harms him: and this up to §. *ex his.* John of Faenza.

**¶Within the Catholic.** *c. sacerdotes,* one reading [holds] from evil Catholic presbyters to *chum,* and *q. 4. c. 1. supra 8. dist. quo iure.* H.

**g. ¶Within.** From this place up to c. *sacerdotes,* he speaks of all chapters concerning evil priests tolerated by the church.
