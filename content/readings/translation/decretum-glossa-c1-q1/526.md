# Causa 1, Question 1

*(Continuing canon C. CXX from p. 525.)*

> The bearer of the present \[letter\], our and your son `[?]`, your love has presented to us, suggesting how he desires to advance in the ecclesiastical order, urging that we too should aid him by some intercession. For you have commanded that we should cause our son `[?]`, who is with us in the order of the diaconate, to be promoted to the priesthood. But since for fulfilling this he does not have what must be given, nor ought we to receive what must be given: he himself is altogether grieved, but we cannot do this.

*ADDITION.*

*In the manuscript copies and in the Lyons edition this chapter is not found.*

## C. CXXI. On the same

*Likewise Symmachus pope, in the Roman synod, c. 1.*

> It does not therefore seem unbecoming if a price is offered for the acquiring of a dignity: those who desire to attain to the power of ordaining stand in need of no recommendations, but their own probity of morals suffices for them.

## C. CXXII. On the same

*Likewise from the Eleventh Council of Toledo, c. 8.*

> If any of the bishops, presbyters, or deacons, or whoever of the clergy, has given money to anyone in order to obtain a grade: let him, condemned as by the calumny of Giezi and Simon Magus, fall from his own grade, and be made a stranger to every ecclesiastical order.

*That ecclesiastical dignity is by no means to be obtained at a price, we are taught by many statutes of the sacred canons and the holy Fathers. But since divine law itself utterly forbids this to be done, we also decree by human law that a crime of this kind must be restrained.*

## C. CXXIII. Judges shall receive their salaries not from litigants, but from the public treasury

*The Emperor Justinian in the* Authent. ut Iudices sine quoquo suff. §. iuramus.

> We swear by Almighty God, and by his only-begotten Son our Lord Jesus Christ, and by the Holy Spirit, and by the holy glorious Mother of God and ever-Virgin Mary, and by the four Gospels, which we hold in our hands, and by the holy archangels Michael and Gabriel: that we will keep a pure conscience and sincere hands, and will give every effort —

**a. ¶The bearer.** Or by the intercession of the peace of the Church. Argument *infra 11. q. 3. illud.*

**¶By intercession.** As if he said: if he commends by exhorting, and not by reason and work, this does not displease.

**b. ¶To advance.** That is, to be promoted in a holy order.

*ADDITION.*

*¶Son.* CASUS. You have commanded us that we should promote a certain son from the diaconate to the priesthood. It is our duty \[to say\] that he ought not be promoted, because he does not have what he ought to bestow, and we ought not to accept. Therefore both he is grieved, and we cannot fulfill your command.

**c. ¶You have commanded.** These words show simony as to the thing given: for he was seeking to receive something for the promotion `[?]`.

**d. ¶Does not have.** As if he said: that he himself cannot \[do\], nor we receive it.

**¶Not therefore.** CASUS. Here Symmachus says it is not unbecoming if a price is offered for the obtaining of a dignity, that you may understand \[this\] not otherwise than by the intercession of the peace of the Church: otherwise the whole preceding series would contradict. Indeed neither does it befit such persons to offer a price, who by their own merits attain to those dignities.

**e. ¶Of those to be ordained.** Namely `[?]` those who `[?]` are worthy.

**¶If any of the bishops.** CASUS. In this chapter it is said that whoever has given money for obtaining an ecclesiastical order, let him be deprived of the order which he has, and be judged unworthy of all other ecclesiastical orders.

**¶We swear.** CASUS. Here the Emperor Justinian speaks of secular judges who, corrupted by bribes, deliver unjust sentences; which according to the canons is simony. Therefore he commands that they should accept nothing at all from those whose cases they judge: but should have an annual salary from the public treasury, and from this sustain themselves. Which law can be applied to ecclesiastical judges, who ought not to receive anything for judgment.
