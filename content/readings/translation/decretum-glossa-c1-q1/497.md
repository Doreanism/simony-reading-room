> ¶And does anyone dare to say that the saving water of baptism and heavenly grace can be held in common with heretics †, with whom not even earthly food, nor secular drink, ought to be in common? Moreover, it is established that the oil, with which the baptized are anointed, cannot be sanctified at all among them, and that the Eucharist cannot be made there, where there is no hope; and where faith is false, where all things are done through falsehood. For the heretic †, in the manner of apes, which, although they are not men, nevertheless imitate human things, wishes to claim for himself the authority and truth of the Catholic Church, when he himself is not in the church. He blesses, though cursed by God; he promises life, though dead; he invokes God, though a blasphemer; he administers the priesthood, though profane; he sets up an altar, though sacrilegious: all these evils, and yet another evil — that the bishop of the devil should dare to make the Eucharist, when the offering cannot be sanctified there where the Holy Spirit is not, nor can the Lord profit anyone through his prayers and entreaties, who has himself violated the Lord. For if those who scorn the church are held to be heathens and publicans, much more certainly are rebels and enemies — those who fashion false altars and unlawful priesthoods and sacrilegious sacrifices and adulterated names — to be counted among heathens and publicans: all of whom it is established have departed from the charity and unity of the church. Therefore, all things whatsoever that heretics do are carnal, and empty, and false; so that nothing of what they have done ought to be approved by us. Things which are boasted of in Christ’s name are not to be received and accepted immediately †, but those which are done in the truth of Christ. For how can they perform what they do, or obtain anything from God by unlawful endeavors, who attempt against God what is not permitted to them? ¶Wherefore, those who patronize heretics † and schismatics are proved by divine censure in their wickedness, and not only the leaders and authors but also the participants are appointed to penalties, unless they separate themselves from the fellowship of the wicked, as the Lord commanded through Moses, saying: [Separate yourselves from the tents of these most hardened men, and touch nothing † of what is theirs, lest you perish together in their sin.] And what the Lord had threatened through Moses, he fulfilled, so that whoever did not separate himself from Korah, and Dathan, and Abiron immediately paid the penalty for their impious communion: just as

**a. ¶There is no hope.** arg. *24. q. 1. ille.* and *q. 5. miles.* and *q. 1. quid culpatur. 8. q. 1. sciendum.* arg. *11. q. 3. si quis eps.* and c. *non semper.* and c. *si dominus.* arg. H. Here is the argument that three seals must not be received at once. arg. *extra de proba. c. tertio.* For *letters must be approved. ff. de procu. si procuratorem. ff. quod cuiusque universi. § quod si actor.* The seal, however, is not rejected, if it does not have the name of the author, *extra de eo, qui mit. in pos. cum sicut.* In the part decided.

**b. ¶Through falsehood.** Here Cyprian erred, as Augustine says, and he corrects him in *32. distinct. §. verum.* below *de consecrat. distinct. 4. quomodo.* Some, however, wish to excuse him here, because he understood this of heretics who do not keep the form of the church. Or this phrase, *all things through falsehood*, etc., excuses him. arg. *de consecrat. distinct. 4. solet quaeri.* Or he said this against the heretics whom he was always pursuing, as also Pelagius. *24. quaestio. 1. schisma.* And there was an excess of truth there.

**d. ¶They have separated themselves.** Here is the argument that a husband knowing himself related to his wife in a near degree of consanguinity, even if he has failed in proving the consanguinity, ought to abstain from her entirely even if the bishop orders him not to abstain. arg. *dist. 50. si quis praepostera.* What then if the bishop excommunicates him? Let him bear it patiently, let him not care, since he is not bound before God, as *11. q. 3. quomodo.* and c. *quid obest.* below same *ut evidenter.* H.
