# Cause I, Question I

*(Continuing canon C. LXXXIII from p. 502.)*

> ...Scripture itself testifies that at the prayers of Jeroboam fire descended from heaven, and again at the prayer of Elijah fire was sent down to consume the sacrifices; from which it is given to be understood that God does not consider the merits of persons but the offices of priests. *And a little later.* To them a visible fire was sent so that they might believe; for us who believe, an invisible fire works. *And after a few interposed words.* Believe, therefore, that the Lord Jesus is present, invoked by the prayers of the priests, who said: [Where two or three are gathered, there am I also.]

*¶Given to be understood. These words are not in the original. But since what precedes depends on what came before, a new sentence begins here: “Consider not the merits of persons but the offices of priests.” Several other variants, indeed of no small importance, are noted in the margin.*

*Likewise Gregory.*

## C. LXXXIV. Not the merits of the priests but the divine power sanctifies the sacraments.

> Many d secular men, when they scrutinize the life of priests more than their own, fall into the crime of great contempt for the divine sacraments. For having their eyes wounded by a great beam, and ignoring it, they look upon the slender mote in the eyes of others; and while they hasten unwarily to pluck it out, they fall headlong into the pit of error — considering too little that the life of the priests would not harm them if they themselves humbly inclined their ear to the good admonitions of the priests. But while, in the manner of hypocrites, they pass judgment more on the reputation of others than on their own life or the negligences of their life, they spurn f the holy admonitions and the spiritual gifts. Whence it happens that not only do they reject the words spoken by them, but (what is worse) they even despise the divine mysteries — so much so that they do not receive the communion of the body or the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ when consecrated through their ministry, thinking that this is less g sanctified if it is performed by the office of those whose life appears base in their eyes. Alas, into how great a snare do they fall, that they believe the divine and hidden mysteries can be more sanctified by others; whereas one and the same Holy Spirit, in the Church spread through the whole world, incomprehensibly h sanctifies those mysteries, and secretly and invisibly, by both working and...

**¶The Holy Spirit was formerly in the sacraments. CASE.** This chapter is divided into four parts. In the first part Gregory rebukes certain men who, neglecting their own sins, reproach the life of the priests and despise their sacraments. In the second part he shows whence a “mystery” is so called, and whence a “sacrifice,” and whence a “sacrament,” and whence the “Eucharist.” In the third part he says that, at the mystical prayers of bad priests, the sacraments of the life of Christ are celebrated. In the fourth part he expounds the word of the prophet Hosea, namely: “I will curse (he says) your blessings.” The second part begins at [Mystery]. The third at [Therefore, whether]. The fourth at [I will curse, he says, etc.].

**d. ¶Many.** This chapter, *4 Sent., dist. 13, v. intelligenter*, on bad priests, speaks generally of all the sacraments. In the first part certain laymen are rebuked who, by reproaching the life of priests, despised the divine sacraments conferred by them — bearing a beam in their own eyes, and wishing to remove a mote from the eyes of others. In the second part it is said that, however bad a priest may be, his sacraments are nevertheless true; nor are they more sanctified by a good one. In the third he shows what a mystery is, and what a sacrifice is. Lastly, the words of the prophet Hosea are set down.

**e. ¶Would harm.** Argument that another’s crime does not harm the innocent. *Same q. 4, c. 1, §. eo. q. fertur. contra.* H.

**f. ¶They spurn.** From this it is concluded that the Mass of bad priests can be heard, even if it is forbidden in a given case, *as below 15. q. ult. c. ult., below 9. q. 3. c. ult., and 19. dist. c. si., contra Hug. Or in 1. dist. 32. nullus, dist. 81. qui presbyteri.* But that concerns the publicly known or condemned [priest]; whereas this concerns the tolerated. H.

**g. ¶Less.** That is, not, etc.

**h. ¶Incomprehensibly.** For we cannot comprehend how transubstantiation under the species of bread and wine becomes the body and blood of Christ. ADDITION. *As in de consec. dist. 2. panis.* And understand that it is incomprehensible.

¶Jeroboam, who was wicked according to some; otherwise Jeroba al, and of the prophet Elijah, who was good.

**a. ¶When Scripture.** Here Ambrose intends to prove that the Holy Spirit works invisibly in the sacraments of the Church in this present time, which he proves from the fact that formerly he worked visibly there. But this does not serve Gratian’s purpose. Jo.

**¶Hierobaal. *original of Ambrose.*** ¶Hierobaal. *as if “the sacred [thing] of Baal,” that is, “destroying the sacred [thing] of Baal.” In Hieron, “lectus” is called “sacred.” For so Gideon was named, Judges 6, whence Hierobaal, as if “destroying the sacred of Baal” or Hieronfortis, as if “strong against Baal.”* Archdeacon.

**b. ¶Testifies, etc.** As if to say: no one ought to doubt here; Gratian inserted [these words] mendaciously, as also [the words] “is given to be understood” up to [the word] “fire”; nor does Ambrose give the reason why formerly [the Spirit] worked visibly, but now invisibly; nor is mention made there of bad priests, as in c. *interposuit*, but only of good ones, such as Elijah, Jeroboam, and other saints whom Ambrose there names. Whence it is of no relevance to the present subject. So Jo. says, against Gratian.

**c. ¶Jeroboam.** Who was wicked according to Gratian’s interpretation, otherwise Herobaal, as if “destroying the sacred [things] of Baal.” In *hieron* “sacred” is meant. For so Gideon was called, *Judges 6.* Hierobaal. *original of Ambrose.* or “the altar of Baal,” as if he consecrated a sacrifice. *3 Kings 18.* Hierobaal, “destroying the sacred [things] of Baal,” or Hieronfortis, as if “if strong against Baal.” Archdeacon.
