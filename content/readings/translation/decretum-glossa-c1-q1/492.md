# Cause I, Question I

*(Continuing canon C. LIIII from p. 491.)*

> [if one does] not utter the words, he does not baptize: for neither the element alone nor the words alone suffice for this. The word approaches the element, and the sacrament is made b. Whence comes this great power of water — that it should touch the body and wash the heart — except by the action of the word c: not because it is said, but because it is believed d. For even in the word itself, the passing sound is one thing e, and the abiding power is another f.

*Concerning those also who are baptized in heresy or schism, Augustine writes in the book on faith to Peter the Deacon, c. 36, saying:*

¶Those baptized outside the Church heap up ruin for themselves, if they do not return to the Church.

## C. LV. Those baptized outside the Church heap up ruin for themselves, if they do not return to the Church

> Hold most firmly, and in no way doubt, that for those baptized outside the Church, if they do not return to the Church, baptism confers a cumulus of ruin upon them. For the communion of ecclesiastical society avails so much t [?] unto salvation, that one is not saved by baptism to whom it is not given where it must be given.

*The same, tract. 13 on c. 3 of John.*

¶The gifts that are had apart from the unity of the Church are weak.

## C. LVI. The gifts that are had apart from the unity of the Church are weak

> Let us hold h, my brothers, to unity. Apart from unity, even one who works miracles is nothing i. For the people of Israel were in unity, and were not working miracles; the magicians of Pharaoh were outside the unity, and were working things like Moses. The people of Israel, as I have said, were not working miracles. Who were saved with God — those who were doing [miracles], or those who were not? Peter is dumb [in the sense that] if he cannot speak, he does not baptize r [?]: and “because it is said, but because it is believed” — this refers to the recipient: because the faith of the conferring [minister] is not required, but [the faith] of the receiver. *De consec. dist. 4. tunc valere*. For from the giver himself only the intention is required, as I said above at ca. *sicut*.

**c. ¶By the action.** That is, in the form of the Gospel: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.

**d. ¶Is believed.** As above ca. *sicut*. It seems, therefore, that a mute man can baptize: because he can contract marriage: especially if he can express the three persons, which I concede. *Infra de consec. dist. 4. detrahe verbum.*

**e. ¶Sound.** There is a vocal sound, and there is a mental sound.

**f. ¶Another.** That is, the efficacy conferred upon it by God. *Infra de cons. dist. 4. c. solet*; and *c. quamvis*, and *c. ecclesia paradiso*.

**¶Most firmly. CASE.** It is said here that baptism does not profit if it be conferred outside the unity of the Church, unless the baptized person returns to the Church.

**g. ¶Heap up.** The heaping up of punishment. *32. q. 7. usque adeo.*

**¶Let us hold. CASE.** It is said here that outside the truth of the Church, gifts — even miraculous ones — do not profit. And this is proved by the example of the people of Israel and the magicians of Pharaoh, who worked miracles and nevertheless were damned; but the people [of Israel] did not work miracles, and yet were saved — just as certain Christians in the New Testament are saved although they have done no miracles, but Simon Magus, who was damned, worked miracles. The following chapter makes [this] plain.

**h. ¶Let us hold.** He proves that baptism can be conferred by evil men, and that they too can work miracles. *Infra ca. prophetauit.*

**i. ¶Is nothing.** That is, as to eternal life.

*Editorial marginal notes: Baptism performed at the shore of the sea does not consecrate the whole sea. We may measure ourselves against God, but we cannot deceive him. Miracles are common to good and evil men. The magicians Jannes and Jambres performed three miracles. [?]*
