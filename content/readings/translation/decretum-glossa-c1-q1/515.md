## C. XCVIII. Although the sacraments are harmful to those who handle them unworthily, they nevertheless profit those who receive them worthily

*Through Isaiah the Lord says:*

> \[*The wicked man, who sacrifices to me a calf a, is as one who slays a dog; and he who cooks* (al. b) *fine flour, as one who offers swine’s blood; he who offers incense in remembrance, as a blasphemer.*\] *With a few words intervening.* ¶Whoever in whatever place offers a sacrifice with such a heart or with such deeds — that he † deserves to hear this c d — brings ruin upon himself, not upon those good people e who receive from them these same sacraments, who according to the prophet Ezekiel groan and mourn over the sins which are done in their midst, although they do not bodily separate themselves on that account. *And below.* ¶The sacrifices of the impious will harm those very persons who offer impiously. For one and the same sacrifice, on account of the name of the Lord which is invoked, is also exalted, and becomes to each such as the heart with which he has approached to receive it. \[*For he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself.*\] He does not say *to others* f, but *to himself*. *And below.* ¶We admonish that almost in all such questions this should be understood: namely, that although all the sacraments are harmful to those who handle them unworthily, they nevertheless profit those who receive them worthily. *And below.* ¶The Holy Spirit so inheres in the prelate or minister of the Church that, if he is feigned \[i.e., a hypocrite\], the Holy Spirit still works through him both unto his recompense g unto eternal salvation, and unto the regeneration h or edification i of those who, through him, are either consecrated or evangelized. But if he is feigned, since it is most truly written: \[*For the holy spirit of discipline will flee the feigned one,*\] \[the Spirit\] is indeed lacking unto his salvation, so that it withdraws itself from thoughts which are without understanding; yet it does not abandon k the ministry by which through him it works the salvation of others.

*α. ¶*Et qui coquit*.\] In St. Augustine it is read: “and he who \[?\]gen, as it were swine’s blood; and he who offers incense, blesses an idol,” and so likewise Ivo: except that he has, “and he who fine flour,” as St. Augustine also has against the Letters of Petilian, book 2, ch. 52. To the common Greek reading St. Jerome’s Version according to the LXX best corresponds: “The wicked man, however, who immolates a calf, as one striking a man; sacrificing of the flock, as one slaying a dog; he who offers fine flour, as one offering swine’s blood; he who gives incense in memorial, as a blasphemer.” But the Latin Vulgate of Jerome from the Hebrew copy has thus: “He who immolates an ox, as one killing a man; he who slaughters a sheep, as one beating out a dog’s brains; he who offers an oblation, as one offering swine’s blood; he who is mindful of incense, as one blessing an idol †.”*

*It is established, therefore, as Innocent says, concerning the other heretics*

**¶Per Esaiam.** This chapter is divided into three parts. In the first part the words of the prophet are set forth. In the second, Augustine says that wherever an evil man offers a sacrifice, he brings ruin upon himself, but not upon those who receive worthily. In the third part he says that the Holy Spirit of discipline flees the feigned.

**¶Et qui coquit.** CASE. First, the words of the prophet are set forth. Second, Augustine says that wherever an evil man offers sacrifice to God, he brings ruin upon himself; it does not work through him the remission of sins. The second part begins at \[*Quilibet*\]. The third at \[*Spiritus sanctus*\]. CASE. First the words of the prophet are set forth. Second, Augustine says that wherever an evil man offers sacrifice to God, he brings ruin upon himself, not upon him who receives worthily. Third, it is said that the Holy Spirit of discipline — namely, as giver — will flee the feigned. For through him he does not work the remission of sins, but he works through the office; and this is to be understood of those evils that are tolerated.

**a. ¶Vitulum.** Such is he, and he acts as if, etc.

**b. ¶Coquit.** Otherwise *worships*. That is, in worship as it were worshipping he offers in sacrifice. And *similago* is the flower of meal, from which the showbread used to be made.

**c. ¶Hoc vt.** What the prophet says.

**d. ¶Audire.** Namely, that it is said to him.

**e. ¶Non illis bonis.** \[Not to\] those who receive the sacraments from them.

**f. ¶Non alijs.** Argument that \[what is said\] of one \[applies generally\], etc., as *dist. 45. discipulina*, where it says *meus non est*. Argument to the contrary below, *23. q. 4. fortè.*

**g. ¶Mercedé.** That is, eternal reward.

**h. ¶Regeneratione.** As of infants.

**i. ¶Vel aedificatione.** As of adults.

**K. ¶Non deserit.** Above, the same chapter, *remissionem.*

**CONSTAT ERGO.** CASE. Above, in c. 1 of this question, Leo says that simoniacs receive nothing but the spirit of lying, and damnation. In c. *fertur*, Gregory says that simoniacs are wounded; they have a soul \[wounded with\] a deadly wound. In the chapter *veniunt*, Innocent says that those ordained by heretics are wounded \[as to\] the head. From which chapter Gratian infers that it is the same of heretics and of simoniacs; holding his conclusion, he immediately objects there at \[*Sed obijcitur*\]. And there, at \[*Immo*\]. He nevertheless concludes that the sacraments ought not to be granted simoniacally.
