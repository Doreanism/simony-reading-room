Do we not daily see three or four men contending for one woman before the commissary or official, even when not one of them has the consent of her father and mother? And yet he who has the most money has the best right, and shall have her in spite of all her friends and in defiance of God’s ordinances.

Moreover, when she has been given by the judge to the one party and even married, then afterward many times the contrary party shall sue before a higher judge, or before another who succeeds the same, and for money divorce her again. So shamefully do the covetousness and ambition of our prelates mock the laws of God. I pass over in silence how many years they will prolong the sentence with quibbles and subtlety, if they are well paid on both sides; and if a damsel pledge \[herself\] to two men, how shameful is the counsel they will give them.
