But such an endowment cannot be established by donors, since they may not attach spiritual conditions to it, but only temporal ones, such as the redemption of captives; see 12. q. 2. “Aurum.” Yet it can be established by those who are bound to perform the anniversary out of their prebend; see the canon “Significatum” \[de praebendis\]. Hostiensis on the same canon “Quoniam” \[holds\] that such a burden can be imposed on the revenues; he says then that it ought to be left thus: “I leave to such-and-such a church annually a pound, to be divided from such-and-such a property only among those who attend my anniversary, and I ask that it be done every year.”

## And whether a canon who goes to the canonical hours chiefly for the sake of the hourly pennies is a simoniac

The reply, according to Hostiensis on the canon “Olim” de verborum significatione, and following him Johannes Andreae on the single canon \[in the title\] “Ne clerici vel monachi,” book 6, is that it is not true simony, since no express pact intervenes; yet he sins gravely and commits mental simony, but makes satisfaction by penance alone; see the canon “Tua nos” on Simony.

## What if a cleric for a price keeps vigil at the office and recites the psalter

The reply, according to Hostiensis, is that if he made a pact to read the psalter for a price, it is simony. If he were keeping vigil of his own accord, it is not simony; but he should know that tacitly he is bound to read the psalter; argument from “de praebendis,” the canon “Significatum,” since in this way he sells the labor which he is not bound to expend.

## What if the greater cross is not carried unless for a certain price

The reply, according to Hostiensis, is that it is a mark of avarice and induces the vice of simony; see the canon “Audivimus” on the same matter. Likewise it is simony when one fixes how much money \[is required\] for a person to be carried in a particular burial place, when there are several there, according to Hostiensis.

## And whether a pact for redeeming a bed in which a body is to be brought to the church for burial is simony

\[The answer is\] no, because a bier and the like is a temporal thing and therefore can be sold, redeemed, and so forth. If, however, the clerics were unwilling to bury \[the dead\] unless a price had first been imposed for redeeming it, this seems to be simony, since a pact is being made about a temporal thing in relation to a spiritual one; Hostiensis \[so holds\]; the last canon of “de regulis iuris,” first part, supports this. Likewise simony is committed if the clerics are unwilling to bury unless security is given them by the heirs of the deceased concerning the receipt of those things which are carried with the funeral; and similarly when they are unwilling to bury without money; see the canon “Quanta,” 12. q. 2.

## And whether it is simony to give money for marriage

The reply, according to Ricardus on book 4, distinction 25, argument 2, question 1, is that insofar as marriage is a sacrament falling under the dispensation of the ministers of the church, it cannot be sold; and therefore one who, for the blessing of those being married, accepts money that has been exacted is a simoniac; see the canon “Cum in ecclesia,” on the same matter. But insofar as marriage is an office of nature, it is permitted to give and receive a price, just as for other civil and bodily offices.

## But what if he is unwilling to bless except when money has been given

The reply, according to Alexander, is that one ought to have recourse to a superior, if he is unwilling, to compel the priest to bless freely; it is safer to abstain than to give an occasion of sinning.
