## To whom must what was received simoniacally for entry into a monastery or church be restored.

Reply: Panormitanus, on chapter 8 b of the same title. If we are speaking of a secular church, then if the one who gave does not have the means to live on, it shall be restored to him, by the argument of *c. veniens* of the same title. But if he has the means to live on, then if all the members of the church have offended, it must be given to the church and the offenders expelled. But if they are not expelled, it must be given to the pope’s treasury, according to Hostiensis, who shall distribute it for pious cases: shameful gains are extorted by the treasury, *l. fiscus* in the question, *ff. de iure fisci*. But if not all have offended, but only the prelate or a particular person, then it shall be given to the church to whose disgrace it had been given. If we are speaking of a regular church: if all have offended, it shall be given back to the one who gave it, so that he may enter a second monastery, as in the said *c. veniens*, since he cannot remain in the first monastery, as in *c. quando* of the same title. But if particular persons have offended, then the money shall be given to the second monastery as regards usufruct only; and when the one who entered has died, it shall return to the first monastery; and so understand the gloss on the said chapter *de hoc*; and Hostiensis and dominus Antoninus agree with the aforesaid.

## What is to be done when it cannot conveniently be applied:

namely, where the money was given to the disgrace of the church, or in whose disgrace it was given—say, because it is in remote parts, or it would seem unfitting for it to be given there, since the church is, as it were, abandoned, and the like. Reply: that it shall be given to the poor.

## And whether one who has received a benefice simoniacally is bound to resign it.

Reply: that yes; and let him restore all the fruits received from it, and those which a diligent possessor could have received, *c. si rec. 14. q. 6.*, *de restitutione spoliatorum* *c. gravis*. Understand this to mean when they knew it was a simoniacal benefice, or one whose status was unknown but afterward they learn of it; for fruits received after one comes to know do not bind \[the conscience\]; but if in good faith, then not, except for those still extant or insofar as one has been enriched, *l. certum*, *L. de re. pen.*

## To whom must what was given for obtaining orders be restored.

Reply: it seems that it should be restored to the cathedral church, to whose injury it seems to have been given, if the man is not ordained to the title of some church; but if he is ordained to the title of some benefice or church, it shall be given to that one, according to Alde. \[Aldericus\] and Rodo. \[Rodolfus\].
