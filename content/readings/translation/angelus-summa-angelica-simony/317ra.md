## Concerning a priest

who is unwilling to give his parishioner permission to make confession to another unless money is given. Reply, according to Rodon., that he is a simoniac. Say the same of permission to receive communion or to bless brides. Note, however, that if he did not intend to sell the permission to confess, to receive communion, or to bless brides and the like, but rather to provide for himself out of the proceeds and offerings that would accrue to him, and no more, he would not be a simoniac, according to Rodon.

## Whether it is simony

to give something by agreement for the blessing of chalices, vestments, and altar linens. Reply, according to Ray., that in that case it is not, since \[simony\] is found only in the case of chrism, baptism, communion, the eucharist, balsam, burial, and a church, concerning which see \[Extra,\] cum cetera; below, in the first question, divina, the chapter placuit, the chapter ut unusquisque ex eo, the title cum in ecclesia. But you should say, more truly, with Hostiensis and Hostiensis, that it is simony; the chapter ad nostram, in the same title. Likewise say the same for the blessing of abbots, that is, of abbesses, or the consecration of virgins, with regard to which blessings and the like \[hold\] according to the aforesaid de Palude.

## Whether an archdeacon

who has the task of examining those to be promoted, or the one who responds in the scrutiny of an ordinand according to the form \[set out\] in the chapter secundo, de scrutinio, or that minister who reads the gospel, since it is part of the ordination, is a simoniac if he receives something from the ordinands. Reply that he is, according to lord Anto., in the second-to-last chapter of \[Extra,\] the same title.

## Whether one who claims knowledge

which he does not have, in order to be promoted to orders or benefices, is a simoniac. Reply that he is not, but he must do penance; the chapter mandato, \[Extra,\] the same title.

## Whether a notary

can, without simony, receive anything for a written record made of an ordination. Reply, according to Laur., and Ber. Bur. follows him, in the chapter sicut episcopus, first question, second \[part\], that he cannot, neither for his labor nor for the paper; rather, the bishop ought to provide this for himself. But Pa., in the first chapter de simonia, holds, together with the gloss there and the doctors commonly, that this is true of a notary who has a salary from public funds; it is otherwise with one who has no salary, since he can receive payment for his labor alone, the nature of the business being considered, not the order — as do those who take nothing for \[a record of\] the clericate, but more for the diaconate and so on, since that is simony; see the argument below, second question, pro carie. Likewise say that it is simony to demand anything for a seal, except the cost of the wax and of the sealing and of the clerks deputed for this purpose — understand this to apply if it is done with respect to spiritual matters and ecclesiastical jurisdiction, since it is nothing other than a certain attestation by the judge concerning that matter.

## Whether one who receives

some promise of a spiritual thing — for instance, of a benefice and the like — for promoting some piece of business at the apostolic see is a simoniac. Reply by the chapter cum eo, \[Extra,\] the same title, and fully as it is there.
