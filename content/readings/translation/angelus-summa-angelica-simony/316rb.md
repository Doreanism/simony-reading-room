## Simony 3

Concerning benefices, dignities, and offices. And first, concerning election or promotion, it is asked.

Whether kings and princes and others who receive money in order to consent to an election or promotion to some dignity or prelacy are simoniacs. The answer of Panormitanus on c. de hoc, in the same title, is that they are; and they are bound to make restitution to that church about whose prelacy the matter concerned — though not, however, from the goods of the church, as is plain in the same c. de hoc.

What of a canon who agrees with the other canons that they should oblige him regarding the reception of a certain canon, and afterward he will oblige them regarding another? This is simony, *c. contra, 1. q. 2.*

What if one of them says, “I will never consent to anyone’s being made a canon unless you consent to mine”? According to Raymundus, he is a simoniac. If, however, there were a custom that when one man had been granted his request in one matter, another should afterward grant a request in another, then one could ask and urge by such a custom, by the argument of *63. dist. quia igitur*, *de constitutionibus, c. cum omnes*.

Whether one commits simony who makes a pact with the man on whom he confers a benefice that he should retain some portion of the benefice’s revenues. The answer of Panormitanus on *c. i. ut ecclesia beneficia* is that he does. Yet, however, a bishop, for a just and necessary cause, can decree that the revenues of a vacant benefice for a certain period be assigned to a certain praiseworthy use; *c. ultimum de v. li.* supports this. Likewise for the payment of his debts — understand, debts incurred in the name of the church; otherwise if he had incurred them in a private name, as the gloss notes on *c. i. de solutionibus*; *c. si pro tua, de rescriptis*, in book 6, supports this.

Whether one to be promoted to the rectorship of some church, and who bargains to pay a larger pension to the collator, is a simoniac. The answer of Panormitanus on *c. cum clerici, de pactis*, is that he is. Likewise also if the one to be promoted binds himself on his own authority to a pension, the collation does not hold, according to Panormitanus in the same place — although Johannes Andreae holds that it does hold.

## Concerning offices

it is asked: what of a priest who sells the office of carrying holy water through his parish? The answer, according to Rodoanus, is that it can lawfully be done if he sells the emoluments that are accustomed to accrue to him by custom. But if he sells such an office, it is not simony but base gain. If, however, he sells the holy water or the blessed bread, then it is simony — although Alexander and Rodoanus \[hold otherwise\], since in the reception of such things the remission of venial sins is conferred.
