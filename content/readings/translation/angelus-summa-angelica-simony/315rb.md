## What if my church or my prebend:

has, upon another’s removal, been assigned to me. Response: that if I recover or redeem it for a price, it is simony, whether I was removed justly or unjustly. It is otherwise before the deprivation, if I was holding it justly, because then it is a redeeming of my harassment. Inno. agrees, in the chapter *“ad aures,”* same title, and Pa. in the chapter *“Matheus,”* same title. Understand this of when one is removed by a higher prelate, because it is otherwise if he were expelled by some tyrant, for then, even after the removal, he could give.

## And whether it is simony if money is given to a bishop so that he will not confer simoniacally:

Response. Pa., in the said chapter *“cum in ecclesie,”* says that yes, on the part of the recipient—but not on the part of the giver.

## And whether a chapter that gives something to someone who impedes an election:

—someone of whom it is established that he has no right in the election—so that he will desist, commits simony. Response: that it does not, if he were impeding so that an election simply not take place. But if he were impeding so that this or that man not be elected, then he could not; nor likewise could the one whose election he was impeding, according to Rodo. \[Rodoricus\].

## The fifth case is when a procuration is given to a bishop:

for the visitation or consecration of a church. *de censibus*, chapter *“cum apostolus”*; and *de simonia*, chapter *“cum sit romana”*; *de praescriptionibus*, chapter *“cum ex officii”*; and *10. q. 3.* *“statuit.”* And although different doctors wish to assign the reason in different ways, you, however, should say that if it is done by the authority of the law, then he does not sin, *23. q. 4.* *“qui peccat.”* Note in what case it can then licitly be given: when it is a moderate procuration, concerning which see below, *“visitatio,”* § 3; otherwise it would not be licit.

## The sixth case is when something is expressly demanded as part of a pact:

which is nonetheless promised, as was said with the preceding paragraph. See the seventh chapter *“oli.”* \[?\], *“el. i,”* *de restitutione spoliatorum*, and Pa. there, and Jo., as the same Pa. says in the first chapter, *de eo qui mittit in possessionem*, because although spiritual things do not receive a valuation, nevertheless the interest arising from them can well be valued, and one may proceed only over that, as much as over a temporal thing, as the gloss says in the chapter *“coram,”* *de officio delegati*.

## And whether a vicar becomes a simoniac by receiving a salary:

Response. Pa., in the first chapter, *ne praelati*, as above: that he does not, because he is hiring out his labors. So too the assessor does not, as in the chapter *“statuti,”* *de praescriptionibus*, 1. 6., nor the advocate, *14. q. 5.* *“non sane.”*
