It is true that Hostiensis and Raymundus say that if such a priest, who is moreover not obligated by his office, has the necessary means of support from elsewhere, he ought to sing the mass freely; and if he does it for money, it will be either simony or avarice. But since it cannot be simony, given that he does not intend to sell a spiritual thing, and no one is bound to serve as a soldier at his own expense, chapter *Cum sit* under the same title — therefore in that case it appears to be base gain, as is clear in chapter *Iudices* under the same title, and by the argument of *12. q. 1.* *Illi qui* — or at the least it has the appearance of evil. But you should hold that three things must concur for it to be lawful. First, that he take nothing except for his expenses or labor. Second, that he not be obligated by his office to minister. Third, that he not have the necessary means of support from elsewhere.

## But if a parish priest

does not have the necessary means of support, can he in any way, without simony, exact something for spiritual things? The answer, according to Panormitanus on chapter *Dilectus* the first, under the same title, is that if he can honestly seek his livelihood from elsewhere — for instance by writing and the like — then he cannot exact it, but ought to labor with his hands, distinction 91, chapter *Clericus*. But if he cannot, then the bishop ought to provide for him, or the people can be compelled to furnish him with the necessities for his livelihood. And the good gloss on chapter *Omnis christianus*, *de penitentia*, distinction 1, supports this, which says that the sacraments may be withheld from the people unless they make an offering. And according to this distinction understand the gloss and the text on chapter *Precipimus*, *11. q. 2.*; and according to the aforesaid, set the limit, because the one does not proceed without the other, *1. q. 3.* *Si quis obiecerit*.

## The third is

when something is given for eternal life or for the remission of one’s sins, according to that saying: “Redeem your sins by almsgiving,” Daniel 4 — *de testamento*, final chapter, *13. q. 2.* *Anime*, distinction 82, *Presbiter*, in the gloss *Romana* — because the aforesaid things are set before us as things for sale, and therefore are obtained for the coin of love and the works of charity, since almsgiving is not the price of eternal life except insofar as it is commanded by charity. It is not so with other spiritual things that are conferred through the ministers of the church.

## Whether one who makes a vow to God

in order to obtain something temporal is a simoniac — see below, *Votum* the first, § 9.

## The fourth is

when something is given to redeem a vexation that is inflicted on one in a spiritual matter, chapter *Dilectus* the first, under the same title. This I hold true with a limitation: when one is certain of the injustice being done to him; it would be otherwise in a case of doubt, because then it would not be lawful. Hence if a bishop unjustly excommunicates me, I may give him money so that he may revoke the sentence and absolve me, according to the gloss on chapter *Ad aures* under the same title.
