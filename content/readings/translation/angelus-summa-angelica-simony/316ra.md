## The thirteenth case

is when money is given for the sale of a temporal jurisdiction. Panormitanus permits this, *c. i. ne pro vices su.*; yet although it is permitted, even if it is not simony, it is a grave sin. The chapter *neo.* supports this, as is noted there in the same title, and *1. q. 1.* in the *Summa*, and the gloss on *1. q. 1. c. quoniam*, *ne presbiteri vi. su.*, which says that it is simony — and this must be understood, according to Panormitanus on the said *c. quoniam*, of the case when the temporal jurisdiction has a dependence on an ecclesiastical office, since through such a connection simony is committed, *d. c. consulere*, in the same title. But when it has no dependence on an ecclesiastical office, it is not simony but base gain; and so read it according to Panormitanus on the said *c. i.*, as in the *Authentica “ut iudices sine quoquo suffragio”* and in the *Authentica “de mandatis principum,”* § *illo*. Dominicus, however, holds that it is simony to give money to procure someone’s promotion to emperor, or king, or to a magistracy, or to the governorship of some city, since jurisdiction is spiritual, *1. q. 3. ex multis*. Hence to sell a stewardship or an administratorship is simoniacal, *1. q. 1. si quis episcopus*, and *1. q. 3. Salvator*: “Dignity, not power, is from God” — and then it appears to be spiritual. But I believe the truer opinion is that of Panormitanus, Innocentius, and Bernardus, that it is also permitted to give something to obtain a temporal office; on which see above in *cui*, § i, and say as there.

## The fourteenth case

is when a spiritual thing is demanded for a spiritual thing — as, for example, one man is supposed to say the early Mass, another the High Mass, and they agree between themselves that one will say the Mass for the other and conversely. Thomas indeed holds that if a pact intervenes it will be wrongful, unless such an obligation is annexed to a benefice or personage — as, for example, to a prebend, namely that whoever holds it is to say the Mass of the Blessed Virgin, as in *Extra, de prebendis, c. significasti*. Ricardus seems to hold something similar in book 4, dist. 25, article 3, q. 1, saying that if two persons are to be baptized, and one says, “I will baptize you on condition that you baptize me,” that is simoniacal. The reason is that in such matters no pact or agreement ought to intervene, *c. tua nobis*, in the same title — and this for two reasons. First, because the thing is bought before there is ownership of it, thus from a minister of sacred things who is not its owner. Second, because it is contrary to the nature of grace that it be sold. But note that when one man is bound to say the early Mass and agrees with the man bound to say the High Mass that, in exchange for saying the High Mass, the other will say the early Mass, this is permitted, because “for” \[i.e. the bargain\] does not denote a pricing but an assistance, since the one cannot say the Mass on the other’s behalf unless he says it for him — and therefore it is permitted. But in the case put by the aforesaid doctors, “for” indicates a pricing, and therefore the pact is illicit; yet according to the aforesaid doctors one could well say: “Today I say \[Mass\] for you,” or “I baptize you, and I ask that tomorrow you say \[Mass\] for me,” or “baptize me afterward,” and the like, without a pact.
