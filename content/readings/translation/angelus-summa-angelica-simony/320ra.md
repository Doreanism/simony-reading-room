For although the bishop is not bound by such a statute of his own, nevertheless the one who plays with him is bound. So in the case at hand, the pope, by receiving money, does not suspend the constitution concerning simony; and Panormitanus has fully argued the same point in the chapter *Extirpande*, in the first paragraph *Quod vexat*, *de praebendis*. But I have said what is written above, Simony, in the seventh paragraph, and it is more true. Nor does the analogy of the man playing at games with a bishop pose an objection, because a bishop cannot bring it about that, with his statute standing, the one playing be not bound; but the pope indeed can. And therefore hold to what I have said, namely that it is not simony, and thus he will be able to retain the benefice.
