Likewise, is one a simoniac who offers his goods to a church so that he may be made a canon, and out of those goods, after his death, a prebend is established for him? The answer is yes. There is a text on this in chapter *Suanos* under the same title \[Extra, de simonia\]. It is otherwise if he had given purely and freely, and afterward humbly asked to be admitted as a canon and to retain the aforesaid goods within the prebend, and the clergy purely consented — for then it would be lawful.

## Concerning him

who establishes a prebend out of his own goods in some church, retaining for himself the power of conferring it on some other person. The answer is that he can, without simony, retain for himself the right of patronage if it is in perpetuity; but if it is over the prebend itself, it does not hold valid unless it is done with the consent of the bishop and his brethren, if any are there, at its establishment.

## Suppose that a statute

provides that one newly received as a canon or monk is bound to pay a certain sum. The question is whether it is simony to pay that sum on account of the reception. The answer, according to Panormitanus on chapter *Dilectus* the first, under the same title, is that it is — since an assessment preceded it, because such a payment seems to be made by virtue of that assessment and not freely. The text of the aforesaid chapter *Dilectus* supports this, as do chapter *Ex multis*, 1. q. 3., and chapter *Veniens* under the same title. Some, however, limit this to be true only when what is paid goes to private benefit, or to that of the canons; it is otherwise if one were to pay for the fabric of the church or for other things pertaining to divine worship, according to Hostiensis, by the argument of *de constitutionibus*, chapter *Cum omnes* and chapter *Ex parte*. For a praiseworthy custom ought to be observed, chapter *Ad apostolicam* under the same title, and chapter *Significatum*, *de prebendis*. Nevertheless the prebend ought not to be retained in the manner of a pledge, by the argument of chapter *Significatum*; or rather it can be retained, if the custom is that no one is assigned there until he has paid such a sum. In this, however, let there be no exchange of one spiritual thing for another, which is permitted concerning a material thing — chapter *Ad quaestiones*, *de permutatione*. Panormitanus, however, on chapter *Jacobus* under the same title, holds that if a statute imposes such a burden on one being received, or one newly received, that he is bound to pay a certain sum either to the church or for the worship of God, it does not hold valid without simony; and chapter *Ex multis* with others supports this. But if the statute imposes the burden on the prebend, then it holds valid in favor of divine worship, by the argument of chapter *Significatum*, *de prebendis*, and the single chapter *Ut ecclesiastica beneficia*, and by the argument in chapter *Si pro tua debita*, *de rescriptis*, book 6. And this latter view pleases me more — yet neither do I condemn the first opinion.

## The second is

when something is given to some priest for spiritual works to which he is not otherwise bound, 12. q. 1. *Caritate*. Note, however, that there have been differing opinions on this, as Panormitanus reports on chapter *Significatum*, *de prebendis*. The opinion that pleases more is that of Panormitanus and the gloss on chapter *Significatum*, and of Ricardus on book 4, distinction 15, article 3, q. 1, and of Hostiensis and Bonifacius and Innocentius on chapter *Quoniam plerumque*, book 6, *de furto* — who hold that a priest may lawfully receive something for masses to be celebrated and for other spiritual things to be administered, provided he does not intend to sell a spiritual thing or to receive money for spiritual things, but rather for the support and recompense of the labors and losses he sustains by tarrying there and for his own sustenance.
