## Whether something may be given to one who litigates against me:

whether by right or in fact, so that I may not pursue some spiritual matter. Response. Pa., in the chapter “Matheus,” same title, says that it cannot be done without simony while one is still seeking the right by legal process, by the argument of the chapter “significasti,” de electione. Nor even by good possession after the thing has been sought, according to Inno. in the chapter “ad aures,” de simonia, and the gloss at 16. q. 7. “decimas.” But when the right has already been acquired, then it is licit, as in the said chapter “dilectus.” There Pa. adds, limiting the foregoing: if, because of the adversary’s power, he could not easily obtain possession before a superior, then, in order to avoid great inconvenience, he could give something without simony, just as was said about absolution from excommunication. But if he possesses unjustly, he cannot give anything without simony in order not to be harassed. Likewise he cannot give something so that the bishop will not justly depose him, or so that he will not be justly accused—indeed, this applies to one who lays claim to a right in a benefice—because then it is not a redeeming of harassment, as is noted in the chapter “nemo,” same title. Here note that the one who restores a spiritual thing which he usurps solely for money—but the one giving may, without simony, redeem it; yet the one from whom it is redeemed is a simoniac, according to Hosti. \[Hostiensis\].

## Whether something may be given without simony to a priest for baptism:

when he is unwilling to baptize otherwise. Pa., in the chapter “cum in ecclesie,” same title, treats this in twelve points: and in the case of an adult, no, because through contrition he can be saved, by the argument of the chapter “debitum” and what is noted there, and “bap.” in the second chapter, de presbitero non baptizato. But concerning an infant there are differing opinions. Some—such as Tho. \[Thomas\], 2. 2., and De. de tare \[Durandus?\], De. de palu. \[Petrus de Palude\] on book 4, and the gloss at the chapter “baptizandis,” 1. q. 1.—say that it is not licit, because evil things must not be done that good may come, 14. di. chapter “quod ait Loth,” and the gloss, 7., 46. di. “sicut.” But Pa. as above, Ricar. \[Ricardus\] and Ladul. on book 4, dist. 25, hold this true in the case of an adult, because in such a case his good will suffices for him. But because someone has a child and no one else is found who can baptize or knows how—for instance, because the one who seeks to have it baptized is mute or has no hand, and he has no other—then he gives licitly, because he is not buying a spiritual thing, nor ought he to intend this; rather he ought to intend to remove the harassment and the impediment, and he ought to intend to buy a temporal thing, namely the water alone—and hold this.
