# Simony: first, what it is

I answer that it is \[a willing\] directed toward spiritual things, or things attached to spiritual things, or things depending on or following from spiritual things, or things antecedently ordered toward spiritual things—where there has first occurred a pact involving a promise, an omission, a condition, a manner, a service, an entreaty, or any temporal benefit whatsoever, whether received or given. I draw this description from the statements of the doctors, and it will become clearer below.

## I ask what is to be understood in the foregoing description

by the term “spiritual things.” I answer, as I gather from Panormitanus on the chapter *Consulere*, de simonia \[Extra, de simonia\], and from other doctors, especially Ricardus in book 4, distinction 25, article 3, question 2, and Alexander \[of Hales\] in book 6, 11, section. “Spiritual” is said in two ways in the present matter. First, by comparison to the Holy Spirit; second, by comparison to the human spirit. “Spiritual” said in the first way is taken in three senses, namely: First, by essence, as grace and the gratuitous virtues, and this kind of spiritual thing can be sold neither by law nor knowingly, because it does not fall within an agreement; and on this you have 1. q. 1. c. 1. and the gloss there. Second, it is taken by causality and disposition, as are the sacred things which are in a certain way the cause of grace and the virtues, since through them we obtain grace and the virtues; and since these are corporeal, they do fall within a pact, but not without simony,
