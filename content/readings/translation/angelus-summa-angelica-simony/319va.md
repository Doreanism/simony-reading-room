This same Eugenius, in another extravagant constitution beginning *Cum detestabile*, given at Rome at Saint Chrysogonus in the fourth year of his pontificate—the second of Paul—in a process which he carried out on the holy day of the Ascension, excommunicates all simoniacs both in orders and also in benefice, and reserves the absolution to himself and his successors, except at the point of death; nor does he hold, as Martinus does, that they may be absolved by virtue of any grant whatsoever unless it makes express mention of simony. Sixtus IV did the same. And so hold that all simoniacs in orders and in benefice alone are excommunicated. Other simoniacs, however, are not; but by the very fact they fall into the penalties imposed on them by the law, concerning which it has been spoken here, in the section *Nec te moveat*, c. *reperiunt*, 1. q. 1. Likewise c. *quacumque* in the same place, and c. *placuit* in the same place. Likewise also c. *ex multis*, 1. q. 3., which, although they prohibit under anathema, nevertheless speak only of orders and benefice; and thus in no law is all simony found prohibited under penalty of excommunication, except, as I have said, in orders and benefice; and therefore those alone are excommunicated.

## The fourth penalty of simoniacs is:

that it commands, under penalty of *latae sententiae* excommunication, that all persons of whatever dignity or status, if they are present in the Roman curia, be bound to reveal to the pope, or to the chamberlain of his apostolic see, or to his vicegerent in general—or within the span of two terms, within two natural days—after they have learned, or it has come to their notice, that someone has committed simony; from which \[penalties\] they cannot be absolved except by the Roman pontiff, save at the point of death. And this is by the aforesaid extravagant constitution of Martinus *Quae damnabile*. To this Eugenius, in the said extravagant constitution *Cum detestabile*, adds that not only those who know that someone has committed or procured simony, but also those who know who the mediators were, are bound to reveal it to the pope or his chamberlain, or to whomever he shall have decided to commit the matter, by themselves or through others, as quickly as possible; and that they cannot be absolved until they have revealed it—which I understand to mean if they conveniently can; and if they know it truly, not through mere suspicions, I do not believe they incur the said excommunication. I also believe that today it does not bind, since it has been abrogated by disuse. Note, however, according to the Astensis, book 6, title 55, that when simony is only mental, without any pact, such a person is not bound by any of the aforesaid penalties; note this.
