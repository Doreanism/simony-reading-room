## Whether someone who promises to demand nothing from the one ordaining or presenting him for so many years is a simoniac

Answer with Panormitanus on the chapter *“Tuas,”* 2, on the same title, that he is; because, since the one ordaining is bound to assign him a portion for the ordination—which, considering its fruits, is something spiritual—such a man remits something spiritual in order to obtain something spiritual, and therefore it is simony.

## Whether someone who gives money so that a person may be received into an order or a religious community, or to confirm an election, or so that churches not be consecrated, and the like, is a simoniac

Answer that he is, from the chapter *“Nemo.”* Say the same when, for the sake of a favor of consanguinity or affinity, one does not reconcile a penitent, or absolves one who is insufficiently penitent, or conceals a sin, as in the said chapter *“Nemo.”* Innocentius says in the same place that it is true that such a man, acting thus for the sake of a favor, is a simoniac by likeness, because he is punished as if he were a simoniac. Panormitanus and the gloss on the said chapter *“Nemo”* hold that it is simony, and this is more true.

## Whether someone who rebukes a judge or witness in a spiritual case for the sake of money is a simoniac

Answer with Panormitanus on the chapter *“Tua nos,”* in the same title, together with Hostiensis, that he is, because of a certain connection these things have with spiritual matters; or it is quasi-simony, by the argument of the said chapter *“Nemo.”* It is otherwise in a purely temporal case.

## Whether to accept something for the investiture of churches is simony

Answer that it is, the text in the chapter 1, *“Tantum,”* in the same title. There Panormitanus says that this holds not only for the investiture of right, but also of fact—namely, for induction into corporeal possession—even if there is a custom there \[to do so\].

## What of those who pay the first fruits of a benefice at the curia, and those who pay the tax on bulls and the rest, according to the practice of the curia

Answer that they are not to be reckoned simoniacs, because such payments are not made for spiritual things but for the support of the curia and its officials; hold thus, that these things have been ordained by the will of the pope. But I judge otherwise in the curias of bishops and other prelates who have means to live on according to decency, not according to pomp; because it is simoniacal to make such taxes for dispensations, absolutions, and the like, *1. q. 3.* *“Ex multis.”* It is otherwise if they were to do this on account of a decency which they do not otherwise have.
