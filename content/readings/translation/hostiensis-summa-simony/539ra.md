## What of monasteries

What of monasteries in which no pact is imposed, but it is nonetheless fixed how much a girl entering ought to give? Indeed, such an assessment is condemned: \[Extra\] eodem, *sicut pro certo*, and c. *in tantum* at the end. Therefore, if a pact is introduced concerning the observance of such a custom, it is simoniacal: as \[Extra\] eodem, *veniens*, and c. *in tantum* §. *cum vero*. \[The case is\] expressly the same if she is received gratis, and afterwards the one received is compelled to observe such a custom: \[Extra\] eodem, *audiuimus*, and c. *Iacobus*. Finally, when every covenant and every coercion ceases, and what is customary is freely given, the simony of the church militant is not incurred — although this be simoniacal with regard to God: argument from \[Extra\] *de usuris*, *consuluit*; C. 14 q. 3, *plerique*. And it is expressed in \[Extra\] eodem, c. fin., concerning which see what is noted above eodem §. 1.

## But whether any remedy can be had in this matter

But whether any remedy can be had in this matter — namely, that the nuns receive the girl gratis and spiritually, and if they are so poor that they cannot provide her with necessaries, that they sue the father for the alimony to which he is bound, as noted above *de prescriptionibus* at the end, and \[Extra\] *de infantibus et languidis expositis*; and if he is dead, that she sue for her share of his inheritance: as in the Authentic *de sanctissimis episcopis* §. *nulla penitus*, col. 9.

## But the bishop who receives a promise

But the bishop who receives a promise or oath from one to be ordained that he will not trouble him about seeking a title — or a presentator who receives a like pact — incurs a penalty. For the presentator is by the law itself suspended from the execution of orders for three years, while the ordainer and the ordained are suspended without definition of time from the conferral and execution of the order, until they obtain the grace of the Apostolic See: as \[Extra\] eodem, *si quis ordinauerit*, *Gregorius*.

## If you ask why these different penalties are established

If you ask why these different penalties are established, I answer: the ordainer and the ordained transgress more than the presentator, since it is in their power to confer or receive the order; and therefore they are more severely punished in this — that no time is fixed, whence the term of three years or of ten years may be exceeded — and thus he punishes more, according to the rule above *de translatione episcopi*, *inter corporalia* §. ult., and *de clericis*, *nihil*, at the end, R. 1, 1, *de clericis excommunicatis ministrantibus*, *latores*. But the presentator too is more severely punished in another respect: because he suspends from the execution of all orders, whereas the ordainer only from the conferral of the order which he conferred, and the ordained from the execution of the same order; and thus they are punished in that in which they transgressed, according to the rule above *de tempore ordinationum*, *literas*, and *de iure patronatus*, *pastoralis*. Likewise, because they are burdened in one thing, they are relieved in another. For this is the approved rule, as noted above *proximo*, verse *sed quod est ratio*, as at the end; and it is proved above *de parochiis*, c. 1, and *de translatione episcopi*, *inter corporalia* §. ult., and ff. *de iureiurando*, *eum qui*.
