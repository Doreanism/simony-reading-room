## But on the contrary, that no custom avails

But on the contrary, that no custom avails: \[Extra\] eodem, *satis* and c. seq. Nor is anything to be given for a church to be carried in procession, or to be consecrated, or for an abbot or abbess to be installed in his see; nor for the investiture of a church is the archdeacon to demand a mark of silver, nor the archpriest a white cow, nor any quantity of money. Nor for the consecrations of bishops, the blessings of abbots, or the ordinations of clerks, although by corrupt usage it has been fixed how much is to be given to this one or that — as some do who wish to have kettles or bowls and napkins: \[Extra\] eodem, *in tantum* and c. *sicut*. And for refreshment too nothing ought to be demanded, as I said in \[the section\] *de eodem* §. *quid sit*, verse *item et ecclesia*; and it is proved in \[Extra\] eodem, *veniens*. Whence neither is the prebend to be withheld from a canon newly received on the grounds that he refuses to give the dinner which the newly received canons have customarily given: \[Extra\] eodem, *Iacobus*.

## Resolution: nothing is to be received by pact

By pact nothing is to be received on the pretext of any custom; but if it be freely offered, it may be received: as noted above §. *quid sit*, verse *in his ergo*, and C. 1 q. 2, *sicut episcopum*.

## Or better

Or better: either clerks demand from laity on the pretext of custom — and then the clerks ought not to retain a spiritual pledge; rather, they ought to bury or give blessings gratis, and afterwards, the truth being known, the laity will be compelled by the bishop of the place to observe the laudable custom introduced by the pious devotion of the faithful toward the church, mother of God: as in c. *ad apostolicam*.
