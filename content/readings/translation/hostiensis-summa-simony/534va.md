But it is otherwise if I serve you, led by this hope as a secondary motive, namely that you do me a favour, but principally I serve you freely. That is to say, if I principally serve you freely, expecting a benefice from you as a secondary thing, I do not transgress, by argument from l. fi. de pact. *Quod proderit* and ff. *de petitione hereditatis*, *Sed si hac* l. §*Consuluit*. But if I serve you principally so that you may give me a benefice and secondarily without charge, then I transgress; yet even in this case it suffices for me to make satisfaction to the Creator by penance, as noted at paragraph 3 §*Quid sit*. And according to this distinction, understand \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Tua nos*, according to Goffredus, and certain other laws which are alleged for and against the proposition that I am able to serve: l. fi. de pact. *Miramur*, D. 59 *Si officia*, D. 74 *Consuluit*, D. 76 *In singulis*, C. 8 q. 1 *Qui episcopatum*, and on the contrary C. 8 q. 1 *Sunt in ecclesia*, C. 12 q. 2 *Habebat*. ¶ Or one may distinguish otherwise, according to Goffredus: for either the service is corporeal or spiritual. If it is corporeal and honourable, it does not induce vice, provided no pact intervenes — as if someone should serve his bishop in honourable matters, for instance by going to Rome, by acting as his advocate, by managing his affairs. And this is hinted at in C. 1 q. 1 *Sunt nonnulli*, where “the gift from service” is said to be service unduly rendered; and you may understand this where one who is worthy and honourable renders the service, as has been said. But if an unworthy person renders it to a worthy one, or a worthy person to an unworthy one, it does not lack the vice of simony. And if the service is spiritual and is rendered by pact, it induces the vice, unless it is annexed to your benefice or to your person, as at \[Extra\] *de praebendis*, *Significatum*, according to Goffredus, and unless \[the case of\] \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], vers. *Et sunt haec*, \[applies\]. ¶ But “the gift from the hand” always induces simony, unless it be so small that it ought not to move the mind of the giver, taking into account the quality of the persons, the cause, and the time.
