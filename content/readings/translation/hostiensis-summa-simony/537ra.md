¶ † But not every pact induces simony, and therefore not every pact deserves the penalty. For something may even be given afterwards so that an oratory may be built upon it, as at \[Extra\] *de restitutione spoliatorum*, *Olim*, 1; and license to build the oratory itself may be given under a certain pact, as at C. 18 q. 1, *Eleutherius*, \[Extra\] *de religiosis domibus*, *Constitutus*, \[Extra\] *de censibus*, *Quanto*, by argument from \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Ex insinuatione*. For such pacts are reckoned spiritual, as also is the case at \[Extra\] *de praebendis*, *Significatum*, *Sicut*, \[Extra\] *de servis non ordinandis*, *Nullus*, \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Significasti*, C. 10 q. 1, cap. 1. And in the handing over of one’s own property anyone may attach whatever pact he pleases, as at ff. *de pactis*, *In traditionibus*, \[Extra\] *de conditionibus appositis*, *Veruntamen*.

¶ But can security ever be exacted before promotion under the vice of simony? Not before, but it may be afterwards, as at C. 8 q. 3, *Talia*, and C. 1 q. 2, *Cum pio* — provided that no pact preceded; otherwise, as at C. 8 q. 3, *Artaldus*. Afterwards, also, the security is given that is approved by ancient custom, as at D. 63, *Quare sancta*. Except a superior, who may exact both before confirmation and afterwards the due security, as at \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Significasti*, D. 23, *Quacumque*, D. 28, *De circumcisione*, \[Extra\] *de iureiurando*, c. D., *Adoptatum*. For the authority of a superior frequently removes \[the taint\], as at \[Extra\] *de pactis*, *Cum pridem*, \[Extra\] *de transactionibus*, *Statuimus*, \[Extra\] *de praebendis*, *Nisi essent*, ff. *de his qui notantur infamia*, *Furti*, §*Qui iussu*. Yet not before the election or promotion, as by the argument at \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Officii*.

## Who is to be admitted to the accusation of a simoniac

Who is to be admitted to the accusation of a simoniac? Indeed, according to Damasus, all persons, even irregular ones — \[Extra\] *eodem*, *Tanta*, §*Si clericus sit male fame*. For since the accusation of simony proceeds in the manner of an accusation of treason \[lese-majesty\], as at C. 15 q. 3, *Sane*, C. *de episcopali audientia*, l. fin., and in the crime of treason criminal clerics are not admitted unless they are of good reputation, as at C. 1 q. 1, *In primis* — therefore neither in the crime of simony. Yet it is certain that, since the accuser does not prove but the witness does, many are admitted to accuse who are repelled from testimony. And so one must subtly distinguish between accusers and witnesses, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Testimonium beli.*, \[Extra\] *de purgatione* at the end. Therefore anyone is to be admitted, by argument from \[Extra\] *de filiis presbyterorum*, *Sicut simoniaca*. But on this it has been said fully, as noted at \[Extra\] *de accusationibus*, §*Quis accusare*, and the following verse, and especially according to Bernardus.
