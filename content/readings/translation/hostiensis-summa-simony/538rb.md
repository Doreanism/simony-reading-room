## Iohannes says that a heretic

Excommunicated and deposed, he can consecrate and administer the other sacraments: above q. 1, *dominus*, and c. *non nocet*, and c. *dictum est*; *de consecratione* D. 3, *non in nobis*. Moreover, what reason would there be why a condemned man could so consecrate so long as he is tolerated by the church, but not when condemned by men? That is to say: I do not see \[any reason\], according to Iohannes.

## But others say

That such men never consecrate, nor do they have execution. For as Pelagius says, if they were to bring the truth, it is not the body of Christ that a heretic consecrates, as above q. 1, *schisma*. For this is the sacrament of unity, *de consecratione* D. 1, *quod passus*; and therefore outside the unity of the church it cannot be consecrated, as below q. 1, *ecclesia*, and c. *sacerdotes*. Which also seems true according to Iohannes the new, since no one can perform this sacrament unless a catholic priest, since outside the church no one is saved — rightly ordained according to the keys of the church: as above *de summa trinitate*, c. 1, §. *una*.

## It is true

That although such bishops, in ordaining, do not transfer any execution which they themselves do not have, nevertheless they do transfer the character which they have, dead and naked as it is; and therefore if the ordained man has been ignorant, he can be mercifully dispensed with by the bishop through him, provided the ignorance of fact has been probable, not crass or supine — otherwise the pope alone could dispense, as above *de ordinatione ab eo qui resignavit episcopatum*, c. 1, §. *quare*. Whence it is plain that he consecrates, since ordinations are not celebrated without mass: as above *de electione*, *q sicut*, §. *super eo*. And that an excommunicate may celebrate and consecrate is confirmed above *de sacramentis non iterandis*, *a nobis*, according to Bernardus; and see what is noted below *de schismate* §. *quid iuris*. And according to this understand the laws that bear upon this point: C. 9 q. 1, *ab excommunicatis*, and c. *ordinationes*; and those that go against it: C. 9 q. 1 c. 1, and *de consecratione* D. 4, *quo*, and C. 1 q. 1, *qui perfectionem*, and c. *verum est*, and C. 23 q. 1, *audiuimus*; with the argument leading to this distinction at \[Extra\] eodem, *per tuas*, 2; and above *de appellationibus*, *si duobus*. On this matter, say as I have fully determined above *de temporibus ordinationum* §. *z q̄*, verse *hec igitur sit fides tua*. Except, however, the deposed, according to Goffredus, as noted there; and below *de clericis excommunicatis ministrantibus* §. 1, verse *sed nunquid depositus*.
