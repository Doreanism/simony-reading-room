Therefore Laurentius said: ¶ “In the giving of gifts attend to these four things: ¶ who gives, who asks, what the thing is, what the cause of the asking.” It is an ancient common proverb: “Not all things, not from all, nor to all.” For it is very inhuman to be willing to receive from no one; but to receive all things and from all is most base, as at ff. *de officio proconsulis*, *Solet* §ult. Therefore it is lawful to accept food and drink which can be consumed within three days, as at ff. *de officio praesidis*, *Plebiscito*. ¶ And briefly one must consider what he offers, D. 19 *Servi*. ¶ Likewise of what sort he offers, D. 49 cap. pen. and ult. ¶ Likewise from whom he offers, by argument from C. 3 q. 7 *In gradibus*. ¶ Likewise for what he offers, C. 1 q. 1 *Dictum est* 2. ¶ Likewise before he offers, *de consecratione* D. 1 *Quod dixerit*. ¶ Likewise with what intention he offers, C. 8 q. 1 *In scripturis* and \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Officii*. ¶ Likewise how much he offers, C. 17 q. 1 *Ananias*. Whence Seneca \[tells of\] a certain Cynic, that is, a “ribald reprover” — for “Cynic” comes from *cynos*, which means “dog,” whence “Cynic,” meaning as it were a “biting” or “doglike” reprover. So a certain such fellow asked of King Antigonus a talent. The king replied that it was more than ought to be given to a Cynic. Then the Cynic asked for a denarius. The king replied that it was less than was fitting for a king to give, and thus he sent him away — though he could have given him a talent as a king, or a denarius as to a Cynic. ¶ In such cases excepted by the law, however, a gift may be received, namely if it is freely offered, at C. 1 q. 1 *Quicquid invisibilis* and q. 2 *Quam pio* at the end, and cap. *Placuit*, at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] *Dilectus filius*, and is noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] §*Quid sit*, vers. *In his ergo*. Likewise where those things concur which are noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] vers. *Sed minus* and what follows. Likewise for spiritual works, as where one is not bound by office to do this, C. 12 q. 2 *Caritate* — which, say, as noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] vers. *Et sunt haec*. ¶ Likewise when alms are given for eternal life and for the remission of sins, which are to be redeemed by alms, C. 12 q. 2 *Animae*, \[Extra\] *de testamentis*, cap. fi., C. 1 q. 1 *Ipsi sacerdotes*. ¶ Likewise to redeem oneself from vexation, \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] *Dilectus*; which, say, as noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] sub §*Lingua*, vers. *Sed nunquid licet*. ¶ The sixth case is in visitations, \[Extra\] *de censibus*, *Cum apostolus* and *Consecrationibus*, \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\] *Cum sit Romana* — for these expenses the visitor or consecrator may receive payment, because he is not bound to serve at his own expense, \[Extra\] *de praescriptionibus*, *Cum ex officii*. And so he does not sin, because he who sins does not do this by authority of the law, C. 23 q. 4 *Qui peccat*, \[Extra\] *de privilegiis*, *Ut privilegia*, where with the pope’s licence \[Mass\] is celebrated without sin in a church under interdict.
