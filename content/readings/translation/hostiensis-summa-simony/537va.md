## But how a partaker of the crime may be repelled

For if he should say, “You cannot testify against me, because you were my partner in the same crime,” he will thus be condemned as a confessed party, since like a fool he confesses against himself, as at \[Extra\] *de donationibus*, *Inter dilectos*, and *de confessis*, capp. 1 and 2, and C. 2 q. 1, cap. 1.

## If he should say, “I did not do what he charges me with,

and if I had done it, you cannot be a witness as one laboring under the same vice” — so he lies, and the law does not teach one to lie, as at C. 23 q. 3, *Qui peccat*. Yet it teaches that a partner in crime is to be repelled, as at C. *de liberis*, l. *Si filium*. But there it is manifest that he was a partaker, since it was said that the father had sold his son to his neighbor; whence to either party an accuser is lacking, because no person so closely joined could allege ignorance, and so the judge can repel him *ex officio*, since it is notorious, as by argument at \[Extra\] *de exceptionibus*, *Exceptiones*.

## According to T.

he may propose thus: “I except against you, because I say that you committed that crime concerning which you wish to accuse me or in which you wish to testify; whence I say that you ought not to be heard, and to show the exception is true I offer myself ready to prove it.” But if the judge does not admit such an exception, that party will appeal, as at \[Extra\] *de appellationibus*, *Interposita*.
