## What then if by custom

What then if by custom a canon newly received ought to make a cope or give a fixed sum of money for the fabric of the church, or for the lights, or for other things which pertain only to divine worship? I say the custom is to be observed as laudable: argument from \[Extra\] *de prebendis*, *significatum*; *de his que fiunt a maiore parte capituli*, c. fin.; *de testamentis*, c. *officii*, c. ult.; and \[Extra\] eodem, *ad apostolicam*. Yet the prebend is not to be retained as a pledge: argument of the same decretal. Or say that it can well be retained, if the custom is that the prebend is not assigned until he has made the cope. For in this case God can demand something spiritual from the clerk made canon, so that in this way a kind of exchange of spiritual for spiritual takes place: as in \[Extra\] *de rerum permutatione*, *ad questiones*.

## And so in this exaction

And so in this exaction only the private utility of the canons is condemned, not the public: argument from \[Extra\] *de constitutionibus*, *cum omnes*, c. *cum M.*, and c. *ex parte*. For avarice is feared — whence even clerks do not demand tithes from clerks: \[Extra\] *de decimis* cc. 1 and 2; *de censibus*, *ea que*; and *de prebendis*, *avaritie*. To laymen, however, grace is to be conferred gratis: as in \[Extra\] eodem, *in tantum* §. 1, and \[Extra\] *de magistris*, *prohibemus*.

## What about those who have separate cemeteries

What about those who have separate cemeteries — one into which no one enters unless he be a canon or brother, and another common one — and it is fixed for how much a stranger can be reckoned a brother, that is, for how much he can be buried in the brothers’ cemetery? I say this is simoniacal: \[Extra\] eodem, *audivimus* and c. *sicut*. For nothing ought to be received for the ground: as in \[Extra\] *de sepulturis*, *obolende*; nor for the office: as has been said above, verse *in casibus*.

## What if the greater cross is not carried

What if the greater cross is not carried unless a fixed price is had? This is without doubt simoniacal, and is the appearance of evil and a sign of avarice, therefore to be condemned: \[Extra\] eodem, *audivimus*, and note above, verse *quid ergo* at the end.
