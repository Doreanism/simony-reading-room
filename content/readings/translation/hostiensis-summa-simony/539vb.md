## What of money given on occasion of simoniacal depravity

And certainly, as for the giver: let him give it to that church to whose injury it was given: as \[Extra\] eodem, *de hoc*, c. *consulere*, c. *audiuimus*, and in the Authentic *de sanctissimis episcopis* §. *sed neque*, r. §. *si vero laicus*.

But the question: that it is to be restored to him to whom he gave it, as \[Extra\] eodem, *veniens*. Solution according to S. \[na.\]: I say that where the whole chapter has transgressed, it is to be restored to the one who gave it, as in that decretal *veniens*; but if one or two, or some outsider, has transgressed, then the place of the decretal *de hoc* applies. Such base gains are to be exacted from the heirs: ff. *de calumniatoribus*, *in herede*, *idem usus*, *tua*. But according to T., if the one who gave the money wishes to remain in the world, it is not restored to him. But if he wishes to enter religion, then it is to be restored to him, lest he be burdensome to the monastery to which he transfers: C. 16 q. 5, *de lapsis*; C. 17 q. 3, *si autem in atrio*; C. 32 q. 1, *si quis rapuerit*; \[Extra\] *de sepulturis*, c. 1; ff. *de iure dotium*, *iure successum est*. And to this add ff. *de calumniatoribus*, l. *generaliter* §. *illud*, and l. *in heredem* §. *sed etiam*.

A prelate, however, who extorts something from the church through simony must repay double: as \[Extra\] eodem, *audiuimus*. But if through pressure, then he shall repay fourfold: D. 17, *quoniam quidem*; C. 1 q. 1, *concussionis*. *Concutere* \[is the verb for what\] those do who, under pretext of an office committed to them, by menacing extort something from an unwilling person: ff. *de concussione*, l. 1, q. 1, and C. 22 q. 1, *militare*.
