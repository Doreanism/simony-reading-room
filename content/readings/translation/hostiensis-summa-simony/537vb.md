## A simoniac, however, established in a benefice or dignity, by accusation

is deposed; by inquisition he is removed, as at \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Per inquisitionem*; and the same by denunciation when that is retained. \[Otherwise\] true penance could not be done, as at *Illo* or D. 12, *Nervi*.

## And a simoniac in a dignity is suspended *ipso iure*

He deceives others, since by administering he accomplishes nothing — by argument from \[Extra\] *de electione*, *Dudum*. The same for one having a benefice with care of others, since concerning these the same judgment is to be held, as in the first decretal at \[Extra\] *de poenis*, by argument from \[Extra\] *de filiis presbyterorum*, cap. 1.

## In a simple benefice, however, if it is hidden

thus he is suspended as to himself, as is anyone constituted in mortal sin; but if notorious, then he is suspended as to himself and as to others, by argument from \[Extra\] *de cohabitatione clericorum et mulierum*, cap. fin.

## But if he commits simony with regard to a monk

received \[into a monastery\]: if by way of accusation both the receivers and the received are convicted, they are deposed. But if it is established by inquisition, the received are removed and placed in stricter monasteries to perform penance; on the receivers a penance is enjoined, and until they have performed it they are suspended from the execution of sacred orders, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Cum dilectus filius A.*

## But why are receiving priors or abbots not removed

even when convicted by way of inquisition, since this is the ordinary penalty of simoniacs, as noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], at *De hac materia*, following? I answer: that has its place in simony committed concerning a dignity having administration; it is otherwise if it is committed in conferring benefices or in receiving monks, in which case that decretal *Dilectus* speaks, as there \[the words\] *Multitudo erat in causa*, \[Extra\] *de tempore ordinationum*, cap. 1; or it was not useful for monasteries to remove old prelates, as at \[Extra\] *de renuntiatione*, cap. fin. Goffredus on this: say as noted at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], 6, *Sub quo per iudicem*, at *Sed quae est ratio*.

## But you, if it please, note thus concerning the penalty of simoniacs

that simoniacs suffer diverse penalties: for simoniacs suffer a penalty *ipso facto*. Likewise they are punished by the judge — sometimes for a time, sometimes in perpetuity.

## *Ipso facto* simoniacs suffer a penalty

For if anyone has been simoniacally ordained, an inward \[force\] kills him, as at C. 1 q. 1, *Quibusdam*, and \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Accusatus*, R., cap. *Quoties*; and because \[he bears\] the mark of theft and thus *ipso iure* is suspended, therefore from every \[liturgical act\] any layman may withdraw — understanding, not those cut off from the Church by sentence of the judge or of the canon; thus the objection concerning the excommunicated and the suspended is removed, since such a one is excluded from participation in sacred things, as at C. 3 q. 4, *Engeltrudam*, \[Extra\] *de exceptionibus*, *A nobis*, \[Extra\] *de clericis excommunicatis*, *Si celebrat toleratus et penitens*. And thus the objection is not valid that is wont to be raised about one constituted in mortal sin, that he is suspended as to himself, as follows — which has its place in enormous and hidden \[sins\] and not in mediocre notorious ones.
