## What if the clerks refuse to bury the body

What if the clerks refuse to bury the body unless a pact for the return of the bier is first interposed? This is the appearance of evil, since a pact is made of the temporal for the spiritual: \[Extra\] *de rerum permutatione*, c. ult.

## What if, after the body is carried

What if, after the body has been carried to the church, the clerks wish to recover the bier, even by violently carrying it off to their own house? I answer: nonetheless the body must be buried gratis and with honour. But since the general or quasi-general custom of the church is that the bier be retained by the clerks, the friends \[of the deceased\] can be compelled by ecclesiastical censure to restore the bier, because such a custom is laudable. In this case or one like it can be understood \[Extra\] eodem, *ad apostolicam*.

## What if the custom is

What if the custom is that the priest ought to have the garments which the dead man was wearing at the time of death — as in the castle of Clariacum in the diocese of Vienne? I say that he is to be buried gratis and the layman to be coerced, namely the heir or him who possesses \[the garments\], to render the clothing: as in the next decretal, by whose authority I judged these things when I was with the lord Iohannes, archpriest of Vienne, then legate of the Apostolic See, who also approves these same things. But the priest ought not to retain as a pledge the funeral rites, which must be conferred gratis even to one to whom he was otherwise obliged: as in \[Extra\] *de sepulturis*, *parochiano*; and *de natis ex libero*, c. unico.

## What if the custom is that one marrying

What if the custom is that one marrying who receives the blessing should give six pence or five shillings? I judge this the same way: that the blessing be conferred gratis, and afterwards he be compelled to pay through ecclesiastical censure — not on the occasion of a written document, which the layman does not request, as is done at Brasle, who, just as he ought not to sell his hand, so neither his pen: as in c. \[*sicut*\] and C. 1 q. 1, *sicut episcopum*. It is perhaps otherwise if those marrying wish to have a document and request it from a lay notary, or even a clerk who is not bound to render the service from his own resources: et q. *et precarie*.

## What if a priest refused to baptize

What if a priest refused to baptize an infant unless a candle or one penny were first paid, alleging custom on this matter — as is done at Vapincum \[Gap\]? I corrected the priests on this point, because he must baptize gratis and afterwards compel the godfather to pay what pious laudable custom has introduced: \[Extra\] eodem, *ad apostolicam*. Thus this decretal is as it were the centre of this title, and in this manner it resolves countless questions.

## Concerning gifts \[*encenia*\]

It is asked concerning gifts \[*encenia*\] whether they induce simony, and it seems that they do not, provided the number is not large in quantity: *ff. de officio proconsulis*, *solet* §. *non ante* and §. fin. For he may lawfully receive food and drink which can be presumed to be consumed within three days: *ff. de officio presidis*, *plebiscito*. Yet these too — even the prophets — used to offer alms; rather were the gifts of the tabernacle esteemed greater \[than those\] of the prophet: C. 4 q. 4, *iudices*. According to Goffredus, say as noted in \[Extra\] eodem, verse *sed mun.*, and in \[Extra\] *de censibus* §. fin., verse *hodie vero*.
