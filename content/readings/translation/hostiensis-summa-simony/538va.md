## But through the judge a penalty is imposed

But through the judge a penalty is imposed; yet it matters whether the crime is investigated by way of accusation or by way of inquisition.

## If by way of accusation

If by way of accusation, and the one accused is infamous, the judge ought to suspend him from the execution of his order for a time — that is, until he has learned whether what he is accused of is true: as below eodem, *accusatum*, and c. *quoties*. The same holds if he is occult, once there are certain indications; and so the chapter below eodem, *sicut simoniaca*, can be understood, or otherwise as I shall say at once.

## But if the accuser has proved his charge

But if the accuser has proved his charge, whether by confession or by witnesses — for it does not matter as to the manner, provided the crime is sufficiently proved — he must straightway be expelled from the church of God, that is, set outside its limits: as below eodem, *sicut*. That is, with the grave infamy that follows, he must be deposed: as below qo. 2 c. vl.; C. 1 q. fi., *presbyter*; C. 15 q. 3, *sane*; C. *de episcopis et clericis*, *si questio*. But if the crime is investigated by way of inquisition: if a secular who has obtained a benefice simoniacally in ignorance is infamous, he is compelled to renounce but is not deposed, below eodem, *ex insinuatione*. But if knowingly, he is also deposed: as above *de accusationibus*, *inquisitionis*. But if he is a regular and is a simoniac in respect of orders, the same will hold, by the argument of what is noted above eodem § *ipso facto* and the following, and below eodem *per tuas*. But if \[the simony is\] in respect of a benefice and they were promoted in ignorance, they are bound to renounce: as below eodem *de regularibus* in fi. Those giving and receiving, however, are to be struck with the canonical penalty: as below eodem, *sicut nobis* § pe. and ult.

## But where they have been knowingly promoted into a regular community

But where they have been knowingly promoted into a regular community, the penalty of deposition is remitted; yet those received around the time of the general council \[Lateran IV\] are removed from the monasteries which they thus obtained and are confined in other monasteries of a stricter rule. But with those who were received before the council mercy is shown by dispensation: such that they are placed in other houses of the same order, or, if for very great numbers this cannot be done, it is permitted that, after a free resignation, they remain in the same monastery with their former seats changed and lower ones assigned.
