## What if a judge will not absolve the excommunicate

What if a judge will not absolve the excommunicate unless he first pays a certain sum of money? Say as noted j. de excel. plā. j. ver. quid si petit rone pluetudinis and the preceding.

## It is therefore lawful for a bishop to redeem tithes from the hand of laymen

It is therefore lawful for a bishop to redeem tithes from the hand of laymen. j. de vsuris. c. f. Which understand if he redeems from a knight; otherwise if he had bought them from neighbors, and those who are bound thereto are willing to pay them — thus notes Ioannes f. q. iij. puenit. But against the express xvj. q. vij. decias. f. q. iij. altare. Whence there were those who said that this is understood when he redeems without the authority of a superior. But with the authority of a superior it is lawful. ar. s. de prbn. nisi essent and c. in lateranesi, and de his que fiunt a pla. cū aplica., and de re. pmu. c. f., and de trasac. statuim. and c. decetero, and c. veniens. ff. de his q no. infe. furti § qui iussu.

## Others say that bishops are forbidden to redeem tithes from laymen for this reason

Others say that bishops are forbidden to redeem tithes from laymen for this reason — lest the layman take it as an example and believe himself to have a right \[to them\], when in fact he does not, as s. de prscri. cū cām.

## That seems truer, that by the authority of a superior this can be done

That seems truer, that by the authority of a superior this can be done, so that the cause of bad example may also be avoided. For he is said \[to act\] on the ground that one detains them from God, who \[detains\] his own soul — since they are spiritual, and since they cannot even be redeemed unless the superior, by interposing his authority, purges the simony which would otherwise be committed on this occasion. Moreover, because the layman is to be induced through the superior to make restitution to the proper parish and to receive a benefice; for if he is willing to receive money therefor, he commits simony — indeed to such a degree that the superior’s authority could not purge it on his side. And add what is noted s. de deci. § si. sub § ij.

## What if I make a pact with the presenter

What if I make a pact with the presenter, that I will not disturb him concerning the title to be obtained — without doubt it is simoniacal. as s. c. p tuas. ij. de hoc. ie. f. § qua pana. sub § p iudicem. ver. sz et eps.

## Likewise, money given or a promise made for the acquiring of a benefice

Likewise, money given or a promise made for the acquiring of a benefice — whether the benefice be secular, or of a secular church, or religious — induces simony, even if given by the parents without the knowledge of the one promoted. as j. c. ex insinuatione, and de regularib. c. sicut nobis. § pe., and c. nobis, and c. tua nos. Which say as noted s. §. j. versi. mihi autē.

## And it is also to be noted

And it is also to be noted that, just as a simoniac is one who acquires a spiritual thing by means of money or pact, so too \[he is one who\] resigns \[it thus\]. j. co. querelam. s. de testi. veniens. For “to give” is understood as \[remitting\] whatever is owed to oneself. viij. q. iij. talia. ff. de cal. quin etiam, and ff. de cdi. cā da. silr. Although there is an argument to the contrary: ff. qui. mo. pt. vel. hipo. fol. l. f. § cū debitor. C. ad velleia. etiā. ff. de his que in frau. credi. si pignus.
