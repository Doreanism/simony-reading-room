## Yet do not understand

Yet do not understand that he may demand anything for the act of consecration itself; otherwise it would be contrary to C. 1 q. 1, *statuimus*.

## But for the labour

But for the labour of the journey \[something may be received\]: C. 12 q. \[2\], *charitatem*. And for the labour itself something can be received even if the church lies within the city: *de censibus*, *venerabili*. For although the bishop labours more in going to the said \[outlying\] church, he also labours in going to a church within the city — so Goffredus. But since such labour is a spiritual one which the prelate is bound of necessity to undertake and which is enjoined upon him by his office, it is better to say that custom gives this right: \[Extra\] eodem, *ad apostolicam*; and because it is equitable that he who sows spiritual things should reap temporal things, as is shown by many examples in C. 13 q. 1 §. *quia ergo*. Indeed this occurs by reason of harmony \[*converitatis*\], as in *de censibus*, *cum venerabilis*. Whence, just as the visitation itself \[cannot be prescribed against\], so neither can the procuration due by reason of the visitation be prescribed against: *de prescriptionibus*, *cum ex officio* §. *nos igitur*, *ad hoc*; \[Extra\] *de concessione prebende non vacantis*, *postulastis* §. *de consti*, *translato*.

## And these things are an argument

And these things are an argument that a priest may hire out his labours if he has no other means of living: D. 91 cc. 1, 2, and 3; C. 1 q. 1, *iudices*; C. 1 q. 2, cc. penult. and ult.; C. 10 q. 2, *relatum*; C. 12 q. 2, *charitatem*, c. *quicunque*, and c. *ecclesiasticis*; and the argument *optimum* at C. 12 q. 1, *illi qui*; \[Extra\] tit. 1 c. fin. But let him take care that his principal intention is directed not to the money but to obtaining remission or pardon. If, however, the priest is dispensed and instituted to a church, such a one ought to receive nothing by pact even on the pretext of expenses: *de consecratione* D. 1, c. *sufficit*. Yet the parishioners are to be compelled to pay what laudable custom has introduced: \[Extra\] eodem, *ad apostolicam* at the end. And so oblations are to be given: C. 16 q. 1, *statuimus*; C. 32 q. 2, *propter hoc* at the end.

## What if the oblations do not suffice

What if the oblations do not suffice? Say as noted at \[Extra\] *de parochiis* §. *in quibus* sub §. *hic tamen sciendum*, verse *vel nisi sacerdotes* and following.

## What if a clerk hired for a price

What if a clerk, hired for a price, keeps vigil with the deceased and recites the psalter? I answer: if he made the pact thus — “for six pence I will read the psalter” — it is simony. But if he said this — “for six pence I will keep vigil with the deceased tonight” — it is not simony, since it is tacitly implied that he ought to read. The argument is in *de clericis*, *significasti* at the end; *de prebendis*, *significatum*; and *ff. de heredibus instituendis*, *si quis sempronium*. And thus he can sell labour which he is not bound to undertake of necessity or by his office — provided it is not spiritual.
