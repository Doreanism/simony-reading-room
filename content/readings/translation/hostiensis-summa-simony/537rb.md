I say that the witnesses, even if criminal, must especially have been amended of the crime, and they must be supported by other corroborations — which I believe necessary when they are criminal and not amended; otherwise, especially when they are not partakers in the crime and testify without bribe out of zeal for justice, and only lesser crimes are objected against them, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Per tuas*, rh. 1, fin. *Praeterea*, in the following at the word *Ceteras*, a regular of inferior grade who is gravely defamed and can easily be removed without scandal is admitted, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Per tuas*, rh. fin., at *Item verum* and following, and at *Ille quidem*, when proceedings are civil and under the temperance of equity not to depose from the order but to remove from administration, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Per tuas* and at *Utrum is*. Therefore such persons are rightly admitted in such a manner, since they are removed for lighter causes, just as the approved custom of certain religious orders requires, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Per tuas*, §fin., and \[Extra\] *de appellationibus*, *Quare nos*, and \[Extra\] *de accusationibus*, *Qualiter*, at the end. Therefore plainly an inquisition concerning regulars must proceed without uproar, since it is not fitting for the servants of God to be entangled in these subtleties. Yet it is true, if they raise such crimes, that those exceptions are to be reserved to them, and that thus no criminal witness — even if supported by corroborations — is admitted, as at \[Extra\] *de accusationibus*, *Olim*, 1, 2, and following, \[Extra\] *de statu monachorum*, cap. *Monachi*, §*Priores*. And so I say that the foregoing distinction is to be diligently observed: if even one element fails, I reject the witness. Thus I do not admit criminal persons without corroborations, even in the crime of simony, nor regulars of inferior grade.

¶ Those who testify from zeal for justice but with the goad of malignity I reject as capital enemies and conspirators, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Per tuas*, §*Nos vero*.

¶ Likewise, I reject the aforesaid if proceedings are for deposition from grade, or even if the crime is denounced by someone in order that he be removed from administration, since then proceedings must follow the rigor of law, as noted at \[Extra\] *de denuntiatione*, under the title *De accusationibus*.

¶ Likewise, I do not admit them if a secular accuses, or a regular of superior grade — for instance a bishop — or one of inferior grade of good reputation, or one who cannot be removed without scandal. Others say that this decretal is corrected by Binganus at \[Extra\] *de testibus*, *Testimonium*. But I say that those passages are to be understood according to the foregoing distinction. For indeed I reckon it absurd to suppose that lord Innocent would have wished to overturn with one word this decretal — so most equitable and so rational, devised by the many vigils of the lord doctor Innocent, father of the law — as noted at \[Extra\] *de clericis excommunicatis*, *Ecclesia*, 2, §*Denique*.

¶ Likewise, partakers are not admitted, as at \[Extra\] *de testibus*, *Veniens*, and cap. *Personas*, C. *de testibus*, *Quoniam*, C. 3 q. 4, capp. 1, 2, and 3, C. 2 q. 7, *Testes*. But on the contrary, \[Extra\] *de testibus*, *Extra mediatores*. But there the talk is of a mediator who is admitted; in *Veniens*, however, the talk is of one who is a partner and who is rejected.
