## Likewise the simoniac is compared to a thief

*s.q.s. ordinationes*, and in theft no distinction is made as to quantity. *xiiii.q.vi.c.fi.* Likewise simony is compared to the crime of usury, since both are committed by intention alone, as noted *s.co.6.qd sit*, and in usury no distinction is made — indeed it is committed even through a small cup of wine or by postponement, for whatever exceeds the principal is usury. *xiiii.q.iiii pleriqz.* Say \[that\] whenever something is given by pact, it is simony, and renunciation is necessary. But whenever, without a pact, the intention has been corrupted, it suffices to satisfy one’s Creator through penance, *j.c.si.* — where, since the corruption of the mind is not established, the circumstances are considered, concerning which you have *j.co.* and the questions according to Goffredus. Say as noted *s.e.s. quid sit. s.quat’r.ver.sed munus* and following.

## Likewise it is asked

whether one commits simony if something is given for a chalice or for vestments or for altar-linens to be blessed. And certain men say no, unless he finds this expressly stated — as is seen in the case of chrism, baptism, communion, the eucharist, balsam, burial, and the dedication of basilicas. *s.q.s. dictum* and *c. placuit. nullus*, and *c. placuit. vt vnusquisqz*, *j.e. cu i ecclesie.* And Bernardus wrote the same to brother Raymundus. But Goffredus notes \[that\] in any ecclesiastical mystery whatever simony can be committed, *j.c.ad nostram*, which I think more true.

## But whether

he who has acquired a benefice simoniacally is bound for all the fruits he has received and which could have been received. So it is, just as with the thief and robber. *s.q.s. ordinationes.* And therefore, just as the thief is always in delay and is held liable for damages — *C.qd me.causa.l.s. C.de condi. fur.l.s. C.de fur.subtracto* — so also for all the fruits, and as a violent possessor, *s.de resti.spo.grauis.* For generally one possessing without title is held liable for all losses and for fruits received and to be received. *ff.de rei ven.si nauis.S.generaliter.* The same holds if he has a title, but such a one as is to be reckoned as no title. *C. de agri. et censi. queadmodum. ff. quod met.causa. sed et partus.*

## “*Lingua caro*” etc.

That is, carnal entreaties which are feared or which one favors — these induce simony. Yet by these, if one promotes someone to a prelacy by his own prayers, especially when conferred by election, simony is committed. And in this case I understand \[that\] no man should take honor upon himself, but he who is called by God as Aaron, as *s.de ele.quat’r. viii.q.j. in scripturis. j.q.vj. sicut is* and *q.j. ordinationes.c.c. nullus itaqz.* For increases of honor should come to each man not by ambition but by labor, as *C. de re mili. publica lib. xii.* however worthy he may be, as *s. de insti.c. vlti.* and *de iure pa, per nostras*, and *de elec. per inquisitionem.* For a simple benefice, however, one may offer prayers on his own behalf, provided he is worthy,
