Whence it cannot be denied that even that expectation, which afterwards came entirely into existence, has been remitted; and thus it is true simony, since remitting is the same as giving: \[Extra\] eodem, *querelam*, and above *de testamentis*, *veniens*. For the bishop or presentator, in the very act of ordination or presentation, is bound to assign a title to the one ordained: as above *de prebendis*, *episcopus*, and c. *cum secundum* §. *aliquod commodum sentiret inde* — if, however, the pact were to be valid; and thus it is simoniacal, as has been expressed: argument from ff. *de calumniatoribus*, l. 1 at the end, and l. 2 and 3, R. 1. \[As to\] why the decretal of Gregorius is to be understood in the penitential forum and not the judicial — what he says is: let the ordainer and the ordained and the presentator who introduces and accepts such a pact beware; for although it be hidden and cannot be proved, nevertheless they suffer this penalty by the very act, by force of the canon, and thus by the law itself one is suspended.

## But if the crime cannot be proved

But if the crime cannot be proved, purgation is enjoined upon the one defamed; and if he fails in it, he is punished as one convicted: as below eodem, *de hoc*, and c. *insinuata*.

## What dispensation, and from whom, is to be applied in their regard

What dispensation, and from whom, is to be applied in their regard is here treated: whether one is a simoniac and was simoniacally promoted; or simoniacally promoted but not a simoniac; or a simoniac but not simoniacally promoted. Likewise, whether the simony is committed in the order or in the benefice.

## If someone is a simoniac in an order

If someone is a simoniac in an order — that is, because he gave money to be ordained subdeacon, or deacon, or priest — here the bishop cannot dispense: \[Extra\] eodem, *nobis*. For he cannot dispense except in adulteries and lesser crimes, as above *de iudiciis*, *at si clerici*. Indeed, according to some, he cannot even dispense with such a one, because the simoniac is bound to renounce that which he acquired simoniacally: as below q. 1, *si quis*.
