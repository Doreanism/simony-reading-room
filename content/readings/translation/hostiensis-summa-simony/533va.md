## It Has Been Said Above

It has been said above concerning accusations in general. And because [the decretals] propose to deal with crimes, he begins to treat of them, first placing the rubric on simony as on that crime in comparison with which others are counted as nothing, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Pertuas*, C. 2 q. ult. cap. ult., and at \[Extra\], eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Per tuas* §*Ceterasque*; whence it is even called heresy, as at C. 1 q. 4 §*Sed licet*. Yet sometimes other crimes are said to be greater in respect of the greater penalty imposed; and thus resolve \[the apparent contradiction\] at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Per tuas* at the end, and at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Licet* §*Praeterea* — for instance, the crime of *lèse-majesté*; but in truth it is the lesser, because it is more grievous to offend the divine majesty than the temporal, as at \[Extra\] *de haereticis*, *Ad abolendam* §*Credentes*. This is to be considered here.
