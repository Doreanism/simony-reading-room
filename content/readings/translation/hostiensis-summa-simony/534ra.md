\[…\] as was said, the Church does not judge concerning hidden things — as may be discussed below at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Quod de pecunia*. ¶ For a second reason, the above description does not seem good, because it implies that simony is committed without a deliberate desire to sell or buy spiritual things, which is false, as appears in what follows below. ¶ It seems to me that it can be described thus. ¶ † Simony is the receiving or giving of spiritual things, or of what is annexed to spiritual things, with a preceding pact of promise, of a gift, of any manner of service, of a price, or of any temporal thing whatsoever, as will appear below. ¶ I call “spiritual” the orders \[conferred\] by the Church, and any spiritual benefice that ought to be conferred freely and of necessity, as at \[Extra\] *de praebendis*, *Relatum*, and \[Extra\] *de officio archiepiscopi*, cap. ult. Annexed to spiritual things are the fruits arising from these spiritual benefices; and so if one of these is bought without which the other could not be acquired, neither is left unbought. For spiritual things without temporal goods avail nothing, just as the soul does not live a bodily life without the body, as at C. 1 q. 1, *Si quis obiecerit*. According to Damasus, I understand this when the fruits are sold in perpetuity, that is, the very right of receiving them, as at \[Extra\] *de rebus ecclesiae non alienandis*, *Ad audientiam*, C. 1 q. 1 *Si quis praebendas*, C. 1 q. ult. *Non satis*. It is otherwise if the fruits are sold for a time and not the very right of receiving them, as will be said below at \[Extra\] *Ne praelati vices suas*, vers. *Item praelatus*, *Sed quare*. But here too one must distinguish: whether someone buys the fruits in perpetuity from a prelate who has the power of conferring \[the benefice\] — and then it is unlawful — or from the one who has received the benefice and from whom he can buy the fruits in perpetuity, that is, for as long as the beneficed person lives, according to Laurentius, by argument from \[Extra\] *Ut beneficia ecclesiastica sine diminutione conferantur*, cap. unic. at the end. And what the reason for the distinction is, see below at \[Extra\] *Ne praelati vices suas*, vers. *Sed quare*. ¶ Likewise, even a church may be given with a certain rent reserved upon it in perpetuity by common consent of the bishop and chapter, as at \[Extra\] *de religiosis domibus*, *Constitutus*, and \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], *Querelam*. But if a pact preceded the donation or the receipt that followed, then neither before nor after may anything be exacted or received; nor again, with no preceding pact, may anything be extorted violently under pretext of some custom — as if it should be customary to give something for pasture, or for pasturage, or for the *ros* \[dew\] or *calamus* \[reed\] without writing, as at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. 1, and C. 1 q. 3 *Si quis obiecerit*, *Si quis praebenda*. But what is freely offered may be freely accepted, as is proved in the case of the court at \[Extra\] eodem \[titulo\], cap. *Dilectus filius* and cap. *Et si quaestiones*, at the end. ¶ Or say that the rights of patronage are annexed to spiritual things, which are forbidden to be sold just as spiritual things are, as at \[Extra\] *de iure patronatus*, cap. *De iure*, cap. *Quia clerici*, and \[Extra\] *de rerum permutatione*, *Quaesitum*, and \[Extra\] *de iudiciis*, *Sto*. A thing is called annexed to the spiritual on account of a certain conformity, namely, that it falls to a layman and is possessed by a layman, since no other spiritual right falls to a layman except the right of burial (ff. *de mortuo inferendo*, l. 1) and the right of electing if his custom is confirmed by privilege, as at \[Extra\] *de iure patronatus*, *Nobis*, according to Bonifacius. The same holds also in many other spiritual rights granted by the pope, as at D. 22 §*Vetus*, C. 16 q. 1 *De decimis*, *A nobis*, and cap. *Tua*, and below. ¶ But annexed to the spiritual is also the right of receiving income by reason of a canonry or prelacy.
