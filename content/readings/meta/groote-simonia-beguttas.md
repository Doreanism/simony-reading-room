Geert Groote's treatise *De simonia ad beguttas* (c. 1380), addressed to the beguines on the question of whether it is simony to buy a place or prebend in a beguinage. Groote answers that, where any spiritual good — admission to the community, the habit, share in prayers, or the right to common life under a rule — is attached to the place, the purchase is indeed simony; he then unfolds the doctrine through a series of distinctions on what constitutes a spiritual good, the difference between buying the thing and buying the means to it, and the legal and moral remedies. The treatise survives in eastern Middle Dutch in fifteenth-century manuscripts from the IJssel and Cologne region, edited here by Rudolf Langenberg.
