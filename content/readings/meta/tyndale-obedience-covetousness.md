Tyndale's catalogue of the exactions by which the clergy fleece the realm. Beginning with their seizure of the tithe — "the tenth of all the realm" — he enumerates their temporal lands, mortuaries, offering-days, and privy tithes, and the endless fees for churchings, weddings, burials, hallowings, and priests' first masses. The indictment climaxes in his image of the clergy flaying the laity: "the parson shears, the vicar shaves, the parish priest polls, the friar scrapes, and the pardoner pares; we lack but a butcher to pull off the skin." He likens the spiritualty to horseleeches, caterpillars, and wolves in lambs' skins, to be known and judged by their works.
