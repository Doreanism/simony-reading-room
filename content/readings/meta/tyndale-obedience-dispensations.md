The close of Tyndale's section on the obedience of children, treating Christian matrimony. Tyndale argues that lawful marriage — made with the consent of parents and friends — may not be dissolved by the Church's vows and traditions, and he attacks the way the clergy separate those whom God has joined. Above all he assails the Pope's traffic in dispensations, releasing men from monastic profession and priestly vows for money, closing with the famous gibe that to sell a benefice outright is reckoned simony, but to resign it on a pension and redeem it again is held no simony at all: "O crafty jugglers and mockers with the word of God!"
