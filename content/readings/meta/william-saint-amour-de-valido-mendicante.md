A scholastic quæstio by William of Saint-Amour debating whether alms ought to be given to the able-bodied mendicant — that is, to one who is poor but capable of supporting himself by work. After arrays of scriptural and patristic arguments on both sides, William concludes that able-bodied beggars are not to be supported, since Scripture and the Fathers everywhere enjoin labor on those who can work; alms are owed only to the genuinely poor — the disabled, the aged, pilgrims, scholars, and others unable to earn their bread. The tract closes with a brief sub-question on whether preachers may beg, answered in the negative lest they fall into avarice, flattery, or simony.
