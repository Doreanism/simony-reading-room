The protocol of the Roman Lent Synod of 14–20 February 1076, preserved in Gregory VII's Register as III.10a. The synod is the climactic event of the early Investiture Controversy: it suspended Archbishop Siegfried of Mainz and the German bishops who had renounced obedience to Rome, excommunicated the Lombard bishops, deposed and excommunicated a series of transalpine bishops (including Hermann of Vienne "pro simonia, periuriis, sacrilegio, et apostasia…"), and finally suspended and excommunicated King Henry IV himself in a formal address to St. Peter ("Beate Petre, apostolorum princeps…"). The record contains the first use of the noun *simonia* in Gregory's Register and provides the canonical text of Henry IV's excommunication — the act that precipitated the king's penance at Canossa in January 1077. The synod is dated *anno MLXXV* in the manuscript, following the Pisan/Florentine calendar style in which the year began on 25 March; modern reckoning places it in 1076 (indiction XIV).
