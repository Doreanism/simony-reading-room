Duns Scotus's commentary on Distinction 25 of Book IV of Peter Lombard's *Sentences*, which treats the validity of orders conferred by heretics and the question of simony. Taking up Lombard's opening question on whether heretics cut off from the Church can validly confer the sacrament of orders, Scotus distinguishes the validity of the sacrament (which depends on the character of the minister and the form of the rite, not on his sanctity or orthodoxy) from the licit exercise of the power conferred. He then turns to simony, defining it as the deliberate will to buy or sell something spiritual or annexed to the spiritual for a temporal price, and examines its species, the three "gifts" (of hand, tongue, and service) by which it is committed, the validity of simoniacal ordinations, the penalties incurred, and special cases involving prelates and the pope. The Vivès edition prints the text with the marginal annotations of the Irish Franciscan Antonius Hiquaeus (Antoine Hickey, d. 1641), who supplies references to canon law, Suárez, and the Council of Trent.
