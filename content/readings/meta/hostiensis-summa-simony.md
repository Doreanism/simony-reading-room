Henry of Susa's title *De simonia* from Book V of his *Summa* on the Decretals of Gregory IX, the central canon-law treatment of simony by its greatest thirteenth-century systematiser. Hostiensis frames the title under his standard schema of questions: what simony is (its definition as the deliberate will to buy or sell spiritual things, or things annexed to the spiritual), why it is forbidden, how it is committed (through the hand, the tongue, or service — *a manu, a lingua, ab obsequio*), the penalties incurred, the obligation of restitution, dispensation and rehabilitation of simoniacs, the species of simoniacal pact and price, and adjacent crimes including extortion under colour of office (*concussio*). Throughout, Hostiensis weaves together Roman and canon-law authorities — the *Decretum* and *Decretales*, the *Codex* and *Digestum*, the glossators and his own predecessors — into the most influential canonist account of simony of the later Middle Ages.
